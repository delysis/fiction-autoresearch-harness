The gravel crackled as Jonah followed Mara out of Fulcrum's lobby. Streetlamps threw pools of uneven light over the grounds. Mara could tell that Jonah had already started reading her: every turn of her body, every change in posture. His expertise in subtle cue-reading was legendary among researchers, especially in understanding small social signals others would miss. She knew this walk was a live experiment for him, a chance to map how someone's physical expressions shifted after the intense pressure of one of their behavioral sessions.

Her neck muscles protested as she kept moving forward instead of curling in on herself. A sharp twist of her body and a deliberate pause, just to see if he noticed - to offer something clear without letting him interpret for her. His footfalls didn't shift pace; no nervous weight transfer to suggest he'd read the signal. She found that interesting, more than welcome. She wanted his reading without his protection, without any smooth phrases he could craft to soften the moment's awkward edge.
