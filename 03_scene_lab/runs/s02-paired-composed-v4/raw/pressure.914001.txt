“Your turn,” Livia said.

“For?” Mara asked.

“You called the channel untrustworthy. Now you get to show us how to make it useful.”

Mara moved one hand off her chair. The muscles in her back protested. She touched a pressure trace, enlarged it, and dragged it alongside the pulse. “You want an answer from these.”

“We want all answers. A reliable channel would be a useful discovery. A channel that can be corrupted would be an even better one.” Livia met Mara’s gaze without asking for a conclusion. “So would a method for telling the difference.”

Mara looked away at last. The screen’s faint heat on her fingers made it feel as if the data had tried to enter her and failed. “I need a cup of tea,” she said. “The kitchen was good to me this morning.”

Livia poured amber liquid from a ceramic carafe and slid the warm cup to Mara. “Make yourself at home.”

Mara took the cup without distance.

“Tea is a small price,” Livia said. “We know where to find the big one.”

The hand that held the cup remained steady. Mara could feel the room watching.

“What you know about me might be evidence you’re good.” She reached for the monitor again. “I came here to test how good.”

“The fear that your test will find something acceptable instead of exceptional.”

Mara put one hand over the other on her chair. “And you think I’d trust your data enough to take the risk?”

“If we’re as good as you hoped.”

The air under the lab door was colder than Mara remembered. Livia had not let go of her.
