 “Good to meet you,” she finished. “I’m Mara.”
He smiled. “Yeah, I got that part.”
They let go and started at each other.
He had a voice like summer rain on the roof of a cabin in the mountains, soft and warm and a little lazy. But he wasn’t lazy at all; Mara could feel it. The way he held himself, the way he moved. Even the way he grinned, a little crooked and a lot genuine, like he knew something you didn’t but was just happy to leave it there for you to find out.
His dark hair was long and shaggy around his face; Mara had to strain to see the sharp edge of the jaw beneath it. But she wanted to. He wore glasses with thick black rims; they made his eyes seem large and innocent when he smiled up at her like this.
And now those eyes were watching her again, taking her in.

Mara was suddenly self-conscious, aware of the way she stood and held herself. She felt exposed, vulnerable in a way she hadn’t since childhood when her mother had made her perform at parties for other grown-ups. But it wasn’t the same thing—not the same thing at all. There
