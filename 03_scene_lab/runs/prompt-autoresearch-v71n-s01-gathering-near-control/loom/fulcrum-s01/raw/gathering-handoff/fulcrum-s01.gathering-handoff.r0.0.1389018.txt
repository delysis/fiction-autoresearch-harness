
Jonah seemed to recover first; he dropped her hand and gestured with a flourish toward the rest of the common room.

“This is where all the magic happens,” he declared.
She blinked. “I see,” she managed to say. “And you’ve already—?”
“What? Been here? No,” he said, “not quite.” He grinned again. “It’s my first time too.”
So they were two newcomers then, together but still strangers. She hoped that the tension in her jaw and the blank look on her face would be temporary, until she could figure out how to begin.
Jonah tilted his head a little closer and murmured, “This feels pretty weird, doesn’t it?”

The sudden proximity startled her a little—she stepped back as he bent forward—and the words surprised her as well, because this wasn’t what she’d expected at all. Jonah didn’t seem the type to admit to feeling anything out of the ordinary, even if that was exactly how she had been feeling herself.
She nodded. “I guess,” she replied, and then looked away before she could catch any further glimpses of him through those dark-rimmed glasses.
