 She smiled and stepped in.
He didn’t say anything, but held up his half-full glass as she moved past him, across the room to get her own. His dark eyes stayed on hers for the whole time.
There was water with slices of lemon floating in it. And milk cartons and sliced oranges and a bowl of granola bars in plastic wrappers.
She poured some water into a paper cup and carried it back to him, settling the glass with the little thud of a set-down before sitting down in the chair across from him.
The younger man looked at the empty cup and smiled back. He was very pretty—his face, his body, his gestures, his soft skin and dark eyes and blond hair that fell across one side of his face when he leaned forward. He looked like a dancer.
She knew enough not to ask his name, but she was still new to the conversational rhythm of the Fulcrum people around her. Her first move would be to pick up on the mood of the room, let him open with the first move, see whether he was going to say something or she was going to say something or whether there would be a long silence before either said anything.
“I’m Mara,” she said. “You must be one of the newcomers.”
He looked at her in mild surprise for a moment, his eyebrows drawing together over the bridge of his nose. “Yes,” he said. “But I’m not new. I’m Jonah. I arrived here two days ago. It’s just that you were—”
A young man came in.
“Excuse me, I’m sorry,” the new person said quickly, setting down a full tray of glasses on the table beside Jonah and Mara before he looked up at them. He smiled at their expressions, laughed at himself for laughing, and set his hands on his hips in apology as Jonah’s eyebrows went up. “Sorry,” he said again. His voice was high, like he was talking to a child who might have an outburst any moment.
“It’s fine,” Jonah said.
Mara looked around the room at the other newcomers and staff who had already come in to talk for a moment before going to dinner, or to find their rooms for the next month. She looked out the window at the night outside and back again to Jonah, who was looking at her with an expression she couldn’t interpret.
“Have you been here before?” he said. “I can’t quite remember. But you must know people here.”
“I don’t remember anyone seeing me,” she said, aware that Adrian must have told everyone not to recognize her; she smiled again, to lighten the tension in Jonah’s face. “Are you always this jumpy?”
A moment of confusion crossed his face, and he blinked rapidly. “You’re saying…it was as though I were a…a frog? Did you do that on purpose?” He sounded skeptical, even suspicious, like he had some idea that she had done something to him deliberately.
She looked around the room at the other people who had come in—and was surprised to see one of them go past the kitchen door, through the back entrance and into the yard. What was he doing? Was he trying to get away from the rest? She tried to remember how far out in the mountains they were.
“Did you do that on purpose?” he repeated. “To make me look like a frog?”
