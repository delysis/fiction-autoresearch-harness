

Simon stood, then sat again after opening the bedcover. "You want to change?"

"Yes."

Their clothes fell together but did not touch.

Simon knelt and found Esther's wrist. Her pulse matched the slow pace of their last four decisions. He drew her hand to where it could find him.

The first touch traced scars; the second held weight; the third followed breath.

When Esther opened herself, she could hear Simon's response. His entrance was careful but certain; his exit left no marks but wetness.

Their bodies spoke through the silence: one voice in steady rhythm; the other responding in rising and falling waves. The space between them filled with sounds they both recognized—water meeting stone, wind passing through branches, breath escaping lips.

After, they lay together while Simon retrieved the record sheet from his pocket. The last decision had been made before Esther left the house. Their latest conversation appeared unrecorded.

She took the sheet and read their joined handwriting. "Our house is secure," she said aloud. "The drive's broken seal marks our risk."

Simon moved closer, touching her shoulder before speaking. "Do you still need to change tonight?" he asked.

Esther ran her finger along Simon's chest, counting scars. "Not tonight," she replied. "But tomorrow I want you to meet the woman who helped me understand how a false alarm could serve two masters."

He looked toward the table where their clothes waited. "And our conversation about risk?" he asked.

"Is already written here." Esther raised their record for Simon to see. The sheet now showed two names in the decision column.

"Tomorrow we'll talk about trusting what we can't explain," Simon said.

Esther turned to face him fully. "I don't need to explain it tonight." Her hand went to his mouth, then his chest. "The change happened when you touched me first and let me take charge of the pace."

He moved his hand from her shoulder to where she could feel the record sheet through her dress. "I didn't know that would happen," he admitted.

"Neither did I," she said. "But I saw what the broken seal meant before you opened it."

"And you decided to let me learn through touch instead of words."

"Yes."

Simon put his other hand against hers where it rested over their written decision. "Shall we tell the record?"

"Yes."

She watched Simon write another joint decision in their operational record. His hand remained on hers throughout.
