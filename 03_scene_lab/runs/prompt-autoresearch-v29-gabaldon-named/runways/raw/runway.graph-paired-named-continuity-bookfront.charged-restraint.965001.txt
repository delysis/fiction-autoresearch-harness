The lab door opened as Jonah pushed me out from behind, his hand on my back, a gesture so intimate it should have made my face burn—or I should have pushed him away. He hadn't said anything, hadn't even looked at me.

"See you in the morning," he said, his voice tight with unexpressed anger. He took off his glasses and stuck them into the breast pocket of his jacket.

He didn't look at me.

He took his jacket and hung it on a peg by the door.

I watched him.

I wanted him to look at me.

He unzipped his leather jacket and hung it up, his back to me. I looked down at his hands, tanned, a little calloused, slightly freckled and pink above the tanned palms. His fingers were long and supple, with strong nails—almost a perfect hand, but not quite—one finger was slightly crooked and the nails had been bitten, at some time or other, but not lately. I wondered if he bit them when he was stressed or if he'd just done it when he was a child. I'd always had a weakness for bitten fingernails.
