"Why don't we go for a walk?" Jonah said.

"I don't want to." I tried to focus on my words as he spoke, but his voice was pulling me down, his low tones weaving in the air, so that my thoughts chased the echoes and I found my tongue wandering.

"Why not? It's a lovely evening."

I realized he was standing in front of me. I pulled in my stomach reflexively—he was always making me think I might have a roll of fat over my waistband, when I was sure that I didn't—and focused on the back of his shirt, at a distance of six inches. The button-down collar was worn and soft; there were loose threads around the buttonholes, and the last button on the front of the shirt was missing, leaving the tail of his shirt flapping up the left side of his fly. I could feel his breath, cool from the air conditioning and the walk from his lab.

I pulled my eyes up past the collar points, and saw his eyes, dark as coal with a fringe of pale eyelashes. They were waiting, half closed in speculation, and I knew he could feel the weight of my eyes as well as my breath.

I felt hot.
