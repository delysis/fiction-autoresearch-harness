Fulcrum’s front door swung closed and Jonah waited, shoulder-to-shoulder with her. He could have taken the elevator from the secure second floor, but he had followed her. He watched the evening light wash their shadows across the stone steps and flagstone courtyard. The sky was a clear blue dome, speckled with stars.

She wanted to ask what he was doing there. She was annoyed and hungry, and not sure she wanted his help, but her mind was already distracted with other things. His nearness shifted her from a sense of isolation. When she touched him, she felt an immediate and powerful shift into connection, both physical and emotional. Her pulse quickened in the middle of her chest. She could feel his awareness of her, his intent. His attention was a palpable presence she wanted to wrap herself in.

She had not anticipated that kind of contact, not here, not after today. His proximity changed her. Her gaze caught on the sharp lines of his jaw and the lean muscles of his arms, his broad chest. Her senses sharpened. The air smelled of wet pavement and new rain, and his warm, spicy scent mingled with the fresh and earthy tang of the forest.
