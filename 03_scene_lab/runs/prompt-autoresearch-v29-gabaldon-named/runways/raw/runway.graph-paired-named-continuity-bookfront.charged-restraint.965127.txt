Fulcrum House stood on the high edge of the city, a stately four-story edifice of local volcanic stone. The street level held the offices and laboratories of Fulcrum’s staff. The second floor was taken up with common areas, kitchens, a lounge and library, and offices for students and senior fellows. The third and fourth floors were residences for those who chose to live in Fulcrum, and the fifth, which occupied only the rear half of the house, was the private apartment of its director and founder, Dr. William Fulcrum.

At Fulcrum, students and staff alike could take as long as they liked over their evening meal, but no one ever stayed long enough to become sleepy. Classes ended at five. After that, the house was a hive of activity, the noise from kitchens, offices, and social areas a constant background.

The late session lasted from ten until eleven, an hour when the house was much quieter and the background noise had dropped to a murmur.

Not that there was much conversation in the late session tonight, which was more or less a disaster. The students were clearly in need of a good deal more practice in human contact, and my own ability to focus on the exercise was scattered.
