 She waited, but his footsteps didn’t stop. He didn’t slow down.
“Jonah! Wait.”
There was no other sound; there was no one else there to hear her. No one else was awake.
Jonah stopped. She heard the sound of his feet scuffing on gravel. Her eyes followed his shadow away from hers to the far side of the sidewalk, and his breath made a white cloud when he breathed out.
Mara didn’t look directly at him. “I think I left my coat in there.” Her voice was high and tight. She breathed, and her shoulders moved, but her back didn’t change.
“Oh.” He breathed, and she heard the sound of his lungs inflating and deflating. “Do you want to go back for it?”
She nodded; she didn’t move. Jonah nodded too.
“Want me to walk with you?” He held his arms by his sides, and she could see his breath from here.
She nodded again. There was nothing else to say. She had no words left. Her coat hung on her arm; it had been there all along. It didn’t make her feel any warmer.
Jonah didn’t move. He stood sideways, looking into the distance beyond the light over Fulcrum’s entrance, just like before.
She said, “Yeah.” He looked down at her without speaking. His shoulders shifted slightly; his hands flexed. He said, “Yeah. Okay,” and turned toward the darkness again.
“Okay,” she repeated. She started walking with him, but didn’t speak again.
