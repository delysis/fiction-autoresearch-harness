
Jonah stopped but did not turn back. She could hear his breath clearly through the silence. His body shook with one sudden burst of air, but it didn’t make any sound, as though he were laughing to himself in the darkness.
Mara waited for Jonah to come back and speak to her. He stood motionless where he was, waiting for Mara to follow him. She heard the soft scuff of his boots against the sidewalk and saw them through the glow of the streetlight.
She caught up with him and started talking without pausing to think. “I can’t—” She broke off. They were standing on the sidewalk across from her hotel, in front of a closed Indian restaurant whose doors she wouldn’t see open until tomorrow morning. She wanted to say something more, but nothing came into her head. “Thanks for not leaving me behind. I’ll—I’m okay.”
Jonah stood with both his hands inside his jacket pockets. His face was in the shadows, and she couldn’t see if he smiled or frowned. She felt a sudden hot flash in her chest and wanted to say, I don’t think you believe me, or at least she imagined that was what Jonah was thinking.
The night was very still. The air smelled of rain and wet earth and diesel fuel from the distant highway. Mara was suddenly cold and looked down at the sidewalk where their feet were side by side. Her feet felt warm in her thick wool socks inside her boots.
Jonah said, “Mara.”
It wasn’t a question or a statement or even a greeting—it was his name for her—and it was a sound that made Mara’s body tense with a sudden rush of warmth through her limbs as she took a deep breath and looked up to see Jonah’s face. His eyes were dark against his pale skin, and his black eyebrows lifted slightly as she met his gaze and held it, not moving.
The streetlamp was directly overhead, and their shadows fell across their feet and knees on the pavement at their feet. Jonah’s shadow seemed darker than Mara’s and taller by a few inches; his right hand rested on top of hers, and her thumb lay against his. His fingers were long and slender and warm, and Mara saw that they both were looking at the shadows on the ground between them.
She let her breath out in one smooth exhale and felt him relax against her, moving closer without touching. He lifted his left hand to his face and brushed some loose hairs from his eyes with his fingertips; she saw his long eyelashes move as his gaze followed his fingers, then flicked up to catch hers. It was the first time tonight that either of them had moved in front of the other, though they had stood still, waiting for each other, all day long.
