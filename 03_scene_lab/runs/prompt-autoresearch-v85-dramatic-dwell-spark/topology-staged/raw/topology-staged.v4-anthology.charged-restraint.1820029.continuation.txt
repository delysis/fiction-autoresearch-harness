
“What’s wrong?” he said. His voice sounded far away, though it was beside her, close to her ear.
“I don’t know,” Mara said. She didn’t know how to answer that question. “Wait for me. I’m sorry I made you wait.”
His steps had stopped on the gravel behind her. He said nothing; he must be waiting. Mara closed her eyes and tried to feel what her body wanted to do. She wished her arms would stop aching, or at least move with something like purpose. She couldn’t feel her own body; everything hurt too much to make any sensation register as useful. She didn’t want him to go away again.
“Come walk with me,” she said, opening her eyes. She turned to face Jonah’s back. She wished he would turn and look at her.
He didn’t look. Instead he came toward her and stopped, facing her. “I’m ready to walk with you. But I’m not going to make you talk right now.”
That was better than walking alone, but it wasn’t what she wanted. She hesitated, then put her hand on Jonah’s arm. He didn’t stop her. His arm felt warm. Mara rested her head against his shoulder for one breath. His skin smelled of sweat and soap. It didn’t smell like sex or cigarettes.
“I’m ready to go now.” Mara turned and started down the road. Her voice made a ringing noise in her skull when she spoke. The sound faded quickly in the open air; Jonah’s feet behind hers made a different noise pattern. She could still hear him. Her eyes adjusted to the light, and she began to see where their footprints had marked the gravel. She walked faster, trying to keep up with herself.
