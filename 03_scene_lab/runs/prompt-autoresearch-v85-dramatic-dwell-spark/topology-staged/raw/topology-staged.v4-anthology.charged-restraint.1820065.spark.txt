
Mara started after him, feeling as though she had forgotten how to walk. The cold air stung her face and chest, but she still couldn’t feel her lungs. She walked behind Jonah, breathing with him instead, a step behind and a beat behind, a second copy of his stride, a heartbeat after. He was an ordinary man with a wide chest and long legs, breathing the cold night air and taking slow steps on the cracked pavement.
He wasn’t looking at her or at anything else. He was staring straight ahead with a blank expression. His hair was dark blue under the streetlight. He had left it long again. It was very nearly shoulder-length and almost as shaggy as in his first published photo. His face still looked like it had then: too young to be serious, though it was a few years older now. She caught herself trying to stare, to memorize him before he changed again. She blinked instead, trying to breathe without him, but his timing had her lungs and she couldn’t escape. He didn’t look back or speed up or say anything; he didn’t do anything that would give her a new breath of her own. She followed him across the drive and out past the gate, a half-step behind and a heartbeat slower.
He stopped before the edge of the sidewalk, but she had stopped when he stopped. She stood beside him, not daring to look, staring instead at the dark street and empty cars, watching his shoulders rise and fall. He was breathing slowly and quietly enough that she could see his breathing more easily than she could hear it, a regular motion that pulled in air from the night and kept her lungs from hers. She couldn’t find any other rhythm in herself to break away or break his hold. She tried to look away, to study the sidewalk, but it didn’t matter—she couldn’t look at anything else because she couldn’t breathe unless he breathed.
He was breathing too fast now. Her mouth felt dry and her body was tightening. Her skin was prickling, her thighs cramping. She closed her eyes and concentrated on breathing and relaxing her muscles until her lungs and calves stopped shaking. She opened her eyes to see Jonah turning left onto the sidewalk and walking without waiting for her. She watched him until she could start again, one foot after the other, a step behind, a breath behind, trying not to look and trying not to think about anything.
It was hard to follow his pace without looking. She walked too fast, then stopped abruptly to keep from passing him, then started too fast again, almost stumbled, and slowed down too much so that she was three or four steps behind, trying to keep his pace while staying in the same spot. His breathing was slowing, and her lungs were moving faster and faster while her muscles turned to stone.
