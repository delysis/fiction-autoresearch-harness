 The streetlights in the parking lot threw long shadows across his body, making his clothes look lighter and his skin darker than he had looked inside Fulcrum. Mara looked at the back of him for a few minutes without moving. He was walking very slowly, but he seemed to be in no rush. Maybe he was thinking about something else, some other problem she didn’t know about or couldn’t see.
She hadn’t noticed him when he entered the Fulcrum reception area this morning. But after she had left him alone there and walked back into the lab to set up for the day, he had become an invisible presence, his image reflected in the lab windows from outside and in the curved mirrors on the lab walls from inside. The reflection had felt like a camera’s eye.
The memory made her skin prickle, and she reached up automatically, scratching her neck. He had been here since this morning. No wonder the session had gone so badly. Maybe she should have asked him to wait outside. She had noticed him at some level all day even before the Late Session began. His body had entered her field of perception even before his voice did. His face wasn’t memorable, but she could tell something about his posture and physical bearing just from watching him move. The effect of that kind of noticing was an electric awareness of the space around her: a pressure on the backs of her ears and neck; a heat in the palm of one hand and a sudden tightness in her back, like the pain from a bruise.
She shook her head to clear it and felt the same heat in her chest. She didn’t want to be distracted anymore. Not tonight, when she had almost failed. The heat spread down her left arm toward the hand she had just used to scratch her neck, but she ignored it. Her body wasn’t the problem tonight.
Her mind was, she thought, as she started walking down the steps. She was tired and shaky. Her body wasn’t the problem right now. And yet—her muscles felt tight. Her knees were stiff and her back hurt from too much standing; it would probably be sore tomorrow. Maybe she should have sat down before she began tonight. She had needed a break after the session yesterday, but she had thought she could make it through today without sitting. She hadn’t thought about the Late Session until the last minute. She shouldn’t have ignored it. She should have checked in with herself.
Jonah moved without thinking; he wasn’t aware of his own body the way Mara was. She had to remind herself to check her posture, to breathe, to move her head. It was difficult for her to do these things. But she did them well enough. He would probably notice if she didn’t.
