 His shirt-sleeves were rolled up, showing slim forearms with dark, nearly black hair.
She followed him.
Jonah walked with his eyes fixed straight ahead. His neck was tight and straight. Mara moved beside him without touching him. He made no effort to turn. She watched the slow motion of his shoulders. His breathing was steady, but it sounded labored; a strained noise between each breath. It was harder to hear over the crunch of gravel under their shoes, but she listened until he breathed more quietly.
She heard the low murmur of his voice ahead and saw his lips move as he said good night, but she was too far back to hear him speak. She’d fallen behind without thinking. Jonah had slowed down so gradually that she hadn’t noticed. He’d been in motion for a long time before she realized that he was walking too fast for her to keep up.
He stepped off the gravel path and onto a soft, muddy trail that wound through trees along a dark ravine. She stopped in the middle of the path, just inside the light of a streetlight.
“Jonah.” She said his name aloud, very loudly. “Wait.”
