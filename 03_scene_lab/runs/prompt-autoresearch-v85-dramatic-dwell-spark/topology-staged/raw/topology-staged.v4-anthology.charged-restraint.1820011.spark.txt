 Jonah moved fluidly, easily. It was all she could do to put one foot in front of the other.
She followed, watching the movement of his body. When they reached the bottom, he stopped at the drive and turned right, following the narrow path that led along the side of the Institute grounds. She wasn’t looking where she was going; her eyes were fixed on him instead, his back, his shoulders, the sweep of his arm as he touched a bush.
His hands were big. Not long fingers but broad at the joints. The palms were not visible; he was wearing gloves, though it wasn’t that cold. Her hands were stiff and aching. She’d noticed him watching her hands earlier in the evening.
She walked along behind him and didn’t know where he was taking her. It was late; she couldn’t remember whether he’d invited her to dinner, or had only talked about it. The walk home, anyway. Not far to Fulcrum; they were staying at a small hotel in the next block. The Institute owned the whole top floor and the adjoining cottages. Everyone took rooms there.
There was another Fulcrum building beyond the Institute’s drive. A cottage with a narrow door and one window. She stopped short, looking for it, then started after Jonah again. Her chest felt tight. She stopped again. Jonah hadn’t paused, just walked on by, taking the path between the front gate and the cottage.
She tried to take a deep breath and couldn’t, so she stopped trying. It was an odd thing not to want to breathe. But she couldn’t force her lungs to work. She leaned against a bush, fighting for air. She was alone in the darkness, cold and unable to breathe. The bushes were dry, rough under her fingers. No sound but the wind. Her eyes were open but she could see nothing. Her heart pounded at her ribs. It wasn’t like her to feel this helpless, and she couldn’t make it stop.
She was losing ground. Jonah was going too fast, his stride so much longer than hers. She had to catch up.
“Jonah!” she called, pushing herself away from the bushes.
He stopped dead in his tracks. “Jonah!” she said again, a little louder; her chest felt tight and hollow, as though he might fade away and not hear. He didn’t answer. He just stood there on the path, waiting. His body didn’t move, didn’t twitch. She watched for some sign of response, anything.
But Jonah was perfectly still, his dark head turned slightly to one side, listening. The night air moved his long hair so that she could see the shape of his face, his jawline and cheekbone in the starlight. He hadn’t stopped at her first call; only when she spoke again. Was he listening, waiting to hear a second time? Her breath came easier now as she hurried past him
