
She went with him, but slowly, letting her legs make her body heavier so she would be one step behind him.
Mara wanted him not to go faster. She wanted to watch how he breathed. Her eyes followed the movement of his hips through the thin denim jeans he wore over black thermal underwear. Mara closed her eyes for one beat and heard the gravel under their feet. His feet made a different sound than hers did; his steps were spaced differently. Mara opened her eyes and stared at the ground. She took deep breaths and slowed, and she let him move away in front of her.
Jonah’s footsteps didn’t falter or slow. She saw his breath as a white cloud, moving with each breath into the cold air.
Her legs went numb below her knees. She realized she hadn’t seen his breath for several seconds, though he must still be breathing. He was gone.
Mara stopped. The sound of Jonah’s footsteps faded away from her left. It made a high, vanishing ring inside her head. “Jonah!” she called, and the sound of her own voice was deafening.
His footsteps stopped instantly and he said her name. “Mara?” The voice came from somewhere to the right of where she stood.
“Wait for me.” Mara’s throat hurt as she spoke; it was as though she hadn’t used her voice for a long time. Jonah stopped immediately. He walked to her side and stood quietly near her. It was like an ordinary pause, as though he waited for her to catch up to him instead of being miles ahead. He smelled of wood and salt. His coat smelled of tobacco.
