The brown-haired man glanced once over his shoulder, saw them standing there, nodded hello, and stepped right into the gathering, holding up his glass of wine—a half-full plastic cup with the Fulcrum logo on it. “Cheers.” He set it on the table against the wall to the left of the door, and stepped forward to shake Jonah’s hand as they exchanged names.

And when he turned away from that handshake, Mara noticed that his own face had darkened.
For one of his kind, he was tall. But his broad frame and soft brown eyes contrasted with the harshness around his mouth; his skin was dark, his hair black and close-cropped on the sides, and his beard thick but carefully trimmed. He was wearing a black T-shirt that covered up all of his arms but left most of his torso bare to show off a bit of flesh—and there it was, just barely visible above his collarbone: the tattoo.

“That’s Noah,” Jonah said as the brown-haired man stepped forward. “He’s been here a year.”
“Good to meet you,” Mara said. And they shook hands too.
“And this,” Jonah continued, gesturing at him with his free arm, “is what we call an associate—a Fulcrum member who comes here for regular work as well as the training sessions and other activities. He’s a grad student in psych, at UC Berkeley.”
Noah nodded at Mara in acknowledgement.
