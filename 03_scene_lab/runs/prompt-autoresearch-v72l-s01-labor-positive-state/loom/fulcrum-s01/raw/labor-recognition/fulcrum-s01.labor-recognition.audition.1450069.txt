And now that they were both turned sideways, Mara saw two others coming into the room from behind her: a pair of adults who looked like friends or family. She still had to find the people she'd come here to interview.

The newcomer nodded to Jonah and to his brown-haired friend in passing, heading for a low table on the other side of the room.
Mara noticed that his clothing was darker than the blond man's: a gray T-shirt and darker shorts, bare legs again but a little longer, and he moved with the long confident stride of someone who had done this many times before, going straight to a spot at the back of the room where he could lean against the padded wall with his elbows bent. His wife followed behind him and gave everyone a friendly smile, looking like someone's mother but younger, in a green T-shirt and shorts.

The man picked up one of the bottles from a metal basket on the floor beside his table, pulled the cork off, and poured two glasses with practiced grace. His wife stood back and watched him work.
Mara looked away and saw that no one else had entered; just the four of them in a quiet, wide, carpeted space with sofas and tables scattered across it.

Her eyes came back to her two interviewees: Jonah and the brown-haired man with a beard, both of them standing there on either side of her as she faced forward into an empty room.
Mara felt a sudden urge to move; she took one step forward and stopped again, then another two and sat down heavily at the same table with the married couple.
