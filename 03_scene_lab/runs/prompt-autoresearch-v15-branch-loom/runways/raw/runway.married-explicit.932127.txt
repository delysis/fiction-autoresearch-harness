The bedroom was cold, and Esther could see her breath in the dim light from the hallway. She pulled the duvet closer around her shoulders, shivering. Simon wasn’t there—hadn’t been, she realized, since they had reunited. Not in their bed together. Not in this room. He’d taken to sleeping in the guest room across the hall, claiming he couldn’t stand the noise of the city.
But Esther knew it wasn’t the traffic or the sirens that kept them apart at night. It was the weight of everything they’d left unsaid between them.
As if summoned by thought alone, Simon appeared in the doorway, wearing only pajama pants. His hair was tousled; one arm hung loosely at his side. He looked tired and haunted, as though he were carrying something too heavy to share.
“Hey,” he said softly, stepping into the room but keeping his distance. “You okay?”
Esther sighed inwardly, feeling both relieved and disappointed that he hadn’t come home with her that night—hadn’t returned from wherever he went without her. “I’m fine,” she replied curtly, though it wasn’t true. Nothing felt right anymore; nothing felt whole.
