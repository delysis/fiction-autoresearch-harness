    Jonah’s house looked like a gallery or an artist's studio rather than a private residence. A red and blue sculpture, a tangle of neon tubes, leaned against the living room window, lighting the interior with its own glow. The kitchen's glass shelves displayed a variety of knives, each a small work of art, gleaming from the lights above the cabinets. An abstract painting hung in the dining room; its colors caught Mara’s attention the instant she walked into the room.
    The guests came in ones and twos through the front and back door. They drifted up the stairs and through the rooms, examining art, tasting Jonah’s hors d’oeuvres, drinking wine and chatting in small groups. Mara felt her hands dampen and her pulse race, and forced herself to relax, to breathe and take in the sight of the gathering. The Fulcrum community was always fascinating and diverse. Some of the men, wearing carefully chosen clothes that revealed just the right amount of skin, looked like models or gym instructors. The women were usually dressed in black, with hair pulled up into an elegant chignon. They reminded Mara of the black-clad dancers she had seen at the Ballet Folklorico.
