With a wince, Esther held up the red, swelling lump between her two front teeth.

“Can you fix this?”

“Only if it was a real emergency.” The dental technician, a young man with a name tag that said “Adam,” looked from Simon to Esther, to the bump on Esther’s mouth, to Simon, and back. “Sorry, we’re not here for emergencies today.”

“Look,” Simon said. “I know you’ve already seen a hundred emergencies like this—”

The technician held up an index finger.

“—but she just got off an airplane. It’s been a hell of a week for her. We’re supposed to have been in Florida, celebrating—”

“What does it feel like?”

Esther opened her mouth and looked at Adam with her mouth open.

He raised both hands, palm up.

Esther wove her tongue back and forth, and she found the bump. It felt like a long-fingered hand digging into her gums.

“What did you eat?” Adam asked. “How did it happen?”
