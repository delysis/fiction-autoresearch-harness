She stepped back, took a breath, and then closed her eyes as a kind of overture. Her posture and facial expression tightened and relaxed like waves, seeking a rhythm. This time her voice came out high and light, a soft smile in the sound as the words landed. She said, "I see that you've changed the way you stand."

He said, "What do you see?"

She said, "Your left foot is facing further out."

He nodded.

"You're carrying your head more to the right. I don't mean turned, I mean . . ."

"Shifted," he said, his voice gentle and encouraging.

"Shifted," she echoed, "yes."

A tiny gap opened between them, and closed again as she shifted in space to make it so. This time he was the one to step forward; her shoulder tilted as he went by, a visible but unspoken answer.

She said, "I see that you're putting a little more weight onto the back leg."

He nodded, his eyes on hers. The game, if it could still be called that, was changing; both of them could feel it, could see it in the other's face.
