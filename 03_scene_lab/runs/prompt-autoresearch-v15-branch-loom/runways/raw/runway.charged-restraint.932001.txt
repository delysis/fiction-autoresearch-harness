A flash of pain in her temple brought her back from whatever haze her body had been trying to escape into. Mara blinked the tears back from the corner of her eye and looked up. Dr. Gray’s back was already halfway to the door, his long, neat-tailored lab coat swinging out with each step. He did not like to be kept waiting, even for a second, and in her state it took at least four seconds to unfreeze her aching body from the chair where she’d been forced to sit for almost twelve hours.

She pushed herself up by planting her hands firmly in the seat and rocked onto her heels, bracing herself for the dizzy rush that flooded her limbs as the blood started to flow freely once again. In front of her stood the boy, staring at her through those eyes of his that had the disconcerting effect of making her feel like a naked, bleeding creature under the gaze of a disinterested predator.

He did not help her. It had never occurred to him to help her, as far as Mara had been able to tell.
