It could be that this is an offering of friendship after a difficult day. It might be a professional kindness. It could be an apology. Or it might be the last thing she remembers before the world spins out from under her.

Jonah makes no move to take her hand, and the silence stretches until the moment threatens to break. She wonders, not for the first time, if this is the moment she will go mad.

The madness does not come. Instead, his lips press against hers in a way that makes her heart skip a beat; it might be because he's kissing her or because he's not. The kiss ends before it has begun, and she finds herself unable to look away from him. He holds her gaze for a moment longer than seems natural, then lets her hand drop back to her side.

She opens her mouth to speak, to thank him, to ask if he needs anything else from her tonight, or maybe even to ask what this means between them now that she knows he's capable of such a simple gesture of kindness without coercion or manipulation. He shakes his head slightly, as if to tell her no thanks are necessary, then turns away and leaves the building.

“Did you just kiss him?” a voice asks from behind Mara's left shoulder.

She whirls around, ready to defend herself, but finds herself face-to-face with an empty corridor. She blinks once, then twice, as if that will cause someone to appear.

Her breath catches in her throat again; this time it has nothing to do with any sort of madness. Someone is there, someone who has no reason to be. She knows because she can smell them; they are familiar and yet completely foreign. She can sense the way they move, but she cannot see them. They seem to move just beyond her vision, as if she is looking through a fog that hides their outline but does not obscure their presence entirely.

Mara steps back from the door and turns towards the stairs, away from wherever it was Jonah had been heading.

She is not sure how many flights of stairs she climbs before her legs give out and she collapses on the floor. Her head spins wildly, as if she has just run a marathon or had a heart attack. She gasps for breath, unable to move her arms or legs. It feels like someone has trapped her in a box without walls and filled it with air that refuses to fill her lungs.

Finally, after what seems like hours of struggling for each breath, the panic begins to subside. She manages to stand up, dizzy and unsteady but still standing. She looks around at the empty stairwell, wondering how many more doors she will need to open before she finds her way back into reality.

Mara stands there in the dark, waiting for her breath to slow, for her heart to stop pounding so loudly in her chest. The silence around her grows deeper and thicker as time passes. She hears only the distant hum of machinery and the occasional sound of footsteps echoing somewhere down below.

Eventually, the silence becomes too much for her to bear. She turns away from the stairwell and heads towards the door that leads out of the building.

A voice speaks behind her, but it is not one she recognizes. She whirls around, expecting to see the ghostly figure of Jonah or even herself standing there in the dark, but she sees nothing. Not a hint of light, not a trace of movement.
