

He tightens his grip as if to assure her he will not drop her; she returns the grip, almost reluctantly. The gesture seems to make their situation real—or unreal, since this does not look or feel like any normal part of their lives, but it is clearly not imaginary.

The weight of his body shifts closer, as if to push against her with his shoulder as they walk. Her own shoulder pushes back; they keep walking.

They pass the door to the break room and the corridor becomes narrower, the air close, the silence heavy. She feels his hand stiffen, and realizes that the grip on her hand has grown firmer.

“It’s almost eight,” she says. “Time to—”

The rest of her sentence is cut off by a sound from her own mouth. She makes no effort to pull away from him; instead, she feels his hand move up to cup her chin and turn her face towards him, the other tightening on her fingers as he draws her close and she leans into him.

He lets go of her chin and it is like falling, except that she lands firmly on both feet and his hands are still warm around hers. He makes a noise in his throat, a low sound of surprise and delight that startles her, then he draws her against him.

“Is that better?” he asks in a voice that sounds slightly raw; she has to blink twice before she can speak again.

“It’s more than better,” she says; she feels the warmth of his hand tighten against hers.

His breath stirs her hair; she looks up at him and meets his eyes for the first time, realizes that she has forgotten to look at him. He smiles—not a big smile, just a small, slightly lopsided smile—and his eyes soften, darkening. She looks back down again, afraid she has seen too much.

He doesn’t release her, and he doesn’t speak again. Instead, he pulls her closer and leans in, lips meeting hers.

She closes her eyes against the shock of him, feels his hand tighten briefly on her own, and then they part. He stands there, one hand still on hers as he waits for her to say something, looking at her as if he expects a reaction.

“I’m sorry,” she says, opening her eyes again. “I just…” She swallows hard, feeling the dryness of her throat.

He nods and lets go of her hand, turns to look down the empty hallway, but doesn’t move. His face is closed, though his expression is neutral; he seems to have gone from eager to guarded in the space of a single beat.

She wants to say something—anything—but can’t think of what that might be without sounding like a fool. She stands there, trying to remember how to breathe.

“I’m sorry,” she says again. “I shouldn’t have—”

He shakes his head and takes her hand, lifts it and kisses it softly. “It’s not that,” he says, his voice low and rough. “You don’t owe me an apology, Mara. You don’t owe me anything.”

She looks up at him, wondering what he means.

“I do, though,” she says.

He doesn’t respond; he just keeps looking at her.

She swallows hard, feeling the weight of his eyes on her face. “You saved my life.”

“Yes, I did,” he says. There’s something in his voice that makes her look away.
