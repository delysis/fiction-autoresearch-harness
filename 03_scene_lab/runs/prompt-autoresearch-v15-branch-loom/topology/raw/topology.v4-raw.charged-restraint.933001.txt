 The touch is warm and real, his palm dry. She feels the heat of him through the heavy cloth of her gloves. She cannot bring herself to move closer, so he steps forward to give her a solid shoulder to lean on, the weight of both of them pressing firmly into the linoleum.

“Can you?” she says. The question is as much about herself as about him.

He squeezes her hand. “Yes.”

They walk, slowly, each footfall an achievement, the air in her lungs like liquid helium, too light and too dense at the same time.

He opens the door, and she has to blink against the sudden brightness of the hall, the color, and movement and sound. The elevator dings and disgorges a crowd; they wait as a wave of people pours past them. She can feel his body heat behind her left shoulder.

When the elevator comes, it is not quite full. They board, but someone takes her elbow to give her a seat, and the motion throws her off balance. She is halfway down before he catches her. Her glove has come off, and she feels the burn of his fingers on her arm.

“Mara.” It’s a warning as much as a call to her attention. The elevator stops at floor twenty-two. The door slides open; a new crowd surges in, shoving her against him. Someone speaks in a loud voice, saying he doesn’t have a fucking dime and that’s what he wants, a fucking dime.

Her breathing stutters and starts; he presses his arm across her abdomen, his cheek into the tangle of her hair. His mouth is close, the sound loud enough for her to hear. She can feel the heat of his words through her clothes, on the side of her neck, and she has to press the back of her hand to the other side of her neck to cool it.

“You’re all right?”

The voice comes again from the man demanding a fucking dime; he smells like beer. Mara stares at the floor, her vision swimming.

“Yes.” It’s faint, almost lost in the noise of the crowd. He nods once, sharply, and she draws in a deep breath. They move with the flow of the crowd; someone jostles her from behind, but Jonah doesn’t move; his arm stays around her abdomen, his head against hers, his body heat pouring into hers. The door slides shut, and the crowd moves as one toward the back, making room for her. She can hear a voice from a few feet away.

“Can I take your gloves for you?”

He takes the proffered gloves from her hands, folds them together neatly, and hands them to her. They are soft, the leather like a warm, damp cat’s fur. The smell of beer is gone, but she can feel the heat of her blood moving through her veins.

He says nothing, just leans his head back against the elevator wall, one hand resting on his thigh. She tries not to look at his mouth, but she feels his eyes on her. She moves closer, stepping over the line from casual colleague to something else. He doesn’t protest or back away; he doesn’t smile or even raise an eyebrow.

The doors slide open on the lobby; the heat of the morning sun hits them like a furnace. He steps out of the elevator first and offers her his hand; she takes it, feeling the warmth of his skin through her gloves.
