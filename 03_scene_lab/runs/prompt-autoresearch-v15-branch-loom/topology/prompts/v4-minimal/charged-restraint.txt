MANUSCRIPT CONTINUATION
Mara and Jonah walk side by side, too close for casual colleagues and too far to make their closeness seem anything other than work. The corridor is nearly empty, the lab nearly silent. Neither says anything until the door shuts behind them; then his voice is too deep for a hallway, her own too low for him to hear.

“Here,” he says, holding out his hand. She pauses, the air like iron in her lungs.

He does not smile at her hesitation; his hand remains extended. He is, she decides, asking whether she wants his help, not offering it, and she can take it or leave it.

The decision is her own, so she takes it, carefully, as if to reject it would destroy the entire day, or month, or year.
