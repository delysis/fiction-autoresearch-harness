Jonah and Mara slipped out of the Fulcrum training space without incident, as they often did. No one said anything to stop them. No one asked what was wrong with them. No one asked where they were going or what they were doing. No one ever said anything or asked anything like that, ever. The staff of the training room didn’t care where they went or what they were up to—as long as they got their assignments completed on time.
That didn’t mean that everyone was okay with them leaving on foot. No. They would be a lot more okay with them leaving if they took a car, and they would be more okay still if they took a car after telling someone that they were going out to do something specific.
What their people wanted most of all, however, was for Mara and Jonah not to keep sneaking off to go who knew where and do who knew what, with no explanation or accountability. That’s not how it worked in their world. Mara and Jonah, though—they were special. They got special treatment. They had special needs.
That didn’t make their handlers any happier, though.
They were already outside, on the grounds. There was no one nearby. All of the other kids and instructors and security people and everyone else were indoors. It was a gorgeous summer day—not too hot, with a nice breeze. Everything looked green and lush in the sun.
