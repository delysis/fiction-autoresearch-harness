The room went dim after the explosion; it was like watching someone draw the shades. The light came from a wide band of crystal that ran along the center of the ceiling, the energy drawn by wires from a generator deep in the heart of the facility. The generator had been damaged, but it still produced enough to illuminate a fraction of the room. That light grew less by the second.

Jonah took stock, moving faster than Mara’s mind could process. He was alive; he’d survived the blast. She was alive too, and that meant he needed to move her out of the way. He picked her up with no sense of effort and held her body close, one arm under the knees, one braced around the shoulders. Her breathing was slow and shallow, almost as though she’d fainted.

He carried her the short distance to the exit from the lab and laid her down on the floor. She made a soft sound as he did so, and his eyes flickered to hers, seeking assurance. He found what he was looking for and smiled. The smile was enough to let him know that she was okay, and that was all the reassurance she needed.

She pulled herself together. “What the hell was that?” she asked. She felt sick; that wasn’t the noise a test was supposed to make, and it had blown everything in the room backward. “Did you get hurt?”
