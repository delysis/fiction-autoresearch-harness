I stepped onto the front steps of the institute in darkness; a black night, no moon, no stars through a dense blanket of clouds. A strong breeze blew down from the east, bearing with it the smell of damp soil and new growth. My breath smoked in front of my face, and I stamped my feet on the cold cement to keep them warm.
Jonah Vale leaned against one of the columns of the front porch, hands in the pockets of a tweed jacket. His fair hair was long enough that it lifted in the wind like a light breeze stirring on the surface of a shallow lake; his blue eyes were pale as ice. I looked at him through my own pale blue eyes and thought I saw nothing at all, but when I touched my hands to my cheeks I found them flushed. He tilted his head sideways, looking at me with an expression I couldn't interpret, but he didn't come toward me until I reached out for him.
"Are you okay?" he asked.
"Fine," I said, and stepped closer into the darkness. The breeze picked up again, blowing my hair across my face and into his. We touched hands without exchanging a word; his were warm and dry in the night air, mine cool and damp with perspiration.
"You don't look fine," he said.
"I'm not fine," I replied. "But I will be."
