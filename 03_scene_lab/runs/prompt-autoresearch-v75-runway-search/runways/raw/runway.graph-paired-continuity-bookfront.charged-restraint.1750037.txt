The session ended in confusion, with a question and no answer. We moved like sleepwalkers through the lab. We packed our bags, locked our desks, took off our badges and put them into their case for recharging. I stood near the coatrack and waited for Jonah. His back was to me as he straightened his jacket. He hadn’t spoken since the end. I doubted either of us was thinking of tomorrow already. We had both been too present just now, too lost in our separate lines of attention.
I slid the leather strap of my bag over my shoulder. It made a smooth sound across the tweed jacket. Jonah’s face turned toward mine for one second and went away again. No question. No offer. No invitation. We had both spent so much time holding questions back from each other tonight that an invitation would be too fast, too easy. I didn’t feel ready yet to talk about the session. I waited for the evening air to cool my face, my neck.
We walked out of the building together. It was cold on the steps. My body remembered the shock and warmth of the room. I felt a muscle relax in my lower back. My breathing deepened as we left the air-conditioning behind. I was looking into the darkness and thinking about it. No cars, no light from neighbors’ windows, nothing that showed this night was alive.
