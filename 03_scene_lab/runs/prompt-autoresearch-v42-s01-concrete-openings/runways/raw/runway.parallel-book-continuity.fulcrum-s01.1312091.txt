A rideshare drops Mara at the Fulcrum compound with one suitcase and
her backpack. She glances around as the Uber turns away, and she smiles.

The trees here, in what she knows is the town of Fulcrum, must be four
hundred feet tall. Some of the pines are five times taller than the
tallest building that she's ever lived in. In the city, there was a
park across from her apartment, but even the tallest trees there had
been less than three hundred feet high.

The trees here tower above the compound and dwarf its redwood-and-glass
buildings. But they are not the tallest ones in the town of Fulcrum.
She had passed far taller ones on her drive from San Francisco, some
well over four hundred feet tall. They looked like skyscrapers that
had been planted straight into the ground, without a foundation or a
lobby.

A long row of pines stretches for about two hundred feet beyond the
parking lot, then runs off to the right. The trees are much too close
to the Fulcrum compound to be safe, but they seem perfectly healthy and
alive. She will worry about them later. Right now, she is here because
the Fulbright program has sent her to Fulcrum.
