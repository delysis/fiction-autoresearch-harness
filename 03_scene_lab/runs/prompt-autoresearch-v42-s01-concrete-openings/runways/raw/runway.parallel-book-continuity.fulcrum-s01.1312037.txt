Mara entered the rideshare after waiting outside her apartment building
for twenty-four minutes, ten of them in a rain so brief and sudden she
had no time to put up her umbrella. The car felt hot and stuffy, as it
might when parked for a long time with the windows up. Mara opened both
the doors, and the smell of the previous passenger, faint and vaguely
familiar, leaked into the cool night air. The car didn't feel
especially clean; Mara didn't mind.

As the car pulled away from the curb, the driver asked, "How long you
been at Fulcrum?" His name was George. Mara told him she had just
arrived.

He laughed. "Just arrived? I bet you have been at least eight or ten
years. And you are about twenty-three." Mara could see that George took
pleasure in these kinds of statements, which he apparently made often,
even though they were seldom correct, even approximately.

"You're not quite right," said Mara. "I've just arrived."
