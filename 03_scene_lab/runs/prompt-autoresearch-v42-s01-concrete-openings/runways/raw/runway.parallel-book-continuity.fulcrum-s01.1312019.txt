It was easy to follow the GPS directions to Fulcrum, at least at first. I’d wanted to fly from New York and had my suitcase ready, but the sudden rainstorm and lightning had canceled all departures. After four more hours of waiting and hoping for a rescheduling, I’d finally bought a last-minute economy seat, with an aisle for me, a middle empty, and a window for a man who fell asleep after taking a tablet.

I’d felt sick through the entire flight from turbulence, motion, and boredom; my body had wanted to lie down instead of sitting upright. I’d seen too much of the man’s face even while he slept. My brain and eyes were tired, and my thoughts went round and round. It was just after 5 a.m. when I entered Fulcrum’s redwood and glass compound.

I’d gotten an earlier Lyft ride from my old family home in Palo Alto, through the usual dense residential sprawl that eventually gave way to open space with some distant hills that I’d seen often enough on my many previous trips but never been able to explore.
