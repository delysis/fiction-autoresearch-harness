 Jonah let go with a smile and gestured to the couch. “Here’s my spot, it’s all yours.”
It turned out he was a fellow visitor, an engineer from Georgia who would be leaving tomorrow night. That was all she could find out while they were alone.
The other two men, Jonah said, were Adrian and a man named Robert who had come up from Southern California. And a young woman would be coming too, “I’m afraid,” said Jonah, “but she’s wonderful.” They would be here by six thirty.
Mara was not afraid of the young woman. The thought that there would be more people here, any people, was already relaxing her. She sat down on the far end of the couch.
Jonah stood there for a while longer, leaning against the wall with his ankles crossed and watching her. He looked like he might take another seat, but he stayed where he was. They spoke about this and that: how long Jonah would be staying, how far each had come, what they knew about Fulcrum—but nothing much came of it. They were both just feeling their way.
Then Mara noticed that someone else had walked in and was standing in the doorway, watching them too.
A tall man, older, with short curly gray hair and a tweed jacket, stood there observing Mara and Jonah. He glanced down at Mara’s hand, still clutching the strap of her duffel bag, and raised an eyebrow. He said nothing.
He was probably the Robert that Jonah had mentioned.
Jonah saw Mara looking at him and turned to the doorway, where this other man remained standing quietly.
“Mara, Robert, Robert, Mara,” Jonah said with another grin, and then added, “Are you going to take a seat?”
It took Mara a moment to realize he was directing this question at Robert.
Robert looked around at the couch, at the low table, and nodded slightly before walking forward. “Thank you, Jonah,” he said.
He picked up his duffel from the doorway, and now Mara noticed that she too was still clutching hers. Robert came closer to where Mara was sitting, set down his bag, and took a seat in the other wingback chair. “I’ll be comfortable here, if you don’t mind,” he said with a gentle smile, his eyes moving from Jonah back to Mara.

She glanced back at the couch. If she shifted all the way down to Jonah’s side, she’d be able to see him too. But if she stayed where she was…
“No, that’s fine,” she said quickly, “that’s fine, don’t worry.”
She didn’t know if Robert had noticed her brief hesitation, and now she felt silly for hesitating at all. Maybe Jonah did notice—she couldn’t tell from his face.
But this was silly, anyway. She was here, among real people.
“Hi, Robert,” she said, standing up and turning so that he could see her. She hadn’t really had a proper look at him before; he was just an older man in a jacket. Now, though, she was impressed by the way he carried himself—straight-backed, with a calmness about him as he rested his arms on his knees.
