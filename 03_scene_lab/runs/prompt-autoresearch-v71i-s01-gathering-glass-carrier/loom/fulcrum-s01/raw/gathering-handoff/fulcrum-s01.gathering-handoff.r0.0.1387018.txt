
Jonah smiled and let go, taking a step back and turning toward the couch as he did so. “Come sit,” he said over his shoulder. She did.
She sat.

It was all a bit awkward. He had introduced himself like a guest and then treated her like one. She couldn’t figure out if that meant she wasn’t one, if it was just manners, or if they were both guests and they were supposed to be making this easy for each other.
Jonah had sat back down on the couch with his bare feet propped up again, not so relaxed as he’d been when she came in but not especially tense either. He wasn’t fidgeting at all; he was just sitting there, looking at her from across the low table. She wasn’t sure what to do next.

“You’re a bit young,” he commented in a conversational way, as if it were a fun thing for them to be able to talk about.
“You are too,” she said after a pause, not knowing quite how to feel about the remark.
He blinked at that and laughed. “Yeah, I suppose I am.”

There was a silence in which Jonah appeared to be considering something else to say.
After a minute, she cleared her throat. “Am I too young?”

He cocked his head to one side. “It depends who you ask,” he said, half-smiling. “What do _you_ think?”
She shrugged. “I don’t know,” she said in a conversational way. “You tell me.”

Jonah shook his head slightly at her response and turned to look away from her for a moment, up at the far end of the common room, where he’d been looking before she came in. He was scanning the empty space as if there were something there that needed checking; he glanced back toward her and then continued studying what seemed like an invisible wall.

Mara watched him intently until the sudden appearance of an elegant middle-aged man with salt-and-pepper hair at the far end of the common room made Jonah turn away from it without saying anything else to her. “Ah,” he murmured. “Well, nice meeting you, Mara.”

She looked around, blinked, and turned back to him. The handsome man was waving a hand as if to acknowledge them; Mara turned back toward Jonah with a little wave of her own.

Jonah grinned at her again, this time with something like amusement in his eyes, but before he could say anything else, the front door swung open and more people poured in behind him: a group of five or six, all carrying drinks. They were dressed up, dressed like professionals in dark-colored clothing, except for the woman who was wearing a light yellow dress and was already talking animatedly to someone from the other end of the common room.

Jonah had stood up again as soon as the new group arrived and moved away from her toward the door, still smiling at her over his shoulder but otherwise keeping his head turned away from her as if he were focused on getting the hell out of there before things got awkward again. Mara stared at him as he left, feeling slightly abandoned and suddenly very self-conscious; the man who’d walked in with the salt-and-pepper hair had started speaking to someone else near the couch on which Jonah had just sat down
