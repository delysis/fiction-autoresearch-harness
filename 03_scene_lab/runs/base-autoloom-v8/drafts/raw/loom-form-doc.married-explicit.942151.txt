 She did not look at him. "This was supposed to stay closed," she said quietly, her eyes on the window. A single branch moved in the windless air.

"I—"

"It's done," Esther said, her voice hardening. Her body followed suit. Muscles clenched along her jaw, throat, shoulders. "Don't apologize. You did what you thought was right."

Simon reached out, hesitated, then put a hand on her shoulder. The muscle was rigid under his palm. "I know you'd never forgive me if I left it shut," he said. "But you're right—it had to be done."

Esther shook her head. "Not right," she said. "It had to be done. The cost is different. And this doesn't change my mind about letting them in."

She paused, swallowing hard before continuing. "It was never a question of you not knowing. It was a question of not wanting to know." Her fingers drifted upward, touching the wet spot on her shirt collar where he had pressed it too hard. "I couldn't ask you to watch and then take it all on yourself. That wasn't fair. Not then, not now."

The branch outside tapped against the window. In the sudden darkness of a cloud passing overhead, Simon caught sight of his reflection—the shadow of his hand reaching for her neck, his open palm, his outline blurred by the movement of rain.

He lowered his hand. "What about you?" he asked. "Will you watch now, when I'm done?"

The question hung between them like the branch, bending but not breaking in the windless night. Esther looked at her reflection, then at him. "Yes," she said finally. "I will watch now. Because it matters that we see this together."

She turned away, moving toward the small workroom she'd set up when the team arrived. Simon followed her in silence, keeping a careful distance between them as she lit the lamp and began to unpack her tools. The hum of electricity filled the small space; the ticking clock in the hallway marked each passing second.

Simon reached out again, brushing his fingers against the curve of her shoulder as she leaned over the work surface. "I can help you," he said gently, "if you'd like."

Esther nodded, her eyes still focused on the half-completed diagram spread across the table. Together they opened the drive, moving carefully as the light flickered and dimmed in one room while staying bright in the other.

The workroom clock ticked behind them as they reassembled the device. When it was finally complete, Esther drew a deep breath and plugged it into the nearest socket. Simon felt a familiar weight settle onto his chest—the weight of all those names he'd tried to protect, the weight of keeping her secret, the weight of letting her bear it alone.

"There's one thing I never told you," Simon said softly as she turned back toward him. "About how this was always supposed to end."

Esther looked up from the device. Rain against the window blurred Simon's features; the dim light left his expression unreadable.

"What did you never tell me?" she asked quietly, her voice breaking on the last word.

Simon reached out and closed her fingers around the handle of the small knife she kept in her workroom pocket. "This was always meant to be a choice," he said. "Yours, at the end. Like we both agreed."

She felt his warmth through the fabric, his touch lighter than air yet heavy with meaning. The weight of the knife felt solid in her hand, as if it belonged there.

"Are you sure?" Esther whispered, her voice thick with emotion.

Simon nodded once, firm and resolute. "Positive," he said. His eyes held hers, steady and unwavering in their certainty. He knew her pain, he knew her strength, and he trusted her judgment more than anyone else's.

They stayed that way for a long moment—him looking into her eyes, her gaze fixed on his reflection in the rain-streaked window behind him. When Esther finally turned away, Simon could see the small smile tugging at one corner of her mouth.

"Okay," she said softly. "Let's do this together."

Simon watched as Esther carefully undressed, his gaze never leaving hers in the dim light. When she was ready, he knelt beside her on the bed and took the knife from her hand, setting it down within easy reach. His touch lingered on her skin as he helped arrange herself for comfort.

"You know I'm going to stay here," Esther said quietly, her fingers brushing the curve of his jaw. "Even after this."

Simon nodded, his own eyes misting over with unshed tears. "I know," he whispered, tracing patterns along her bare shoulder. "It's okay. You'll always be safe with me."

They remained motionless in their shared silence, Simon holding Esther close as the rain continued to fall outside. The faint rhythm of raindrops on glass punctuated their quiet intimacy until dawn broke through the clouds and cast its gentle light across the room.

The shadow of her knife traced a new path on his back as Simon left the room, moving carefully through the thin strip of light that marked the doorway. His hand touched Esther's bare shoulder briefly before he disappeared into darkness again. The clock in the hallway ticked steadily, counting out time until their next meeting.

Esther watched Simon step through the beam of light to enter another room. The dark wet spot on his shirt caught her eye, a reminder of that morning's intimacy and the choice they'd made together. She reached her hand towards him as he crossed the threshold—the gesture meant for more than just this moment, an invitation for their next decision.
