He crossed the room and set his hands on her shoulders, careful with the water-damaged prosthetic. "Yes."

She let herself be pulled to her feet. "Then why?"

"I was certain you'd give it back," she said, watching his other hand open at its limit.

The prosthetic clicked again. She dropped the book on his palm. The second time, it took Simon two clicks to release the new object—an empty water bottle with salt traces inside. His fingers kept moving without his willing them. The water bottle rolled across the table and stopped against an old photo of the two of them.

Esther took his good hand and guided it to the table, keeping it between them. Her nails were too long. He couldn't feel the cuticles beneath them, but he knew the shape by touch alone. She always kept that single nail longer than the rest.

"Show me," she said.

His hands hesitated for only a second. She closed his fingers over the photo, then another object: the envelope half-torn where water had loosened the seal. "You already saw it," she said softly.

The prosthesis clicked again.

Esther held the envelope out, then took his good hand in both of hers.

"Look at me," she said. "We have to look at them now."

"Yes," she whispered. "Together."

She set the candle on the table, and they watched the wax drip down while the room filled with their breaths.
