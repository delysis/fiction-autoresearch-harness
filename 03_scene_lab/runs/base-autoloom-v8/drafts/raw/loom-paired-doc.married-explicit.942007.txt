 The salt had dried between his fingers into white dust.
Esther came to him at the door, her eyes tracking up his wet body until they stopped. Her hand raised as if to touch his chest but hesitated halfway.
“You went in,” she said.
“Yes.”
“And left the breach open.”
“Yes.”
Silence held them where the current had washed them.
Esther closed her eyes and pressed a palm against his wet chest. His pulse marked her skin.
“I thought you’d failed,” he said.
She let out a breath he hadn’t realized she was holding. “I did.”
“That’s why you never told me how close you were.”
“Yes.”
“And that’s why I didn’t fight to go in myself.”
“I don’t know. Why didn’t you, Simon?”
He turned away. The drive waited under the lamp like an accusation.
Esther followed him with one slow step. She held his gaze a breath longer before dropping her hand, leaving the weight of a question she wouldn’t press.
“That’s what I wanted to know,” he said quietly.
She looked down at her own hands for so long he thought she might not answer. Then, very deliberately, she touched him. Not at his chest but along the line of the scar where his skin had knit back together, closing a wound deeper than skin alone.
Her touch brought back the taste of blood and salt in his mouth, the red blur that had become their life. Her fingers traced the length of it, a pale scar against dark skin under lamplight, and when she reached its end, she kissed his neck without ceremony or hesitation.
The contact was brief but absolute, and when it broke, it left a shape behind it that couldn’t be hidden.
“How is it?” he asked.
“Healed,” she said, her voice low. “But not forgotten.”
“Nor this,” he said, turning his back to show the bruises his ribs still bore from the final engagement.
She traced those marks with equal care, naming them one by one as if committing them to memory.
“This is from the blast,” she said against his skin where the rib protruded slightly more than flesh alone should manage.
He made a sound in his throat. “I’ve been better.”
“We’ve been better,” she corrected. “But we’re still whole, Simon.” Her voice had lost its edge.
Her hand moved across his chest to where the prosthetic met flesh, and this time he didn’t pull away. He’d shown her worse scars than these, though none that held such weight between them as the knowledge that either could have failed alone and neither wanted to bear that burden again.
“This is yours now too,” he said softly, turning his head to catch her eye as she met flesh with metal where no skin remained. “All of it.”
“Yes.” Her voice was barely more than breath against his ear. “That’s why we’re here now.”
Her hands moved down his body without hesitation. The scar was no boundary but a threshold he’d chosen to cross when he chose her again.
They stood in that quiet room while the water from the dive dried slowly on his skin. She touched him exactly where she’d said she would and nowhere else. Her palm found its way beneath his shirt until their bodies pressed together more firmly than words could manage.
“You know I’d have come with you,” he said against the curve of her shoulder as her fingers explored the length of his scar. “I wouldn’t have left you alone.”
“I know,” she whispered, her breath warm against his neck. “But you didn’t know then. Neither did I. That’s why we keep doing this—because it matters that we’re still here together.”
Her hands moved lower along his torso until they found the weight of his prosthetic again. Her touch was matter-of-fact yet reverent; there was no shame in what remained nor glory either—just fact held between them like an answer given without question.
When she looked up at him again through wet hair and salt-dried skin, there was nothing uncertain in her eyes. Her hands moved deliberately but gently across his ribs where bruises still marked his skin. Her touch traced each line with equal care whether scar tissue or fresh injury.
“I’m not afraid of these,” she said quietly, pressing a palm to the tender spot where one rib protruded slightly beyond flesh alone should manage, “because they’ll heal and you’ll still be here.”
“And I won’t be afraid of yours because we’ll have new ones together tomorrow,” he answered, catching her wrist before she could pull away again.
Her smile held more weight than words could bear alone. The room remained dim except where lamplight fell on his chest and her hands as if marking territory claimed without resistance or question.
“You don’t need my permission for this,” he said softly against her shoulder when she drew back from tracing another line with her palm, “but I want you to have it anyway.”
Her fingers hesitated then traced the scar more firmly than before—the knowledge that his skin had knit itself back together beneath her touch visible in the way her hand trembled slightly as if bearing both proof and promise at once.
“Then let me do this,” she said quietly enough for only his ear to catch but clearly enough for neither doubt nor hesitation to follow in her voice or touch as she moved lower on his body where metal met flesh again with less caution than before as if claiming territory already yielded without question or regret.
Her hands found their way beneath his shirt once more until fabric no longer separated skin from metal—the boundary crossed deliberately without shame or glory but simply fact held between them like an anchor dropped in deep water where currents couldn’t shift it anymore.
“You still have questions,” he said, catching her wrist when she moved toward the scar tissue on his thigh with equal deliberate care, “but I don’t want you asking them while we’re apart anymore.”
“And I don’t want to answer them while I’m looking away from you either,” she said softly enough for only his ear to catch beneath salt-dried hair clinging stubbornly to skin still wet from seawater clinging stubbornly against light falling steadily across room dimming slowly toward darkness waiting outside windows thick with silence held between them until neither moved again.
“Then let’s finish this together,” he said quietly enough for only her ear to catch before moving both bodies forward into contact deeper than touch alone could manage until no boundary remained except choice freely given without question or hesitation holding fast against tides threatening still to separate what had been joined deliberately by choice freely made without fear or regret weighing heavy upon them both until neither moved again except together in motion deliberately chosen without question or hesitation following through until neither moved again.
Their hands found each other where no words could follow—metal met flesh where flesh met skin met metal where scar tissue knit back together beneath touch holding fast against tides threatening still but failing now utterly because neither would yield ground already gained deliberately by choice freely made without hesitation or question holding firm until neither moved again.
