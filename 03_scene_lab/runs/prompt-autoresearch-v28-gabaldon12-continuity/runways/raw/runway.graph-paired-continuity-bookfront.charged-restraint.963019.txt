A man and a woman stand on the porch of a plain brick house, high above San Francisco. Below, the Bay Area lights spread north and west into the distance, gold on the black horizon, silver on the water.
Above them, stars blaze against black.
“Where should we go first?” she says. She leans against a stone wall under the porchlight and looks out over the city.
The man stands next to her, his back to the wall, his eyes on the black hills.
They are both slender and athletic. Their clothing is simple, worn but elegant, each in black. The woman wears a knit top and loose pants; the man wears jeans and a shirt.
“Where do you want to go?” he says. He turns his head toward her, keeping the black hills in the corner of his eye. He wears a watch with an old-fashioned leather band and a luminous dial. The bright green numerals face the ground and the seconds sweep the circle silently.
She keeps her back to the wall and doesn’t look at him.
“I don’t know,” she says.
