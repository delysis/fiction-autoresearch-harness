Outside the lab building, the air is cool and humid; summer in northern California. A few clouds roll overhead, not enough to block the stars. Jonah and I stand on the top concrete step, watching the rest of the class file out.
The experiment ended before I did, but I'm still not talking. I keep my attention on the group. They move slowly, each step dragging a little, like the whole class is tired and not just me. Most of them are yawning, yawning yawning yawning—
Jonah's warm finger touches my shoulder between my shoulder-blade and my collar-bone, and then lifts to tap against the skin just above my ribcage. He doesn't touch me in the neck muscle or the breastbone. I look at him and let my eyes widen.
He gives me the kind of look people give a dog, the one that says, "Did you want my hand?"
I nod, still silent. He turns his hand and stretches out his fingers. I take it.
The first thing I notice is that his skin is warm, not hot.
The second thing I notice is that it feels better to touch his hand than to watch him.
