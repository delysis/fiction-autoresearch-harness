It was all too easy.

“You’re not angry with me?” Mara Voss said. She stood on Fulcrum’s front steps, watching a colleague’s face in the streetlight. Jonah Vale looked back at her. He’d never raised his voice in her presence.

“Yes,” he said, and smiled.

“Don’t be, then.”

“Why?”

“Because you’re supposed to make me happy.” Mara put on a brave face. It took the effort of a hundred years to look like a child. “Aren’t you?”

Jonah did not touch her. He might have kissed her hand or brushed a lock from her face. Instead, he stepped closer. He met her gaze without moving his mouth. His dark eyes moved to hers. He held her there until she forgot her lines.

She tried again. “Can you tell I’m scared?”

His silence was not reassurance. “Of me?”

“I have to find a way to get out.” The words hung between them. Mara had forgotten where they were, even how long she’d lived at Fulcrum. She could not find the strength to answer.
