 She followed.
They were walking out of town without talking. Her legs hurt, but Jonah’s stride was long and even; he kept picking up speed. She glanced over at him and saw that he looked tired, but there was something different too. He walked like a boy, shoulders thrown back, head lifted. He breathed loudly through his nose with every other step, then softly with the next two—three in and out, three, four, five.
He wasn’t talking to her, and she didn’t want to talk to him. She wasn’t sure how to walk. Her shoulders were locked, and her elbows were straight, but her hips felt loose as they swung back and forth and her legs worked on their own. She’d never walked this fast at night before. She had trouble keeping up, and the cold air was making her feel colder than she wanted to. Her arms hung down and swayed from the shoulders when she moved them, but the elbows didn’t bend. She felt as though she were a puppet made of sticks with strings attached at the joints—she wasn’t walking; her body was walking, not her.
