Mara stood outside a bathroom door that Livia had failed to vacate. Jonah arrived from the main room with his coffee and a sheaf of paper.

The coffee was unremarkably brown. The sheaf was too neat.

“How long is long?” Mara asked.

Jonah did not look at her. His gaze stayed on the bathroom door. The paper crinkled between his thumb and forefinger.

“Half an hour,” he said. “With a generous definition of half.”

“She always does this?”

“Yes.”

“And no one has built a reminder into the plumbing yet?”

He gave the door a light kick. Mara’s foot took a step and stopped.

“Or threatened to unbuild it?”

“No,” Jonah said, and Mara could hear the lie in his voice.

The door opened before the conversation could become an agreement. Livia’s eyes were pink from either crying or a fight with her eyeliner. She gave Jonah the kind of hug that showed she had missed him, then pushed him out of her way.

She offered Mara her cheek instead. Her skin was warm beneath Mara’s knuckles.

“Come,” Livia said. “Let’s finish the tea and the session separately.”

Mara had no other excuse. She followed Livia into the pavilion with Jonah’s neat sheaf in one hand and her own exhaustion in the other.

A low fire burned in the stone hearth. Jonah sat on the floor between the two couches and pulled the sensor terminal toward himself with his toes. The other fellows occupied the main room like people who knew the rules and did not know where Mara stood. The low chair she had taken four nights earlier was already occupied, and the small table beside it bore a ceramic carafe filled with amber liquid.

Livia sat on the other couch and poured tea into two mugs. The steam caught on the cold air.

She slid one of them across to Mara. “I’ve been telling them they’ll like you.”

Mara lifted her mug to the room. The people nearest her returned the gesture.

“You’re making it harder,” she said. “I can tell.”

“It’s in your best interest to find someone who does.” Livia watched Mara drink from the mug. “You’ve taken too much of yourself with you.”

The tea tasted of ginger and earth. Mara’s head cleared by a degree.

“I’ve been running hard,” she said.

“Yes. Because you’re afraid someone will look closely and find nothing.”

Mara put the cup down and let her shoulders ease away from her ears. That was not her fear, but it sounded close enough that she had no prepared answer for it.

“You want the work and the place,” Livia said, “and you’re afraid both might look less exceptional than you thought. We know you want us. We’re counting on it.”

Mara touched her mouth. It felt slightly looser than before she drank, but that might have been the room.

“I was told this was a fellowship.”

“You’re new here. Fellowship means we know how to be hungry in public.”

“I have my own methods.”

Livia tapped the coffee table with one fingertip. Mara could see the effort it cost her.

“I’m not going to leave you with them.”

That cost her a breath. Jonah made a sound at the terminal like a kettle boiling. Livia did not look at him.

“Then what do you want?” Mara asked.
