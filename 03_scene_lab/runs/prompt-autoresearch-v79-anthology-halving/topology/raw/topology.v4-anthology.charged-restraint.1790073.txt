 She felt her own breath grow ragged despite herself and pulled in sharply on her diaphragm to bring it under control.
Jonah said nothing more, and Mara was relieved. She couldn’t have talked right now, even if she’d wanted to. She wasn’t ready to face him, or to meet his gaze. The air tasted gritty, like dust after a long rainless season. Her breath came in fast, harsh little rasps as the cold stung her throat.
Jonah moved across the street and turned toward town. He walked at the pace of a person with nowhere special to be, not hurrying or loitering. She felt like a spy as she followed his lead. If he stopped to admire the moon or a flower or a passing dog, would she stop too? Would she stand by his side in silence while he took his time, or would she go home alone? She wanted to know. She wanted him to be the one to decide.
His presence steadied her as they moved slowly along the quiet street. Her ragged breath settled to a quieter rhythm, and the dry dustiness of her throat lessened. Her neck and shoulders began to ease as well.
When Jonah did stop, he stopped short enough to surprise her, and she nearly bumped into him. They were on the sidewalk beside a small house with a bright moonflower vine trailing from the windowsills. The vine was overgrown; it made a pale arch over the steps, and hung down in great dripping lanyards from the eaves.
Jonah stared up at it. His breath stopped so fast that she realized she had been reading his breathing almost since she got outside, checking his pace against her own. She didn’t consciously try to watch, but somehow his movement made a framework for hers. Without it, her breath stopped too, as though they were sharing an invisible thread of air.
She heard herself pull in a quick gasp and felt his head jerk to look at her face. His eyes were open wide; his eyebrows went up and he parted his lips. He hesitated for a second, and then gave a slow, sad laugh that seemed to echo hers. “I’m sorry,” he whispered, as though she had been hurt.
He stepped away from the vine toward her with his hand held out flat, palms down, as though offering a gift she couldn’t see. His eyes were kind and clear in the moonlight. They stayed that way. He didn’t look away or take back his gesture. She could feel the tension between them grow again, but it was different from what they had shared inside. The distance this time seemed real enough to reach out and touch.
Jonah’s eyes kept shifting: half-lidded one moment, then open again, but still with that sad smile. She wondered if he saw what she was seeing: the dark, shadowed face of someone she thought she knew, someone she’d loved once and then lost. Her throat tightened again, and she swallowed hard.
“I don’t want to go home.” The words came out in a whisper; she didn’t know if he would hear them. Maybe it was just as well that he didn’t. She had said too much already, and it made her stomach churn again.
“I can walk home alone.” Her voice sounded so tired and defeated that even she winced. But Jonah still looked at her steadily, his hand held out for her to take.
