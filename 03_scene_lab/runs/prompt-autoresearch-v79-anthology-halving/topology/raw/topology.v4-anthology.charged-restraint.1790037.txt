 He took long strides, as if he were out for a morning jog. It was all so easy, he seemed to say, but she couldn’t match his pace and still think about it.
“Jonah,” she said, a little short of breath, “wait a minute.”
He stopped at once, turned back toward her. His stride had been deliberate, not automatic. He hadn’t been hurrying to get away.
The front of his jacket was rumpled. She couldn’t see his face in the darkness. He held the lapels together with both hands, as though he might button it, but there were no buttons. He didn’t seem to be shivering.
She didn’t know where he’d gone in his thoughts, what he’d seen. She only knew she wasn’t with him. He was a shape moving beside her, dark against the cold black sky. She felt cold inside, even though the night was warm enough and she wore a light coat over jeans and a sweatshirt. Her hair smelled of the heavy perfume of a colleague’s expensive body spray; it made her want to throw up. Her hair was still damp with it.
They turned off the main drive, walked north toward town under the moonlight. He kept his hands in his pockets. The night was very quiet.
Mara glanced down, saw the darkness of her own feet as they moved along the drive. They made no noise on the blacktop. Her sneakers were still clean. Her jeans smelled of the artificial flowers in the woman’s bathroom. Jonah smelled like the man he’d been today. He didn’t notice. His steps fell steadily, quietly behind hers. He wasn’t asking her questions.
She walked at him, then past him again. She reached for his arm. His jacket was wet and rough. It was soft underneath. Her palm was cold on his skin; she let go. She’d meant to feel his warmth. She had only imagined it. He smelled of the sweat that ran down her legs when she walked, even in the evening air. She couldn’t see him under his clothes, but she knew what he looked like; she could make him out of her own sweat and hair.
The sidewalk was rough underfoot, cold and damp against her bare feet. They were damp too from the sweat that ran down her legs. His hand touched her lightly on the nape of the neck, where it was bare under her hair. He didn’t touch anywhere else. She wanted to turn around and ask him what he was doing, but she couldn’t move her head. She couldn’t remember what his face looked like, only that he was tall and thin. His hands were long, the nails trimmed short; the fingernails of one hand were stained with ink from signing papers.
The air smelled clean and cold, like autumn leaves. She took a deep breath. The sky was clear; she could see a few stars overhead. She felt lighter than air, as if she were floating above the ground, but her legs were cold against the pavement. There wasn’t a breeze, but her hair moved slightly in the stillness, as if there were one. His footsteps fell behind hers; he wasn’t close enough for her to hear him breathe.
She didn’t know how much longer it would be before she could go home.
