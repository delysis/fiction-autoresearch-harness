 His shirt hung loose over his shoulders, and the thin cotton barely moved as he breathed.
The moon was full, and its light on the pavement had the same blue-white cast as Fulcrum’s bright front lamps. Jonah was bareheaded, and she could see his skin: the hollow in his throat, the straight line of his jaw. He looked younger than thirty—younger even than his real age, like a boy pretending to be a man.
He paused at the bottom step to wait for her. A few paces into the street, he glanced over at her. His eyes were almost black, and he was watching her intently, though his voice was calm as he spoke. “It’s a long walk, Mara.”
Her tongue tasted like cotton. “You can take a cab if you want to, Jonah.” She had said something similar once before, a week ago, when he offered to drive her home. He had declined the offer then. He wouldn’t offer again; that wasn’t his style. If he wanted to ask a ride, he would just ask.
He looked at her for a moment, then turned away. “It’s easier to get back this way.” He took another step and hesitated again. “Do you want a ride?” His voice had an odd note in it, almost like hesitation: the soft sound of someone else’s breath on a microphone. She hadn’t expected that. She wouldn’t know how to answer if she tried, so she just kept following him.
She didn’t know where he was going; they were on a side street now. Her mouth felt thick and heavy when she spoke, her voice low and rough. “I don’t know where I’m going.”
Jonah stopped. The street was almost empty this late. A taxi passed a few blocks away, the lights on its rooftop flashing the yellow reflection of Fulcrum’s entrance on the asphalt. Jonah turned back to look at her, his head slightly tilted. “I can go home with you,” he said.
She had wanted to walk home; she had told him so before he left Fulcrum that evening. She hadn’t meant to change her mind; she couldn’t explain why she had. “No. It’s okay.” She forced herself to stand still, trying not to move at all.
She wasn’t breathing; she couldn’t feel it at all. Her heart pounded in her ears as though it were a clock, ticking out the seconds in an empty room. The cold wind felt like knives slicing through her shirt; she shivered, but there was no relief from it. “It’s just easier to get back this way.” She said that again because he had said it first, and it sounded better than what she wanted to say: It would be better for me not to go home.
“You should get something to eat,” Jonah said, turning back toward the sidewalk, his face turned away from her. “It’s been a long time since we had dinner, and I didn’t sleep well last night.” She could almost read the words on his lips, even though it was too dark for her to see them clearly.
“I know.” That wasn’t what he meant; he meant to say that she shouldn’t worry. She wanted to say that she wasn’t worried about him sleeping badly.
He turned his face to look at her, and she could feel herself tense as his eyes fixed on hers. The silence between them stretched out like a black void into which they might both fall. She had almost forgotten how to breathe.
