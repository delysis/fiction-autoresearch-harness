 The air was dry and cold enough to sting her face but cool his body. He’d chosen a light jacket, close-fitting around the shoulders.
She let her eyes trail along the jacket’s outlines as she followed. There was more inside that jacket than she’d ever see unless she asked him to take it off. She had to guess whether he might want to. She had to guess when and where.
That guessing was her job now. She needed to figure out his answers when he wasn’t saying anything. He wasn’t going to diagnose her or make sense of her discomfort; she needed to do that herself. It didn’t matter how close they might be or how many secrets they shared if he kept on holding back. She could guess at him, but she wasn’t going to guess what he wanted.
She drew herself up and forced her body to relax. She took a deep breath and let it out through her mouth. Her voice felt dry and rough when it came out. “I can walk alone from here.”
