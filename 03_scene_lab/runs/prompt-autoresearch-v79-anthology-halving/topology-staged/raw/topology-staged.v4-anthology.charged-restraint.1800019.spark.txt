 He wore his usual clothes, black pants, black boots, and a red wool shirt under a faded leather jacket. She couldn’t see his face, but she felt she knew what he was thinking. He would be wondering what had just happened inside, why she had behaved as she did; he would be wondering how to ask without making it worse; she could almost hear him forming and discarding questions as they walked.
He was right to be careful, she thought; the evening had been bad enough without that. Her back was straight, her spine taut inside her thin white sweater, but she knew there was nothing more to say about what had happened in the lab. She didn’t look at Jonah. Instead she scanned the street for evidence of the two of them walking together: reflections in passing headlights and shop windows. She saw that she was blushing and lowered her head, concentrating on her feet, her shoes brushing the gutter, step by careful step.
