 The only thing out of place was that he wasn’t speaking. That was more frightening than the drive, the darkness, and the strangers all around them.
She caught her foot on a crack in the sidewalk but managed to stay on her feet. No one was there to see. He didn’t look back. If he hadn’t looked when she was screaming, he certainly wouldn’t turn now.
He moved quickly, farther than she could keep up. She felt faint. She swallowed, and her throat was tight again. The silence was closing in around her, and her lips were chapped and sore. She looked at him. He didn’t notice.
“Jonah.” Her voice sounded odd and high to her ears. It sounded weak.
“Yes, Mara.” His tone was easy, pleasant. He didn’t look at her, or slow, or turn around. There was nothing unusual about him. It was as if tonight hadn’t happened.
