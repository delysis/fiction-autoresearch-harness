Prose movement
“Mother,” called Diana, a handsome girl a little older than Winifred,
“I may have wine, mayn’t I?”

“Yes, you may have wine,” replied the mother automatically, for she was
perfectly indifferent to the question.

And Diana beckoned to the footman to fill her glass.

“Gerald shouldn’t forbid me,” she said calmly, to the company at large.

“All right, Di,” said her brother amiably. And she glanced challenge at
him as she drank from her glass.

There was a strange freedom, that almost amounted to anarchy, in the
house. It was rather a resistance to authority, than liberty. Gerald
had some command, by mere force of personality, not because of any
granted position. There was a quality in his voice, amiable but
dominant, that cowed the others, who were all younger than he.

Hermione was having a discussion with the bridegroom about nationality.

“No,” she said, “I think that the appeal to patriotism is a mistake. It
is like one house of business rivalling another house of business.”

FULCRUM

Setting
Contemporary Fulcrum, a private psychotechnology institute studying attention and nonverbal cue-reading. Adult newcomer Mara is the only viewpoint. Every unusual inference retains an ordinary sensory or behavioral explanation. Restraint and refusal can carry social cost.

Continuity
Adult newcomer Mara is at the contemporary Northern California Fulcrum compound for the first time; she has never visited or interviewed here before. The blond man from the couch is adult Jonah Reed, and Mara now knows his name.  Mara is the only viewpoint. Unusual cue-reading always retains ordinary sensory and behavioral explanations.

Exact dramatic payment for the audition
The people already present are Mara, Jonah, and the unnamed brown-haired visiting fellow. A previously unseen older catering worker enters and retrieves the visiting fellow's abandoned glass without acknowledgment while his conversation continues. The visiting fellow and worker remain unnamed. Through observable action, Mara notices who assumes whose labor. End on that concrete recognition before anyone turns it into a lesson.

Manuscript so far
When Mara walked into the common room at six fifteen, someone was already there. Someone younger, it seemed.
She couldn’t make out the face behind the blond hair. But the body was fine enough, leaning back on the couch with bare legs crossed and arms flung in opposite directions, one palm pressing the padded wall and the other wrist resting on the low table beside him.
She stood stock-still by the doorway for a moment, aware of her breath and of his awareness that she was here. He stirred and tilted his head; a face turned toward her, expressionless.

The two pairs of eyes held each other through an entire exhale.
And then the young man had pushed himself up on the armrests, stretched to his full height, and strolled over to shake her hand.
“Mara?”
He was closer than she would have guessed from where he’d been sitting, broad-shouldered and strong-chinned in a white T-shirt, grinning at her through dark-framed glasses as he reached out for the handshake. She took it.
“Jonah,” he said.
“Hello,” she replied, and then they stood there, still shaking hands, not knowing what to do next.

And that was when a door opened down the hallway behind her, releasing two voices into the room. One female, one male; one asking a question, the other replying; neither loud nor excited, just casual conversation. Jonah released her hand and turned away to look down the hall at them.
When Mara heard the voices and followed his gaze, she saw one young man who wasn’t Jonah, but close in size and shape; he had brown hair and a beard and was carrying a glass.

The brown-haired man glanced once over his shoulder, saw them standing there, nodded hello, and stepped right into the gathering, holding up his glass of wine—a half-full plastic cup with the Fulcrum logo on it. “Cheers.” He set it on the table against the wall to the left of the door, and stepped forward to shake Jonah’s hand as they exchanged names.
