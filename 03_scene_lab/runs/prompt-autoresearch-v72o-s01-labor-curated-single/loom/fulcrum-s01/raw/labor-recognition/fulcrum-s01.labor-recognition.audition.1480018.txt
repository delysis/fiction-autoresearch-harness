The brown-haired man was a visiting fellow who was staying at the retreat center while writing a book on psychology, Jonah told her—which was how she learned Jonah’s last name too; the brown-haired man was doing a project on Reed’s theories about the language of gesture. He said something else that made Jonah laugh and lean forward.
They fell back into easy conversation right in the doorway. They all seemed to be in their late twenties or early thirties, although she might have guessed Jonah was closer to twenty when she walked in—and now, talking to this other man, his posture held its weight easily on his feet, his chin up and shoulders down. They chatted in small groups about books and philosophy, without seeming to notice the glass sitting there, forgotten, on the table by the door.
After a few minutes, she did hear some footsteps behind her and saw a gray-haired older man walk in, look around, and reach for the wineglass before it could overflow and start to run down onto the floor. She didn’t recognize him or catch his name as he carried the glass away and retreated without a word, leaving the three younger men standing there in the middle of the room.
