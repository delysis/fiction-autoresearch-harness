The brown-haired man had raised his own glass now. “Mara, nice to meet you.”
She smiled and accepted his handshake. “I’ve never been here before.”
“It’s beautiful, isn’t it?” said the brown-haired man. “And comfortable. The couches are great to lie down on—like a hammock.”

The conversation drifted on to the visiting scholar’s subject area as Mara stood at the back of the room near the doorway and listened; she’d always found it hard to listen when people had already been talking. But she had just found out that the younger man’s name was Jonah Reed, which made him another piece of Fulcrum’s public face that she was beginning to see in person for the first time.
She took a step back so she could watch his face.
He smiled at the other man’s response; then he took his turn again, gesturing with his hands as if they were puppets that could speak in place of his mouth. She listened and followed his body language as he walked around the room, and only when they paused together for a moment did she see it—the catering worker was here.
Her hair was gray now and her uniform looked new, but Mara knew that face. It was Ms. C., who’d been with the preschool when she was little.
