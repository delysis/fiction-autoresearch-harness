“It was good to meet you in person,” said Mara when it was her turn to introduce herself, “and to have confirmation that Fulcrum hasn’t turned entirely into a fraternity.” She didn’t know him at all and could not have guessed how his sense of humor worked, but she took a chance, hoping that he’d be amused. He was; his eyebrows raised a fraction.
She watched Jonah’s face in turn, studying his expression for reaction while the new arrival spoke.
The visiting fellow had a smooth, slightly high-pitched voice that sounded almost too educated in the first few syllables—the sort of voice she associated with NPR fundraisers.
“How long have you been here?” he asked Jonah as they both moved forward to join her.
She hadn’t thought of asking him that herself; they seemed about the same age and she just assumed he’d been there longer, as someone more established and confident than she could ever manage. She had no idea how old any of them were—but with the clean-shaven faces, it was easy to forget that they weren’t teenagers, just boys.
“Two years,” said Jonah with a hint of a smile, “and I’m hoping for two more.” He glanced at Mara.
“And you?” The brown-haired man turned his smile on her, looking her over again with another silent evaluation. “You just arrived, is that right?”
