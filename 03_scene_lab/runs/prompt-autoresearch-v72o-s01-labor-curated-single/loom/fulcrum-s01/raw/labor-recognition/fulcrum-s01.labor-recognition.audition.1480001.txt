Mara took two steps toward them, trying to look relaxed, wondering whether she should put her phone down before anyone else arrived and expecting that Jonah would introduce her any moment. Instead the brown-haired fellow turned toward her with the same unsmiling expression and held out a hand.
She was about to reach for it when Jonah stepped in between them—literally, stepping to the left in front of her.
“You already know Mara,” he said.
There was no reason for him to say that: neither of the two men had met Mara before today. They hadn’t even been there when she and Jonah had arrived. She wasn’t sure if Jonah was joking, and neither did the brown-haired man. Mara noticed his eyes flit from her to Jonah’s face and back again, trying to work out why Jonah said such a thing, until Mara simply offered her own hand instead.

“Mara,” she said with a small smile. “Hello.”
He took it. She looked at his eyes as she said her name, but when his mouth was still closed in the same straight-mouthed smile that she had seen before, she followed his gaze over Jonah’s shoulder instead.
She wasn’t sure why the brown-haired man’s expression hadn’t softened more quickly; they hadn’t even spoken yet. And when she glanced at where he was staring, it didn’t help her make sense of the situation.

The door was still open where they had walked into the room; the light and noise were coming through from the hallway outside, and someone else had come along behind them, standing there on the threshold.
