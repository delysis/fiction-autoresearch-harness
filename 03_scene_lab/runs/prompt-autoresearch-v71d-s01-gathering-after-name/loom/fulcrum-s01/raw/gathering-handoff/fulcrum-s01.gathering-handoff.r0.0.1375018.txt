
He had an unusually firm grip. It took her a moment to realize he wasn’t the sort of guy who’d insist on keeping eye contact; in fact, he’d broken it himself and was now watching his right shoe pivot on a gray-painted concrete floor. She let go.
“You look nice,” he said suddenly, straightening up from looking at his shoe. She laughed.
“Thanks. You, too.”

The blond fellow turned her way again, and this time there was no sign of the blank look she’d seen in him a few moments ago; his expression had changed to something more easygoing and welcoming. But underneath all that openness there lurked an air of mystery about him that intrigued Mara.
He led her back toward where he’d been sitting before. “Do you like tea or coffee?” he asked. “We can fix you up with something.”
She followed him, still not sure what to do with this stranger who wanted her to feel at home here when they both knew she didn’t belong yet. It was disconcerting—a little frightening even—to be reminded so quickly of how different their lives were going to become if either one chose a career at Fulcrum.
She managed not to show it on her face as they passed into another large room beyond the couches and armchairs. As if by magic, every piece of furniture was exactly where he said it would be: an old-fashioned sofa covered in red velvet; low-backed black chairs facing each other across a wooden table; glass doors opening onto lush gardens beyond them. It looked like any other house she’d ever lived in—except for the absence of walls.
