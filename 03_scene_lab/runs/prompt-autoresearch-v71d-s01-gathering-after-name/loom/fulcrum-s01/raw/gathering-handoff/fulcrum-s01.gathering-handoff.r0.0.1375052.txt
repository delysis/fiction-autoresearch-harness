“I’ve never seen this place,” she confessed. “Is it all right to come in and hang around?”

He looked surprised, and then he laughed too. “I’m pretty sure the entire purpose of having a common room is that you’re allowed to come in and hang around. As long as Adrian’s not in here.”

“He wouldn’t object?”

“Oh, he would,” said Jonah, laughing again. “But if you’re here for long, you’ll probably get used to that. He’s always objecting to something. Right now we’ve been banned from bringing laptops into this room.”

Mara was already thinking that she liked him; she liked the way he looked at her as they talked, and the way his grin pulled his cheeks back.

“And what did you say your name was?”

His smile froze. Mara’s laugh froze. His mouth snapped shut and his eyes widened as if he was realizing something, and for a moment she thought he’d taken offense that she didn’t remember his name and she felt the blood drain from her face, but then his head tilted to one side and he started to smile again. “Well, I’m glad you said that.”

“What do you mean?”

He held up a hand as if to forestall further questions. “I mean, just as you say that you don’t know what it is, a couple of things happen, right? You blink, and your voice changes pitch, and the last word goes up and down like this—” He imitated her inflection.

Mara laughed again.

“And then you get this look on your face—I’m not sure I’m seeing it right, but it’s something like this.” His face moved through a couple of expressions, settling on an inquisitive frown.
