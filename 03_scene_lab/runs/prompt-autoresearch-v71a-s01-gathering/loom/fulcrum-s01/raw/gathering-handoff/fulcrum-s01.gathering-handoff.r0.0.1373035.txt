
She swallowed against the sensation that she was already being taken advantage of, and tried again. “Your name. What’s your first name?”
His eyes narrowed slightly; they were a deeper green than she’d thought, darker than emerald or jade. She waited as his gaze drifted over her face; it was a strange sensation, as though he was reading something in her that she couldn’t feel or know about herself.
“You look tired,” he said finally, without moving his gaze away. “How long has it been since you slept?”
She blinked once, twice, trying to hide her surprise. She’d only been here for a minute; how did he know that she was tired at all? It couldn’t just be her imagination; there was something about the way his attention rested on her that made her feel suddenly very vulnerable, very exposed.
“A while,” she said finally, after a beat too long. She shifted her weight to one foot, then the other; she didn’t want to appear defensive or evasive, but neither did she want to give him the satisfaction of knowing just how much it meant for him to ask that question when he didn’t even know her yet.
His expression remained neutral. “I imagine you don’t get much rest where you work,” he offered at last, gesturing vaguely toward the wall behind her as though that were enough to explain it. “Not many people do.”
“And you do?” she asked sharply, raising an eyebrow.
He laughed again, this time louder; she could hear the amusement in his tone now and it grated on her. “Of course I do,” he assured her cheerfully. “Everyone here does.”
“And you’re not tired at all?” she pressed, unable to help herself.
