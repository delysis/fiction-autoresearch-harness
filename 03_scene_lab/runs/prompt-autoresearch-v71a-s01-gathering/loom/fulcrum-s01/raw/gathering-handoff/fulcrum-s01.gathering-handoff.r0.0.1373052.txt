 He tilted his head back again toward the padded wall, a wry smile curling his mouth. “What do you think?”
“I think maybe we should just stick to names,” she replied carefully.
He studied her intently for a second or two.
“Fair enough.”

Mara exhaled and sank into the chair, feeling a bit weary. She wondered how long she would have to wait here before someone else came along; if anyone ever did. The blond young man stayed on his couch, seeming content just to look at her. She tried not to feel uneasy as he studied her. It was like being a specimen in an exhibit for some kind of high school science fair.
“So you’re staying here?” she asked after what felt like an eternity of uncomfortable silence.
He grinned. “Yep.” He sounded oddly gleeful about it.
She frowned.
“Are you sure this is what you want to do with your life?” she asked cautiously, still feeling a bit wary of him.
“What else am I going to do? You have a better idea?” The grin stayed in place and his tone was light; but underneath that was something hard. Like a predator waiting to see if he could afford to move or if he had to kill first.

“Maybe not,” Mara said slowly. She glanced down at her hands again, wishing she could just fade into the wall and disappear entirely.
He seemed to relax slightly, as if relieved to be left alone with his own thoughts for once.
It was odd how comfortable she had begun to feel with him though – how easily conversation had flowed between them. And yet now there seemed to be a distance between them too wide for simple chat. He stared into space again and she knew better than to ask another question. She could hardly imagine saying anything else except to make small talk, which would serve no purpose other than making both of them feel awkward.
