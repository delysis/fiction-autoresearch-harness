

This seemed like a joke; she wasn’t sure how to respond. But the room was full of sounds other than their voices now, and there were other people in the doorway. Men and women, talking amongst themselves but clearly coming to join them here; one young fellow carried a glass. The door closed softly behind them as Mara turned to face it.
A woman—a woman who’d already said something about Mara’s backpack—was looking at her with wide eyes. She was pretty in an ordinary way, short dark hair framing her face and a soft voice that seemed to catch the edges of the room with each word she spoke. Mara’s heart raced as they drew closer; she felt like she could see herself reflected on those pale cheeks, see the young woman looking at her own reflection for the first time.
“I’m so glad you came,” the woman said brightly. “Welcome home.”

Her name turned out to be Livia Sloane; she worked in human resources. And the young fellow with the glass? That was her intern, Jonah Reed. He said hello when his eyes met Mara’s, but his smile seemed less welcoming than the other two.
She took a slow step closer to them as they arranged themselves around the couch. Livia was still looking at Mara expectantly; when she saw that Mara wasn’t moving forward any farther, she put one hand on the back of a chair and let out a short laugh.
“You’re so shy!”
Mara blinked; Livia reached out for her backpack with both hands.
“Oh no,” Mara protested immediately. “I can carry that myself—”
But Livia had already taken the bag and turned away from her; Mara stood there awkwardly while the other woman disappeared into an office down the hall, then returned. “I brought your new badge,” she explained cheerfully, holding it up for Mara to see.
“Thank you,” Mara said slowly
