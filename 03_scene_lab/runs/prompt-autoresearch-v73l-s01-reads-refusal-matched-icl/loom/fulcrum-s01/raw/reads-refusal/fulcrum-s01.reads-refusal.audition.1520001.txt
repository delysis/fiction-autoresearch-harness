

Continuing the scene
Adrian kept looking at her, watching her face, nodding occasionally, until she stopped and released his hand. “All right.” He lifted her hand off his own; then, reaching into her empty palm with his right hand, he took back the brass key and the wooden token and let them rest in the curve of his left fist, one on top of the other; finally he released Mara’s hand entirely, so that she was left holding the stone in both her palms again, and then he said, “You chose the limestone.”

“You are good at this,” said Mara.
Adrian smiled again. “Not all of us are good at this,” he replied, glancing briefly at Jonah; and Mara turned to look at the younger man, who now stood facing them with a cup of wine in his hand. She understood what was meant here.
“Thank you.” Her voice had risen to an odd height. It felt wrong to be looked at by Jonah; she lowered her eyes.

Adrian, who had not looked at Jonah again, stepped past the younger man and put the cup down on the same table where the last one had been—the table by the hallway, now covered with empty glasses, which he gestured to Mara to join him in examining. She saw a lot of the same plastic cups here, all half-emptied into each other. There were several more people in sight through the doorway—and through the doorways beyond those: tables full of people standing or sitting.
“Fulcrum,” said Adrian. He pointed toward the hallway. “This is what Fulcrum does.”
Mara considered her stone again, rolling it from hand to hand.
“We talk to each other, in ordinary words and gestures like this.” He had stepped back from her, moving back into the middle of the room with Jonah now on his right side. “We pay attention, when we speak and when we listen—we pay attention to the details, we notice how people feel and what they need, and we keep that in mind. We can’t help noticing, because Fulcrum is always working; but then when someone does tell us something, when they say what they want to say out loud in ordinary conversation, we can tell right away whether it’s the same thing we were just reading about from the gestures, or something entirely different.” He spread his hands toward the cup-strewn table again. “Which happens often.”
“I see.” Mara was looking at Jonah now; her eyes were a little wide; her head felt too light.

Adrian nodded. “You do. I saw you do it,” he said softly; and when she looked around to find him, there he was again, standing by the doorframe at her side with a hand on the jamb above his head, watching her through another long pause in the conversation.
She didn’t turn away from that. She took a breath, and then she asked, “How?”
“From your body.”
