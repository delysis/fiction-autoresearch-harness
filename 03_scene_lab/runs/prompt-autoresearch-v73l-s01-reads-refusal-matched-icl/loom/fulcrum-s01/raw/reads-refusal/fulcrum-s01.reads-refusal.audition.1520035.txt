"I believe I know what you’ve chosen,” Adrian said quietly. “I’m not going to tell you which one, because there’s a chance that I might be wrong. And if you’re playing in a group, I think everyone should keep their choices private.”

He looked at Livia, and they exchanged glances. Mara couldn’t understand the look. Adrian turned back to Jonah.

“Can we see what you chose? Just your cup, please.” He nodded at Mara. “You can set your object down.”

She did, setting all three on a side table. She saw the brown-haired man turn and notice.
Jonah turned his glass over to Adrian; Adrian turned it over to Livia.

“One moment,” she murmured, as though deep in concentration, though Mara couldn’t see her doing anything at all. She paused again, breathing, eyes on the cup, and then she opened her right hand in front of her—and showed the key in her palm.
Mara looked down at the table where her three objects still rested: one stone, two tokens. She took a quick glance toward Livia, and then back to Adrian, who was watching her closely.

She wondered whether Livia had just got lucky. And then she turned back to Livia to say so—but the woman wasn’t there. The woman had stepped back to stand beside Adrian; and her head was now tilted, listening.

Jonah’s voice said, “Livia, may I see you outside?”

Manuscript after the boundary
Mara caught sight of movement behind them; someone else had entered through the double doors at the end of the hallway. She glanced to her left. A woman of indeterminate age, carrying a folder; dark hair, short. And in this light she couldn’t make out more than that.

But from this woman’s stance, as she came closer, and from what little Mara could see of the set of her eyes—though her face was half in shadow, looking back at Adrian—this was an experienced reader, even more certain of her skill than Livia or Jonah, and entirely focused on Mara.
“If you’ll come this way,” she said quietly. “We’re ready for your interview now.”

Mara looked at the folder in the woman’s left hand—it held papers; that was all she could see—and then she glanced at Adrian again. She didn’t think Adrian had recognized her. But he said nothing about it, and moved on, past her, into the room beyond.

Jonah stood watching, one elbow resting on the low table, his head still tilted back in the same stance as before, eyes never leaving her. “How did that work for you?” he asked.
She shrugged, and took another sip of her drink. The taste reminded her that the light was dimming. The darkness seemed to be closing in around her. But she wasn’t going to let them see her afraid. She put her empty glass down on a table and stood up straight, looking back at him.
