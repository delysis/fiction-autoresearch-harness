She looked up at Adrian’s face as she asked him to name the object; he nodded back and said, “We’ll find out together, then.” He drew the palm of his left hand across the table toward the plastic cup Jonah was still holding. “Jonah?” he prompted.
“Oh!” Jonah pulled his right hand back. “Wait—”

Before anyone could say anything else, another female voice spoke up from the doorway to Mara’s left—not the worker from earlier, but a different voice. She turned that way, saw a slim brown woman leaning in through the open doorway and looking up at her; she had to crane her neck upward to look the girl in the face.
Mara looked down into that face. The newcomer had short dark hair, a strong mouth, and eyes so dark they looked almost black in contrast with the pale blue circle around them; they were also—she noticed almost at once, as their gazes met—looking directly past her left ear, so the woman appeared to be looking straight at Mara but was in fact not making any kind of eye contact at all.

“I’m Livia,” she said, and held out a hand in greeting, palm down toward her knee. “Nice to meet you. May I help with something?” She smiled at her words, though her smile didn’t seem directed toward Mara in particular—it might just as easily have been at any of them.

“Livia, this is Mara,” Jonah said as Mara returned the handclasp. “She’s interested in seeing the Institute.”
Mara felt herself blush, and looked up toward Adrian’s face to find his eyes still on Jonah and Livia, not looking at her.
Livia turned to look at Mara more closely. Her eyes were still slightly off-center, but now she looked more directly at her own hands as they clasped. “The Institute is always interested in meeting visitors,” she said, her tone neutral. She looked toward Mara again and smiled; Mara thought that might be because she sensed the way she was reacting to this woman, rather than any particular content of her words.
