

He rolled his eyes, as though she were the slowest person in the world, and shifted just enough for her to read his nametag: "Adrian"
Mara smiled again. “Adrian. I’m Mara.” She waited for an answer this time, but none came.
After a moment, she cleared her throat and asked, “Where’s everybody else?”

Adrian shrugged his shoulders again and turned back toward the wall, showing her the back of his neck.
Mara sighed and walked over to the low table by the couch, leaning down to pick up an empty coffee cup that someone had left behind on it. The faint scent of cinnamon reached her as she took it. She tried to remember if Adrian had been drinking anything. She didn’t think so.
She carried the cup back across the room toward the dishwasher near the doorway. It felt good to have something purposeful to do, and she wondered what would have happened if she’d asked someone else in the common room where everyone was.
She poured out the coffee grinds, then dropped the cup into the bottom compartment of the dishwasher next to a few empty plates and bowls. It took less than five seconds to finish the job. She turned on her heel to walk back toward the couch.
Adrian had gotten up off the couch while she’d been doing dishes. He was now standing by one of the large windows at the far end of the room, staring into the dark forest outside. She followed him with her eyes until he noticed that she was looking at him and turned his head slightly to acknowledge her presence.
Mara blinked, startled at how quickly he had turned his attention away from her when she wasn’t watching anymore. She supposed he must have heard her footsteps on the hardwood floors. Then again, there hadn’t been any footsteps either time she’d done dishes. She stopped trying to figure him out and instead crossed over to the couch again. Adrian glanced at her but didn’t say anything. She sat down.

“I guess you’re all alone here, huh?” she said.
Adrian snorted lightly and took a sip from his cup without answering. He looked like he was trying not to laugh at something. Mara wanted him to look at her so she could see what was going on in his mind, but he kept his eyes fixed on the forest outside the window. She tried not to feel annoyed at being ignored again, but she couldn’t help it.
“I don’t mean to be rude,” she said after another long silence, “but if you’re going to ignore me like this, maybe we shouldn’t spend so much time together.”
Adrian shifted his gaze back to hers again. His face softened slightly. “You’re new,” he said. “I thought you might want some privacy.”
Mara shook her head. “No, I mean...you seem so...distant. I don’t know you at all.”

Adrian’s expression changed again, this time into one that looked almost apologetic. “I’m sorry,” he said. “It’s just...I find it easier to relate to people who aren’t quite as...” He paused for a few seconds before continuing. “...human.”
Mara blinked several times, trying to figure out what he meant by that comment.
“I don’t understand,” she said finally.

Adrian looked away again. “Never mind,” he said, clearly embarrassed by what he had said. He took another sip from his cup.

Mara felt uncomfortable talking about things she didn’t understand, but she didn’t want to just drop the subject either.
“Are you an alien?” she asked hesitantly.

Adrian choked on his drink, coughing and spluttering for a few seconds before he could speak again. “What makes you ask that?”

Mara felt herself blush slightly as she explained her confusion over Adrian’s comment from earlier.

He cleared his throat and glanced around the room before answering. “It’s not exactly true,” he said slowly. “I’m not sure why I said that.”
