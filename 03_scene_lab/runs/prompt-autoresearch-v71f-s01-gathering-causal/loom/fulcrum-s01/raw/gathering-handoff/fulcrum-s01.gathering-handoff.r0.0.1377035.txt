
“Sorry,” Jonah said, and let go of her fingers. “I’m not one of those Fulcrum people who actually works here, so I’m not even allowed to know how to say hello to new people.” He grinned at his own joke, gesturing for her to walk around the room with him. “My name is Jonah Reed,” he said, “and I’m a hardware-security researcher in the private sector. I work on ways to hack into the chips and firmware on things that have been built, and ways to stop other people doing that. It’s not especially relevant to anything I’ve done at Fulcrum.”

“It sounds interesting,” Mara replied. She didn’t know what to say next. “I work in infrastructure security, too, but mine is all software-based. I haven’t learned that much about the hardware side of things.”
“Infrastructure,” Jonah repeated. “Is that like making sure that stuff doesn’t crash?”
“That, among other things,” Mara replied. The way he’d said it implied that she was missing his point, or that there was no point to be missed. She wasn’t sure.
“I’ve been coming to Fulcrum for about four years now,” Jonah continued. “I’ve always been more interested in the work than the people—but it’s great that we keep getting new people who don’t just stick around. That means there are still people like me who actually want to learn things.”
He smiled again, his eyes dark and playful.
“I’m not saying you’re like me, or anything,” he added, laughing slightly. “It’s just a general observation, you know?”
Mara didn’t reply, though she couldn’t decide if that was deliberate or not. Jonah didn’t look as though he expected a reply, and he carried on speaking.
