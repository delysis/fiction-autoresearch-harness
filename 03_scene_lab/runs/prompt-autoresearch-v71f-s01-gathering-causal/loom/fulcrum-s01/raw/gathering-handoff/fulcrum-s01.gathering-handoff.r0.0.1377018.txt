
He grinned some more, and let go. “Have you been here before?”
She hadn’t. But it was like the other Fulcrum facilities. There was the polished concrete floor, the wide open spaces with small clusters of furniture—that couch, a dining table, a coffee table—and an emphasis on sunlight and views of the outside world.

“No, not here.” She looked around at the common area and the huge windows that opened it up to the evening view. She recognized the furniture she had seen in the photos from her interview, but there was no one else yet. “I didn’t realize I would be early.”
He looked embarrassed. “Sorry. I just—” He hesitated for a moment before going on: “I just wanted to get out of the house. Get some fresh air and a good view.” His smile was more open and less apologetic now. “You can blame Livia.”
Mara frowned slightly, feeling an urge to object to her mother’s name being used as an explanation for his actions, but at the same time feeling that he was probably right. She had spent two weeks at Livia Sloane’s home outside Chicago, watching her train with the rest of Fulcrum’s senior staff.
She remembered Livia turning away from Adrian, her hand on his shoulder as she looked out at the room full of people they would be welcoming in the morning, saying, “If we don’t let them see us as people first, they won’t stick around long enough for the rest.”

Her voice had been soft and matter-of-fact. Now Mara tried to imagine how that same voice would have sounded telling someone to come outside and enjoy a good view.
