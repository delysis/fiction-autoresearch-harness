

Jonah had very warm palms; there was calloused skin on his right one, around the fingers but not the thumb. He didn’t look nervous, but his grip was firm and his smile seemed a little brittle, as though he were trying to keep hold of something.
Then his grip loosened. “Um—” His eyes flickered past Mara toward the open door. A low voice was speaking out in the hall—a man’s voice, saying something she couldn’t quite catch, and another voice replying in a woman’s light tone. She heard a small click and knew they would be in the room any moment now.

“I’m Mara,” she said into the silence.
“Mara,” Jonah repeated, letting go of her hand at last. “Hi. Good to meet you.” He turned slightly to one side, lowering his chin toward her shoulder and giving her just enough of a smile, again, to signal that they were done talking now—just as the people from the hallway stepped into the common room.
It was a middle-aged man and a younger woman; he was carrying a glass, so Mara assumed this was Adrian. She could feel his attention fall upon her like a warm, heavy weight. He smiled without taking his eyes off hers—not like Jonah had done, with his quick glance aside and brittle grin; this smile felt more solid, more real somehow, even as it drew Mara closer than she would have liked.

Jonah must have sensed something too; he leaned in close beside her and whispered into her ear: “You might want to watch out for him.”
His breath tickled against her skin as he spoke; his voice was low, husky and full of something like warning or caution—but she could feel no fear behind those words. Rather, there seemed to be amusement mingled with a hint of sadness there; as if he knew what was coming but didn’t know how to stop it from
