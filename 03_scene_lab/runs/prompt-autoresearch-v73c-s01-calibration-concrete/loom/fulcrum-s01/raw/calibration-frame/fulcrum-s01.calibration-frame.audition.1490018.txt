She heard his footsteps but was watching Jonah when Adrian spoke, so she spun to look. He stood near her with the same calm self-control of a man in his thirties who expects everyone’s attention.
“Jonah, good to see you again,” he said, shaking that same hand she’d just been holding.
Jonah grinned back at him, but for Mara it felt as though his gaze skimmed her without settling or touching. She was beginning to feel that she might be a wall. She needed to open up, let him get to know her—
“And I hope you’re doing well today, Mara?” Adrian had turned to her, one arm extended in greeting.
Mara smiled at him, trying not to let him see the flicker of surprise at his use of her name.

“Thank you,” she replied. “Yes, I am.” She watched as he extended his hand to her; when she took it, he shook it gently, fingers only lightly clenched and one other hand just resting on the wrist, in a gesture that spoke of politeness without any pressure to be pleased.
As they released each other, she realized she didn’t know what to do next, now that her cue was over.

Adrian held her gaze and waited without pressure or obligation.
In fact, he seemed genuinely interested in her choice; even his expression held the subtle inflection of wondering.
She opened her mouth to answer but found only the question “What do you want me to do?”
Then Adrian gestured slightly toward the low table, and she followed it. The smooth wooden surface bore three objects, one at each corner: a brass key, a white stone, and a wooden token.
“I’ve seen this done before, in lectures and seminars,” she said, feeling suddenly uncertain about the stakes. “Is this the exercise I’m here for?”

Adrian shrugged. “Maybe. Let’s try it and find out.”
Mara swallowed and looked down at the table, at the three objects. They were small enough to hold in her palm; each had a few chips or scratches on its surface; two looked smooth and shiny while one was duller than she expected.
