And then there was another person coming down the hallway, also dressed in a black-and-white striped uniform, walking purposefully toward them without looking at them; until, glancing up in Mara’s direction, he paused—took an instant to process something she saw him notice in his periphery.
That pause gave Mara time to focus on this newcomer, who wore a white baseball cap with the Fulcrum logo over his neat brown hair, and turned out to be Adrian. He looked around for a seat, saw the couch where Jonah had been sitting, and sat there—while Mara and Jonah made the introductions they would have made if he hadn’t interrupted.

They still were, she realized, waiting to see what he would say.
When he looked up at her, she saw it in his eyes first; the same faint flicker of curiosity she’d noticed when he’d stopped in the hallway, before he knew who was here. Then, looking down at the glass on the table next to him, he said, “Did you notice?”

“Yes.”
“Me, too. Tell me what you saw.”
Mara waited, still uncertain.
Adrian grinned. “Just tell me what you noticed. I want to hear how you frame it.”

She hesitated another second.
He prompted her again with his eyes—and then she knew that she was going to have to say it out loud. “I just noticed how she saw that we weren’t finished with that drink. How he gave it to her, without any words. It seemed…” Mara paused again, not wanting to say what it seemed like.

“Well,” said Adrian, “I noticed the same thing.”
He waited, then asked, “What did you want to say, about how it seemed?”
“It just seemed like a lot of work to do without pay. Or even notice.”

Adrian smiled, eyes twinkling in thought; and Mara knew that she hadn’t just said some random thing. She had given him an opening into a thought he’d already been having.
He nodded slowly, looked down at his own cup of wine—still untouched. “She saw that glass as part of her job. She sees our garbage the same way, and other people don’t even notice. And you said, ‘no pay’? That’s very true.”
