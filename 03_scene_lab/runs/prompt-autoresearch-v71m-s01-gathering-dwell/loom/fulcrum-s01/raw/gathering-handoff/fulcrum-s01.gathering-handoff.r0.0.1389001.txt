
He was looking down, and now he looked up at her, his forehead crinkling for an instant. He let go. “What should I call you?”
The question startled her; she had to think for a moment before she answered. “Mara,” she said again, trying to decide whether that was what she meant, or just what sounded good for some reason.
“Okay.” His smile broadened, but he didn’t say anything else. He stood there with his hands in his pockets, waiting for her to speak. She wanted to ask how the two of them had gotten the same name—how often did that happen?—but she didn’t dare do so yet. Instead she glanced around the room, looking for something else to comment on. There were couches and tables, chairs and a bookshelf and a big-screen TV in the corner. “Is this where you guys meet?” she asked, gesturing at the furnishings.
“Yes.”
“And are all these things here because that’s what people like?”
“No, not really. More like—” He gestured at one of the couches. “—this one was free.”
