Livia poured amber tea from a ceramic carafe and slid it to Mara. “Your hands are shaking.”
   Mara picked up the warm cup and placed it back without drinking. “It’s the data. Too much information, too fast.”
   “It’s not the data,” said Livia, looking up. “It’s not your ability. You’re afraid they’ll find out you’re not special. That you’re only capable. That you’re just another body.”
   Mara tried to smile, but the attempt turned bitter. “No, no. I’m too ordinary for that. I’m a normal girl with a normal fear of normal failure. My mother told me so.”
   “You’re not ordinary. Your mother was wrong.” Livia’s voice remained calm, as if she were reading from a medical chart. “You’re exceptional, but you keep it hidden.” She tilted her head. “There’s a part of you that wants to be found out.”
   “Of course,” said Mara. “Who wouldn’t want someone to notice they were excellent? I’ll tell you a secret.” She leaned toward the interface, her voice dropping to a whisper that was not meant for the screen. “I’m not excellent. I’m not exceptional. I’m not even capable, not really. I’m just an idiot who doesn’t know when to stop.”
   Jonah turned and watched her for several seconds before continuing to type. Livia reached across the table, her hand resting near Mara’s cup. “That’s not what I meant.”
   “I know,” said Mara. “I’m just being dramatic. I’m a little tired. We’ve been at this for hours.”
   “You’ve been at this for days,” said Livia, her eyes never leaving Mara’s face. “You haven’t slept properly in nearly two weeks. Your body is telling you to stop. Your mind is telling you to keep going. I can see both. Which one do you want me to believe?”
   Mara opened her mouth and closed it. The truth, she realized, was that she wanted both. She wanted to be exceptional and she wanted to rest. She wanted to matter and she wanted to be left alone. She wanted to prove her worth and she wanted to hide from everyone who might discover her failure.
   “I don’t know,” she said finally.
   Jonah finished typing and saved the sequence. “It’s late,” he said, standing. “We’ve done enough for tonight.”
   Livia turned to Mara. “I’ll let you know when the report is ready.”
   Mara nodded. She could feel her pulse in her fingertips as she gathered her things. She wanted to say something else, something that would make Livia look at her differently, but she couldn’t think of what it might be.
   Instead, she said, “Thank you.”
   “Don’t thank me,” Livia said. “You’re the one who did all the work. I just happened to be watching.”
   Mara walked to the lab door. As she reached for the handle, she felt Livia’s gaze still fixed on her. She wanted to turn around and look back, but she didn’t dare. Instead, she pulled the door open and stepped outside into the dim corridor, the weight of the past three days still heavy in her limbs.
