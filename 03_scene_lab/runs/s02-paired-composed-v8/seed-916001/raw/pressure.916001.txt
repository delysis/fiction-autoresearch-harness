Livia’s hands hovered near the data like a bird on a branch. After a long moment, she let go. “What do you see, Jonah?”

“Two-dimensional patterns with strong time and space correlation,” Jonah said, still typing. “It doesn’t match any existing technology.”

Livia’s voice was calm, even. “I can read the analysis. What do you think it means? Is this why I came?”

Jonah looked at Mara, then turned back to the monitors. “It means that someone wanted us to find it in that specific order.” He pressed the <Enter> key and the screen refreshed with new values. “It’s a signal with a narrow target list. Fulcrum’s own equipment detected nothing except the one time it was directed to look. The patterns were left there waiting.”

Mara watched the clock again. “So the patterns weren’t designed to do any harm,” she said. “At least not to Fulcrum’s hardware. And they aren’t visible until we look for them.”

“Exactly.” Jonah leaned forward in his chair, his posture stiff but relaxed. He met Livia’s eyes as he spoke. “I think someone is trying to get our attention, but we have no proof they’re coming after us again. We won’t have proof until they attack. Not really.”

“Jonah,” Livia said. “Your job is to think clearly. Is this why I came?”

The question stopped him. He looked back at the telemetry. “I don’t know.”

“Let me make it easier,” Livia said. She turned to Mara. “You have the ability to see and use patterns other people cannot even recognize. Is this why I came?”

“No.” Mara heard herself say. She felt Jonah’s eyes on her face. “I don’t know.”

“And it isn’t just a matter of training,” Livia said. “You were already doing this as a child.”

Mara felt the warmth of Jonah’s gaze. She did not look up. “I had the data, yes,” she said. She touched the glass on her monitor. “You can’t use what you can’t see.”

Livia looked back at Jonah. “And you,” she said. “Your job is to make the invisible visible. It’s the reason you exist.”

He gave her nothing. “I’ll try to give you more tomorrow,” he said. He pulled his own laptop closed. “For now, we still need a way to confirm what just happened. I can make an analysis report that should get you what you want, but the telemetry itself will stay locked.”

Mara watched him stand and then sat still. He took his laptop and walked to the door. Livia rose too. Mara kept her hand on the table and waited for the door to close.
