Example 1

Dramatic design
Offer (15%): Mild amusement
Counteroffer and resistance (25%): Impressed observation
Complication and embodied negotiation (35%): Bland smile versus graceful deference
Consequential turn (15%): Slight shift in perceived power
Concrete stopping state (10%): Transition from demonstration to interactive experiment

Prose movement
Will accepted the test—a fairly severe one; and, sure enough, the Seer
was right. Carefully blindfolded with one of those moulded wraps,
invented for the purpose, which prevent all possibility of looking down
through the chinks, he yet took each card behind his back in one hand,
held it up before their eyes without moving his head, and gave out its
name distinctly and instantly. The audience was impressed. There was a
touch of magic in it. But the Seer smiled blandly.

“Oh, that’s nothing,” he murmured aloud, with a deprecating little
laugh; “a mere matter of choosing between fifty-two alternatives—which,
after all, is easy. With Mrs Palmer’s consent,” and he turned in a
gracefully deferential attitude to Rue, “I can show you something a
great deal more remarkable. Here are pencils and papers. Each lady or
gentleman will please take a sheet as I hand them round. Write anything
you like, in English, French, German or Spanish, on the piece of paper.
Then fold it up, so, and put it into one of these envelopes gummed down
and fastened. After that, as this experiment requires very great
concentration of thought”—he knitted his brows, and assumed an
expression of the intensest internal effort—“with Mrs Palmer’s kind
leave, we will turn out the electric light, which confuses and distracts
one by revealing to the eye so many surrounding visible objects. And
then, without breaking the envelopes in which you have enclosed the
pieces of paper, I will read out to you, in the dark, what each of you
has written.”

FULCRUM

Setting
Contemporary Fulcrum, a private psychotechnology institute studying attention and nonverbal cue-reading. Adult newcomer Mara is the only viewpoint. Livia's accuracy retains ordinary cue-reading explanations; Jonah's restraint costs him status and changes Mara's attention.

Continuity
Adult newcomer Mara is at the contemporary Northern California Fulcrum compound for the first time; she has never visited or interviewed here before. The blond man from the couch is adult Jonah Reed, and Mara now knows his name. Adrian has entered the gathering. Livia has joined the gathering. Mara is the only viewpoint. Unusual cue-reading always retains ordinary sensory and behavioral explanations.

Earlier pages
When Mara walked into the common room at six fifteen, someone was already there. Someone younger, it seemed.
She couldn’t make out the face behind the blond hair. But the body was fine enough, leaning back on the couch with bare legs crossed and arms flung in opposite directions, one palm pressing the padded wall and the other wrist resting on the low table beside him.
She stood stock-still by the doorway for a moment, aware of her breath and of his awareness that she was here. He stirred and tilted his head; a face turned toward her, expressionless.

The two pairs of eyes held each other through an entire exhale.
And then the young man had pushed himself up on the armrests, stretched to his full height, and strolled over to shake her hand.
“Mara?”
He was closer than she would have guessed from where he’d been sitting, broad-shouldered and strong-chinned in a white T-shirt, grinning at her through dark-framed glasses as he reached out for the handshake. She took it.
“Jonah,” he said.
“Hello,” she replied, and then they stood there, still shaking hands, not knowing what to do next.

And that was when a door opened down the hallway behind her, releasing two voices into the room. One female, one male; one asking a question, the other replying; neither loud nor excited, just casual conversation. Jonah released her hand and turned away to look down the hall at them.
When Mara heard the voices and followed his gaze, she saw one young man who wasn’t Jonah, but close in size and shape; he had brown hair and a beard and was carrying a glass.

The brown-haired man glanced once over his shoulder, saw them standing there, nodded hello, and stepped right into the gathering, holding up his glass of wine—a half-full plastic cup with the Fulcrum logo on it. “Cheers.” He set it on the table against the wall to the left of the door, and stepped forward to shake Jonah’s hand as they exchanged names.

As he set down the glass and moved out of its space, a previously unseen catering worker entered from the hallway behind him and retrieved it. She wore the same black-and-white striped Fulcrum uniform shirt, though she looked old enough to have had that job since the Institute was founded; and she gave no outward sign of the social fact that she was taking their discarded drink back to the kitchen, while the rest of the conversation went on.

Mara watched this woman return to the hallway where she belonged, and noticed herself noticing who assumed whose labor.

“It is a glass of wine,” he said, looking at Mara. “An ordinary plastic cup, such as is now being handed out by that woman, carrying wine in it. If you see anything different, you may certainly tell us.” His gaze still on hers, he placed his right hand flat against the table beside them and said to Jonah, “Hold this, will you?”
Jonah picked up the cup. “Do I want the contents first?”
Adrian waved that aside. “We won’t break the cup, don’t worry.” He held up his left hand, palm outward, toward Mara, and placed three objects in her open palm: a brass key, a white stone, and a wooden token.
Mara looked down at his hand, then up into his eyes, waiting to see what was wanted next.
“Choose one of those three,” he said. “Silently, please. Which one will you keep?” He smiled, leaned closer, and said, “You don’t have to answer right now, mind; but if you don’t want to play at all, we should stop.”

Mara considered for a moment. She wasn’t sure what this game proved, except for the fact that she had agreed to play it, though it seemed simple enough. “I will play,” she said quietly, and looked down at the objects again.

She examined each one carefully. The key was brass, or maybe copper, with no teeth but a wide bow; it might once have been part of a toy, but was now worn and unidentifiable. The white stone felt like limestone to her, weathered smooth, flat on top. And the token looked like the kind they used in a local arcade back home: she remembered them as round wooden chips with numbers printed in blue, though this one was blank.

Mara considered them for another long moment, looking down at her palm. She closed her eyes, and then opened them again, and then—without looking at Adrian—placed one of the three in the palm of his left hand and covered it over with her other hand. “There,” she said.

Exact dramatic payment for the audition
Livia is now present and has not yet attempted a read. Without touching the objects, she identifies which one Mara selected. The result startles Mara, but her visible attention and posture remain an ordinary possible cue. End on Mara's reaction before Livia attempts another read.

And then Livia came in.
Mara sensed her arrival by an almost-imperceptible shift of air, followed a second later by sound; a soft heel on hardwood, just a single step, and then silence again. And then Mara turned around from where she was standing in the middle of the room and saw her there: a tiny figure in black, framed against the background of bright-lit hall beyond the doorway. She held in one hand a bottle of something, and in the other another one of the plastic cups.
