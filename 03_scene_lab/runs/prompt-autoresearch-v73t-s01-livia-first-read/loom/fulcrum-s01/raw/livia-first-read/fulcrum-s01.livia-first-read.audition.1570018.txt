

She was shorter than the others by far, a mere five feet, as dark as a moonless night and just as still; her narrow face was perfectly composed, mouth half-hidden behind a curtain of black hair hanging down below her chin.
The girl moved fluidly, silently, stepping gracefully over the threshold of the room to join the gathering without shifting her eyes away from their faces, and then her gaze came to Mara. Her black eyes went dark and luminous as a pair of pupils in Mara’s face, and then she nodded to say, “I am Livia,” as though offering an invitation.

Mara nodded back. She didn’t know quite what to say in the presence of such directness; instead she said, “Good evening,” and smiled at her. She felt like a stranger meeting royalty, and she watched her movements from under lowered eyelids, wondering how anyone could move so without touching a surface. She seemed to glide about like a feather, or like mist before dawn, and Mara had the impression of being in a dream.

Mara felt the weight of Livia’s eyes on her, a feeling of lightness and ease as they moved toward each other until they were almost touching, their faces only a foot apart. Then she stepped back and spoke.
“I see that you have chosen one of these three objects,” she said to Mara in a low voice, and turned around to take a step away, leaving the cup in Mara’s open hand.

Mara blinked twice as Livia disappeared into the hallway beyond, then took a closer look at what was left behind. Inside the plastic cup lay two tokens, both round, one with numbers printed in blue ink and another with a single star embossed upon its surface; beneath them rested a smooth white stone, flat on top, its edges rounded from constant use.

And beneath them all was the third token—a brass key.
Mara looked up, blinking hard, to see if she had dreamed it all: what Livia had taken away from her, and what was left behind; how easily the girl had read her mind, even before she had spoken.
