
For a moment, no one else noticed her entry. Jonah was still holding the plastic cup in his right hand and the wine bottle in his left, standing quietly with Adrian while they waited for Mara to decide; she stood alone in the center of the room with three objects in her cupped palms. For a few long seconds the two worlds existed simultaneously, until the young men became aware that Livia had entered the room and turned to face her; and then, as if at some silent signal, she stepped forward through the threshold into Mara’s space and vanished, gone without warning or trace, disappeared in a flash of motion, no sound, nothing—and Mara found herself facing two full seconds after she would have expected to, standing face to face with her, a stranger, who was not only not an elderly woman but was shorter than anyone Mara had seen so far.
A sharp breath was all Mara could manage; and she kept it inside, still looking at the tiny woman as Livia nodded and smiled—and then placed the wine bottle, unopened, into Jonah’s left hand and the plastic cup into his right, where they already were. She didn’t even glance at them, just moved them aside as she slid her own right hand between them and the tabletop, and laid something there.
“Here you are,” she said. “I thought you might be getting hungry by now.”

She held both hands up palm-up and moved them slightly toward Mara as if offering her a gift.
And this time Mara could see the objects clearly: they were three small coins. One silver dollar, two pennies. The pennies were dated 1931 and 1938; the dollar was so old that she didn’t recognize the coin at all—it was worn smooth and almost round, with a strange raised figure on one of its faces, something that might once have been a head or maybe an animal.
She knew that Adrian would like these coins as much as she did. And without any conscious thought about why they were there, Mara chose the coin she most wanted to take home with her; and this time it took only a glance down at Livia’s palms.
