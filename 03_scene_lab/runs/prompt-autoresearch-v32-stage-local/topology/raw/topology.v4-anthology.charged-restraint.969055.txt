

Their joined hands hung between them. She wasn’t sure where to put hers when he spoke, still watching her.

“Did you need me to go home with you?” He turned his palm up and tucked her fingers closer into his. “I’m okay either way.”

She knew what he was asking. She nodded without looking at his mouth. “I just need to talk for a while.” She knew that too: words wouldn’t change her feelings. They weren’t supposed to.

His hand stayed in hers when they left the porch and turned into the street. The light from the Institute front steps fell on a redbrick path. The scent of jasmine thickened the air.

A breeze brushed her face. She looked up for stars through the high eucalyptus, but she couldn’t see through the tree canopy. She could hear night birds, owls mostly, but no crickets or other insects.

She didn’t know how long they stood, waiting. Her left foot ached as if it had been asleep but the rest of her was awake. Jonah didn’t say “Let’s go” or “Wait” or “Where do you want to walk?” When she turned he stepped forward with her.

They walked without talking, the only sounds their feet and their breath. She didn’t look at him except to feel him moving beside her. They passed a small house with a dark window above the front steps. Then the next one and the next, then no more houses. When they passed the first dark trees she felt the night close in.

The pavement ended where two driveways had met. They could have been on a dirt road, but there weren’t any streetlights or other cars. It was dark enough for her to see his face without him turning toward her.

She wasn’t sure when they stopped walking. She might have stopped moving first, but he didn’t ask if she had meant it. When he put his hand on the small of her back it was just there, a little pressure that wouldn’t move her or let her stumble.

His lips touched the corner of her mouth when she turned toward him and his left hand slid to her neck. His mouth moved over hers once, twice, three times as if he didn’t know what she would do next. She turned more deeply into him when he put his right hand under her chin to rest with hers. He didn’t break the kiss; she lifted her hand to let it rest where it wanted to be, on his jaw and the curve of his neck. When her fingers touched his hair his whole body sighed out a long breath and he cupped the back of her neck.

“That was a scary thing to do.” His lips brushed the corner of her mouth. “I’m glad I did it.”

She could feel him breathing on her skin, every breath. He didn’t pull away from the kiss or touch anything else. He kissed her more slowly now, as if he needed to taste every movement of her mouth on his. His fingers moved on the nape of her neck, his thumb brushing just behind her ear.

“I don’t want to stop kissing you.” Her voice was soft. “But I need to talk.”

She could feel him turn his head against hers and catch her breath when she spoke again. He breathed deeply enough to vibrate the skin of her neck where she could feel it against her face. He rested his forehead against hers for what felt like forever without closing his eyes. His hand rested in her hair as if he might put it there forever.

“What do you need to say?” He brushed a whisper over her lips.
