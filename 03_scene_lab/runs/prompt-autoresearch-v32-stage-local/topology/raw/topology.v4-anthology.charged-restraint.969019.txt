

“It’s cool in the woods,” Jonah said. “The wind makes it feel colder. Do you have a coat?”

“No.”

“I’ll carry your bag.” Mara carried nothing, but she’d told him so twice in two weeks. “Hold on to me when you need to, okay?”

The hand he held still felt light, barely touching her. Mara looked at their joined wrists and elbows before looking into his face again.

“Will you be here tomorrow?”

“I don’t think so,” Jonah said. “My schedule’s full.”

Mara looked at the hand on his belt buckle and waited.

She saw him notice. His eyes went to her elbow, then back to her face. “Do you want to tell me something?”

She didn’t. Mara looked down again, saw their wrists linked by a single strip of leather. “If you put your hand there—” She pointed to the gap between her elbow and his— “my hand will have somewhere to go.”

Jonah bent and waited.

She slid her fingers through the strip and felt her arm against his shoulder. Her hair smelled like the cool, dry air they’d just left.

He’d stopped smiling but his shoulders stayed back. Mara set her cheek on his shoulder and tilted her head so that his eyes and hers met in the mirror.

“I’m scared.” She let his shirt and shoulder hide her tears. He turned and held her in one arm, her bag in the other, and she felt warm skin on her cheek. “It’s dark,” Mara said. “I like holding on to you.”

“Okay,” Jonah said into her hair. “How do you want to go home?”

He meant which way, but Mara had no idea. She said, “I don’t know,” and let her tears run.

Jonah touched her with his palm just below her ear, held her there until she stopped. “You look like you’re asleep.”

“I’m not.”

“I know.” The hand didn’t move until Mara did, then came down to her shoulder and rested there. Mara tilted her face into his hand, felt the skin there cool and soft. He lifted her up from his shoulder, turned their joined hands to rest her on his arm, and held her there without touching her wrist or elbow.

“Should we stay here?” Jonah asked.

“No,” Mara said. “I want to go home.”

He didn’t look down as they walked through a cold, still night. His eyes went up into the trees until the wind changed the smell and touched his cheek. Then he looked past her and kept looking in that direction as if it were the only one.

When their shoulders and arms met in the same way Mara knew how his body felt from every side. If he kept looking somewhere else they might have nothing left to say, but if he didn’t look she’d never know where they were going.

Mara turned her face into the wind. Her skin still smelled like him.

“I don’t see anything,” Mara said. “Do you?”

“I don’t know,” Jonah said. “Maybe. Why?”

“I thought we were going to look.”

“Okay.” He raised his left hand and touched his cheek, then brought it down until she saw what he meant.

“That way,” Mara said.

“Okay,” Jonah said.

The wind stopped and they walked through a quiet air. Mara turned toward his touch. She kept walking without looking down.
