

“Tell me what we did,” Jonah said after a moment. “I couldn’t keep track of you.”

She told him. He made a small surprised noise when she described the touch with the stranger and frowned when she described leaving the group. Mara finished and they were quiet for a few breaths.

“I can tell you about my side if you want to hear.” Jonah paused again, eyes on hers, his breath audible in the night air. “Do you want to hear?”

“Sure,” she said. She cleared her throat because it was tight with the strain of listening closely for any sign that he didn’t want to talk about himself.

He spoke and she felt that her left hand wasn’t in the right place. She couldn’t focus on his voice if her eyes were on their hands and she could tell if her eyes weren’t on their hands because his left thumb moved under hers when he paused between sentences. She knew that if she could keep still for long enough it would make sense to her.

“That’s what we did, anyway,” Jonah said, closing one hand on her arm just above the elbow and watching her eyes. The pressure was light, almost absent—less than a caress and more than a touch of reassurance. He was waiting to see if she moved or stepped away.

Mara had the idea of pulling her arm free so she could turn and face him. Then his face was too much to manage and she knew that the arm would make sense if she kept still for long enough. His pressure hadn’t changed but it didn’t stop being there either.

“I’m sorry about your session,” she said after a few breaths. Jonah’s face shifted as if he were about to speak. Instead he smiled and held still without looking away. Mara’s lips moved with the idea of kissing him but stopped before his face was close enough.

“I want to kiss you,” Jonah said when the air became too dry to bear any longer.

“Okay.” She shifted her weight to her left foot and felt the muscles in their arms stretch without touching.

Jonah shifted to a hip angle and looked at her lips instead of her eyes. Mara held still until she felt that moving would make sense. Her face turned toward his shoulder as her feet made her turn toward his left side. Jonah’s breath changed to a deep, calm pattern without sound, so calm it might have been apnea.

Then they touched and the rest was a series of movements so small she could only tell which happened first by remembering what it felt like.
