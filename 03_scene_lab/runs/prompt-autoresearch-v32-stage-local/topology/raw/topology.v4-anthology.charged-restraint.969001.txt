

Mara stepped out onto the concrete of the wide porch and let him lead her down the stairs.

“You look so different when you’re tired.”

Mara looked up at his side profile, surprised to see him watching her face. “Is that bad?”

“No, I like it. It’s like you’re a little younger.”

“I didn’t think I could get much younger.” She made herself say that as lightly as he’d said the first thing.

He smiled without turning toward her and kept his voice matter-of-fact. “You don’t look tired. You look thoughtful.” He looked over at her, a brief half glance that seemed to say “I like you here with me.”

“My eyes just feel heavy.” She was getting used to feeling his fingers close around hers, steady and firm but not squeezing. It kept her from feeling how heavy her legs felt walking on the black asphalt of the parking lot.

He nodded slightly. “Your forehead’s creased, but they don’t look red and irritated like sometimes.”

Mara’s eyebrows went up a fraction and she gave him an odd look. She didn’t want to know how her eyes looked.

“That’s not what I meant,” he said, noticing the way her eyes went big and startled for just a second. “You can drop the guard. That was your problem.” He meant something other than eyes, something like her muscles, her breathing, her whole body.

He didn’t say anything else as they walked together in the dark toward the city and the lights of the bay. She made herself look up at the sky with his eyes and breathe as slowly and easily as him. They were both in sneakers, walking without touching except where their fingers held each other. The warm night air smelled like a hot metal roof and flowers from an open window.

“You said I could ask anything.”

“Yeah?” He tilted his head toward hers. “What is it?”

She looked down at the black pavement under her feet and tried to get her breathing to stay slow and easy. Her skin felt tight around her bones.

“Would you help me if I asked?”

He nodded slowly. “Yeah.”

“I think I might want to ask for something.” Her voice was a little shaky.

“That’s good,” he said. His eyes stayed on her face and his right arm kept her steady. His voice stayed low and calm even though she could feel his muscles tightening with tension. She was so tired she felt like she’d been running for hours without stopping. Her hands were trembling and the sweat on her back felt cold in the breeze.

“What would you want me to do?”

“Just hold me.”

He stopped walking and let go of her hand to cup her cheek with his thumb and forefinger, holding it gently against his palm. His eyes never left hers and he let her keep looking at him without turning away. She felt herself relaxing in a way she never had before, like someone else had taken control of the parts of her body that didn’t want to work.

“I’m sorry,” she said, feeling like an idiot for having to admit she was scared, but not able to stop.

“Don’t be sorry,” he said, brushing her hair back off her cheek with one hand and touching her forehead with his lips. It felt good and warm, so soft and gentle.
