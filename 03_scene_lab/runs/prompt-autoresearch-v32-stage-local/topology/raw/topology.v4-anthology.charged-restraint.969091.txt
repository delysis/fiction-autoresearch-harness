

He walked down the steps at her pace without touching her. As they walked he fell in a quarter turn ahead so her left arm brushed the back of his hand every two or three steps. When he reached for her shoulder on a hill she moved into the pressure to show him her weight was on its way. He walked as if he didn’t need to know when she’d get there—as if he knew it already.

Her shoe stepped in a soft spot of mud; Jonah stopped in front of her and put his left hand near her face, palm down. He balanced her on the inside of her forearm with just enough pressure to keep her from falling but not enough to make her stumble.

“Careful,” he said.

She set her left hand on his forearm and used his arm for balance, letting him guide her forward with his thumb as she lifted her foot out of the mud. He let her test the ground around his shoe before moving his own foot, so they never stood on mud at the same time.

The path widened, but he didn’t let go; as her hand stayed on his forearm he moved it down to his upper arm in one fluid motion, then back to his forearm after three steps, like a silent exchange. Mara felt a pull in her throat and stopped walking.

“Can I do this?” she asked, and wrapped the other arm around the forearm she’d just let go.

She heard a deep inhale and held her breath. After a slow second she felt his arms come up around her waist without releasing her forearm—almost a cradle, but not quite. Her shoulder rested under his chin. His lips were cool on the top of her head.

He didn’t answer, so she spoke again. “Can I?”

“You’ve already asked twice,” he said in an easy voice, and dropped his arms without saying yes or no.

His steps stayed sure as if the night wasn’t dark at all. Mara had to look ahead of her feet to avoid a rut in the road, but Jonah stepped past it as if he could see it by touch alone.

When the road flattened out into an asphalt shoulder Mara stopped walking. “I don’t need to hold on now.”

“You still have my arm,” Jonah said without stopping.

“Not now.” Mara let go and felt her breathing deepen. “Now I’m okay.”

“Okay?”

“To walk alone.” She looked straight ahead, wishing she had the words for what was in her mind.

His steps slowed to a stop. She heard him inhale without saying anything—not for breath but for time. His throat worked as he swallowed. Then he walked ahead, slowly. “I could walk beside you,” he said.

Mara lifted one hand to pull her hair back from her eyes and felt the dry warmth of his forearm. Jonah’s hair was as soft as fur, smooth beneath her fingertips. A low exhalation told her that he was breathing again—not shallowly this time. He was looking into her eyes; she met his gaze without looking away, watching him without noticing.

He took her hand without pressing on it, waiting for her to open her fingers. The fingertips were cold against the warmth of her own; when they touched the cold went away and left only the feel of the warm skin. Mara let him touch her arm, the shoulder, the side of her neck. The skin under her hair was dry and warm and he touched it with a quiet pressure.
