“I know I’m being a coward. And I know I’m not very good at what I do,” said Mara, sitting at the kitchen counter with her head in her hands, and feeling the heat of Jonah’s body against her back as he stood behind her. “I know it all. But it was a long day—no, more than that. I guess I’m having trouble with the whole thing right now.”

Jonah waited without responding. She felt the steady pressure of his left hand at her shoulder, just below her ear. “Let me say it again.” He lowered his lips and mouth near her ear so that her hair moved between them. “You are good at what you do. And you’re a good partner for me. I can tell.” He didn’t add the I like you, Mara part because that, in this conversation, seemed to her a given. He’d said it once, a year and a half ago, and she’d agreed. Since then, they’d never talked about it because it seemed so obvious that they liked each other. But today, for reasons that neither of them understood, their obvious feelings had become unspoken.
