Jonah walked beside her, offering his open hand. She ignored it and put her free hand into a pocket. “Your palm was sweating,” she said. “I felt it in the handshake. Like it was something new. Your mouth felt new too, like the first time.”

“There’s nothing new about it. It’s been six years. There’s nothing to be embarrassed about. You were the one who broke it off.”

“You were the one who walked away.”

“It was too much for you.”

“No, it was too much for you. You decided for both of us.”

“You needed to pull back. You weren’t ready for a relationship. You said so.”

“That’s different.”

“It isn’t different.”

She could feel him looking at her. His gaze stayed on her as he glanced left and right and watched where they were going. She liked that. She wanted him to keep watching her and not say anything.

“What isn’t different?”

“Six years. We’re still living parallel lives. That’s the way we keep it safe.”

“Nothing’s safe.”

“Six years is a long time to go on being careful.”

“That’s what it means to keep things safe.”

He took his hand from his pocket. The street lamp showed its lines. He’d been working on his thesis at the computer
