 Then he sat up and leaned forward, setting both feet on the floor and bracing one arm on the chairback behind him.
“Oh, great,” he said. “New person.”
She stood where she was for another beat, taking this in.
Then she walked straight across the room and offered her hand. “Hi.”
He stared at the offered hand for a second before standing, taking it, and shaking it firmly. She let him decide which way to go, and he pulled her to the couch beside him, sitting down. She sat beside him as they both waited, not looking at each other.
“Jonah.”
“Mara. I’m the new one,” she said. “I’m sorry; I didn’t know the party would be so late, or—”
“It isn’t a party,” he said, glancing up at her face. He didn’t look older than she’d thought from behind; maybe not even eighteen. But the face was very open as it searched hers, and she met his gaze. “We don’t have parties here. This isn’t that sort of place. You haven’t heard of us?”
“I heard your name, but—”
“Fulcrum. We’re Fulcrum.” He’d gone back to looking around the room as he spoke. Now he glanced at her again and she was reminded that he hadn’t really been listening either. She shrugged and looked away as he looked away, wondering who she’d talked with in the hallway.
“There was a couple of older guys; one who talked about having a late meeting, and another guy. With the woman. I didn’t see anyone else.”
He nodded, his eyes still on the wall behind her head, and she caught a hint of surprise or distaste as his lips twitched. “Adrian and Livia?” he asked, turning to look back at her.
She nodded again, wondering if this was a test of some kind. The Fulcrum people had mentioned that he would be in here by seven, but hadn’t specified where or that she should be waiting for him here.
