
The floor felt like ice under her feet as she moved across it toward the sofa. He rose and made her a formal bow, then gestured to the seat he’d occupied. His eyes never left hers for an instant; she knew this had to be one of Livia’s men.
She took her place on the couch, aware that he was standing behind it. “Hi,” she said. “I’m Mara.” She glanced up at him, aware of the height difference and of the oddness of talking to someone who stood above her and didn’t offer to sit down.
“Jonah,” he said, his head nodding slightly. “I am Fulcrum.” His voice was deep and smooth.
Mara blinked at him; she hadn’t understood a word, though she knew that she’d heard correctly. “What?” she asked.
“Fulcrum is the name of this institute,” he explained. “We study communication here, all kinds of communication. It is also the name of what we do here. It is the name of my training and of Livia’s. I am Fulcrum.”
It all made perfect sense. She nodded, slowly. She could see no reason why he should offer to sit down. “And,” she added, “so is Livia. Or so she says.”
“And so do we all say,” he told her. “You may do this too.” He nodded slightly in response to her look and stepped back from the couch, as if he’d answered her question.
She turned her head to look at him. He was wearing a black shirt that strained at the seams over his chest; it was open at the neck and showed a gold chain around his bare throat. His pants were black too and hung in straight folds from his hips to his ankles. Black sandals covered his feet. He had no hair except that blond mass that flowed down from his head and nearly touched his shoulders in back.
“Where does Livia sleep?” she asked impulsively, aware of the fact that her body was as bare as his except for a sheet of thin cotton and a thin film of sweat. She didn’t know how many people were going to walk in here in the next five minutes, but she had to get used to it; she wasn’t leaving until tomorrow afternoon, at the very earliest.
