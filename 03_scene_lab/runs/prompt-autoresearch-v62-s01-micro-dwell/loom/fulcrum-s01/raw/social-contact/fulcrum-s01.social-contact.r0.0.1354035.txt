 She thought it might be the boy with the dark hair she’d met in the hall last night, but he had brown eyes. She cleared her throat and said hi.
“Hi,” he echoed, without enthusiasm.
He shifted then to sit upright, legs uncrossed. The arm resting on the wall came down onto his thigh, fingers curled around his knee. Mara blinked, thinking at first that it was the angle of the light that made the hair on his wrist seem so fine and fair, but then she saw that his lower arms and legs were covered with what looked like down, fine golden hairs against his pale skin.
She’d read about hypertrichosis, though she’d never met anyone with it. She was pretty sure that this wasn’t the congenital kind, though—not with that one spot on his chest she’d seen last night, and not with the light color of the hair and the delicate curl and length to it.
“How come?” she asked.
He looked down at his legs in surprise, as if he hadn’t realized how he was sitting.
“Huh? Oh.” He let one hand rest on his knee and gave his arm a small pat with the other, as if it was a dog he’d called to his side. “Livia said I can’t do the training if I’m not taking the drugs. So I stopped.”
He was talking about what happened with her hair, she thought: Livia had told her about it before they came here, though not in detail. Had the hair gotten shorter? She couldn’t tell. It seemed like it might have.
“So did it work?”
He looked away, then, into the open space between the sofa he sat on and the low table where they ate.
“It worked fine,” he said. He wasn’t looking at her any more, so she couldn’t be sure of the inflection to his words. “It worked too well.”
She wanted to ask how, but decided not to. He would probably tell her when he was ready. She waited a moment, and then turned her back on him to walk toward the kitchen, feeling that he had made the opening for the conversation narrow and steep as the blade of a hatchet, and now she’d missed it.
