
She smiled in a friendly way, but he didn’t respond. She thought for a second he was a statue and wondered why no one else seemed to have seen him yet.
Then his shoulders moved, subtly, in the direction of the couch. It seemed less like an invitation than a challenge.
Mara stopped in the middle of the room and looked around, at the empty chairs and tables, at the wall lined with books. She saw no place that looked more inviting than the couch, but she didn’t want to make him move or turn again if he didn’t want to. So she stood still.
He didn’t look away. She realized that he was going to wait her out, and decided to see who would blink first.
The pause stretched. The sound of voices rose faintly from somewhere outside the building. He leaned back further against the wall, and she could almost feel his muscles stretch and relax.
After what seemed like minutes, she felt a slight sensation in the back of her neck, as though someone were breathing there, or had laid a hand lightly against the nape of her neck.
She turned around, but nobody was there.
“I’m Mara,” she said aloud. “What’s your name?”
