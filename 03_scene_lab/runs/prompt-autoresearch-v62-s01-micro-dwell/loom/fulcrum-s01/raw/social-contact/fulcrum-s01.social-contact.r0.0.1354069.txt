 The lips might be smiling and might not. His eyes were dark enough that Mara couldn’t make out the shape of the iris.
Then he spoke in a clear voice. “I hope you didn’t wake me.”
That made it smile. Mara opened her mouth and closed it, not certain how to answer. After a moment, she said, “You’re Adrian, then?”
“I’m not your new partner,” the dark eyes clarified. “But I’m the other one.”
She was even less certain what this meant, but decided to take another stab anyway. “Who’s my partner?”
The blond head tipped a fraction to the right; that made the face into a profile and deepened Mara’s uncertainty. “You didn’t have one yet. But Jonah will be back soon.”
A pause lengthened between them. Adrian didn’t move again, and Mara wasn’t sure what to do with that. She cleared her throat before she could stop herself, and said, “What should I—”
A man’s footstep came through the door behind her, and her heart thumped harder. Was that Jonah? She turned toward him. The same dark hair as Adrian’s, the same narrow face but with a mustache that cut the space in two. The same casual stance in a blue-and-black robe, one palm raised in front of him.
A palmful of steaming green tea in a small cup.
Mara blinked and tried again. “What should I—”
The third man’s voice spoke above her, slow enough that she caught herself blinking again. “Just hang out till the others arrive.”
Mara looked up again. This man wasn’t wearing slippers; the worn toes of his black sandals peeped through the robe, and the hem was ragged with age.
He had one palm lifted toward her but the other was clasped with Livia’s. When she looked between them, the fingers were intertwined; his large brown fingers were the background for the smaller white ones.
Livia and Adrian smiled at each other; that made Mara notice she wasn’t sure how to smile in return. She did smile anyway. “Hello, I’m—”
She stopped and waited. They knew who she was.
“Are you the new person?” The question was from Jonah. “I thought so.”
