
“Hi,” she said, going on in, “I’m Mara.”
He was a boy, younger than she had guessed, no more than a teenager.
He blinked and sat up to offer her his hand. “Welcome to Fulcrum. I’m Jonah.”
Mara shook his hand. It was pale and cool but not damp, the fingers smooth but firm.
“You’re one of the scientists, I take it?”
“No. I’m a subject.” His voice sounded slightly strained. “Like you.”
She blinked at him. “I thought most of the subjects here were adults. It sounded like that from the application materials.”
“Not all of them,” he said shortly. “You’re an adult, and I’m a boy. So there.”
“Well, I didn’t mean to sound like—”
A door opened behind her and she turned toward it. Adrian came in, wearing a plain black shirt with gray jeans and black dress shoes, and smiled at her. “Mara! So nice to meet you—and I see you’ve met Jonah already.” He shook his head. “So sorry we weren’t able to receive you on your arrival. Livia’s down with the flu, as it turns out. It must have been quite something to find yourself here with no one at hand to show you around.”
He looked at the two of them where they stood facing each other on the green rug by the coffee table. The silence lengthened, and Mara opened her mouth, but Jonah spoke first.
“No one was here when I came,” he said stiffly. “I just went and took the first room I liked. You haven’t picked out a room yet?”
Mara hesitated, then shrugged.
“Not yet. I was looking at a few, but…”
She looked at him. Was that a challenge in his expression? If so, she wasn’t going to meet it.
“I was waiting to meet everyone first, actually.”
“Good choice.” Adrian turned to him with a smile. “Why don’t you show Mara the rooms she hasn’t looked at yet, and I’ll take her on the tour. What do you think, Jonah? Would you like that?”
