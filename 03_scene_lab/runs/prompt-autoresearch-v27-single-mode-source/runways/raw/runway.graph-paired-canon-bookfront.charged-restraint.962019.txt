After the experiment, it was early morning.

Jonah’s hand was outstretched when he turned to her, like he was waiting. Or ready. For what, she wasn’t sure. The door’s weight didn’t slow him. As he caught the cool night breeze, he looked back into the room, then let it close. He turned back to her, standing on the sill with a hand extended.

“I’m walking home tonight.” The lights dimmed when the lab went offline. It was nearly two hours before dawn. She wasn’t sure if she should take his hand. He was waiting for her, like he wanted to. He turned away after waiting and was quiet.

Her hand went up into his palm. It was warm. He didn’t close it or pull. She turned away too, as if not watching him watch her was important. “Are you going to tell me about the experiment now?” Her back was turned and she had to strain to see him in the streetlights.
