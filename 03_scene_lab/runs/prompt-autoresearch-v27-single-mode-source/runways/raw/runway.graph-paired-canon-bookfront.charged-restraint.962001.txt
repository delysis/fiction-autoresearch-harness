Jonah stood silent, waiting for me. He'd offered his hand to hold as
we walked the four blocks home in the quiet dark after the Fulcrum lab
closed. We had been through a long late session, the kind where everyone
pretends to be someone else and then everyone has to guess. This session
had gone badly, badly in a way that left a residue of accusation and
self-protection in the air.

Everyone had gathered for the debrief, talking over what each person
had done, why they'd made certain choices. Each of us had spoken at
length, not to justify our choices so much as to make each other forgive
them. Everyone agreed that I had given the most difficult performance,
so the debrief had taken a long time. It took an especially long time
because when it was my turn, I chose not to defend myself, which
apparently caused an even more complicated feeling. I felt it, the way
everyone looked at me as I sat there not speaking.

And Jonah. It was bad because when it was Jonah's turn to give his
justification, he didn't speak.
