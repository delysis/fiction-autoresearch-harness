The door opened with a soft mechanical whir.

Jonah peered in. “Mara, are you in there?”

She wasn’t sure how he found her in this labyrinth, but he knew to look here, on this floor, behind this door.

“Mara, come on. It’s late. We need to go.”

She turned her back to the door, pretending not to hear.

Jonah’s voice, now through the door’s closed glass, said, “Mara? Talk to me? Please?”

Mara didn’t move. She looked down at her bare feet, and at the floor. The floor’s grey tiles were small squares, like the squares in the grid they were studying.

Jonah came in and sat on the floor beside her, legs crossed. They faced the wall, the one with the two large screens and the grid. The screens were now dark. The grid was lit, but no one was in it.

“Want to talk?”

The grid was a small room, its walls made from clear plexiglass. Mara sat on this side of it; Jonah sat on the other side.
