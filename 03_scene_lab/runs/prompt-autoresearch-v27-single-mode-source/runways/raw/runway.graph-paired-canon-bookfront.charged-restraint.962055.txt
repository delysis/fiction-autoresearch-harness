There had been no easy way to explain. The experimental session ended and we all gathered in a circle of chairs to process. For forty-five minutes we talked around and through it, never landing on the root issue.

I thought it was the way we read each other.

Jonah thought it was the way we read the subjects.

Both of us thought it was how we were reading it all.

I know how to talk to other people. I’ve lived and worked and thrived in the world. But when the camera is rolling I forget how to read all the human cues. If we were good at that, we’d have just moved on to another run and forgotten what just happened. We’d have known which things to hold on to and which to let go. But we didn’t.

I couldn’t find a way to say how hard it was to watch those young researchers. I felt myself trying to figure out how Jonah and the other analysts were seeing those students. What would be a good outcome from the session? What would be the right thing to do? What should I have done differently? But I couldn’t seem to think.
