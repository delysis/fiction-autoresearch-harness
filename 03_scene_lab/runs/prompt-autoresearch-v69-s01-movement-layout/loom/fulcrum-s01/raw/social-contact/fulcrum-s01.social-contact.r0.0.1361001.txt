
“Not you?” she said, as if talking to herself.
“Sorry,” he said in a low, careful voice. “I didn’t hear the door.”
His eyes were not dark or light. They took no stance toward her; they had a neutral shape. His mouth was a little dry, she thought. She could not place his accent.
“This your first time?”
She nodded and walked over to the table. “How long have you been at Fulcrum?”
“Six months,” he said with another slight twist in his voice. He made no move to rise; he had only given up a little space in his chair. She sat down across from him at one end of the low table and looked away, as if expecting someone else. “I’m Jonah,” he added. “Reed.”
“Mara Vale.” She watched him watch her and did not reach out for his hand.
He was looking for a place to put it, and she felt almost sorry for him. A little further off, a glass held water and two bottles of beer. She was surprised; she thought he was younger, or not this young. She wondered what his hair smelled like.
“Did I startle you?” He was smiling now, trying for easy, as if to compensate for her first impression. “You look a bit startled. Or just impatient.”
She laughed. “Yes, both of those. A little.”
