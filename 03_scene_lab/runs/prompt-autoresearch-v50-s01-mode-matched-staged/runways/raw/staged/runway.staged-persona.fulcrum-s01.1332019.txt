The path leads from an outer gate to an inner gate and a security
console, then across an expanse of well-kept grass. The console’s
fingerprint reader recognized Mara; she was expecting this after the
email. A white cat in a bright patch of sunlight walks with a flexible
gait, not straight into Mara. The path ends at a redwood-and-glass
structure where people are entering and exiting through several open
glass doors.

Mara has only a small suitcase. It doesn’t feel too heavy, but she
finds that the wheels and the suitcase's handle keep adjusting to each
other, so Mara must correct the suitcase’s course. She stops to adjust
the handle’s height, then corrects the course again. The suitcase does
not go straight into other people. Mara takes a few more steps, and
then, from the other side of the redwood-and-glass building, Livia
Sloane sees Mara through an open door, calls “Mara?”, and comes out with
a friendly smile. The building is not tall enough to obscure people
from each other. Mara wonders whether her appearance at the door is
predictable, how much of it is timing.

Beauty and asymmetry

Inside, Adrian Voss welcomes Mara to Fulcrum. He's in his sixties and
speaks easily, his voice smooth and warm. He introduces himself as a
co-founder of Fulcrum, an institute studying attention and psychotech—a
term Adrian uses casually, with the expectation that Mara knows it.
Mara does; one of her sisters is involved in the research. “Fulcrum’s a
place for people to learn how to read posture, voice, timing,” Adrian
says, standing next to Mara in front of an empty glass wall. He seems
to expect Mara to understand him by noticing the pace and context of
his words, rather than from the words themselves. “People come here for
many reasons. It’s a place to learn how to read people better—or just
to be around them.” His voice is friendly, but also authoritative—it
sounds like an invitation, but also a command. Livia Sloane and Adrian
both stand near Mara, looking at her as she listens to Adrian speak.
Both of them seem to want to help Mara understand something important.
They don’t smile at each other; their faces are serious.
