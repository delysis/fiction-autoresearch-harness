The conversation drew Mara’s attention in. She almost forgot how much she was focusing on their movements, and how many ways they were expressing themselves through nonverbal cues. The brown-haired newcomer, still carrying that same plastic cup with the Fulcrum logo and the red liquid in it, was now explaining about a recent project when he spotted Mara, took two steps toward her, and paused a little way off to shake hands and exchange names. She reached out for his hand, but when he didn’t take hers he dropped down into the low seat instead, letting their fingertips touch just before the handshake was supposed to happen, and he leaned in closer than she expected; she straightened a bit more formally, trying to remind herself that they weren’t yet friends.
The man who’d started this all off laughed. “I didn’t realize how long it had been since we did the whole ‘handshake’ thing,” he said, and she watched the two of them ease back a little, the newcomer still leaning casually toward her, and they all smiled together as though to say that they had a common cause with each other now.
“Anyway, that was something I was working on for awhile,” the new fellow said to both Mara and Jonah. “It started with this idea I had, a sort of—” His voice faded as he turned toward the table where his plastic cup still sat, and he gestured vaguely. The idea had taken him over for some time, but now it was apparently difficult to explain. Jonah didn’t look particularly interested.
