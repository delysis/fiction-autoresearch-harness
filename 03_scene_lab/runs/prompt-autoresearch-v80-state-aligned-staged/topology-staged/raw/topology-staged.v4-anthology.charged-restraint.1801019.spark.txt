
Mara felt her entire body tense, as though she might leap in pursuit, but his steps were slow, regular, unhurried, as if he had nowhere to be, no urgency.
She let out the breath she had been holding in for a long time and took a deep breath of cold night air. She could hear her heart beating in her ears; the rhythm was fast. Her shoulders were tight and heavy, like the weight of stones. Her eyes felt like they were on fire. She had an urge to tear them out of her skull.
Jonah moved slowly across the drive. It wasn’t even a walk. She didn’t know how he could stand still for this long.
Jonah reached the bottom of the steps, paused, and turned slightly. He waited, his feet motionless on the blacktop drive. She saw his lips twitch and he raised one hand, waving at her as though he were a thousand miles away, not just twenty yards across a silent parking lot.
He knew how long it would take her to make up her mind. He knew she wouldn’t go back in the front door of Fulcrum, wouldn’t run
