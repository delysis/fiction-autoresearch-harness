 The man’s heartbeat had barely changed at all during the night. She caught herself glancing toward her chest and quickly averted her eyes, as though to check her pulse would give away some secret.
He paused at the bottom of the steps and offered his hand. She felt a faint trembling in her own hands. Her right palm was sweaty. She slid her left into the soft leather and gripped his hand. He squeezed hers back: a handshake between friends or business partners, nothing more.
She took a deep breath. “Good night,” she said, her voice cracking slightly. “I’ll see you tomorrow.”
“I suppose so,” Jonah said. The dark eyes narrowed, almost closed, and the corners of the mouth turned up. It could have been amusement, or maybe surprise. She couldn’t tell. “Are you going to be all right, Mara?”
She cleared her throat, but the voice was still hoarse when it came out. “Oh, I’ll be fine.” Her left hand trembled again in his grasp. She felt like a kid who’d just learned how to shake hands: the thumb and forefinger pressed down tight as she forced herself not to squeeze his hand back. He squeezed hers. “You should go, Jonah. I’m going to have to walk back to the hotel alone.”
“Alone?” he said. There was something like concern on his face now, as though he thought she were in danger. Her heart skipped a beat at the thought.
“Of course,” she said. “I’m not going to stay with you all night.” She felt a blush creeping up her neck and cheeks. It would be obvious in the soft moonlight, no doubt. “It’s a long walk. I’d rather not do it alone.” She could feel her left hand tightening around his again. She swallowed hard and forced it to relax.
“I don’t mind,” Jonah said, looking up at her. He was smiling slightly. The corners of the mouth twitched. “But I suppose that’s up to you.” His gaze dropped to the sidewalk, where he continued staring. It was nearly two miles back to the hotel, she thought. He hadn’t said he would follow her. Hadn’t even asked if he could. He would walk home, and she would walk home, and it might be several days or weeks before they saw each other again.
Her right hand rose almost on its own. “Wait,” she said. Her breath came out in a short gasp, but she couldn’t stop herself. There was nothing else for it now, though, was there?
His left hand turned palm down, and his fingers brushed hers briefly. She swallowed again. The trembling started in her hands and spread to her legs and back, until she was shaking all over. He stood stock-still; the only movement came from his eyes as they moved between her face and the sidewalk. The darkness beyond the front gate was thick and close, blotting out the distant street lamps that gleamed like tiny stars.
She cleared her throat again. “I suppose you don’t want to go with me,” she said. Her voice wavered slightly. “But I wouldn’t be able to sleep if I stayed here. And it’s a long way.” She stopped and took another deep breath. “If we go together, at least there will be someone to talk to.”
