 His back was straight as a rod; one side of his jaw ticked rhythmically as though he were chewing something.
At the bottom of the stairs, she touched his arm as though asking permission to walk alongside him. “Thanks for this,” she said quietly, looking away. “For…tonight. For everything.”
He nodded slightly, his body still turned away from her.
“No.” He took her hand instead of returning the touch. “Thank you, Mara.”
She let herself be led into the night.

They walked a little faster along the street; the cold air did nothing to lower the heat that still crawled in her head. She was hungry, but she didn’t care; she wanted the heat to go away and leave her body alone. Jonah’s hand stayed where it was: his fingers tangled with hers, warm, close to her heartbeat. She stared ahead, trying to read the signs on buildings, but they were too dark or too hard to focus on. A couple of people went past in the opposite direction. She saw a homeless person huddled in a doorway; they walked by silently.
It wasn’t far to the hotel. Mara felt a prickle of static across her skin as she crossed the street. The city was quieter now, no more traffic. Jonah let go of her hand, and the sudden cold made her flinch; her grip on his arm tightened and she pulled him back a little.
He stopped, surprised. His face was pale in the light from a streetlamp, the outline of his lips almost lost in shadow.
“You okay?” She meant to ask if he’d been hurt before, tonight, but her voice came out as a whisper.
“I…”
There was nothing between them—just the darkness of a narrow space in an alley behind some businesses that had closed for the night. He looked away and then back at her, his eyes dark and thoughtful. She felt like an object he had noticed in the night’s background and had only now chosen to look at seriously. He took her hand again; his fingertips moved gently over hers as if touching the surface of water.
“Are you?” she asked. “Really?”
She could feel him hesitate. His breath was warmer than the cold air; she could see the mist from his exhaled words, like smoke from an extinguished candle.
He opened his mouth to speak but stopped, as though unsure what to say. Instead, he lifted his other hand and touched her cheek gently. She leaned forward without thinking, into his palm. His fingers were soft as silk against her skin.
When she stopped, his hand fell away from her face.
“I’m fine,” she said quickly, not wanting him to see that she wasn’t.
He watched her for a moment, his expression unreadable. He pulled away and started walking again, faster than before; she followed, still holding his hand.

She almost turned back as they reached the hotel door. The heat in her head was like a drum, too loud to be ignored. Her whole body felt ready to jump. She didn’t want the night to end, or for him to let go of her. She didn’t know what to say, so she said nothing at all and let herself be led.
He paused just inside the door, the automatic lights blinking on around them. He turned to face her, standing close but not touching. “We can get some food, if you like,” he said quietly, glancing around as though making sure they were alone.
“I can’t eat anything,” she whispered. “It’s too soon.”
