He was a silent dark line ahead of her. His head turned back to look at the stars, and she watched him looking.
Jonah stopped, a few paces from her. “Are you okay?” The question was quiet and serious, but when he turned, there was no worry on his face. She saw no signs of it in his body or his hands. He didn’t wait for her to answer. She looked at him until his gaze drifted down into her face. His eyes rested for a moment in the hollow where her throat met her neck. His hand was near his hip. There was no tension in his posture, but he held his weight evenly on both feet. He didn’t seem impatient, or anxious to leave.
She heard a sudden movement behind her and stopped walking. She felt Jonah’s gaze pass over her again and return, and she turned her head to watch him.
The shadow of a van crossed the drive in front of them as it turned around on the road. Its tail lights disappeared; its doors slammed. Mara’s shoulders tensed, and she heard herself exhale without knowing how much breath had gathered in her chest. Her armpits prickled; she felt an itch below one of her ribs, but didn’t move. Her eyes went to Jonah’s face. He watched her with no expression but waiting. Her back and thighs tightened together again as her legs turned and she continued walking after him.
His hands came loose from his hips and he slowed as he went. The cold wind ruffled the hair at the back of his neck; his shirt caught faintly on his skin again. Mara saw his knuckles lighten as his fingers spread and closed on his shirtsleeve, but there was no force in the movement. Her head felt light. She stopped walking and tried to focus on her breath. The air came in through her nostrils, cool and thin on her tongue. Her eyes rested on Jonah’s back as she forced herself to breathe.
His shirt settled around him once more as he walked, its sound small against the rustle of his breath. He crossed a stretch of bare earth and stepped onto a path of worn flagstones, each stone uneven beneath his feet. His shirt settled on his skin again, but his breathing remained slow and even. She couldn’t hear it any more than she could hear her own, but she knew it was there.
His pace slowed once more. She heard a faint noise in his throat—the last of the breath released from his lungs, a small, soft sigh. Her own breathing quickened, then stopped entirely. She tried again, but it didn’t help; she still couldn’t catch her breath. Her ribs felt tight against the inside of her ribcage. She could feel the walls of her chest pressing in, like the hard wooden sides of a tiny box.
