He still wasn’t looking at her, but when she reached him, he held out a hand without saying a word. Mara had known he would do it. It was the right thing to do. That didn’t make it easier to refuse.
She looked at his palm: bare, callused and warm.
He was tall, but he wasn’t too tall to touch her. She leaned up and pressed her cheek into the warm curve of his shoulder; she felt the smooth cotton of his shirt. Her face was hot again. She breathed in hard: he smelled like woodsmoke and clean rain.
He reached past her shoulder, still without looking at her. His hand cupped the nape of her neck.
“You did all right, Mara,” he said, voice soft, so soft. “You were brave tonight. Don’t be scared.”
Her heart stopped. Her whole body stopped, still as a statue: a girl on a hill. A frozen moment.
She knew the story. She knew he didn’t mean it. Still she hesitated. For the first time in two years she hesitated. Her body stayed still when her mind said go.
Jonah shifted his grip, his palm warm against her neck. He was so much taller than she was: he had to lean down to kiss her.
Mara closed her eyes and lifted her face. His breath moved warm and heavy against her lips. His mouth was cool. She couldn’t remember the last time she had touched someone. She put both hands on his chest. The fabric of his shirt was a thin barrier between them.
He kissed her again. Mara tasted him, and her eyes flew open. There was a sharp clean taste of rain, woodsmoke and dust.
She lifted a hand to cup his face. Her fingertips moved through the cool damp silk of his hair. He let out a low, slow breath and stopped moving, his weight solid against hers. She felt the small shudder of his shoulders; his breathing changed, deeper and slower.
It felt good to touch. Mara lifted her head to meet his mouth again, to let her hand slide over his jaw and then lower, into the curve of his neck.
He was still. No move. No protest. She hesitated a heartbeat and pulled his shirt open, unfastening the top buttons one by one. His bare skin was cool under her fingertips.
The air was cold and the stars were bright. Jonah’s hand rested against the side of her jaw, his fingertips warm in her hair. She pressed both hands against his chest, feeling his ribs and his pulse: fast and strong beneath her skin. The stars flickered when he moved.
She lifted her face to meet his mouth. His tongue was soft, warm against hers. She felt her own breath quicken; the scent of rain and woodsmoke rose with it, filling her head. Her thoughts blurred and slowed.
The kiss deepened, deepening, and then Jonah let go of her. His hand dropped away. Her mind cleared.
He let her go, and she knew he was about to do it again. He hadn’t meant to. She opened her eyes. She looked up at his face: tall and wide and calm.
