What was wrong?
Jonah didn’t notice. He breathed through his mouth, in and out, and didn’t speed up his stride. Mara slowed hers to keep the same distance behind him. The street was still and quiet, a row of dim, unmarked windows behind which people slept.
Mara’s heart continued to beat, hard and fast, and she felt her lungs ache. She didn’t have words for what was wrong.
He started up a side street. His shoulders rose and fell with each breath. His hands stayed quiet at his sides, swinging very slowly. He slowed without looking back, waited for her.
Jonah didn’t offer comfort. He didn’t touch her. He stood beside her in silence. He didn’t expect anything, so she stopped thinking about what she could give him. She stopped trying to analyze her own reactions: whether they were normal; whether he was responsible for them. She let herself look up at the empty windows, the darkening sky.
He took a long, quiet breath.
She let out her own breath, slow.
He exhaled through his nose and tilted his head back, looking at the sky.
She let out another small breath, then one more. Her lungs were empty and aching again; she felt faint. She was shivering too.
Jonah didn’t speak or touch her, but he stayed by her side. She relaxed her shoulders, straightened up, and stepped closer.
She could see the sky: distant clouds, a few stars, a low-lying moon. Jonah had said something about it when they first came outside, though she hadn’t heard him at all. She hadn’t noticed anything except the cold and the empty sidewalk and the strange, silent house.
She turned to face Jonah again and met his eyes. His pupils were wide in the dark; she couldn’t see his irises or the color of his eyes. He watched her breathe: he was waiting.
The first thing she felt was the heat from his skin. He wore a long, dark coat over a thin gray shirt, which was open at his neck. She saw the smooth skin of his chest, exposed to the night air. His chest was warm. His cheeks were flushed from the cold, but his lips were still cool, and she felt her own pulse in her temples as he looked down at her.
Mara stepped closer. Her lips were dry; her heart beat faster with every breath.
She moved closer still. Her hands found his shoulders. Her left hand held tight to him while she lifted her right hand to touch his face. The cold air moved through the darkness, and Mara’s breath quickened again as he tilted his head toward her.
The skin on his face was warm and soft, as were his lips. He breathed in and out, slow and deep. She reached up with both hands to hold his face; she felt the hard muscle of his jaw, and she moved her lips over his.
He didn’t respond at first, but he didn’t pull away or try to stop her either. After a moment his mouth parted, and he let out a small, soft sigh.
