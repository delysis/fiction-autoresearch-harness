 The other participants had left immediately after the final debriefing. A group had been forming to go somewhere else; she knew from previous sessions that they would talk over drinks, or smoke, or eat something sweet to compensate for their hunger. She could have gone with them, but she hadn’t moved.
The drive was deserted. A few bright stars showed in the clear sky. He took long steps and swung his arms with each breath. The pavement was dry from a sunny afternoon. They walked down the driveway, side by side but without touching, out to the street. She wasn’t sure whether she should match his gait exactly or not. She matched his breathing instead, letting it guide her pace and rhythm.
The gatehouse stood empty; Fulcrum’s guards had already returned to their quarters at the back of the campus. The gate swung shut behind them with a dull metal thump.
She moved her left foot from one rectangle of cement sidewalk to the next, trying to feel each step as solidly as she could, then the right. Then her left again, then the right again. She took a deep breath, filling her lungs completely. Jonah kept his breathing steady, almost silent.
The street was dark but for a few scattered car lights in the distance. They’d crossed the empty drive to reach it; he’d crossed without hesitation, and she followed him. His left foot crossed to the opposite pavement in midstep without breaking rhythm; she did the same, landing on the far side at the same moment that he did. A few cars were parked by the curb; they passed in front of one. It was an SUV with dark tinted windows; no light showed inside and there was no sound. She passed it just a little ahead of Jonah and felt the wind turn and blow her hair out straight from her face as she went.
She walked as rhythmically and solidly as she could. She watched him at the same time, trying to keep their breaths in time, trying not to glance away.
“Can we walk in silence for a while?” he asked after a few seconds. He looked sideways at her as though waiting for an answer.
“Yes.” The word came out on an exhale. “If that’s what you want, yes.”
“I do.” There was the briefest pause, a hesitation as though he expected to be interrupted before continuing: “There’s too much adrenaline in the air right now. It would be better if we just moved and didn’t try to talk about what happened inside.”
Her hands were sweating; she could feel her palms stick to the damp cloth of her slacks with each step. The night was cool enough that her fingers felt cold in the breeze. She still felt too hot inside, as though she might throw up. If she walked faster it might go away, but she kept her steps even because that was what he wanted.
They reached the end of the street without another word. She still couldn’t decide whether she should try to match his footsteps exactly. His shoes made a steady click on the hard asphalt pavement, not too loud, but definitely audible. Her boots were quiet in comparison; they didn’t sound like footsteps at all, just a soft rasping and creak of leather.
Her lungs ached slightly from breathing too fast and she was afraid that she might need to pause for breath if she tried to say anything. She wanted to speak more loudly, or faster, to make him talk about what had happened, but he wasn’t ready yet; she could tell by his silent footsteps and slow breathing.
