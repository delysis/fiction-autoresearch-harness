 The muscles in Esther's shoulders tensed and released in a single movement. Her hand left his face, moved toward his shirt, and stopped there.

She spoke the words slowly, as if she might change her mind: "Let me see."

Simon pulled the collar away from his chest. Beneath his shirt, two days of saltwater had turned a fresh bruise purple and swollen.

Esther's fingers moved over the skin before he felt the touch. Her touch mapped each moment—the initial impact, the spread of bruising, the shallow gashes left by whatever Simon hadn't caught in time.

"I thought you carried the message alone." Her voice was steady. Too steady. "And this?"

Simon traced his thumb over the jagged scar on her right wrist. It caught light and shadow in ways it hadn't when she'd shown him after rescue. She flinched at his touch, then stopped herself.

"The water," she said, her voice roughening. "And the line they used to drag me back. They kept changing direction."

Her skin pulled tight over the bone. He could see muscle fibers standing out as she moved the joint, testing for damage.

Esther straightened, taking his chin in her hands and tipping his face upward. Her eyes were bright. Furious. Determined.

"We had an agreement," she said. "No secrets."

Simon swallowed hard. "I know."

She looked down at her empty right hand, then at the bruise on his chest. The hand moved to the wall, fingers gripping the smooth surface until the knuckles stood out.

He took hold of her wrist gently but firmly. Her breathing hitched as she felt the pulse beneath his fingers.

"We need to talk," he said softly. "Now."

She pulled her hand free, but didn't step away. He saw the hesitation in her eyes before she turned to the table and lifted the drive with careful fingers. The cracked seal caught the light like a broken mirror.

She spoke without looking at him: "How much have you seen?"

Simon stepped closer. Too close. Esther took a deep breath but didn't move back. The drive remained steady in her hands. He knew how she gripped it—a small comfort in the midst of conflict.

He looked her straight in the eye and spoke the words that could still save them, if she would listen.

"Esther," he said. "The signal strength from Marta and Maria's tags was dropping. The current had already taken them past the nearest contact point. I made a decision. I stayed back. I couldn't—I wouldn't—leave them behind again."

She didn't drop his gaze, but he saw the pulse in her throat quicken.

Esther spoke so softly he barely heard her. "What was the alternative?"

"Direct communication."

Her eyes narrowed. "With who?"

"Me."

The silence deepened. Esther's right hand fell away from the wall, moved to her chest, and traced the pattern of a heartbeat she wasn't trying to hide. Her other hand gripped the drive tighter, its weight anchoring her in the moment.

"That was your choice," she said.

"That was my mistake."

Esther's eyes locked onto his, bright and hard. The drive turned in her hand like a challenge. Simon knew how she held it—a warning to herself rather than to him.

"No one else makes those choices for us," she said. The air between them seemed to hold its breath. He saw her hesitation and something else—determination hardening in the lines of her mouth
