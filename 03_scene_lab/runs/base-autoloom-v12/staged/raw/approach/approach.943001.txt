

His scar tissue burned at the memory of the wrong name whispered on a different sea floor. He felt the pressure against his mouth before he saw it—Esther's teeth pressing down on her own inner lip.

"I made a mistake," he said.

Her fingers tightened. "You made a decision."

"A wrong one."

"Both."

Simon opened his mouth to speak and met the force of her anger without words. Esther's eyes stayed locked to his, the pressure of her thumb still on his lip. Only when she pulled it away did he understand her intention—one word had passed between them already.

He took a deep breath.

"I left their names inside," he said.

Esther drew in her breath sharply.

Simon braced himself against the wall, feeling the cold of the concrete through his shirt. A quiet, steady pressure rose from Esther's right hand to his chest, her fingers curling around his ribs. Her other hand went to the side of his face, thumb tracing the line where his jawbone met salt-hardened stubble.

The contact was specific rather than embracing: she was examining a wound she hadn't seen.

"Who?" she asked.

He spoke before the word could turn back to memory. "Marta. And Maria."

The air between them changed. Simon caught her next breath like an anchor.

"Their mother?"

He nodded.
