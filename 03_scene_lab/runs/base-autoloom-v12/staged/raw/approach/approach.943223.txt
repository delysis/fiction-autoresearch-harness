

Simon pulled back, reached for the tea cup. He drank. The cup shook in his hands.

"Esther—"

"Answer me first."

"I had to copy."

She dropped the remaining button and moved close, her palm against his sternum. "Because I hadn't said yes?"

"We needed time," he said, but didn't look away when she waited.

Esther exhaled through her nose. "I made that decision alone last night," she said, tracing a fresh scar under his shirt. "While you were unconscious."

He moved the tea cup away.

"You didn't wait," she said. "So you made another plan."

"I was afraid you would leave," Simon said, "without me."

She kept her hand against his chest. "Now we'll both stay up all night opening and closing boxes."

He ran a thumb along her jawline. "We will verify the data before dawn."

"Yes." She pressed her palm harder for a moment. "Then I'll tell you what I decided. And then—"

Esther stopped and waited. Simon understood. He bent to kiss her, soft first, then deeper when she opened to him.

"Esther," he murmured between kisses, "may I—"

"Yes." Her hands came up to unbutton his shirt, one at a time, deliberate.
