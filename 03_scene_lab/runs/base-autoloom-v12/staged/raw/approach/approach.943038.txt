

"You haven't seen him."

"Then tell me."

His fingers found her waist. "You ask too many questions."

Esther caught his wrist before he could pull her close. Her expression told Simon everything about his decision had been wrong.

"That isn't my question."

He dropped her hand and went to the window. Rain tapped against the glass like fingertips testing a surface.

"What did you give him?" she asked, her voice just above a whisper.

Simon turned back, fingers gripping the frame so tightly his knuckles blanched. Esther stood in the center of the room, shoulders squared, hand still resting on the cracked drive.

"Everything."

Silence hung heavy between them.

Esther stepped closer, her movement deliberate. She lifted his right hand, held it gently, and traced the faded scar across his palm.

"I remember the first time," she murmured. "When we were new at this."

His throat tightened. "You said don't touch it."

She moved his hand away from hers and placed it against her lower belly, where the old scar lay hidden beneath layers of clothing. She drew a line with her finger, from her wrist up her forearm, leaving a trail of goosebumps in her wake.

"You did anyway," she said. "Because that was what we needed to do together."
