

The air between them sharpened with old questions. "I didn't trust him to follow protocol."

Her hand fell. The drive's broken seal echoed louder than anyone speaking.

"He was better than me at data verification," Simon added. "I couldn't ask for more than he gave."

"And I can't ask that of you?"

Esther leaned against the wall. Her shadow lengthened across the table and crossed over his. Simon traced the line with his fingers. The drive had changed because he had touched it. He could not untie that knot.

"If anyone touches a drive that could destroy us," Esther said, "they make me choose between them and my life."

The drive sat on the table like evidence he could not refute. Simon felt the pressure of it against his chest, in the room's sudden stillness. The mission mattered too much for either of them to waste air on meaningless words.

He pressed his forehead between Esther's shoulder blades and breathed the scent of her shirt. The cloth was rough against his face. Her hair caught between his fingers.

"I know," he whispered. "And I know why it matters."

Esther stayed very still beneath his hand. Her spine curved upward slightly as if asking for permission to speak. Simon tightened his grip until she turned her head.

He caught the way her gaze changed when they stood so close. The line of her mouth sharpened as if cutting through old reasons.

"This feels impossible," she admitted into the space between them.
