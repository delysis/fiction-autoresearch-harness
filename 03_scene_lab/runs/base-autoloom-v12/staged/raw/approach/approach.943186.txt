

His breath quickened first, then his pulse. She counted both while watching the small beads of sweat form on his upper lip.

"One," she whispered.

"I had no choice."

"Two."

"The house was compromised."

"Three."

"It wasn't secure."

"Four." Esther traced the outline of his mouth with her finger. "The question I haven't asked yet is: Why is my husband afraid to stay alone in our house?"

"You weren't here."

"Five."

His pulse slowed again. Esther removed her hand, leaving wet marks on his skin where it had been.

"Let me be specific," she said. "I have been absent before. Why this time?"

"Because you were gone longer than usual." His hand closed over the drive. "You're never gone this long."

She touched his temple where a single strand of hair had gone white since their last meeting. "Is there another reason I haven't thought to ask?"

He turned toward her touch. "I missed you," he said. "More than usual."

Esther caught his jaw in one hand and kissed him briefly. She traced the old scar where a bullet had taken bone, her lips lingering as if testing its shape.

"Is it possible to have too many reasons?" he asked against her mouth.
