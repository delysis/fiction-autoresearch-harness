

Simon looked away. His hair still had that sea smell. Esther resisted the urge to ask about salt and sun and where he slept under which stars. The drive's seal had broken before any of those questions would matter.

"The data," he said, "was copied."

She raised an eyebrow. "You mean they took the original and left a clone?"

He nodded. "I wanted to confirm."

"By breaking the seal? Without consulting me?" Esther circled around him, keeping his gaze. "What if they had made two copies?"

"Then we still have one."

Esther let out a slow breath. "You said the drive was undamaged when you recovered it."

"It was. Physically intact."

"What did you find on your copy?"

"No evidence of cloning software. Just the original files." Simon touched the damaged seal again. "But I wanted to be sure."

Esther sat on the edge of the bed and crossed her arms. "How much time did we lose verifying that your method was safe?"

"Two days."

She closed her eyes for a moment. When she opened them, she traced the outline of his collarbone with her fingertip. "You spent forty-eight hours alone making this decision."

"I know."

"Do you?"

Simon's hands clenched at his sides. His breath changed. Esther watched as he opened himself to the consequences of his choice.
