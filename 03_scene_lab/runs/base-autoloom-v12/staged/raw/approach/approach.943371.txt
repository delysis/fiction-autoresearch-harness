He looked down at their crossed hands. "I opened it myself."

The admission made the air tighten between them. Esther released his wrist and folded her arms across her chest, her face showing neither approval nor refusal.

Simon moved toward her but stopped when his left hand touched the empty tea cup. It had been cold long before his arrival. The tea stained the floor around it with a small ring, as if the drink itself could not contain itself.

The drive waited in silence, its open port exposing data that might still prove valuable. But Simon knew they were both more concerned about what Esther's father would say about this action—if he ever found out at all.

"I understand you better than you think," Esther said, finally. Her voice held neither anger nor forgiveness, just the weight of a woman who had spent the last months trying to preserve something when the rest of their world had turned to ash. "Do you want to know what I think?"

He nodded.

"I think your mother told you to open it."

Simon closed his eyes at this. He didn't like being read so easily. But she was right.

"What did you find inside?" Esther asked, her tone revealing nothing of her feelings.

"Nothing worth the risk," he admitted.

"And now?"
