

Simon traced the pattern of her scar tissue with his thumb.

"They got me in the arm first," he said, "right above the elbow. I thought it was the weapon but it was just the shockwave. Two more of them came and I wasn't fast enough."

He turned his forearm toward the window light and showed her the bruise where the shockwave hit.

"Simon, I've already asked you twice to send your code."

"I did that," he said. "On the drive."

She leaned closer to read the handwriting between his eyes.

"Did you need a third person to operate it?"

"No," he said. "They had more than one device, but one would have been enough. If I had been faster."

Esther stepped back and removed her shirt. Her scar tissue glittered under the lamp. Simon's breath caught in his chest.

"Simon," she said, "you opened my drive and changed my plan. Again."

"Yes."

She moved to the bed and sat, knees spread slightly apart, one hand on each thigh. The pattern of scar tissue ran like a second skin beneath her fingertips.

"May I touch your scar?" he asked.

"Only if you show me yours first."

He walked to the window, raised his forearm, and waited until her gaze met his before turning his body. The bruise ran from just above the elbow to past the wrist. The color was almost black where it crossed the bone.
