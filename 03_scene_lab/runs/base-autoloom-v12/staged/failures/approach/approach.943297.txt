

Simon touched the fingertip.

"Not another unilateral decision," Esther said. "Not again."

"The seal wasn't damaged when I got there," he said.

She dropped her hand, but only to cup her wrist. Her bracelet caught the light in hard blue flashes.

"And what else was not damaged when you got there?" she asked.

"I was following protocol. If I hadn't gone in—”

"I would have found another route.”

"You never know what might get destroyed in the process." He paused, collecting his next words. "You could have gotten hurt."

"That's not an argument." Her voice stayed flat. "Because we both know you didn't go in alone."

The tension between them sharpened when Esther looked down at her left thigh. The scar showed a precise cut that hadn't healed straight. The sea water had ruined the sutures, making the final healing follow the path of least resistance.

Simon took a breath. "You were already there,” he said. “I don't know why."

"You had a choice.” Her voice was steadier. She looked down at his arm. "Tell me about this one."
