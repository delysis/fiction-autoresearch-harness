
He had fine features and gray-green eyes. She blinked at his slow smile, unsure what it meant.
“Welcome,” he said. “You’re here for the new program. I’m Adrian.”
“You’re my instructor?” She’d only known to bring the barest essentials with her, not to expect a warm reception from anyone who would be involved in training her. She hadn’t had any other choice of instructor.
“No,” he said, laughing at her expression. “I’m here to learn. I just get to move in earlier because—that is—I’ve been living here for some time. I work here, too, as you probably guessed, and I have a place here.”
“I see.”
“Are you tired after your flight?”
“Very.” Her voice sounded dull in her own ears. “Thank you.” She could hardly speak to the man sprawled out before her, this stranger who had no reason to be polite and had nothing to offer except his willingness to stay in place.
“What’s your name?”
“Mara. Mara Bresson.”
“I like it.” He nodded and looked past her.
