
A long face and a thick straight nose, with a hint of a moustache around the mouth. And bright eyes that didn’t seem to have decided how to look at her.
“Hi,” he said.
His voice was pleasant.
She crossed the room and sat down in a chair. The back of it faced the window.
“Hello,” she replied. “My name’s Mara.”
He nodded slightly but didn’t say anything else, or get up. She wasn’t sure what to expect from him.
The couch was quite far away. She could see only the surface details of his body; the way his shirt stretched across his shoulders, the curve of an elbow against the arm of the couch. The rest of him was hidden by the upholstery.
He hadn’t introduced himself yet, but she was sure it was going to happen soon. He wasn’t going to leave without knowing her name and that would mean knowing his own name too. And then they’d go on from there, talking and looking at each other until they both understood who they were here.
Maybe he knew things about her already, he had certainly seemed alert when she came into the room. But now that he knew that her name was Mara, she could be anyone. He would have to decide who she was again.
And if he thought anything of her arrival he kept it to himself.
Mara tried to hide her discomfort by looking around. There were two sets of windows, one in each end wall; the one nearest her faced north, toward the compound’s back gate where she had been dropped off after checking in with security. The other window faced south toward the mountains behind them, which were blue and distant through the haze. She couldn’t make out any detail in that direction but she was sure there were more buildings over there somewhere.
The walls were white, the couch black leather, and the carpet navy blue. Nothing else of interest. No doors but the entrance that had already shut behind her.
It wasn’t a small room, and yet with only one person sitting in it everything seemed too big for them both. Mara felt as if she was being watched from every surface, and when she moved it was as though they were all moving too. She didn’t know what to do next, whether to move closer or farther away from him.
She had no idea what he was thinking.
He continued to sit without speaking; he might even have been looking at her when he wasn’t looking anywhere in particular. But still he didn’t get up and she had the feeling that if he did, then everything would change.
She shifted her position slightly. The room changed as well: a black chair appeared where a wall should be; the window shifted sideways; the couch moved forward a yard or so.
