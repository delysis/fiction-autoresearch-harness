
Adrian stared down at his hand, expressionless; Mara waited, aware of his breath and of Jonah watching them both. After another moment, he nodded and lifted his arm away. “That’s fine,” he said.

Mara had placed the white stone in his hand; it still held her place in the palm where it had lain in hers. Adrian lifted it, turning it over in his hand, while Mara stepped aside to retrieve the key and the token from the table, laying them faceup beside each other.
“I’ll read on this one,” he said to Jonah, setting down the stone between the key and the token; he was referring to the third empty space that was now left unoccupied.
And then, for the first time since they’d come into the room, there was no sound at all.

Mara stood near the table where Adrian had put the objects, watching his face as he stared down at her chosen stone with the rest of it in focus, a blank look in his eyes, his expression frozen and unreadable. Jonah was standing beside Adrian, close enough that their arms were brushing against each other’s; Mara couldn’t see his expression, and didn’t know whether he was reading or not, but there wasn’t anything else to do here right now, so she moved away.
She turned back toward the door. If someone was on their way into this room to be read, maybe she could get some water from the hallway while that was happening. She stepped through the doorway and onto the tiled floor outside; when she’d first walked in here, there’d been a wall of silence behind her, and now it was the other way around.
