
He was nodding. “Yes, all right.” And he turned to Jonah again, still holding her hand against his palm, and said, “Give me the cup now, would you?”

Jonah held the cup out toward him. Mara let her hand move away from his palm, to watch.
She saw that the white stone had disappeared and the wooden token was back on the table.
“Cheers!” Adrian turned to look at her. “That is the one I expect, too.”

And then Livia came in.
Mara sensed her arrival by an almost-imperceptible shift of air, followed a second later by sound; a soft heel on hardwood, just a single step, and then silence again. And then Mara turned around from where she was standing in the middle of the room and saw her there: a tiny figure in black, framed against the background of bright-lit hall beyond the doorway. She held in one hand a bottle of something, and in the other another one of the plastic cups. She did not seem to notice the two men yet; all attention was focused on Mara, in a way that felt familiar, as if she had seen her before.
“I think it’s time,” Livia said softly, not taking her eyes from Mara for a second, and set down the plastic cup of wine. “Would you like me to take them out for a minute? We don’t have to do this right now.”
“It is time,” agreed Adrian, and held open his left hand toward Mara again—not quite offering her anything this time.
