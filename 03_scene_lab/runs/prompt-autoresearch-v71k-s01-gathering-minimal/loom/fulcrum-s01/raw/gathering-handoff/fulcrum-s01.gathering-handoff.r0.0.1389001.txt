
He let go and lifted his wineglass in a silent gesture of inclusion; she did likewise with hers. They nodded at each other as though in agreement, turned, and walked back together to the couch where he’d been sitting.
“You can sit in the middle,” she said. “I’ll keep you company from my armchair here.”
He nodded again, looking mildly amused. She turned away to find a place to set her glass, and when she looked back he was already sitting, one leg propped against the table, fingers running through his hair to push it off his face.
She sat down and looked away toward the window.

He said nothing for a long time.

It occurred to her to look over at him. He was staring out into the night, still very still, his hair falling forward again to shade his face. She studied him, aware that she had no idea how to interpret this posture—it might mean he didn’t want to talk, or it might mean nothing at all. He did not meet her eyes, and there was no way for her to know whether he wanted her to wait patiently or break the silence.

She looked down.
