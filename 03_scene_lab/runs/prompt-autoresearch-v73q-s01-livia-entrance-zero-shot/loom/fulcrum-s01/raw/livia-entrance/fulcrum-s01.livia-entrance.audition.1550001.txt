Adrian grinned and looked down. Then he turned up Mara’s right hand to reveal his left palm, and read the choice.
“A key?” he said, sounding surprised, as though he knew already which one she was going to choose. But he shrugged, dropped it in his pocket, and held out the other two: stone and token, both still in plain view.

“It’s a simple test, really,” Adrian said, smiling at her. He reached into his jacket pocket with his free hand, produced something, and put it on the table, then stepped back from the small pile and held up his right hand to invite Livia to move closer.
And when she did, the contents of the pile—now three objects and a black cloth bag—came into fuller view: the bag first, which must have held his tools, the stone next, and the key after that.
The key was brass or copper, with no teeth but a wide bow; it might once have been part of a toy, but was now worn and unidentifiable. The white stone felt like limestone to Mara, weathered smooth, flat on top.

Livia examined them in turn, holding each one up in the light to look closely at the surface, moving them in her hand, then setting them down and stepping back, hands out of sight again. Mara saw how her shoulders moved in a way that looked like sighing or listening to the objects.
She turned back to the gathering, met Adrian’s eyes once more, and smiled. “The key,” she said.
