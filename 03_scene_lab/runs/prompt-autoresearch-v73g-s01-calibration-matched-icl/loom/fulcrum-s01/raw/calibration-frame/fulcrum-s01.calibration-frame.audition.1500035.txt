 It was a useful observation; and then it was over. But Mara had nothing to say about it, so she just kept watching, waiting, not moving. She didn’t even know whose labor was assumed by whom.
Then there were more people in the hall behind them—another woman’s voice and a male voice. A blonde woman entered with the same dark-framed glasses that Jonah had been wearing. The man had shorter black hair, but both of them nodded at Jonah as they came in; they did not seem to notice Mara at all.

They were not speaking aloud, but the woman made a gesture toward the table by the door—and in response, Jonah put his right hand on Mara’s shoulder and walked her over to the table. He introduced Mara to Adrian and Rue with an obvious level of familiarity that he had not extended to Mara yet: “This is my friend Mara. I met her today while she was doing interviews.”
And then, like a conductor leading the group into a new section of music, Jonah raised both arms in front of his chest as if holding someone back from them and spoke to Adrian:
“She’s a really good guesser,” Jonah said, turning to address Mara again with his face while keeping the gesture of his arms still pointed away. “Do you remember the last time she surprised us?”

Mara smiled awkwardly, looking from Jonah to Adrian, and then saw her opening to take charge. She stepped forward, still standing behind Jonah as he blocked the other two people out with his arms.

“I don’t know how I am at that,” Mara said carefully, looking back at Adrian again. “I don’t even know what it means.” She looked down at their shoes, and then looked up slowly, meeting each person’s gaze in turn from left to right—Rue, Jonah, Adrian.

“Well, we can start by trying a demonstration,” Adrian said, walking toward Mara with his hand extended for a handshake. Mara took it. He was solid, firm; when Mara pulled her hand back, he let it go, and took her arm instead. He moved her easily over to the table on the opposite side of the door, as Jonah stepped out of the way.
