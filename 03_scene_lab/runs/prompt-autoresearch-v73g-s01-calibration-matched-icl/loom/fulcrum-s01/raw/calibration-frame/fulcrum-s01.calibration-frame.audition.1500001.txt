
“It is a glass of wine,” he said, looking at Mara. “An ordinary plastic cup, such as is now being handed out by that woman, carrying wine in it. If you see anything different, you may certainly tell us.” His gaze still on hers, he placed his right hand flat against the table beside them and said to Jonah, “Hold this, will you?”
Jonah picked up the cup. “Do I want the contents first?”
Adrian waved that aside. “We won’t break the cup, don’t worry.” He held up his left hand, palm outward, toward Mara, and placed three objects in her open palm: a brass key, a white stone, and a wooden token.
Mara looked down at his hand, then up into his eyes, waiting to see what was wanted next.
“Choose one of those three,” he said. “Silently, please. Which one will you keep?” He smiled, leaned closer, and said, “You don’t have to answer right now, mind; but if you don’t want to play at all, we should stop.”

Her eyes locked with his as she said, “I think I would rather play than not,” and let her eyes fall to her palm, where she had three objects laid out. She closed her left hand around two of them and opened it again with only the brass key remaining in her palm. She turned her hand face down, keeping the brass key against her palm and the other side of her hand facing the group as she looked back up into Adrian’s eyes and nodded.
“Thanks for playing,” Adrian said, and took the brass key from Mara’s hand and set it back on the table next to the cup.
“Well, that’s nice and subtle,” Jonah commented.
Mara thought about the game they’d just played, and asked, “What is your goal? Is there a point to this?”
“To figure out your level of skill,” Adrian explained. “One of our students—another one of the staff—” he nodded back down the hallway, indicating one of the rooms they’d just left, “—will try to tell which object you picked, only from watching you. We’re interested in seeing how hard a problem they will have with your choices.”
