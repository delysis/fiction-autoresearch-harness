There was something in the way the wind held, something in the dark way that it lifted the leaves and bent them back into the light before letting them go.
    Jonah touched Mara's elbow and guided her across the road, into a park that was dark but for two dim street lamps at each end. He had never touched her in such a way.
    The air felt like an exhale, or a clearing. They walked over the grass without talking.
    Her back ached with the tension she had learned to carry, the tension she had carried for so long. She looked up at Jonah's face, trying to read something there.
    "I would say you were tense," he said. "But that might be stating the obvious."
    He said it very lightly, but it made her more aware of herself, the way she moved, the weight of air in the silence between them.
    Jonah was looking at her, too, as if he could see more than her. She felt seen without being penetrated. It made her uneasy.
