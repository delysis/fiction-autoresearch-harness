 Then he raised his free arm slightly. A hand held a cigarette, one finger flicked the smoke in the air as he turned, then the hand dropped to rest on his thigh while he looked at her.
A quick glance showed a large paperback open on the table, words and pictures in squares and speech bubbles across the page, the comics of Mara’s youth, though she didn’t recognize the particular titles. He went back to his reading before she could get near enough to identify them.

“I’m Mara,” she said, pausing with her hand on the doorknob as he kept his eyes down on the book.
He half turned and looked at her with cool blue eyes, still reading. “Welcome.”
No other greeting, but he sat up, pulling his feet to one side and stretching the other arm out along the back of the couch. He gestured to indicate a spot beside him. “I’m Jonah.”

She went over and sat down on the sofa, turning to face him. She waited for another word of greeting or introduction, but he didn’t say anything else. He kept glancing up and looking at her in between moments of reading, but there was no visible response except for slight changes in expression, and he didn’t try to interact any further.
This wasn’t what Mara had expected, based on the brief and informal introductions that had taken place during orientation. There were three people living here now, the two men she had met in orientation and the woman who had arrived after dinner. Mara couldn’t quite remember names, but the two men were very friendly, and the woman seemed shy yet cheerful.
Mara’s first impression had been that everyone in the house got along well; they talked with each other and did things together, and seemed at ease with one another in a way that didn’t leave much room for newcomers.
When she had come into the dining room for dinner, Livia had welcomed her again and sat her next to a woman who hadn’t said a word to Mara since their first introduction that morning, even when Mara had tried to make small talk about orientation. The others were very friendly and welcoming, but this woman sat staring at Mara like she was an object instead of a person.
Now the others were out somewhere, and she was alone with Jonah.

He took his time with the book and let her wait, turning the page slowly when she expected him to put it down. The pictures and words flowed over her in a wash of color and movement that didn’t make sense to her. When he turned another page, she watched him.
The gesture seemed practiced, automatic, yet the speed of his hand caught her attention. It slid smoothly across the page as though the skin was made for such movements and had no friction; it slowed down and sped up, a delicate balance between control and carelessness.
Jonah reached out and took up a second book from a stack beside the first, turning a page before he even looked at it to see what it was. His hand moved with a confidence that caught her attention in a way she didn’t quite understand.

“Are you new here?” He finally looked up, meeting her eyes for the first time since she had come in.
