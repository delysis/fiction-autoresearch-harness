 She let out a small sound.
“I’m sorry to disturb you,” she said. “I didn’t mean to intrude.” She was about to take a step back when she noticed the book on the table: The Complete Works of William Blake. Not exactly a privacy warning sign.
She paused, trying to judge what he might want or need. Was he reading or sleeping? Waiting for someone else, maybe? Probably reading. He wasn’t wearing his glasses but his eyes were open. “I thought I could do a little writing in the study,” she added tentatively, “but the door’s locked. I was going to leave a note that says I’m here, but I don’t know what to say.”
“I thought it was locked because Adrian isn’t supposed to be in there,” the young man said absently. He put his hand behind his head, tilted back farther, and closed his eyes. She recognized him as Jonah, one of the other newcomers. His profile looked younger than when he was dressed up for the group meeting. “I mean, I guess it’s also to keep the rest of us out, in case we want to do something in there without Adrian’s permission.”
Mara’s mind ran back through her training and through what Adrian had said earlier. The idea that Adrian would let the other residents have free rein in their rooms had never even crossed her mind. Jonah was right, it didn’t make any sense; of course he wouldn’t do that. She felt a little self-conscious, realizing she’d assumed the worst on Adrian’s part. “Right, of course.”
“Do you want me to show you?”
Mara turned to the study door again. “Is it possible to open?” she said, mostly to herself. She put a hand on the handle. She moved away from it, unsure if that was really what she wanted. “I don’t know,” she answered. She looked at Jonah again. “Can you—could I—”
“Take a look?” His eyes were still closed and his head was still tilted back, but he moved a little and patted the cushion of the couch.
She took one last look out the hallway window at the green lawn, then let herself in. He reached for something on the coffee table and held it out to her.
“Here,” he said. “Heh, I wish he’d put something like this in the welcome packet.”
Mara took the yellow sheet of paper from him. It read:

WELCOME TO FULCRUM. THE STUDY IS CLOSED TO NON-RESIDENTS. PLEASE LOCK ALL DOORS WHEN LEAVING ANY ROOM, AND CHECK THE LOCKS BEFORE YOU ENTER.

She stared at it for a few seconds, taking in the details one by one. Then she looked up at Jonah again. “Thank you,” she said quietly.
“You’re welcome.” He was looking back at her, his expression unreadable. His gaze traveled slowly downward to the yellow sheet of paper in her hands; he paused there for a moment and then looked back up at her face.
Mara felt a pang of regret. “I’m sorry I assumed Adrian was being overprotective,” she said, still holding the note. “I shouldn’t have assumed that.”
