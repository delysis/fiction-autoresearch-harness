
She felt a sudden, strong sensation of being looked at.
“Hello.”
The greeting was low and quiet. It didn’t make her flinch; it wasn’t rude, just unexpected.
“Hi.”
The hand holding the glass had been resting on his knee. It curled casually around the rim and raised the glass to his mouth.
His hair fell into his eyes when he drank. He set the glass on the table, leaned forward and pulled the hair from his forehead, pushing it back into place with his fingers.
She saw the tattoo on the back of his hand then, a small triangle of green inside an even smaller black triangle.
“Do you live here, too?” she asked.
“No.” He shifted his position on the couch so that his feet lay flat against the opposite arm rest. “I’m going to Livia’s class tonight.”
He seemed perfectly at ease in his own space, but didn’t seem to feel obliged to make himself a comfortable space inside her head. She was momentarily uncomfortable with the imbalance.
“Oh? She doesn’t usually—” She stopped herself.
“Usually what?”
She shrugged and shifted the overnight bag to her other shoulder. “It’s unusual for people who live elsewhere to come just for classes.”
A look passed across his face, then vanished. He laughed suddenly, turning the laughter into a cough by covering his mouth. “Unusual, eh?”
“Unusual,” she said, nodding. She shifted again, searching for some position that felt less exposed than any other. “Why don’t you live here? The group rate makes it easier.”
He shrugged. She didn’t know why she was talking to him.
She was going to say good-bye.
But there was no reason to walk out of the room and leave him behind, was there? It didn’t make any sense for either of them to avoid being here. He could have left.
“I do have other interests,” he said, suddenly leaning forward. “It’s not like this place is the only one in the world.” He laughed again. “It would be pretty sad if it was.”
His face held no trace of humor; but his voice had a certain lightness she found disturbing.
He picked up his glass and drank some more of the cold liquid, then set it down on the table beside him and straightened his shoulders. His chest rose slightly as he took another deep breath.
“What are you going to do tonight?” he asked suddenly. “Are you taking Livia’s class?”
She shook her head and put her bag on the floor by the couch where he sat, letting go with a sigh of relief. “It’s my first night here,” she said. She hadn’t felt ready to admit this earlier, but now that it was out, it didn’t seem as awkward. “I don’t know what I’ll be doing yet.” She glanced toward the door to see if anyone had come in behind her, then turned back toward him.
He had leaned back again and spread his arms out across the top of the couch cushion. He sat there gazing at her, waiting for her response, looking curious and amused.
