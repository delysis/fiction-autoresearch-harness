

Mara let go a quiet exhale of her own and put her right hand in his left. His fingers closed round hers at the same time she gripped his, so softly the touch felt like a double shadow on her palms.

“Want to walk tonight?” The voice had that deep, easy tone when he spoke quietly.

“Yes.”

He walked with the same weight-and-pause rhythm that matched hers at first without thinking. She took the slow pace for granted but noticed the balance anyway. His steps never jostled hers.

Mara turned her head and breathed the night air. She tried to ignore the smell of sweat still clinging to her hair, the stiffness in her shoulders. She’d hated that session. The whole thing had been a terrible idea.

“You don’t like it either?” He walked at her elbow, his left hand still linked with hers, right hand swinging with the step.

“No.” Mara wanted to talk about the session; she wanted to stop thinking about it. “I never do when they’re in a pack like that. I want to help one, but—” She stopped, remembering how much her hand had slipped.

Jonah squeezed her fingers without shifting their grip and kept walking.

They walked across the street under the big fir tree and down the hill away from the parking lot. Mara let it get dark enough she couldn’t see the cars. They turned into the alley between the bank and the warehouse and walked out of sight of the road.

“We’re alone,” he said quietly.

Mara was too far from the cars to worry about their headlights. She felt the last tension leave her shoulders as they entered the empty alley. Jonah let go her left hand so they could swing into a new stride. She waited until the streetlights were behind them and turned to look at his eyes.

His eyes met hers before she found them. His body moved with her turn, his eyes shifted with her focus, his breath relaxed in a quiet sigh when they met. Mara let their gaze settle and waited as his eyes softened under hers and his lids widened so she could see his pupils widen too.

“Is this better?” he asked softly.

Mara let her mouth curve. “Yes.” She felt her muscles loosen with the grin. “Thanks.”

He smiled at her, his body loose and relaxed. The alley’s night smell of earth and wet leaves surrounded them as they walked together.

“I don’t know what I’d do without you there,” she said softly, turning her head to meet his eyes. “You save me so much time.”

She hadn’t meant that to be flirtation, but he grinned when he heard it. “I’ve been thinking about how I’d like to kiss you,” he said, his eyes bright in the dark.

Mara felt heat creep up her chest. “You really want to?”

He held out his left hand and she felt him hold her eyes with his. “Is it okay?” His voice was low and soft.

She felt a smile ease its way across her lips. “I’ll say ‘stop’ if I don’t.”

He took her right hand and pulled it to his side as he drew her in with the other. His hands were warm and dry, his breath soft on her face as he moved close.

Mara felt his breath and his touch before she felt his lips. He waited for her eyelids to fall before his did and when their eyes were shut Mara’s forehead eased into his. She pressed forward and they met without a rush and it was a clean, good kiss.
