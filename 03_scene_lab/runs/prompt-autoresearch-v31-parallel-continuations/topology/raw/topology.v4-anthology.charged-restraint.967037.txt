She shook her head. They walked in easy silence.

His hand on hers made it easy to walk without watching her feet. His posture let her read his breath without touching. The steady flow between them gave her confidence but never interrupted her thoughts. It felt as natural as air or walking, like the world was working as it should.

This is good. This is right.

Jonah took his hand back while Mara was looking at a tree. It smelled dry in the darkness. Her fingers moved for the contact that had gone away. Jonah took her left hand again.

“What do you think?” he said. He wasn’t sure she could hear him, but Mara knew what he meant. She’d heard it in his walk, the way his feet met the sidewalk and moved apart, as if he’d just lifted a weight and put it down. It wasn’t as if they walked separately; they were walking together. But not always the same pace. They matched and unmatched each other, like breath.

She let him speak. The thought was familiar, like a sound she remembered making. She looked straight ahead, so he might think she’d stopped listening.

“I think what we saw tonight was what happens when people focus on how they want to look and forget who they really are.” Her voice didn’t change, as if it had nothing to do with her body or her thoughts. “It’s like there’s a point where you lose the real person and can’t find your way back.”

His hand shifted slightly in hers. He was waiting, like he might have something to say if she was finished.

Mara looked down, watching her breath in the darkness. Her face relaxed and her shoulders loosened. The smell of wood and wet moss shifted with the wind. She watched his hand move toward her wrist as if it might not go there. The fingers didn’t squeeze but left a mark on her skin.

“What was that like?” he said, and she didn’t have to see his face to know what he meant. “When you lost yourself.”

His voice never changed pace. It kept the same distance and pitch as if it had nothing to do with the question. It was the kind of question only someone else could ask, something she couldn’t notice about herself. It was the difference between “What would it be like?” and “What happened?” Something that was always happening instead of happening once.

Jonah looked at her face, as if he wanted her to tell him how it felt. As if it mattered that she could feel it without saying.

“It’s like there’s a wall,” she said, and looked away as if he wouldn’t hear. “Sometimes I try to remember who I really am, but then there’s a wall. I just get confused. Or I feel stupid or angry. Like no one understands, or no one cares. Like everyone else is faking. Everyone but me.”

He waited. Her face changed without changing. As if she’d started something and couldn’t stop it. It was the difference between “I could do this” and “This is me.” Something that wasn’t separate. Not Mara speaking to Jonah. Like a person talking and moving at the same time. A human being doing what he did, thinking what he felt, without knowing how it happened.
