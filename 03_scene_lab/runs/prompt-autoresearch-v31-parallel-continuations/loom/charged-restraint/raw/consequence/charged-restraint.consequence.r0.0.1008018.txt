 A single light showed the road and the open space around her, but behind her only the dark trees filled the sky. The sound of traffic still told her she was far from any open field, yet the instant brought a sudden fear that made her heart race.

“I don’t want to be alone.” The words came out as an almost-scream in the night air. The noise frightened her, and Mara let out an even louder scream. A car went by and she felt a flash of cold while she stood in the road. She forced herself to go back to the sidewalk.

Mara stopped after four steps and tried to find a steady thought. Her breath came fast and she made herself take three deep breaths through her nose. When she heard a second car pass she opened her eyes to a view of the empty street.

“I’m here, I’m safe,” Mara told herself aloud. “I’m not alone, and I’ll be there again.” The words were steady and her breath slowed to normal.

Mara went down the street in the direction Jonah’s truck had gone, taking one slow step at a time. She made herself look straight ahead, then stopped again when she saw the next corner.

“What if he’s just using me?” She spoke aloud to hear what she couldn’t say inside herself. “What if I only exist for him as something he’ll throw away when he’s done?”

The fear came back and Mara said it aloud so she could hear it clearly. “What if he’s another man who will just use my body and then leave me? What if I only mean what he can have for himself tonight?”

A second car turned the corner and Mara stopped while it passed, then spoke again. “That’s not who he is. He doesn’t treat me that way, and he never has. He won’t take anything I don’t freely give him.”

Mara tried to find a clear thought and repeated herself as if she were telling someone else what to do. “I’m fine. I’m safe, and he is not going to hurt me. I choose to stay alone with him tomorrow and we will be together willingly.”

Mara tried to remember the touch of their joined hands and the feel of a real kiss. When she could make her body still she let herself take three deep breaths. “I’m fine,” she said again. “And I’ll be there when he comes.”
