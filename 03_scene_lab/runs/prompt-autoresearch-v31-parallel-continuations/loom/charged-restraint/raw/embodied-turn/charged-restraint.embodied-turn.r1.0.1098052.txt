

He looked at her steadily and said nothing. She knew there was no reason he shouldn’t ask about her last night. Jonah looked away again to let her walk as long as she chose. She could tell him to stop whenever she wanted to.

“I wish we could just walk home together.”

Jonah said nothing when she turned back toward the apartment, only followed her with steady steps until Mara finally turned at another street. She knew he was not far behind as she paused to watch him turn and walk back the other way. When the truck started up again Mara listened for his steps and felt her own pace quicken.

She opened her door alone and waited to hear the sound of a lock. When the sound came she opened the inner door and leaned against the wall, waiting for footsteps. When none came she sat down on the couch and let go a long slow breath. There was no sound from the apartment above except distant television.

Mara went to her room alone and closed the door, then let herself take off one shoe at a time as she sat on the bed. Her hands shook when she reached for her earrings, but she let her fingers move slowly as she unbuttoned her blouse. Jonah’s voice echoed in her mind as she undid the clasp and let it fall to the floor.

Mara turned off the light and went back out. She sat down on the couch with a beer in hand, watching a news show until it turned into commercials. When Mara finally leaned back with a sigh her hands were still trembling.

She got up again and looked through the cabinet under the sink for her inhaler. She took a measured dose, then went back to the living room and sat on the couch with the television off. Her mind kept drifting back to Jonah’s face when she finally let the empty can drop on the floor.

Mara watched her feet move as she went to her bedroom door alone. When the last light was off she got into bed and tried to sleep, but kept watching the small light from the hall. When Mara finally fell asleep it was to the distant sound of the train.
