

“Are you all right?” His voice was low, devoid of judgment or pretense.

She exhaled, a shaky breath. “Not really.” But there was no edge in her tone—no defiance. Just truth.

He nodded, a slight curve to his lips. “Then let’s walk.”

They moved slowly, their steps echoing softly against the quiet street. Mara kept her hand in his, needing the sensation of skin against skin to remind herself of reality. Each step felt like an act of defiance, a refusal to let the day's events define her.

After some time, they turned toward the riverfront, the wind stirring the last tendrils of twilight. He stopped by a stone railing, his hand still holding hers. There was no coercion in the gesture—no implication. It was up to her.

She looked at him, reading the question in his eyes: Do you want this?

Her heart quickened at the thought of him crossing that invisible line. But instead, she pressed a light kiss to his lips, a spark of connection that caught them both off guard.

The kiss deepened, each breath mingling with the other. There was no rush, no expectation, just the slow unraveling of tension and fear. She could feel every muscle in him relax, his touch gentler yet more insistent. Her own body seemed to respond in kind, every nerve tingling with the possibility of what could come next.

But then he broke the kiss. His fingers lingered on her cheek for a moment longer before he stepped back, a hint of disappointment in his eyes—and something more. Regret, maybe. Relief.

“I didn’t mean to—” he started, his voice faltering as she raised a hand, stopping him.

“No,” she whispered, shaking her head. “It was fine. It felt right.” But her heart wasn’t convinced. There was an emptiness in her chest that the kiss hadn’t filled, something she couldn’t quite explain. She wanted more—she knew that now. But saying it out loud felt like admitting to a weakness she couldn’t afford.

He nodded, but there was no warmth in his expression. Just silence. And then he turned to walk back toward the city, leaving her alone under the darkening sky.

Mara stood there for a moment longer, feeling hollow and unsure. Had she made a mistake? She couldn’t tell. All she knew was that there was something inside her that wanted more than he could offer, something raw and unbridled that she hadn’t yet found the courage to face.

When she finally turned to follow him back, her mind was already racing with questions she wasn’t ready to answer.
