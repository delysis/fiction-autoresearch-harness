

Welcome

Mara smiled and went in, approaching the couch from the far end to avoid closing it off. “Hi. Mara.” She put down her tote bag and sat in the single chair opposite him.
He was gazing at her, not just at her eyes but her whole face. Mara felt something like a blush under his gaze, and a quick sense of being seen. There was a hint of a smile or some kind of lift, as if her presence or her movement had given him a little joy.
“Jonah.” His voice was soft, with the slight lilt of the south.

“You live here?” she asked, sitting forward.

He tilted his head again, and this time the gesture seemed to have some significance—was it a question? She watched his eyes as she said, “I’m the new postdoc.”

Still he only gazed at her.

Mara tried to relax into the chair. After a pause, she said, “You were just here yesterday, right? When I came for the interview.”

“Yes.”

“I saw your picture on the wall yesterday. You won some kind of award?”

He said something in a tone too low for her to make out, as if he’d heard himself too much or felt awkward about hearing.

“The award,” she repeated.

This time his mouth closed without speaking. There was something more complicated than shyness in his manner, and some hint of an exchange between them, although she wasn’t sure if the meaning was real or imaginary.

Mara looked away, giving herself a little more time. She watched his hands for a moment, how they twined around each other, one thumb rubbing the other thumb.

She turned back to his face and met his eyes again. “Well,” she said, “welcome.”

“I’ve been here awhile,” he said quietly. He sat up, shifting his legs and sitting with a little less slackness in the body.

Mara smiled. She wanted to smile, for some reason. “Oh, right,” she said. “Sorry—you were here when I came by. Well then, I’m new. Welcome.”

He laughed softly and shifted again, this time crossing one ankle over the other knee. The leg movement felt like a gesture of some kind, but it was gone in an instant, the significance lost if she’d even seen it right.

Mara wondered what to say. She scanned the room, her eyes settling for a moment on the clock, which had just struck half past six.

“So,” she said after a pause. “This is a strange place.”

He made another quiet noise of agreement, this time a sound that might have been assent. He seemed to be looking at her hand; she made a note of her thumb stroking her middle finger and shifted her weight in the chair.
