A single pale mark waited on the screen beside a comment in Livia’s handwriting.

? PRESSURE ?

The mark had appeared in the final two seconds of the session, well after the subject had withdrawn her hand. It sat alone near the end of a steady gray line. No corresponding trace appeared for breath, pulse, or room audio. Jonah’s sensor had recorded nothing. Mara’s own body had recorded nothing.

She checked the clock again. It changed as she watched.

Jonah’s right hand had moved away from the keyboard. He sat straighter. Livia looked up.

“There,” Mara said.

The mark vanished.

Jonah brought a file explorer to the top window. Mara leaned closer to his screen. The directory contained only one log—tonight’s—and its file size had decreased. The pressure trace showed a blank space where the mark had been. The annotation remained beneath it.

? PRESSURE ?

Jonah opened the audit log. Mara read the file last-modified time and said nothing.

The final two seconds had disappeared.

“We had a mark,” Livia said.

“We did.”

“It’s gone now.”

“Correct.”

“We need to make a note.”

Jonah pressed a key. A new line appeared on the trace. Mara watched him delete the line twice, copy the deleted line onto the annotation layer, and align the timestamp manually.

His right hand stopped on the keyboard.

The room held still. The air conditioner’s white noise had begun to feel deliberate. Mara counted three breaths and began a third attempt.

“It’s gone,” she said.

Livia looked at her. Her expression had not changed.

“We need to make a note,” Mara said. “We had a mark. It’s gone.”

“Yes.”

Mara looked at Jonah. His right hand had remained where it was. The log had not changed.

“It wasn’t the telemetry,” Livia said.

Jonah exhaled through his nose. “That would have been simpler.”

“If this is going to be simple, I prefer it to be obvious.”

The comment remained on the screen beneath a blank field. Jonah moved the mouse cursor over the line but did not open it.

? PRESSURE ?

Mara glanced toward the door. It remained closed. Someone—Miriam, she thought, or perhaps Adrian—had left without announcing they were leaving.

“How did the mark appear?” she asked Jonah.

His fingers did not lift from the keys. “The subject’s wrist depressed the sensor while she was removing her hand.”

“That isn’t how it appeared,” Mara said.

The room became still enough that the fire crackled behind Mara’s shoulder.

“Yes,” Livia said.

Jonah glanced at her, then at Mara. “The timing doesn’t match the cuff removal.”

“Yes.”

“It would have had to be before or after.”

“Correct.”

Jonah let out one breath. “Then you’re saying—”

“We’re saying,” Mara said.

Miriam’s observation log lay open on the side table. Mara traced the file path, found the end-of-session timestamp, and opened the matching record. The log showed the same mark on the telemetry: one pale line beneath the annotation. No corresponding change appeared on the audit trail. The file size had not decreased.

Jonah tapped his screen twice with his thumbnail. “Can we agree it wasn’t the cuff?”
