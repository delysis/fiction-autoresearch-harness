Livia noticed anyway. “Adrian had to speak to someone. Miriam was needed elsewhere. You know the system.”

Mara kept looking at the terminal clock. “You could tell Adrian to leave.”

Livia’s eyes moved to the monitor. “This is interesting.”

Mara’s stomach chose that moment to protest dinner’s absence. Livia’s head tilted. Mara made herself breathe in slowly and said, “It is.”

“It’s interesting to you. Not only interesting. Necessary. Because you have a hypothesis you cannot yet test, and your hypothesis concerns—” Livia’s eyes went to Jonah’s back. “The people you cannot yet test it on.”

She left the sentence open. Jonah typed another line and stopped. His wrist rested beside the keyboard, and Mara thought of the small arc his thumb had made there on the first night. She did not mention it.

She did ask Livia, “If that’s true, why wouldn’t Adrian let you have the data?”

Livia studied her, then the monitors. “Because it might destroy a good idea. You are an excellent idea.”

Mara looked down at her own hands. “And the hypothesis?”

“That remains yours.”

“It’s not enough.”

Livia’s gaze softened. “It might be. We will not know until we find out. But you’re hungry, and that would make it impossible to work. Let’s find something.”

She stood. Mara stayed where she was.

Livia’s foot kicked Mara’s chair once. “We eat, then work. That’s the order.”

Jonah looked over from the screen. The hunger had not left Mara, but she let it go.

“Yes.”

She was supposed to eat.

CHAPTER THREE

The small table behind the kitchen counter was empty when Mara reached it. She found a bowl and spoons without assistance. Livia had gone to check the oven. She left the door open, revealing the top of a loaf that might have become bread if anyone had let it bake.

Jonah came up beside her, wearing the same black shirt he had worn the night of the calibration game. He opened the refrigerator and took out a jar of peanut butter. The label said he had bought it that morning.

“What’s the allergy count?” Mara asked.

“No allergies, just preferences.” Jonah studied the butter as though he had not made the choice himself. “Someone will ask whether you like it.”

“They don’t have to.”

“They will. Fulcrum people have to ask about everything.”

“And when you want something, what do you do?”

His eyes went to the bowl in her hands. “Wait until you ask?”

His mouth tilted, but the question stayed. Mara’s arms felt heavier than they had an hour earlier. She did not ask.

Jonah took out the bananas and set them next to Mara’s bowl. The jar of butter went back in the fridge.

“I can put bread in the toaster,” he said.

“That seems like a better solution.”

“But I’ve already made the butter. And this is the healthy option.”

Mara let him assemble a bowl and arrange it beside hers. Jonah set the spoons between them. His hand moved past hers, fingertips brushing her skin. The contact did not feel accidental, and neither of them drew back.

Livia entered with the tea and set it in front of them. She left the kettle boiling. Jonah watched her leave before beginning to eat.
