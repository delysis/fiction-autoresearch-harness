Jonah pulled a water bottle from the refrigerator and held it without drinking. Livia noticed. She opened her own bottle and slid Mara’s cup along the table.

“It’s going to be amber,” Mara said.

“Do you object?”

“I’m allergic to nothing, but I have preferences.”

“Tea,” Jonah said. “She’s right about the color.”

Livia poured without looking at Mara. “Your liver will thank you.”

“My liver wants you to be more specific.”

Livia’s expression did not change. Mara took the cup without hesitation.

The tea was warm, slightly bitter, and tasted more expensive than coffee. She drank half of it, then sipped the remainder slowly enough that she did not have to decide whether to ask for more.

Livia watched her. “How much sleep?”

“Last night, two hours?”

“Today you had breakfast in your office.”

Mara let the joke remain unspoken.

“The caffeine has left your system,” Livia said. “Not the stress.”

“That would explain why I feel awake and angry.”

“I’ll take the credit.”

“I’ll take the debt.”

The room was quiet around them. The shared screen showed nine rows of labeled blocks, each block containing two or three subblocks with their own annotations. Mara’s eyes remained on the clock. Jonah’s attention was on the keyboard.

“Tomorrow,” Livia said, “you could take the raw stream home.”

“Yes,” Jonah said. “It’s possible.”

“I’m not sure I follow.”

Jonah looked up. “Adrian said no telemetry outside Fulcrum. Not that you can’t leave. We don’t lock the lab doors.”

“Unless someone’s using them as a fulcrum.”

Livia smiled.

“No one’s using the lab doors as a fulcrum.” Jonah made a small gesture with the bottle. “Miriam’s keycard is in the tray, though. If she’s working upstairs, she’s not going to notice for fifteen minutes. The summary can be done without her.”

Mara put the teacup down. “That’s extremely specific.”

“It’s how I’ve been keeping my notes.”

She looked at Livia.

Livia kept her hands flat on the table. “You can find the patterns better than I can.”

“Because you trust my eyes or my patience?”

“Both.” Livia’s gaze held steady. “Your eyes are skeptical and precise. Your patience is finite. We need someone who can keep finding anomalies until something becomes regular.”

Mara turned toward Jonah’s screen. The live traces continued to run. The shared interface had not changed. The amber tea had warmed her hands and not her chest.

She stood and walked to the refrigerator.

Jonah pulled the keycard out of the tray as she opened the door. His back was to her. The traces on his screen jumped once, then settled into a new pattern.

“When I go to the store,” Mara said, “I come back with more than one bag.”

“You’ve been coming in pairs.”

“So have you.”

He reached for the milk jug. “I need a water bottle to transport the stream. One bottle.”

“I’ll give you two.”

“Then you need to come with me.”

“I was already going.”

“Not with the stream.”
