The session is over. I let go the doorframe and stand up from my crouch. The whole lab is still and empty, the floorboards creaking and cool underfoot. I put my palms on the doorframe for balance.
“Hey, I’ve been looking for you,” says Jonah. “Did you forget the way to the women’s room?”
“No.” I turn to face him. He looks like one of those old-time photographs I saw once in someone’s attic; my sister’s former husband’s mother had a drawer full. A tall, dark-haired man in a suit and tie, his arms at his sides, his face almost completely blank. A very slight upward curve to his mouth, the corners turned up by about a quarter inch. The eyes are blank, too. I turn back to the door.
“Mara?” His voice is quiet and gentle, as if he were trying to soothe a baby. “Do you know you’re about to walk through the closed door?”
I turn back around. “Not trying to, actually.” I move away from the closed door to the open one.
“Oh,” he says. “Good. Want a ride?”
“No.”
