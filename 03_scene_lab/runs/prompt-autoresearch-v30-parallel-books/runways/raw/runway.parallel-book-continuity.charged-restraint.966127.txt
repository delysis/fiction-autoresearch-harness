Jonah turned a small circle, like a fox before sleep. One hand on the doorframe and one on the door’s iron knob. The heavy wooden door held against his weight and my weight. His fingertips let the knob’s cool cast iron become his skin. The outside air already held his breath, his skin’s heat. I knew what I knew, but I still didn’t understand why he made me do that.

“Just the way you do,” I told him.
“Do what, Mara?”
“When you know.”
Jonah exhaled into the night. I saw how the air took him.
“I don’t make you do anything.”
“You’re the one who always says we can’t choose anything outside ourselves. Do you really think you don’t make me do this?” I pressed the door inward harder. Jonah’s weight held the door shut.
I turned away, facing the dark side lawn.
Jonah stayed at the door, still and waiting.

I let my weight off the door and felt a different door swing open.
I heard him close the door behind him, step outside the building. He walked at my shoulder, so close I heard his breath.
“I think I’m going to walk home, if you don’t mind, Mara.”
