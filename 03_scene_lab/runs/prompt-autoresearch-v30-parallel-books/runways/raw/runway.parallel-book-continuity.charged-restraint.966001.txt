He didn't touch her, which was right. Instead, he offered his hand—not a clasp, just his fingers extended, warm and open. The air felt cold on her bare arm. She left her arm free, just brushed her own fingers against his before letting them drop away again.

“I'm coming with you,” he said, voice soft. “It's too late for you to go home alone. I'm not letting you go.” He wasn't touching her or demanding an explanation. His voice wasn't soft to be kind, either: that tone was soft to carry farther.

“Why are you doing this, Jonah?” She stopped walking, caught the way his fingers twitched into fists at the sudden absence of movement beside him. “Why not just go back inside and help the others?”

He met her eyes and she held them, willing him to explain.

He dropped his head, put his hands in his pockets and turned back toward the steps, not touching her but not moving away. His voice came quietly, still pitched to carry over the soft night noise.

“I told you, Mara, it's too late. I'm not letting you go.”
