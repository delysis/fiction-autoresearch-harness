I sat on the Fulcrum building's concrete steps and stared straight down at my hands in my lap.
One finger pressed a thumb in small slow circles, feeling each fingertip, each nail.
I waited to feel warm and human.
I waited to stop thinking.
I waited for anger.
Jonah stopped beside me, a step above me and a step apart.
I watched my own hands.
He offered his hand, open palm up, fingers a little curled.
My hand came to him slowly, fingers splayed like a starfish's legs.
I laid my hand over his. He turned it gently, palm down.
His hand stayed cupped around mine, soft and warm and real.
He put his hand over mine again.
I felt safe and not safe at the same time.
Jonah kept his head turned slightly down.
His eyes stayed in my peripheral vision.
He did not tell me to breathe, to stop thinking, or to slow my heart.
He let me ask.
His hand stayed.
I stayed.
I looked down again, past my own hands to the worn concrete step below.
“I hate it when they tell me what my face means,” I said. “I hate when they tell me how they felt or what I was thinking. I don't care!”
