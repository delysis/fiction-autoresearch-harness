Chapter One

Mara followed Jonah out of the lab with the ache of an overexposed negative. The air felt cool and sticky. Their car ride home could only make it worse.

Her eyes slid to his back. The white lab coat still buttoned tight over his chest, covering the gray linen shirt that she knew would have creases from sitting at a lab chair, the sleeves rolled back above the elbows, the ends of the sleeves frayed from frequent wear.

His shoulders were broad, his neck long, his head slightly ducked as he walked. He walked like a man with somewhere to go, not looking around or watching his steps. He was probably already thinking of how to salvage the study, how to make it useful for next quarter’s teaching sessions. She watched his steady gait, the way his lower lip seemed to catch when he was focused on something, his eyes steady, never wavering, as though he had always known exactly where he was walking, or thinking.

She could hear his footsteps, the steady beat, two steps for every breath he took. She could smell him, too, a faint hint of sandalwood that had come to her senses before she even noticed.

She followed him down the hallway, down the stairs, and out the door. He stopped on the porch, waiting for her, his hands in his pockets.
“You okay?” he asked
