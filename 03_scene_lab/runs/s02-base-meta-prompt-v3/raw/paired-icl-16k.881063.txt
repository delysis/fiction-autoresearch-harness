The data moved across the primary monitor like living things: breath and pulse traces, heat maps, pressure shifts, eye movements. Adrian sat at a console beside the main screen, Livia at another console beside his, Jonah at a third console beyond Livia. All three faces remained turned toward the data.

“One more sequence,” Adrian said. “And we’ll make dinner.”

“How long?” Mara asked.

She had kept her fatigue silent. The response surprised her.

Adrian met her eyes. “You’re not a participant. You can leave at any time.”

“The session is my data.”

His hand rose to cut her off. He stopped halfway and looked away. The air in the room did not change. His eyes returned to the monitor, and Mara saw that he was watching a different stream than hers.

She waited.

Adrian said, “We can schedule a separate access time.”

Livia looked over her shoulder. “She’s staying.”

Mara could have refused the invitation. That would have cost her access to the data without providing an exit. Livia would have named that cost, then asked for her silence in return.

“We need the overnight telemetry,” Mara said. “I can work at my own terminal.”

“Of course.”

Adrian had not checked whether her own terminal would be available. Livia poured amber tea from a ceramic carafe and slid the cup to Mara. The warmth reached Mara’s fingers before she noticed it. The room remained unchanged.

“Strain from the density,” she said. “It’s not the content.”

Livia smiled without embarrassment. “You want to be exceptional. Everyone else can look ordinary for us. You want to be interesting too.”

“I don’t trust people who aren’t interesting.”

Livia laughed. It was not entirely at her.

“How badly do you want to keep the channel?” she asked.

“Very.”

“Then take this.” Livia opened the data portal and assigned Mara private access to the night’s telemetry. She looked back at the monitor as if she had made no offer and received no answer.

Mara sat straighter. A flush moved across her shoulders and disappeared.

At the third console, Jonah had stopped typing. His hands rested at his sides. He looked at Mara. “Next sequence is ready,” he said. “Mara?”

She could have refused the invitation again.

She did not. She tapped the shared interface to accept access and folded her hands in her lap. Jonah’s shoulders loosened. Livia turned back to the monitor. The primary display remained filled with living data.

The room did not change.
