Adrian had left forty minutes before the promised end.

Livia looked up when Mara stood. “Still with us?”

“You were the one who said it would be a high-dimensional problem.” Mara tried to look calm, because her head ached and her mouth was too dry for speaking to feel neutral.

Livia glanced at the screen. The sensor trace showed Mara’s pulse too high and her breath too shallow. The room was dark except for the glow of the monitor and her tablet. “I was also the one who said it was optional.”

Mara was not.

“If we get a good read, I’ll let you choose the control.”

The control was already decided, which made Livia’s offer even less useful. Mara was not surprised when the next prompt appeared on the shared tablet. She was surprised when Jonah stopped typing.

The red light above the monitor blinked. Her breath did not change.

Livia watched her. “Something wrong?”

Mara took one slow breath. The sensor trace moved. She did not.

“The data’s too dense,” she said, as though it were a new and unexpected thing. “I need air.”

The monitor reflected a dark ceiling. Mara stood in the reflection with her hands flat on the desk.

Livia made a soft sound of understanding. She slid the ceramic carafe toward Mara with the bottom of her hand. Amber tea filled the cup to a rounded line.

“For the record, it’s not your job to be comfortable,” Livia said.

“The data’s dense,” Mara repeated.

Livia sat back in her chair. Mara heard the motion rather than saw it.

“You’re not lying,” Livia said. “You’re worried about what the lie might prove.”

Heat rose from the carafe. The air in the room tasted of sweat and old sugar. Jonah had gone to the sink to fill a paper cup from the filtered tap. He took his time drinking it, and Mara noticed him notice that she noticed.

Livia leaned forward. “You want the data to be dense enough to prove you’re exceptional.”

The cup’s handle was warm. Mara set it to the right of the keyboard, the place she would have reached for water.

“I want the data to be dense enough to prove I belong,” she said.

“You do.”

“How many times will you let me prove that?”

“As many times as you ask.”

Mara drank tea that had been ready for her. Livia had offered it before Mara had even imagined she wanted it.

The cup tasted too sweet and too good.

Livia rested a wrist on the table and looked at Mara from under her eyelashes. “If you’re staying, you can have overnight access. Tell us what you find in the morning.”

Mara sat.

A soft beep told her the tablet had registered the request. Livia did not smile, which Mara disliked because it was unfair. She felt the smile arrive in the darkness; she could see her own face reflect the invitation.

Jonah’s voice came from the other side of the desk.

“The next sequence is ready.”

Mara pressed her thumb against the tablet and accepted the access. Livia turned back to the monitor.

The light blinked again. The tea cooled untouched on the table.
