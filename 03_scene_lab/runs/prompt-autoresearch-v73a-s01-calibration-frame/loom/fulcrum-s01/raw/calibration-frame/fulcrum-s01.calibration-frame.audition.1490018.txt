The third man from the hallway followed the other two into the common room, a long-haired, big-boned individual in a deep voice that said “Good evening” and “Adrian.” He didn’t make for either Mara or Jonah but instead paused by the table beside the door where he picked up a plastic glass of wine, walked out into the middle of the floor, and held his hand up in a toast. “Cheers, everyone.”

The other two raised hands with cups or glasses, Jonah making do with his glass of water, and they clinked together briefly, before taking turns sitting on the long couch. Jonah invited Mara to join him there and she sat down at the end opposite him; when Adrian started speaking, he directed his attention mostly at her. “Good to meet you, Mara. What can I tell you?”

Mara blinked, taken aback. It was a little early to jump straight into questions. “Er, you’re one of the Fulcrum directors?”
Adrian nodded, sitting on the floor at Mara’s feet and looking up at her from between the couch cushions. “Yes. What else do you want to know?”

“Well,” she started, still mildly shocked but turning it toward the next idea. “What’s the purpose of tonight’s party?”

“That,” said Adrian, his eyes twinkling with some inner amusement, “is a very good question. And we haven’t actually talked about it yet, so why don’t we? Jonah—have you and Mara discussed that question?”

Jonah gave a slight shake of his head, watching the exchange closely from where he lay sprawled at the other end of the couch, eyes narrowed behind those heavy frames of his.

“So,” said Adrian again. “Why does this party exist, do you think?”

Mara paused a beat to register the invitation to speak. And then, having no reason not to try it out: “I think this party exists because you’re not going to tell me very much of anything without it.”

The corners of Adrian’s mouth turned upward in a slight smile.
“That is a good hypothesis,” he said.

Jonah snorted, not quite audibly.
