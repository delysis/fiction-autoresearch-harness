

"Your password is written in sweat," she said.

Simon closed his eyes for half a second. "I thought I was being careful."

"You were careful until the moment of truth."

"And then I failed."

She let her hand fall. "You failed me first."

Simon watched her pace to the window. The winter light made her hair shine where it escaped from beneath the borrowed hat.

"I didn't think you would come," he said.

Esther turned, still keeping distance between them. "You didn't want me to come."

"Yes." His voice broke on the admission. "No. Not when it counted."

She stood motionless for a long moment. Then she unbuttoned her shirt with one deliberate movement.

The cold caught Simon first in his chest, then behind his eyes.

Esther had been carrying their last conversation as a trace against her skin, a scar from the warehouse extraction where he had abandoned her to save his own life. Now she put the raw salt of it before him and waited.

Simon reached out—not for comfort, for evidence. His thumb touched the thin line of tissue where the old wound had healed poorly. "I can see it better in sunlight," Esther said.

"You've been waiting."

"For what? This moment?"

His hand hesitated over the scar.

"Tell me again," she said. "Before you touch me like someone who deserves to."

Simon's voice came out as a whisper. "I wrote my password in sweat to unlock the safe house. A mistake. One that almost cost you everything. And now it may still."

Esther looked at him directly. "Then we do the rest together."

Simon took her shirt off completely. Her hands came up to frame his face as he drew back her hair, tracing the same scar with his lips where his thumb had gone first.

"You were cold," she whispered.

He nodded. "I'm still cold."

"Not because of the water."

"No."

The air between them thickened. Simon stepped close enough to feel her heartbeat.

"Show me," she said, and unbuttoned his shirt.

The bruise from the warehouse appeared under his ribs, old blood like spilled ink against the pale skin beneath his collarbones. Esther traced the bruise with one finger, then moved closer until she could press her palm flat against it.

"This is what I'm afraid of," she whispered.

His hands went to her waist. "Tell me why."

"Because it's real. Because it happened."

Simon drew her closer. "And this—now?"

Esther's breath caught. "This is how we begin to talk."

They fell onto the bed together, not with passion or desire but with the weight of a truth both had been carrying for too long. The sheet caught beneath their bodies. Esther shifted slightly to avoid putting pressure on the bruise.

"Carefully," she said, her voice low.

Simon moved slowly, watching her face rather than following his own desire. Her breath quickened, not from pleasure but from the effort of keeping them both whole.

"Again," Esther whispered when he hesitated.

This time her hips met his. The salt on their skin mixed with fresh sweat. Simon could feel the way she was watching him, the way he was watching himself.

"I see it," she said softly.

"What?"

"That you're trying. That this matters."

Simon pressed closer, careful not to hurt either of them. "It always matters."

Esther's hands slid up his chest to cup his face. "Then let it matter here too."

She shifted against him, her body following the pattern he had left in the safe house. Simon's breath caught when she reached between them and put two fingers on his pulse point.

"I was afraid I wouldn't find you," she whispered.

"I was afraid you would."

Simon closed his eyes as her fingers traced the scar on his chest, then moved downward in a rhythm neither of them had planned. The pressure built between them without the safety of inexperience or the luxury of trust earned easily.

"I never meant to hurt you," he whispered against her mouth.

Esther's response was a single word that sounded more like a prayer than any request: "Now?"

Simon pulled back, his hand going to the back of her head. "Carefully."

Her body answered him with an honesty neither of them had dared before. The salt dried between them as their skin heated. The drive on the table remained untouched, its broken seal no longer the most obvious fracture in the room.

"I left you alone," he said against her neck.

Esther's voice came from somewhere deep inside both of them. "I know. Show me you can choose differently now."

Simon's hands moved slowly, following the curve of her ribs to the place where they met his own. Her breath caught as their bodies mirrored each other, not in desire but in something much more dangerous.

"You can see it?" she whispered.

"Yes."

"The risk?"

"And the price."

Simon felt the truth of this settle between them like the water he had spent too many nights wishing could wash away what had happened in that safe house.

"Not yet," Esther murmured against his lips. "But soon."

Her body answered him in ways he had not expected, the pattern of their connection revealing something neither of them could have planned. The drive's broken seal became less important than the salt staining the sheets beneath them.

Simon closed his eyes as Esther shifted their weight slightly to avoid pressure on the bruise. Her breath mixed with his in a rhythm neither of them could control but both of them needed.

"Again," she whispered against his neck.

This time Simon moved more deliberately, following the curve of her body where the salt had dried white against her skin. Her response came from somewhere deep inside her, a sound that was neither pleasure nor pain but something else entirely.

"I see it now," she said softly, her hand tracing the path of water between their bodies. "The pattern."

Simon reached out to take her hands in his, his fingers following the same path she had mapped on his chest. Their bodies mirrored each other perfectly, two halves of a truth neither could deny.

"Show me," Esther whispered.

He followed her movement carefully, the pressure building slowly as their skin warmed against the cold sheets. The salt dissolved between them in tiny crystals, leaving a white path that marked both their failure and their continuation.

"Now," she said when he hesitated.

Simon moved slowly, matching her rhythm exactly. Her breath caught as they reached the center point, the place where their bodies mirrored each other perfectly.

"There," Esther murmured against his lips. "The truth."

Her hips rose to meet his. Simon felt the salt dissolving between them, the water leaving its mark on both their skin and the sheets beneath them.

"Again," Esther whispered, her voice steady despite the pressure building between them.

This time he followed exactly, tracing the path she had mapped across his chest until their bodies formed a perfect circle of truth against the cold winter light.

"Now," she said softly.

The pressure reached its peak as their bodies closed around the scream that would have been both failure and redemption. Simon caught Esther to him carefully, following exactly the pattern they had made between them with salt and truth and skin.

"Again," she said when he paused. "One more time."

He followed exactly, tracing the white path of water until their bodies formed a circle against the window.

"There," Esther whispered against his lips. "The truth."
