
It would have been the sort of person she would ordinarily have ignored; one more unconsidered body among many. But now, in this place and at this time, he was the only other human presence she knew. She let out an unconscious sigh and stepped forward toward the couch, stopping to slide her backpack from her shoulders and set it beside the door.
The person sat up with a rustle of cotton fabric. As he moved, Mara saw that he wore what looked like pajamas. The black shirt was unbuttoned to show the upper part of a pale chest, and the right arm was bare above the elbow, where he had rolled his sleeve. The other arm stayed covered.
“Good evening,” she said. She could hear her own voice in the quiet room; it sounded very young. “I’m Mara Vale.”
The person looked blank. Then he made a small, abrupt movement; his eyes met hers with what looked like recognition, and he smiled.
“Good evening,” he said. “You’re new, right? I’m Jonah Reed.” He got up from the couch, a bit too tall to make it comfortable to sit with crossed legs, and held out his hand. “Adrian told us to expect you.”
She took the offered hand and shook it, feeling the strength in his fingers. He didn’t seem to be a newcomer. Mara couldn’t decide what sort of person he might be here—older, younger, smarter, less competent, more powerful. He made her nervous; he was good-looking, and she could feel him looking at her. She wanted to get away from that feeling.
But then something very strange happened. His hand stayed in hers, holding her wrist so that he could look at her. Mara felt a small tremor of uneasiness. What did he think she was? Her heart began to pound. He was staring at her, but his face held no expression at all, like a blank page.
Then he smiled a little and released her hand. She let her arms drop to her sides; the movement felt strange.
“Sorry, you startled me.” He laughed. “I mean, I can’t see what’s so alarming about you. Are you feeling okay?”
Mara wanted to ask what he had just said, but he cut her off before she could speak.
