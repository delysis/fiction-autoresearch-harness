 The head came upright and the lips drew back in a smile that didn’t reach the eyes, and he sat forward with an audible creak from the couch.
She took a slow breath and stepped into the room. He said nothing; the smile dropped away into a plain, curious expression. His arms lay on the arms of the couch as if to hold him still in place. His face was young, without the sharp lines she associated with the other people here—a few years older than she was, perhaps, though the hair might be dyed. He might be a researcher; his face had that alert, slightly abstracted quality of someone who understood the work being done and wasn’t afraid to do it. He was an excellent-looking specimen, well put-together and not self-conscious about it. She would probably have found him interesting enough for a conversation at a party, if she’d met him elsewhere.
He sat motionless, letting her take her time about him, and it occurred to her that he could be a test. A kind of trial; something she was being observed doing or not doing. But he was looking directly at her, openly curious, and she could not imagine Adrian Voss staging such a thing, with all the attendant risk that someone else might happen upon them and have a conversation. Besides, why would it matter if she talked to him, or not? She was here, already, for an interview.
“You’re here early,” she said in her usual dry manner. “Welcome party?”
“Maybe,” he said, smiling. “What’s the occasion?”
“Oh, I suppose they decided that since it was too late for me to back out now, they might as well get on with it.” She grinned, crossing her arms. “Who are you? You look familiar.”
He relaxed a little, seeming to understand that the conversation wouldn’t end. “I am. You don’t come over often.”
“Never.”
“They told me you were coming,” he said. “I thought I should get to know you while I have the chance. You’re the only one of us who didn’t spend her high-school years playing chess with Adrian.”
“How’d that turn out for you?” Mara asked, with interest.
He spread his hands. “Well enough, I guess. It depends on who’s doing the judging.”
