 They looked down at their joint effort, then looked up, and met each other’s eyes again. Mara’s brow knitted itself together briefly and Jonah released her hand so that she could smooth it out, his lips parting in a small half-smile.
“I’m looking forward to the next couple days,” she said. “Thanks for inviting me.”
“And I you,” he said, smiling a little more. He glanced over her shoulder to check if she wanted to walk in farther—but she was already here.
It was as if he had already guessed all of what she would do.
It wasn’t really like that—and yet it was. He did seem to read her mind sometimes, to anticipate where her thoughts might lead and guide them. As though he were aware of her before she was. He wasn’t, but his awareness preceded hers all the same, somehow.
Jonah had come up behind Mara and rested his hands lightly on her shoulders in a gesture of camaraderie—his palms warm and dry on her arms—and as she turned to look at him she realized with a start that it wasn’t just his awareness that was larger than her own but also the scope of his attention: he seemed able to hold many things at once, to notice multiple streams of information all simultaneously—and yet not miss anything either. It was like being watched by several people at once and only one man at all times: unnerving when she first realized it, because she couldn’t decide whether he would see each detail separately or everything as a whole.
