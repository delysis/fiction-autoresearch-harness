"I don't do that," Mara said. She'd felt the urge to say it that way ever since Livia had suggested the idea, though she had no idea why. It was stupid and stubborn to refuse when what she needed most was touch and comfort--which meant she would probably give in before long.
But when her stubbornness took over, she usually gave in while clinging to whatever stubborn scrap of self-righteousness she'd insisted on, so she didn't feel any more embarrassed than usual about refusing again.
"I don't do that," she repeated. "Not to people."

Livia had made no move toward Mara, but as Mara refused a second time, her gaze dropped to Mara's feet and lingered there. Her expression was neutral, almost puzzled, and she stood quite still, giving Mara the impression that Livia wanted to move closer but wasn't sure how.
"Then what do you do?" Livia said at last. "If not to people, then to what? If you want me to help you." She spoke with a gentleness that seemed to come straight out of her voice and from nowhere else; the shift was too fast and smooth to be anything but natural.
Mara glanced away and thought about it. The only thing she could remember doing that felt right was making that circle of trees in the clearing at the end of the path through the forest--and the trees had been willing. She looked up and found Livia still watching her, a patient stillness on her face. Mara shifted restlessly and then sat perfectly still, trying to find a way to make her mind slow down.
