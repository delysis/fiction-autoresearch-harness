His voice carried the weight of guilt, the shadow of choices made in isolation—choices that had left Esther feeling abandoned. Her heart pounded against her ribs as she tried to find words that didn’t sound angry or hurt.
“Simon,” she began, taking a deep breath to steady herself. “It wasn’t just about what happened; it was about not being there... about feeling like... like—”
“Alone,” Simon finished for her; his eyes met hers with sincerity. “I never meant to make you feel that way.”
There was no room for denial now—their shared past demanded honesty. Esther turned away briefly; she couldn’t bear having him see tears welling up in her eyes so soon after they had been apart for so long.

He traced his fingertips along her jawline—a gesture both tender and desperate—trying to draw out whatever pain lurked behind her eyes without causing further anguish by probing too deeply too quickly.
