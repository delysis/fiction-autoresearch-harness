Adrian stood by the wall of the main building, welcoming people warmly with his usual smile. Mara could see he was working the room, but she didn’t want to make a special point of meeting the founder at her first party. She listened instead as a young man named Chris started to explain a new AI project that he said would completely change how we think about intelligence.

“Wait,” Mara said, “where’d you get that glass?” She pointed to the low wall where a half-full glass of wine rested against its edge, forgotten in the crowd. It was surrounded by empty bottles and plates, a clutter building up fast despite the catering team, which kept cycling out of the house, refilling platters and clearing tables.

“From one of those waiters, I guess?” Chris replied, distracted, before launching into a complicated explanation about intelligence as a feedback loop that Mara did her best to follow.

The party continued, moving indoors after sunset, and when it finally began to wind down, Mara stood at the end of the hall, watching people say their goodbyes and disappear toward the stairs that led up to the dorms. She felt tired and out of place, glad to be heading up to the quiet of her room, but still unable to shake her curiosity about the party’s details. It wasn’t until Adrian stepped in front of her that she realized it was all a test, a way for her to calibrate against herself.

“Mara!” Adrian said, grinning, “welcome to Fulcrum.”

She smiled back, still processing.

“I heard you’re one of the new hires working on infrastructure security. You’ll be joining my team—there’s an office down at the far end of the building, next to the servers.” He clapped her on the shoulder and nodded toward the hall. “That’s where I’m going. Want to join me?”

Mara felt a flicker of excitement as he led her past a few others, their eyes following her for the first time. He’d seen her, he was looking out for her, but something about him still felt off-center. She let herself follow, turning away from the stairs, curious to see where this path would take her.

“Do you mind if I ask,” she said, following his footsteps through the crowd, “who picked up that wine glass?”

Adrian didn’t answer, but she saw his face twitch just slightly as he reached out to open the heavy steel door. It clicked shut behind them, sealing out the party’s noise, and the quiet of the office space seemed almost as welcoming as the dorm. She stepped inside, still watching him closely, trying to make sense of the new reality
