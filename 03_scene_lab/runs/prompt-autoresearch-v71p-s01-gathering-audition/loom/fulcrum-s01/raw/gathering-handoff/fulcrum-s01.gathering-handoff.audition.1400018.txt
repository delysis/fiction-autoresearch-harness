"Oh! Who’s that?"
She knew before she saw, and as the voice emerged from the other doorway, a young woman came into view: a black girl maybe Mara’s own age, dark hair drawn back and dressed casually in loose pants and a long-sleeved shirt, open collar, a dark skin that almost matched the shade of her eyes.
Mara didn’t remember her face. She should have paid more attention in the lobby. But the body was new to her. The voice had been new too. And if it was possible to be unaware of a body for any length of time, Mara had just done that.

It was not true. Mara had already seen Jonah’s body several times. It was not true. Mara had never looked at this face before; she had never heard that voice before. The other doorway—the right one, as Mara looked across—was the one the girl had used, and Jonah was in the left one when Mara had entered, so Jonah’s voice, as she had first heard it, was the sound of a mouth moving a foot below its eyes and a foot to the side; she had already forgotten it.
Jonah let go of her hand. “Hi, Livia,” he called back, taking a step toward Mara’s right.
Mara looked back in surprise. They had said the name together.

Livia came closer and joined the two of them at the left door.
