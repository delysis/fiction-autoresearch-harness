Mara’s first impression of him had been that he was almost too young for this place. He seemed very young in fact. But now he shook her hand with the assurance of an adult man, confident enough not to let go quickly. When he did at last, his smile hadn’t faded a bit; and then she felt silly to have let herself wonder what kind of man he was. Of course he knew this place; of course he knew what he was doing here; and of course he was confident of her welcome.
Mara wasn’t sure what else to say to him, but luckily Jonah didn’t seem to expect more conversation than “nice to meet you.” Instead he asked after Livia. Mara confirmed that Livia was still in town and had promised to attend tonight; the room was empty for the moment, but dinner would be served soon and Livia usually arrived after they began serving. She was often late, she added.
Jonah nodded in approval, and at this point Mara began to suspect that Jonah knew something about Livia.
She was already beginning to feel comfortable with him—as with other men his age, she reminded herself—when the voices arrived at the doorway, interrupting their conversation. She turned her attention toward them.
