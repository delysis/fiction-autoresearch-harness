
“Adrian.”
Mara wasn’t sure at first if the answer came from the boy on the couch or the back of her mind. She looked over her shoulder at him.
His voice was soft, but deep for someone so young. Adrian, she thought, like the book. She hoped it didn’t show on her face that she was reading everything she could.
Then his eyes met hers directly again and the hair on her neck stood on end.
Mara felt as if her whole body were under a kind of scrutiny, even the back of her neck. But she had no idea whether Adrian had done it himself or not. She thought he might have done something with his eyes that made it seem as though he were touching her physically, but she couldn’t imagine how or why anyone would want to do such a thing. It seemed ridiculous.
So instead of thinking about what could have caused this feeling in the back of her neck, Mara wondered why Adrian wasn’t saying anything else.
“Do you have something you’re working on?” she asked. “Or maybe a favorite place around here?”
She was conscious that the sound of her own voice made her feel more vulnerable. The boy on the couch was looking at her, and she felt under the spotlight once again. But this time the attention felt different. It wasn’t just curious any more; it seemed to contain something else as well. A touch of hostility? A threat?
Or perhaps Mara was projecting. After all, he hadn’t actually spoken to her in a way that suggested either one thing or another.

But then Adrian spoke again: “I don’t like this place very much.”
He was looking at the books lining the wall as he said it, and she was about to make a joke about the reading material when suddenly he looked straight at her. His expression had changed again. There was something almost triumphant in it now, as if he had been waiting for some sign of agreement from her before he spoke again.
But the strangest part of all was that his tone hadn’t changed at all. He sounded just as calm, almost disinterested, as ever. If Mara hadn’t seen his face first, she wouldn’t have believed there could be any difference in how he felt about the place than before.

Chapter Two
Observation

“Where do you like to spend your time?” Mara asked. She hoped he would give her an easy answer, something obvious, so that she could pretend she didn’t have to think too much about it and keep the conversation light.
But instead he shook his head slightly as if to say no. It took her a moment to realize what it meant—she hadn’t been thinking about where to go until now. There was some library somewhere, wasn’t there? And then there were all those classes.
“I usually spend my free time in the lab,” Adrian said after a pause. “The one with Dr. Reed?”
She felt her eyebrows go up in surprise again at the sound of her voice. What made her say that? She didn’t have any idea why she would ask where he liked to work, or what kind of answer he might give. But he did give an answer and it was a perfect one; she could use the same kind of reply herself when asked what kind of work she wanted to do.
Maybe I’ll get lucky and end up with a lab like his, Mara thought. Then maybe someone else will think that we’re both doing something similar and invite us to lunch together or something.
Adrian was still looking at her intently as though trying to read something on her face, or perhaps waiting for some kind of signal that would tell him what kind of answer she wanted next. She glanced around the room again, trying to look like she had decided something about where she wanted to be without seeming too eager. But then suddenly he spoke again.
“Do you like working alone?”
