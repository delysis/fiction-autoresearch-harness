Mara entered Fulcrum’s visitor parking lot through an automatic gate set far back on a narrow mountain road. The sign at the gate identified the company as “Fulcrum Technologies, LLC,” but she had yet to find a Fulcrum employee who used the word “technology” for what the company made. The word was old and associated with things that worked—machines that had replaceable parts. It wasn’t a good fit for Fulcrum.

It was evening—seven p.m.—and the lot held a single Tesla, parked haphazardly across two stalls. She recognized the make but didn’t know what model it was or how much it cost. Maybe eighty thousand dollars? That would have been expensive enough to mark it as special, she thought, and parked her rental behind it—a Dodge Caravan she’d gotten a good deal on.
