There was a long table. White linen tablecloth and napkins, gold-plated silverware, real glasses. The light of the sunset touched everything, filling it with rose and fire.
The food was almost too much. A huge grilled ribeye, rare. Whipped potatoes made out of Yukon golds, silky and creamy and buttery. Broccoli florets with a lemony sauce. Asparagus spears, a few carrots. A huge glass of ice-cold water.

In the center of the table stood the bread and butter.
The bread was sliced sourdough bread that had been turned into thick slices, then brushed with olive oil and toasted. And they had left it whole.
