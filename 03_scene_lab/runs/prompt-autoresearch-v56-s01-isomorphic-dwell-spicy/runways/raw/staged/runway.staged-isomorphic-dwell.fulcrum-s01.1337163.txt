At the gate Mara had shown her ID to a gray-haired, ruddy, bearded man in a leather cap who had looked at the ID but not at her face and seemed to know what he was supposed to be doing even though she had no idea how he fit in. Was he security? Did the company have some sort of formal visitor identification protocol? But the gate didn’t have an intercom and hadn’t seemed to be under any kind of video surveillance, and once Mara had entered, the lot was empty except for a few scattered bikes, and it had taken her a minute or two to find a spot where the tires of her rental wouldn’t sink into grass.

Her manager at work, John, who’d hired her right out of grad school, had said the parking lot would be empty because Fulcrum was “all about bicycles,” which was one reason he’d advised her against renting a car. He hadn’t said anything about security, but that was part of the Fulcrum myth: There was no security.

She carried her backpack to the only building she could see and went up some broad wooden stairs to a deck that faced north, with a view of redwood trees against a blue-and-cloudy sky. At the far end of the deck was an open sliding glass door that led into an empty hallway and beyond it, another hallway, lined on one side with office doors that were either slightly or very open. She hesitated, but there was no sign or doorbell or any indication that anyone was supposed to be there, so she went through the second hallway, feeling embarrassed about walking through the open doorways of strangers. At the end of the second hallway she entered a space that was neither a room nor a corridor: A row of wooden bookshelves on one side separated it from the next room, while the other side was entirely glass and looked out onto what might have been either a garden or just a wilderness of weeds—it had a pond but no visible plants.

Then she heard voices, and a tall dark man with close-cropped curly hair and a thin black goatee walked up the stairs that led down from this level. He glanced at her backpack then smiled. “Hi! I’m Adrian. Are you here for Fulcrum?”

“Yes,” she said. “I’m Mara.” She showed him her ID.

The dark-haired man smiled even more. He looked like a movie star who didn’t need to try very hard to be cool. “Nice to meet you, Mara. Welcome to Fulcrum!” He shook her hand warmly, and she wondered how long it had been since he’d been through the gate, because he smelled like he’d recently taken a shower and put on clean clothes
