When the last car in Mara’s small group of interviewees arrived, and when all the new recruits had received their badges, they were escorted in a single, quiet caravan from the front desk to the main building.
The new building’s interior was nearly as spectacular as the exterior. A high glass atrium overlooked the main entrance, and even on that bright afternoon, warm sunlight poured in, and illuminated the tall, slim palm trees standing in neat rows on the ground floor.
A long walkway bridged the upper floors, making them into a single open space. Employees were moving quietly to and fro. The sound of typing, phone conversations, and the hum of several hundred people talking softly was muted by the high, white ceilings and glass walls.
Mara did not know what she was looking at or who anybody was. A lot of people were wearing headphones, which made it look like a library. But there were no books anywhere. Everybody was dressed in clothes that looked like regular jeans and t-shirts and hoodies. Only the most eagle-eyed might note that all the hoodies were uniform dark-gray Fulcrum-branded sweats.
The woman who had checked Mara in followed the procession to an enormous table set with a few laptops.
“These are your computers, and this is where you’re going to plug in. You already know how to do that, right?”
The young men chuckled. Mara nodded.
“Great.” The woman gave them their badges, which were on lanyards. “When you plug in, you need to scan these in at your workstation.” She held up the badge with Fulcrum’s logo on the front and a big QR code on the back, the same ones Mara had worn to work at her old company. “If you need any help, you know where we are.” She gave them a warm smile that made Mara feel like a college student who’d finally made it. The woman walked away quickly and the new recruits were left staring at the big table, at each other, and then at their new computers.
Nobody did anything for about a minute. Then one of the young men picked up his laptop, unplugged it from the power outlet, and slid into the nearest seat. He scanned in his badge, logged on, and began typing rapidly, using both hands to slide the keyboard around as he got comfortable.
Mara looked at the others. A couple of people seemed to be waiting to see what he was doing. The man at the table had been doing the same thing, but now that there was one person going it seemed like they should all go together.
