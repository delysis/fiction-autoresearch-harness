 He broke the awkwardness.
“I’m sorry if I startled you. I was expecting someone else, and—I guess—I was a little nervous myself.”
He didn’t let go of her hand just yet, so she couldn’t see his face without turning sideways. She felt herself smiling as she gave a short laugh, and then realized he had just been making a joke at his own expense.
“How long have you worked here?”
“I’ve only been here for two days,” he said. “But Adrian told me about you when we were at lunch today.”
He finally let go of her hand so that she could look him in the eye again, and smiled his big boy-next-door smile.
She had never seen anyone who could pull off glasses like those before; they didn’t look stupid or nerdy but rather somehow serious, intelligent even. He wasn’t wearing any other kind of jewelry besides an ordinary wristwatch; it didn’t seem to have anything to do with his style at all.
“That’s great.” Her voice was low, trying to be casual as she let out a small laugh that sounded false even in her own ears. “I’m glad he did.”
She turned away from him and glanced down at the coffee table near the couch where they had been standing just moments ago; there was nothing on it but two empty glasses for drinks, one larger than the other with ice cubes in each. Jonah followed her gaze curiously.
“He said you’re coming here tonight for an interview.” He gestured back to the open doorway behind him. “Are you waiting?”
She nodded absent-mindedly and took a step towards the door.
“Well then come on in—I’m sure it won’t be much longer now.”
