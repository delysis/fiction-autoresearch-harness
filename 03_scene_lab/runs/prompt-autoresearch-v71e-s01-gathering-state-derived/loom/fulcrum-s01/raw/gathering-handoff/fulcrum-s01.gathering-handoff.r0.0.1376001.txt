 They held the contact with each other’s hands, but let their eyes wander around the space. The couch. The fireplace. The window behind them and beyond it the twilight over the bay.
Then Jonah dropped his hand to his side, but leaned in closer so that his shoulder was touching hers, just for a moment. His glasses caught a little glint of light off the lamp behind her.
“Did you have a chance to look around while you were waiting? Your room has a pretty good view.”

“The bay,” Mara agreed, looking toward the water and the lights already coming on across it, “and the highway.”
It was odd being alone with another recruit, since all their communication up to now had been through Adrian’s email. Or she’d been alone, except for those silent meetings in the parking lot when the two of them had had their backgrounds checked.

She saw a man walk in. She glanced at him: mid-twenties, tall, dark, slim.

“Did you arrive this afternoon?” she asked.

He smiled at her and leaned in closer, then gave an awkward shrug. “Last week,” he said, grinning. “And I’ve been driving around town, trying to get lost, all day. It’s a very small town.”

Mara nodded.

“They can’t be too strict about their rules,” she said. “If they let you leave the premises.”

He gave her another smile. “I don’t know whether I left or not. There were always people watching me. I don’t think it’s the kind of rule they enforce.”

His voice made her relax her shoulders, just a fraction. She decided that the two of them could be friends. If she ever made it to the next round.
