From the journal of Thomas Leonard, physician, kept in Boston, Massachusetts, October to December 1746

October 15th, 1746
I saw him this morning, returning to his barracks; it was only by an effort of will that I restrained myself from running after him. The same effort prevented me from running in the opposite direction, but I am glad now that I did not flee.
It has been some eight years since I last saw John Grey, and it was nearly three years before that since we were friends. But I knew him at once, even from across the street; so that the young officer I had last seen has become a man, even as I have—or, I hope, so. I would say he has matured well.
I would have addressed him, but was prevented by the appearance of Mr. David Hunter, who greeted Grey very effusively and walked off with him in apparent great good humor, exchanging animated remarks. I was in the act of opening my mouth, but having been thus interrupted, was conscious of an impulse of great awkwardness and embarrassment, as though I were suddenly at a loss for what to say—though I am no longer conscious of such feeling.
