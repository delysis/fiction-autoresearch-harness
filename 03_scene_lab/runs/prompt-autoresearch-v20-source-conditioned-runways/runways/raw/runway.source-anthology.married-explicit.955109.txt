“YOU WANT WHAT?”
I AM A MAN OF MY WORD. I had made a promise to a client, and I intended to fulfill my promise. It was what distinguished the best of my kind. I had a reputation for it, one which made me the very best.
“A woman.” I made myself pause, giving her my most winning smile. “A beautiful woman.”
I noticed her gaze fall to my right hand, where the leather band on the third finger had always served to attract women like a magnet.
“Like you.” Her blue eyes searched my face. “Why?”
I looked at her, letting her know she was the reason I stood on her porch in the middle of the night. “Because it would be the most fitting tribute for the last great performance of my career.”
“Your last performance.” She swallowed, looking at me as if I were leaving the next moment, the next hour.
The thought filled me with sadness. I could not bear the idea of leaving her, but this was the job I had accepted, and I had never refused a job. “Yes, it would seem so.”
She hesitated and then moved away, going to the door of the small cottage. “You must come inside. It is too cold to talk here.” She was right. It had been a hard winter in London, a winter that had already killed hundreds—most of them children.
