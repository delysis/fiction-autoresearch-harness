THE HOUSE ON THE RIDGE

Jamie walked into the yard, carrying a box of books in his arms. It was evening now, and the air was full of the sounds and scents of night. Insects buzzed and chittered in the grasses. A night bird called, far off in the hills above. The warm air was heady with the smell of newly cut grass and the rank scent of wood smoke. The little house was dark; the only light in the yard was from the faint rays of the setting sun, reflected from the windows on the ridge.
There was a noise, and he looked up in surprise. The upper windows of the house were ajar, and he could hear a faint murmur of conversation inside.
He opened the front gate and started toward the house. He had a sudden feeling that he knew the voice. He pushed the front door open and went inside. The living room was dark and cold. The only light in the room came from the small, round window over the doorway. The curtains had been drawn shut, but someone had left the window open. The moonlight shone through the narrow opening and into the room, where it fell on a pair of dusty leather chairs and the old oak table.
