Afternoon, Saturday, June 21, 2014

This place is a nightmare, I thought, staring at the glass wall.

I might have said the same of myself. A half hour ago, I was an ordinary woman.

I’d spent most of my day at home in a state of disheveled bliss. I’d finished a novel in which the hero got everything he deserved but never really wanted. I’d helped my children clean up their messy play space. I’d even helped my partner clean up the kitchen—and we’d done this together, cheerfully—with only three words of argument.

We’d had a good sex scene at the table. We’d had an even better one on the living room floor, in plain sight of our sleeping daughter. We’d had a third scene in which the heroine went to the hero to be soothed.

Now, my life was in chaos, and I was in a cage.

My daughter, Claire, lay asleep in a Fulcrum holding chamber. She might not be breathing. She might have stopped being. The cage was too far away for me to be sure.

Fulcrum staff hadn’t even offered to let me hold her hand.

“You’re making me feel sick,” a voice from my right said. The man holding it sounded irritated. “It’s like staring into the sun. Stop.”

I did not stop
