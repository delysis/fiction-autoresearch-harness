“So I asked the doctor if he thought this was hereditary, because, you know, I thought there was some kind of family connection, but he said he’d never heard of a case with any history of that and I told him that I would think it was likely, because if it came up in one woman of the family, well, there would be others, and then he gave me one of those looks and I knew what he was thinking, so I said, ‘I mean, in her family, not mine—I’m a man,’ but then he gave me a smile like he was thinking that was just as well, since the whole thing sounded pretty unnatural, if you catch my meaning, and I told him, yes, I caught his meaning and he did have a point there—”
“Father!” The speaker paused. “Are you talking to me?”
“Sorry, Henry, I didn’t notice I was out loud,” the elderly man said, a little shamefaced, looking round at the young man leaning over his shoulder. “It was just something I was saying to myself.”
Henry glanced across the busy roadway to where his father was pointing.
