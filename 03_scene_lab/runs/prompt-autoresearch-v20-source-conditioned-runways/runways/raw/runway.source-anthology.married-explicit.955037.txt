A TALL MAN SAT ON HIS horse by the side of a long road, watching an interminable caravan slowly approach along a long dusty road. He watched it with an interest that he couldn’t have defined. He knew the general nature of what was going on, but the details were obscure to him.
There were men on horses, men on wagons, men on foot; there were dogs, women, and children as well. All were marching north, along the road through the town of Cross Creek toward what he knew—in his mind’s eye—as the Mohawk Valley. It seemed that the sun shone everywhere.
A single man rode out of the caravan, coming up beside the tall man’s horse. The horses stood, heads turned, watching as the man dismounted. He came round to the side of the horse where the tall man sat, looking straight down into the face of the man sitting atop it. He asked a question.
The tall man shook his head slightly. “No,” he said. “I’m sorry. I do not know him.”
He was not surprised that he couldn’t. His name was John Grey—or rather, the man in the saddle bore the body of Lord John Grey, former officer in the British Army—and the name of the man before him was William MacKenzie.
It was the voice that gave him away.
