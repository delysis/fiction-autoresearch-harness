Mara

The Fulcrum’s lab was always frigid, but now it was downright glacial. The walls and floors were of white plasterboard, the only decoration a red-and-green wall calendar from last year. I couldn’t see Jonah; I was alone in a corner, facing a screen and a small table. Behind the screen I could hear a woman typing, then the faint whine of a printer. A sheet of paper emerged and she handed it to me, then settled me in a chair.
“Now, would you please write down the most important thing you learned today?”
The first words that came to mind were “My friend’s dead,” but I took a deep breath and thought a minute. I’d gotten an A minus on the biology exam; I’d never gotten an A minus. And I’d been talking to the nurse at school about my problem, and I’d just bought a new dress and a new book, and I had new shoes on the way in the mail, and—
I turned back to the sheet of paper and wrote: “I learned a lot today, but the most important thing I learned was to live life to the fullest every day, because you never know when it might end.”
