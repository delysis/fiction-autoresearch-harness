A small, well-groomed man, in the company of a very tall, very old wolf, enters an ordinary hotel restaurant and seats himself at a table near a window. His order consists of a glass of ice water, a small pot of hot tea, and a plate of fresh-baked bread. The wolf orders a raw steak, rare.

The waiter says, “I’m sorry, sir, we don’t serve animals, and that goes doubly for wolves.”

The gentleman turns and says to his pet, “Did you hear that?”

“Yes,” says the wolf.

“Well,” says his owner, “that was rather rude.”

“Yes, it was,” the wolf says, standing up and giving the waiter a polite but distinctly wolfish grin. He then walks over to the next table and eats the gentleman in the dark suit.

When he’s done, the gentleman looks down at the remains of his erstwhile guest and turns his head, saying to his friend, “Do you mind?”

“Not at all,” says the wolf. “He’s not using it.”

“Very good,” he says. “And will you be needing salt or pepper?”

The waiter says, “It’s all right, sir. He’s only asking for bread.”
