The sun, which had been sinking fast in a cloudless sky, turned a blinding orange as it reached the horizon, reflected back by the ice in a dazzling corona. I sat back against my saddlebag, trying to squint one-eyed at it, but my good eye couldn’t stand up to the blaze, and I dropped my face against my forearm.
“Not long now, ye ken.” Claire Fraser was sitting cross-legged on the frozen ground, hands cradling a mug of hot tea. “Once it’s gone, it’ll be full dark in minutes.”
She held the mug out, and I took it, sipping with care. It was hot—and it was whiskey tea. I could taste the faint sweetness of the herb, but the bitter fire of the spirits warmed my guts and spread its warmth outward, easing my bruised muscles.
I raised the cup in acknowledgment. “Thank ye, Sassenach.”
She nodded, the tips of her red curls catching fire from the sunset behind her.
“Aye, well, it’s just a small dram; the sun’s gone soon, but it’ll be some hours yet till we’re home, and I’d not want ye passing out on me, and getting killed by some sodomite out for a stroll in the heather.”
