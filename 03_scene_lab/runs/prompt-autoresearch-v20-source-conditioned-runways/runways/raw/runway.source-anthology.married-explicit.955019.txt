“A kiss.” He looked at her in sudden alarm, as though expecting her to throw herself at him; he took a step backward, putting his hand to the small of his back as though to support it.
“Och, aye, you’ve got it,” he said, jerking a thumb toward himself, but keeping his hands firmly by his sides. “It’s the only kind ye can get, I’m afraid, the way I am.”
“I would be content,” she said, lifting a hand to his shoulder, “were you to simply look at me. But if you should choose to kiss me, I shall not refuse you.”
His face came so close that she felt the coolness of his breath on her skin, smelling of the crisp spring wind, the flowers, the scent of earth, of the damp earth.
“And what then, when I have?”
The heat of the day, the heat of her own desire, pressed up round her, so that it felt as though she were being slowly wrapped in a silken blanket. But she could not touch him for that very heat; could not touch him, for fear.
His lips touched hers lightly as a butterfly’s kiss, the soft, quick caress of spring wind on cheek. He paused, breathing lightly.
