The first night after I got married, I did not go to bed alone. I had a roommate.
Her name was Anka, and she was a Russian princess. Her mother, Grand Duchess Maria Petrovna, had been in America visiting the Czar, and the baby—the Grand Duke Andrei— had been born unexpectedly at the grand Hotel de L’Ocean in New York.
It had been a premature delivery, and I had been summoned urgently from the house in New York, where I was nursing my husband’s elderly aunt. I had stayed a week, caring for the royal mother and child, before being summoned home again. My aunt Jocasta had died, and I was expected.
Anka was a small child, only five years old, but she’d taken it upon herself to attend me with much solicitude while I was in New York, hovering round the bed where the Czar’s infant son slept under the watchful eyes of his nurse and nanny, his governess, and assorted attendants, who hovered, cooed, and fussed.
“He’s not much good to anyone but you,” Anka had said. She watched with narrowed eyes as I examined the infant, noting that he seemed well enough, but was, indeed, the tiniest little thing I had seen in years.
