In the rideshare from SFO, Mara made small talk with the driver. "Have you taken many guests to Fulcrum? Any tips?"

The driver, who had been distracted, turned a surprised look back at her. "Oh, that place! My partner went on retreat there recently. I'm taking him back after this run."

"Really? What was it like?" Mara felt an involuntary frisson of anxiety. She'd been chosen as one of the few who would be accepted that week, but she still wasn't sure what to expect.

"Well, it's a little strange at first," the driver said, with an amused glance in the rearview mirror. "Like some kind of secret society, you know?" He nodded to a side road that led toward Fulcrum. "You can tell by the way they don't show up on Google Maps."

Mara laughed nervously, but her curiosity was piqued. The driver continued, "The way they talk about things can be a bit...abstract. They seem to focus on these invisible rules that everyone knows but can't really explain."

Mara nodded slowly, absorbing this. "So what did you think of the place when you dropped him off?"

"Oh, it looked fine enough," the driver replied, shrugging. "Kind of upscale, you know? Like a fancy retreat center or something. But there was something about the way people interacted there, like they were all on a different wavelength."

Mara leaned forward, intrigued. "What do you mean?"

The driver looked back at her again, his expression thoughtful. "Like there's an invisible code that everyone understands except you. Like being dropped into the middle of a private joke, and you're the only one who doesn't get it." He chuckled. "My partner came back all full of new jargon, but he wasn't much help explaining any of it."

They continued in silence for a moment before the driver turned back to Mara with an apologetic smile. "Sorry if I'm making it sound too weird. They say my partner really got a lot out of the experience. He seemed happier, you know? But it does take some getting used to."

Mara's mind raced as she processed this information. A place that was hard to find online, filled with people who operated on some kind of invisible wavelength. It sounded like something out of a movie, and yet here she was, about to step into it herself. She took a deep breath, trying to steady her nerves, and gave a polite nod in the rearview mirror.
