Livia poured amber tea from a ceramic carafe and slid the cup to Mara. The porcelain warmed her fingertips. She did not refuse it or comment. Livia smiled.

“How do you like your tea?”

“With honey.”

Livia held up a jar. “You’ll fit in beautifully.”

Mara stirred the honey until the tea stopped darkening. The warmth entered her body before she could stop it.

“This is the part,” Livia said, “where you explain why you’ve been quiet since the first hour.”

“I’m waiting for someone else to try the coffee.”

“I’m serious, Mara.”

Mara raised the cup to her lips and did not drink.

“Your silence isn’t a statement,” Livia said. “It’s the absence of one. You’re afraid close inspection will find something you can’t afford.”

The tremor returned. Mara put the cup back on the table before it showed.

“Maybe it’ll find something,” she said.

Livia nodded once. “Maybe it will. But you’ll stay until you know.”

She gave the word know the shape of a promise.

Mara stirred the honey again, this time with intention. “It’s the data density.”

“It’s fear of what the data might say. What if it finds nothing exceptional?”

The remark cut closer to the true shape of the fear than any theological reasoning would. Livia’s insight was not a kindness, but it was not cruelty either. Mara recognized the difference.

“The data isn’t exceptional,” she said.

“It doesn’t know that yet.”

“The data doesn’t care.”

“But you do.”

Livia’s fingers grazed Mara’s wrist. The touch was light and gone before it could be named.

Mara lifted the cup to her lips again and drank.

The tea tasted of chamomile and lemon. The steam rose between them, sweet and warm. The room felt quieter for a moment, as if the air itself had decided to listen.
