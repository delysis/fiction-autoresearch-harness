Livia poured amber tea from a ceramic carafe and slid the warm cup to Mara.

Mara took it.

“You think your exhaustion proves something about the alignment’s difficulty,” Livia said.

Mara looked up. “It proves that I need sleep.”

“No. You think it proves your lack of gift.”

“And that it’s visible to anybody who cares to look.”

“Yes.” Livia smiled. It was the sharp kind of smile that made her beauty harder to ignore. “But it isn’t.”

Mara turned toward the shared monitor. Jonah was still typing, his elbow braced against the table. The code under his hands changed.

Livia’s foot tapped.

“I can get you telemetry access,” she said.

Mara almost put her cup down. Instead she took a sip. “No.”

“It’s an ordinary privilege.”

“No.”

“It lets you see the trace between data points.”

The temperature of her tea began to change.

“You could study it.” Livia’s smile was not mocking. It contained something like hunger.

“I could publish it.”

Her foot stopped moving. “That’s not a good use of the method.”

“Or the fellows.”

“It’s not a good use of you.”

The pulse on her neck was visible above her collarbone.

Mara put the cup on the table.

“I have enough privileges,” she said. “I can keep one for myself.”
