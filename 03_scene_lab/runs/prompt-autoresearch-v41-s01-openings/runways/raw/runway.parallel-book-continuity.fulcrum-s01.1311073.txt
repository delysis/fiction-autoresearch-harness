The place surprised her, especially as it seemed to have been a pleasant surprise to everyone else. The grounds were lovely—an old redwood grove at the edge of what used to be rural Northern California and was now Silicon Valley. The buildings, as well, were old. A stately mansion like an antebellum plantation house dominated the property, flanked on one side by a cottage, on the other side by a studio-like addition. On this day, the day before a major conference at which their work was to be presented, they had invited members of the local press. They all ate a nice meal—steaks for those who liked it, seafood, vegetables. No one mentioned funding or competition; a few people mentioned that this was their favorite place in the world to work.
Mara joined a tour group which left after dinner—Livia as tour guide. She was introduced as a junior person working on some of the experiments.
The other Fulcrumians, as they called themselves, had already begun to notice her. “You are new,” Livia said, confirming it. “You must be very curious about all of this, then. We will explain as much as possible.”
