Mara pulled her luggage from the cab’s trunk and rolled it through the long curving drive, gravel crunching under her feet.

The Fulcrum compound reminded her of old photographs of her family’s ancestral home back East. A long low red-brick mansion with a dark shingled roof and a white portico, surrounded by a wide lawn dotted with shade trees and old-fashioned roses. The house had been burned down in the Civil War, a sad story that always ended with some variation on “But the family bloodline carried on.” Now here was its apparent twin, as if transported through time to this place of dry hills and wild winds.

Two young women approached her from opposite directions as she reached the steps. One had curly brown hair pulled back into a ponytail, the other long straight blond hair that caught the light. The brunette was very tall and thin, with an oval face; the blond was shorter and broader-shouldered, her cheeks and nose freckled.

“You’re Mara, aren’t you?” The brunette held out her hand as she arrived at the bottom step. She had a shy smile and an accent Mara couldn’t place—
