“The place is alive,” Mara said. “The air moves.” Her voice was hushed. “It’s alive.” The man didn’t speak. She could almost forget that he was there, in the car, driving her away from the compound she had called home. Almost. She glanced sideways at him as he navigated the tight curve at the crest of the hill and then slid into the wide pullout overlooking the Pacific Ocean. “Is it always like this?”

He paused for several moments before answering. “Like what?”

“Like what?” She shook her head. “Don’t do that. I feel it too.” The sun sank over the Pacific, golden light streaming through the windshield and catching his hair. The same blond hair she had seen on so many others like him, the same hair, Mara thought, that had convinced her mother to marry and bear his children. The same hair that she had not shared with her brothers. She looked at the blond hair, at the man’s strong brow, and then turned abruptly and stared straight ahead out the windshield.
