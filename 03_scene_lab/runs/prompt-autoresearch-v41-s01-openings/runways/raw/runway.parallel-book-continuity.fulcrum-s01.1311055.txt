As the taxi crawled up the narrow drive, I recognized the main house. I had seen it a dozen times from a distance on my hikes: a big, low, rambling building of dark stone set in the midst of woods and fields, its windows bright with the lights of a late spring sunset. Now I realized there were two additional buildings—one an ordinary-looking bungalow that had somehow eluded me all this time, and one a kind of glass pavilion set on stilts over a small creek. From here, near the end of the drive, I could only see the underside of it.

I had made my way down from the north end of the bay. The taxi came up through a field of wildflowers—a startling display of scarlet monkeyflowers and golden poppies that went on for miles, and for once did not appear to have been deliberately cultivated. At last the road wound through woods, where the shade felt like an unexpected gift after hours in the hot sun. Here, on a level patch between the main house and the bungalow, stood the glass pavilion. Its ceiling was visible as a flat rectangle of pink and purple lights reflected from below.
