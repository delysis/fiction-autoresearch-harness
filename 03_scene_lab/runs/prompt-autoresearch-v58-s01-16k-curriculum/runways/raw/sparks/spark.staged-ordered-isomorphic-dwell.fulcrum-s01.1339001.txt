46 The Glass

The compound's central space was a large redwood box whose walls had been sawed through into hundreds of rectangular apertures. On the walls were old wooden windows. A dozen young men, each with a laptop, sat around a coffee table and sipped white wine from paper cups. A small bearded man with a baseball cap addressed them in the tone of a college lecturer. When Mara stepped through the doorway, his voice stopped. She had not intended to interrupt anything. The room was full of people, most of whom were younger than her. Their T-shirts hung loosely off them like the rags of the poor or the tents of Bedouins; their eyes, pale and sleepless, gave a haunted quality to their gazes.
