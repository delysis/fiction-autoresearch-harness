In her black silk cocktail dress Mara was a woman to watch in passing traffic, but in this garden under redwood arches and glass she seemed part of a landscape design, a sculptural element not intended for conversation. Not that anyone stopped to speak to her or made conversation easy by a smile or eye contact. She had known it wouldn't be, here on the edge of San Francisco Bay among people who didn't care what clothes cost, but it still annoyed her.

An older man in gray jacket and black trousers with an invisible label, presumably one of Fulcrum's contractors, passed by with a tray of half-full glasses, his eyes fixed on a point just beyond her right ear.
