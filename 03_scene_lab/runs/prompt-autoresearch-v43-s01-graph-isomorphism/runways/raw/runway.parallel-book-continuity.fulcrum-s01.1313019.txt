It was a rideshare, so the driver wouldn't know where this address went.

I looked out at the redwoods, the glass-and-wood architecture, the dark and the quiet. It was later than I expected. If the driver thought this was someone's home, it was one of the strangest homes she would ever see.

"Here." She stopped. I gathered my suitcase and paid her. "Welcome to Fulcrum. I hope you'll enjoy your stay."

The compound's gate stayed open; no guard or checkpoint waited. The driver pulled away through the twilight.

I'd heard that Fulcrum's Northern California location was in the redwoods, but I wasn't prepared for this. The ride had been a long series of curves and hills with little traffic. For miles now there hadn't been another building on either side of the road; there were only patches of redwoods. They grew on both sides, rising up into the darkening sky, blocking each other's light and leaving nothing for undergrowth. There had been no sign of human habitation except a few mailboxes and the occasional unpainted wood house at the end of an unpaved driveway.
