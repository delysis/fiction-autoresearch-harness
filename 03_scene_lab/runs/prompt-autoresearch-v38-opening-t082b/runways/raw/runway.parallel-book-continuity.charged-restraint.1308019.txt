The night wind blew from the ocean, carrying a sharp chill. Mara zipped her jacket, tugging it closed against her ribs as she stepped outside. Her sneakers crunched on the asphalt parking lot, the sound echoing in the empty space. Jonah waited by his car, hands in his pockets, leaning casually against the hood, watching her approach.

His gaze caught her first, quick and assessing. It made her skin prickle, the way he always seemed to see too much, know too much. A part of her wanted to pull away, to hurry past him into her own car. But another part—the part that had spent three weeks watching him, studying him in the controlled chaos of Fulcrum's experiments—kept her feet moving forward.

"Rough night?" his voice rumbled, low and casual. The words didn't match his expression, which was carefully neutral. That mismatch said more than his question did. He wasn't asking if she was tired. He was asking if the session had broken something inside her.
