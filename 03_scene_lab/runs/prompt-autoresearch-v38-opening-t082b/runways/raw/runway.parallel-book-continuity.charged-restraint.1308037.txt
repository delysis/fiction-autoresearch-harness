Chapter Seventeen

One hundred and sixty-one nights since they met.

Mara and Jonah walked out of the Fulcrum building at eleven forty-seven p.m. onto the concrete pad beneath the lights, the evening cool and soft, no one in sight. She felt her body move as if through water, the whole day’s tension settling into slow motion. They started down a path through grass and trees toward the parking lot.

Jonah let her set the pace. He stood next to her without crowding. He let her hold on to the concrete disturbance from the experiment before he did anything else.

Mara was still upset. She had just spent the afternoon in a Fulcrum study on nonverbal communication, paired with the only subject who refused to be nice. Every other participant had been polite and friendly. But this one had said nothing at all and crossed his arms. Mara had tried to open him up, to read him, to make contact. She’d failed. No matter how hard she tried, he stayed stone-faced, unreadable.
