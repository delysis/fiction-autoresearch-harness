
It wasn’t anything the woman herself had done, but an observation about space and motion and responsibility. Not just that everyone knew this glass was finished with, and that someone needed to take it away—which she already knew. But also that the invisible pattern of order-taking was so smooth here that no one could really deny having seen her do it. They were all witnesses to each other’s invisible labor, and the silence that followed it. She noticed how her own mind went along with that expectation of silence, even as she wished for something different—wishing she were able to find another way to be here, without adding to this pattern of who sees and who is unseen, who is acknowledged and who is taken for granted.
 

The brown-haired man finished exchanging names, and shook Mara’s hand in turn. His name was Adrian, and he seemed to notice how long it took her to reply; he kept his grip and waited while she found words to say.
“I’m really glad to be here,” she said, her hand still in his. “Thank you.”

Adrian smiled and released her hand, then turned his gaze to something she wasn’t quite sure he was seeing yet.
“So what did they say at your interview?” He gestured casually toward the back of the room, where a second glass had just been placed on the same table—the brown-haired man’s empty beer bottle, retrieved by someone Mara hadn’t even seen enter, a younger woman in black-and-white stripes. She seemed to be holding back for some reason, hanging behind Adrian’s right shoulder as he took stock of the other two people in the room.

“I really can’t say anything specific,” Mara said slowly, thinking about the question.
Adrian didn’t take this as an answer, or maybe he knew she couldn’t answer; but he looked back at her as if expecting something else.
And that was when Mara recognized what her body was telling her, without her conscious mind realizing it until now: she felt the pressure of his gaze, and her own resistance to it, before she felt anything like the urge to speak.
The three of them were already caught up in this dance, with every gesture a question and every question a kind of request for a gesture—and how they answered those requests would define the shapes their bodies made in this room, and who spoke first and who listened longer.
