The man with the glass walked around to his right to shake Mara’s hand too, and she turned with him—but she felt herself turning. This was going to be one of those things too. The conversation in her ear, the two men walking forward from either side to meet her there. The social expectations that surrounded her, how her face had looked when the door opened, the smile they were waiting for…

And suddenly the social world she found herself in had been pulled out of thin air, a little magical stage that someone else was setting up for her as she arrived and took center stage. The world would vanish behind her if she walked away; a world not made by her or anyone’s conscious will, just assembled by habit or convention, with no choice but to let the expectations gather around her face and body—but it was happening anyway.

She could walk away.
She didn’t want to.
This was the world she was in, right now, in front of these two men. What would it look like from outside?

A voice called her name again: Adrian’s.
“Come in.” He wasn’t at the table this time, but sitting on the couch opposite hers. A tall black-and-white cat sat beside him on a pillow, watching her walk in. Mara noticed the cat before she saw the man. “Sit down,” Adrian said, his face tilted in a half-smile that never quite reached his eyes.

Her palms felt sticky and warm, like after an exercise session or maybe just because she’d been out in the sun. She walked over to one of the two empty seats that faced his and picked up her phone, keeping it in her lap while she sat down.
He started in immediately: “Do you know why you’re here?”

“Not exactly.”
He cocked an eyebrow. “How much of that is just caution? Is there anything specific you don’t understand?”
Mara hesitated for a second, trying to catch her breath and look at him and keep holding the phone in her hands.
