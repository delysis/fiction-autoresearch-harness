  She realized it was the first time she had seen any servant class here, other than the chef and staff; and she tried to imagine how often someone of a different station must have served her food, collected her garbage, and washed her laundry at some point over the years without her full awareness. She didn’t try to make an estimate, knowing that it wouldn’t be accurate. But she decided that she would never underestimate it again.

The young man with the brown beard and the glass of wine turned his attention to Mara, grinning in an almost identical manner as he shook her hand; she shook it back, and realized she must not yet have relaxed her own face, because he was still giving her a wide smile after their names were exchanged. “Hello, I’m Adrian,” he said again.

“You are?” Mara echoed, now feeling like she might be about to smile too. She hoped not.
“That is to say, I’m Adrian. Pleased to meet you.” He let go of her hand and leaned back to give a sweeping gesture to the room in which they now all stood. “Welcome to Fulcrum!”
Mara found herself smiling now. “Thank you.”
Adrian was still standing right in front of her, and it occurred to Mara that it was probably up to her to step out of the way for her own sake at this point. But instead, she just waited for him to do it.

Adrian took the initiative first by taking a half step backward. Mara decided to play it safe and let him have that much distance, which allowed Adrian to continue his welcoming tour. “As you can see, we are the luckiest people in the world. We’re here.” He smiled with his whole face, his teeth and the small crinkles around his eyes, as he gestured again to the room. The smile seemed entirely natural and not at all forced. Mara decided not to like it quite that much, though, because she was fairly certain that a person who could smile like that would have been trained in its use, whether or not they were aware of it.
