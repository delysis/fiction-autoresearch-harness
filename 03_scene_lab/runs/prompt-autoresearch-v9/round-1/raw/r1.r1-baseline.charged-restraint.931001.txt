 He could have walked on; he had his keys in hand. He did not. She had expected the rest of the circle to leave after Adrian had dismissed them. Instead the others had gathered around Jonah’s desk as though it were an open book.

“Mara?” he said.

She came around the lab bench. The air outside was cooler than she’d thought possible in San Francisco, and her skin took its time adjusting to the difference.

“I wanted to see you after the circle,” she said.

“Was I in danger?”

“You were unremarkably safe.”

“Good. Then it was a social hazard.” He opened his hand and let the keys fall into his palm. “If you want to know what I said to Livia about the trace, we can go to the commons.”

She went without answering. Her feet felt bare and unprotected against the stone steps.

“You said you had a hypothesis,” she said when they emerged into the central courtyard. “I didn’t want you to get hurt because of what you said to me.”

Jonah looked away. “That wasn’t why I was upset.”

“No?”

“No.”

Mara took a deep breath. The night was colder than she liked, but there was something in the air that wasn’t city smog or car exhaust. It was clean, almost medicinal, and it made her feel more awake than she had in weeks.

“I think you like being read,” she said.

He stopped and looked at her, his face an impossible combination of curiosity and skepticism. “You think I enjoy it?”

“Not just enjoy. You need it. You’ve built a system around it.”

Jonah’s lips twitched. “I built a system to escape it.”

“Really?” She crossed her arms against the chill. “Because I keep thinking of you in the circle. How you always choose the edge of the room, where nobody can read you without looking.”

“That’s not—” He broke off. His eyes moved from her face to her mouth and back again so quickly that Mara wondered if she imagined it.

“What is it?” she asked.

“Nothing. I just—I want to know something.”

“What?”

“Did you enjoy being read?”

The question caught her off guard. “What?”

“Earlier. When Livia was interpreting you. Did you like it?”

Mara hesitated. “I suppose so. It was… interesting.”

“More interesting than being right?”

“You’re dodging the question.”

He sighed. “I know. I just—”

“No. Finish that sentence.”

“It’s just… I thought I was getting better. About you.”

“About me?”

“Yeah. About not being readable.”

Mara stared at him. “Why would you want to be unreadable?”

“Because it’s safer.” His voice dropped. “People can’t manipulate you if they can’t read you.”

“And you think I’m manipulating you?”

He looked away. “I don’t know.”

“So you’re trying to protect yourself?”

“Maybe.”

Mara took a step closer. “Then you need to know something.”

“What?”

She hesitated, then took his hand in hers.

It was warmer than she expected. She was holding the same hand that had held her wrist during calibration, but now the weight of it rested in her own palm. The skin beneath her fingers was smooth, and the veins that stood out against the paler flesh seemed to tell a story of their own.

“I’m not manipulating you,” she said quietly. “I just want to know you.”

His eyes flickered back to hers, and for a moment there was nothing between them but the faint glow from the nearest lantern.

“I know,” he said. “But… I still think it’s risky.”

Mara squeezed his hand. “I won’t hurt you.”

His pulse jumped under her fingers. “You can’t promise that.”

“No. But I can promise I won’t try.”

He looked away again. “I know.”

She waited, her thumb drawing small circles over his knuckles. The gesture wasn’t conscious, but she didn’t stop it either. The feel of his skin beneath hers was oddly comforting. It anchored her in a way she hadn’t expected.

“Come to my office,” he said at last.

Her breath caught. “Your office?”

“Yeah.”

Mara let go of his hand and took a step back. “What for?”

He looked embarrassed. “Just to talk. I… I want to tell you something.”

“And it can’t wait until tomorrow?”

His eyes narrowed. “No. It can’t.”

She studied him for a long moment, her pulse pounding in her ears. Then she nodded.

He led her back to the main pavilion and up a narrow flight of stairs to his office on the third floor. The space was cluttered with books and papers, but it felt somehow less impersonal than the common areas below. The scent of old leather bound volumes filled the air, along with something sharper that reminded Mara of rain on pavement after hours of dry heat.

Jonah shut the door behind them.

Mara turned to face him. “What do you want to tell me?”

He took a deep breath. “I—I think you’re the most interesting person I’ve ever met.”

She raised an eyebrow. “Is that a compliment?”

He laughed, the sound catching in his throat. “I’m trying to make it one.”

Mara smiled despite herself. “So you want to get to know me better?”

“Yes. If you’ll let me.”

She hesitated. The space between them felt suddenly small, and she was acutely aware of how close he was standing to her. She could see the faint stubble on his jaw, the dark circles beneath his eyes that spoke of sleepless nights.

“Why me?” she asked quietly.

“Why you?” His voice dropped to a whisper. “Because I need to know you. Because I can’t stop thinking about you.”

Mara closed her eyes. She could feel the heat radiating from his body, and the faint scent of sweat and sandalwood that hung in the air like a promise.

“Tell me,” she whispered.

He stepped closer, and Mara braced herself against the desk behind her.

“I need to know if you want this,” he said, his breath warm against her ear. “If you want me.”

She opened her eyes and looked up at him. “I want you.”

The words were barely a whisper, but they hung in the air between them like a challenge.

Jonah moved to press his lips to hers.
