

She approached slowly. His shadow turned as she reached the far side, making her outline visible on the ground between them.

“Do you always work until they stop you?” Mara asked.

“They don’t have to stop me.” Jonah’s voice sounded too easy after the tension of the session. “I have a bed.”

“Does it have a sensor puck?”

“That’s the thing people buy for their pets.”

Mara walked beside him. The grass crunched under their boots.

“Your pulse went up when you started taking apart the new design,” she said.

“That’s a private state.”

“So was wanting to touch me.”

He glanced at her. “Was that private?”

“Yes.”

“How did you know?”

“I learned.”

Jonah held out his arm without interpreting the offer. The sensor band was already off. His veins looked faint through the pale skin at the wrist.

Mara reached for him. He met her halfway.

His hand closed around her wrist. Not hard. Not soft. Just there.

The contact did not stop the conversation.

“You could have kept working,” Mara said.

“I didn’t want to.”

“And the room would have let you go?”

“Maybe.”

“‘Maybe’ isn’t a plan.”

Jonah let out a breath that could have been a laugh or relief. He kept her wrist warm in his hand.

“How long have you known I like you?” he asked.

“Since you called me punctual.”

“That was Adrian.”

Mara’s pulse skipped. “You were watching me.”

“You came in with the church box.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“It’s not nothing.”

Mara caught his hand and put her other one over the back of it, palm down. They walked together for one breath. Two.

The warmth traveled up her arm. Her stomach twisted.

“We could—” Jonah began.

Mara pressed her hand more firmly against his. His pulse jumped.

“Yes,” she said.

He squeezed her wrist, not hard enough to make contact physical but hard enough for the gesture to have weight. She felt the pressure there rather than in her chest.

“And then?” he asked.

Mara caught the air between her teeth. The question could mean so many things.

“Stop,” she said.

“But we—”

“I know.” Mara pulled her hand away. “I just told you.”

“And now you’re taking it back?”

“I’m taking a second.”

Jonah’s pulse raced. She felt it where their hands had been touching. The heat between them had not disappeared.

“I don’t want to stop either,” Mara said.

He let out the breath she’d made him hold. “So what’s the plan?”

“I want to stop before you do.”

Jonah looked at her like there was a joke she’d missed. The pulse under his fingers had settled.

“You could say what you want,” Mara said.

He raised a brow. “I just did.”

“Not ‘you could say it.’ I mean, say it.”

Jonah’s grin sharpened. It was a good grin, dangerous and genuine at once.

“You’re not leaving either,” Mara said.

“Maybe not.”

“Because I’m here.”

He hesitated. “That’s the thing about plans.”

“I didn’t want a plan,” Mara said. “I wanted a yes.”

Jonah’s attention shifted like a dial turning. His hand came up between them, fingers curled but empty.

“Are you asking me?” Mara said.

“Would that be yes?”

Her skin tightened with anticipation. “I’ll tell you if you ask me.”

He let the question hover between them. Mara closed the space without waiting to be told.
