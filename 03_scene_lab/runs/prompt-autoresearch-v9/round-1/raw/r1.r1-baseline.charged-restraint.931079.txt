The moon had not yet risen. Mara could feel the boundary between sidewalk and grass beneath her shoes but the light could not distinguish them. The Fulcrum pavilion behind them rested against the redwoods, quiet and black.

His left hand settled beneath hers. His thumb came up beside hers without brushing her skin. The pulse beneath her palm slowed, steadying against the restraint that remained a restraint only until she chose to meet it.

“I think I know what you were afraid of,” she said. “I want to test whether I’m right.”

The space between his brows relaxed. His other hand fell away. He closed the distance between them not by pressing forward but by opening more space beneath her hand, by letting her make the choice whether to continue the contact.

“Do it,” he said. “If you know what you’re doing.”

The space between them narrowed until her breath moved in his hair and his mouth was an inch from hers.

She could feel him not moving, not touching, not breaking the choice that held them here.

“I’m not testing for the machine,” she said. “I’m testing for myself.”
