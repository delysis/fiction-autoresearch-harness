Example 1: a complete prose movement
“Oh, we’re quite out of our depths with these old hats,” cried Laura
Crich. “Dry up now, Gerald. We’re going to drink toasts. Let us drink
toasts. Toasts—glasses, glasses—now then, toasts! Speech! Speech!”

Birkin, thinking about race or national death, watched his glass being
filled with champagne. The bubbles broke at the rim, the man withdrew,
and feeling a sudden thirst at the sight of the fresh wine, Birkin
drank up his glass. A queer little tension in the room roused him. He
felt a sharp constraint.

“Did I do it by accident, or on purpose?” he asked himself. And he
decided that, according to the vulgar phrase, he had done it
“accidentally on purpose.” He looked round at the hired footman. And
the hired footman came, with a silent step of cold servant-like
disapprobation. Birkin decided that he detested toasts, and footmen,
and assemblies, and mankind altogether, in most of its aspects. Then he
rose to make a speech. But he was somehow disgusted.

FULCRUM

Setting
Contemporary Fulcrum, a private psychotechnology institute studying attention and nonverbal cue-reading. Adult newcomer Mara is the only viewpoint. Livia's accuracy retains ordinary cue-reading explanations; Jonah's restraint costs him status and changes Mara's attention.

Continuity
Adult newcomer Mara is at the contemporary Northern California Fulcrum compound for the first time; she has never visited or interviewed here before. The blond man from the couch is adult Jonah Reed, and Mara now knows his name. Adrian has not yet entered the gathering. Livia has not yet joined the gathering. Mara is the only viewpoint. Unusual cue-reading always retains ordinary sensory and behavioral explanations.

Exact dramatic payment for the audition
Begin directly with a visiting young fellow—not Jonah—leaving a half-full glass on a wall while his important conversation continues. Within the first third, an unnamed older catering worker—not Livia—retrieves it without acknowledgment, and Mara notices who assumes whose labor. In the second half, Adrian turns that exact live observation into a nonverbal calibration game. Livia does not enter or appear in this movement.

Manuscript so far
When Mara walked into the common room at six fifteen, someone was already there. Someone younger, it seemed.
She couldn’t make out the face behind the blond hair. But the body was fine enough, leaning back on the couch with bare legs crossed and arms flung in opposite directions, one palm pressing the padded wall and the other wrist resting on the low table beside him.
She stood stock-still by the doorway for a moment, aware of her breath and of his awareness that she was here. He stirred and tilted his head; a face turned toward her, expressionless.

The two pairs of eyes held each other through an entire exhale.
And then the young man had pushed himself up on the armrests, stretched to his full height, and strolled over to shake her hand.
“Mara?”
He was closer than she would have guessed from where he’d been sitting, broad-shouldered and strong-chinned in a white T-shirt, grinning at her through dark-framed glasses as he reached out for the handshake. She took it.
“Jonah,” he said.
“Hello,” she replied, and then they stood there, still shaking hands, not knowing what to do next.

And that was when a door opened down the hallway behind her, releasing two voices into the room. One female, one male; one asking a question, the other replying; neither loud nor excited, just casual conversation. Jonah released her hand and turned away to look down the hall at them.
When Mara heard the voices and followed his gaze, she saw one young man who wasn’t Jonah, but close in size and shape; he had brown hair and a beard and was carrying a glass.
