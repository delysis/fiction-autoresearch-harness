Jonah walked away from them, toward the center of the room, leaving Mara behind. She heard his casual greeting—not at all an introduction, just an “evening”—and then the voices carried on speaking to each other as Jonah came back toward her again, smiling and saying, “Mara, this is Adrian.”
Mara turned around and found herself standing in front of an older man—older than Jonah; maybe the same age she was, but he’d probably say he wasn’t, if she asked. He was also much taller, and holding the glass Jonah’s companion had been carrying. Mara gave her own “evening” greeting to Adrian as she moved to stand next to Jonah on the couch, with Adrian now facing her across the table.
“What’re you drinking?” she heard Adrian ask.
She opened her mouth to make a comment about her preference for water, but Jonah spoke for her.
“Water,” he said, and it was more of an answer than an explanation.
Adrian smiled at her and nodded. “Water it is,” he said.

His turn to bring back the glass Jonah had left behind.
Mara noticed him do it before he noticed her notice, and then they all noticed each other noticing. She tried to figure out which of them to focus on and where to put her gaze as they all exchanged silent looks at Adrian carrying the glass across the room.

The question Mara asked herself—Did I do it by accident, or on purpose?—was the one she had wanted to ask the group the whole time. But in order to ask it of them, she first needed to ask it of herself.

This was the first movement of the story that included a direct comparison with Lawrence's text, so I went ahead and transcribed a few dozen lines instead of just referring to one sentence out of context. We'll get to know the characters better before we go beyond this section of the original, so it won't be hard to explain the differences when they occur.

I kept the most literal detail from Lawrence's text that I could without disturbing the narrative flow; I also wanted to avoid the use of servants, so that the comparison of the characters in this story wouldn't feel like class-based stereotyping. The same thing goes for Laura: I have Mara in that position for now, but once a more interesting female character comes along, she'll take over as viewpoint and we won't go back.

I did not think the scene needed two footmen; if I'd been stuck using them, I might have made Adrian and Livia the same person, but it seemed clearer to divide them.

The line "He watched his glass being filled with champagne." made me uneasy, because I could not immediately decide how much to change or keep. I didn't like the idea of a character watching any beverage being served into a glass; that would require the glass to be empty, which seemed unlikely and too contrived for this kind of gathering. But if I did nothing but change "glass being filled" to "half-full glass," it was a lot of change, and also not terribly dramatic. So I had Jonah put it down, and it sat for long enough that Adrian felt the need to retrieve it.

And, of course, I didn't like the champagne. I don't like anything effervescent, and so far nobody here seems to, either, so I kept that to water. If we end up using a glass of champagne at all, it will be an empty glass that Mara is surprised to see after the fact. But I didn't want to bring Livia into this early movement, because her role is going to be too prominent to introduce this casually.
