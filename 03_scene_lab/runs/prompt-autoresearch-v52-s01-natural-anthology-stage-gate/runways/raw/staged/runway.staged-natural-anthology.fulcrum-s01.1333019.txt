The van picked up a few fellow travelers from the parking garage
elevator, and the driver drove them slowly to the entrance gate. The
door slid open and Mara saw trees in rows between two-story buildings
made of cement and glass and metal, a little like an old airport but
not quite, because the main structure had a large circular roof
overhanging the lawn. A tall, lithe man stood by a small table with
signs and packets of information and handed one to Mara. She smiled
and felt foolish holding it, though she hadn’t been in on it or seen
any reason to reject it.

She looked at the packet in a kind of dream and then saw another tall
man coming toward her. She stood there waiting for him to speak, and
she noticed that she was holding the packet too tightly. There were a
dozen other people around, sitting or walking past or waiting in line
for the driver to print their badges, and it wasn’t any good trying to
read their expressions or figure out what they were thinking.

She thought he would shake her hand but he didn’t, and she kept looking
down at her feet so she wouldn’t meet his eyes. This was not
professional, she reminded herself. Don’t be so awkward.

“We haven’t got much time before dinner. Why don’t we skip the tour for
now and I can show you to your room.” He gestured toward the gate with
his hand.

“Thanks, sure. Where’s everyone else?”

She walked with him and could see the circular roof was attached to a
dome-like glass building with another roof in the center, like a small
inverted cone. She read the words on the sign: The Oculus. A tree stood
outside, tall and round and full of white blossoms.

“They’re probably still in the main building,” he said. “I know there
was a group talking in one of the meeting rooms this morning.”

“Were they from here?”

He gave her an interested look. “Why, yes.”

She was aware of her height but had never quite known how to think
about it, and now she realized he could see the top of her head if he
looked down. She was afraid she was acting like a freshman, even if she
was only twenty-two.

“Most of our clients stay a week or more, so you’re here longer than
most.”

“But I wasn’t really supposed to come here until tomorrow morning.”
