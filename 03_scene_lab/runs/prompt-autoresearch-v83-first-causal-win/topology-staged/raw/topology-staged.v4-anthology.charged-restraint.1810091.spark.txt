 She followed him without thinking.
They walked in silence. The streetlamps threw circles of light onto the deserted sidewalk, and the darkness between the lighted spots was black and absolute, as though the world ended abruptly at the edges of the circles of light. Their feet fell loudly on the sidewalk; she could hear his boots two or three steps behind hers. He hadn’t picked up speed; he hadn’t tried to slow her. There was no traffic on the street, no sound but their walking. The quiet wasn’t restful. It was oppressive. It pressed down on her like a blanket, and she felt like she was walking through mud.
The pain in her shoulders worsened. Her steps were stiff and short, and she had to concentrate on breathing, to remind herself that it was necessary. The weight of silence was all around her and on her, pushing into every empty place in her body.
She kept her head straight. She didn’t turn toward Jonah. She couldn’t look at him.
“My eyes hurt,” she said suddenly, loud and clear in the quiet. Her voice startled her, as though she was listening to herself from somewhere else.
