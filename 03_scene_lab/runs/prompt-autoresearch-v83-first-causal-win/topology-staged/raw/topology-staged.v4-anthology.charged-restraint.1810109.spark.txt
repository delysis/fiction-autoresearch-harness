
They’d come together. He was driving her to the airport at dawn and he could just as easily have dropped her at her motel before heading home. But there was nothing ordinary about tonight.
She watched him take his first step down from Fulcrum’s portico.
She didn’t move. She wouldn’t let him do this to her, not again. He had every right to head home. He had been through as much as she had, tonight. If not more. But if he walked away now, he could forget everything and go to bed; she’d be stuck with it forever.
He paused at the top of the first flight of concrete steps. He must have felt her stillness behind him. For a split second, he looked over his shoulder; he caught her glance. She swallowed, stared right back. “Come on,” he said softly, and kept walking down the stairs.
She followed him because she couldn’t bear to watch him walk away alone.
