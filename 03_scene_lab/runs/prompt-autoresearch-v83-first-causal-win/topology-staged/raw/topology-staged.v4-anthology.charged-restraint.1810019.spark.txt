 She followed.
They were walking out of town without talking. Her legs hurt, and she was still too hot, but at least she was getting air. The temperature dropped as they moved away from the street lamps. She was almost walking backward; Jonah was so much taller than her that he left the lighted area in seconds. The empty sidewalk was darker ahead, where he walked, but behind her it was bathed in artificial light.
She concentrated on watching his feet, trying to keep pace. She felt like a little girl, following an adult, afraid of losing him. But he wasn’t her father. He was tall and young, and he moved with the deliberate stride of a well-trained athlete. Her father had been fat and short; he could have been a good walker but he never walked. She didn’t remember him walking anywhere, except around the house. He would have looked ridiculous if he’d started walking as she did now.
