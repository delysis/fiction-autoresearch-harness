
He relaxed at once and pushed himself up on the couch, looking her up and down with interest.

“Adrian,” he said, with a hint of an accent that she couldn’t place. He nodded toward the chair by the couch, but she was still wary.
“Thank you,” she said. “How do you like it here so far?”

She thought his shoulders twitched, just slightly, but the only sign of amusement was in his eyes. The rest of his face remained expressionless. He had a small scar on his lower lip.

“It’s fine,” he said. He crossed one leg and leaned forward with one hand on the armrest, so that one side of his body faced her squarely.

She sat down in the chair he had pointed at and crossed one leg as well, careful to keep her eyes on his.

“And where are you from?” she asked.
“South Africa.”

Mara nodded. She waited for more.

He looked past her, out the window.

“I was interested,” Mara said, “to hear that you were in the program in South Africa.”

She saw him exhale slowly. He put his hand down and looked back at her, unsmiling.
“Were you?” he said.
Mara was not sure how much she wanted to say. She nodded.

He waited for more. She shrugged.

They sat there silently. It seemed as if he was thinking about saying something, but then he decided not to. Instead, he turned back to his original position and closed his eyes. She could see that his body had relaxed again.
“You must have been quite young,” she said after a moment.
His body tensed again, but he didn’t open his eyes.

“Not that it matters.”

She took a deep breath and glanced away, out the window. She hadn’t meant to imply that it was strange that he was still so young. He didn’t say anything.

After another long moment, she said, “Are you going to do the same program here?”
He opened his eyes and turned to look at her.
“No.”

She saw something like amusement in his eyes, but his body stayed completely still.
“So what are you here for?” Mara asked.

Adrian smiled, but he was not really answering her.

Mara tried again. “You’re working here, then?”

His shoulders relaxed again.

“Yes.”

There was a pause again, but this time Mara could see that he was thinking about something.

“I’m going to work here,” he said. “For a while.”

“That sounds interesting.” Mara wondered how long was a “while”.

“It’s all very different here than it is at Fulcrum,” he continued. “I think I might like it.”

Mara wondered if that was true; he seemed tense despite himself. But his body relaxed as he spoke, and she realized that he was telling her something important.
“What do you mean by ‘different’?” she asked.
