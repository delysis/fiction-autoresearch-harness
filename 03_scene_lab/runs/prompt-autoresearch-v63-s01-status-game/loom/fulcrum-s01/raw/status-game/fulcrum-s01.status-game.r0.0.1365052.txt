
A faint smile brushed the young man’s lips.
“My name is Jonah,” he replied, his voice soft and smooth as water running over pebbles. “And you’re not in my mind, Mara.”

“I wasn’t asking about that.”
He said nothing more. She waited for a second, then sat down in the chair by the window. It had a good view of the surrounding trees, which were green and lush in the afternoon sun. She thought she saw a deer pass by, but it was gone before she could be sure.
“I don’t think I’ve ever seen you around here,” she said, still watching the woods beyond the window. “Are you new?”
“No,” he replied. “I’ve been here for almost a year now.”

“And what are you doing right now? Or rather, what were you doing?”
He didn’t answer. She looked up, startled, but he was still looking at her. A moment later he spoke, his voice like liquid silk. “I am just reading a book about telepathy,” he said.
“What does it say?” she asked him, curious.
His mouth twisted a little in what seemed to be amusement. “It says that telepathic abilities are developed through meditation,” he replied.

She looked at him again. He had closed his eyes as though to hide something from her; the expression on his face was neutral and unrevealing.
“How do you know about that?” she asked. “Who told you?”
He smiled a little, but did not reply. He was clearly enjoying this conversation very much, she thought, although he seemed not to realize it. She had to admit it was amusing herself; the fact that she couldn’t get any direct information out of him was both intriguing and annoying. It reminded her of trying to talk to her younger brother sometimes, who could be like that too. But somehow this felt different, as though something were missing from his response.
She shook her head at her own thought and looked away, not quite sure what she wanted next. The quiet settled down over her again, and she began to feel restless. She stood up and walked across the room, towards the open door.

It was another minute or two before Adrian’s voice came through the doorway from down the hallway, calling for her. She turned back, ready to answer him, but stopped when she saw someone else had entered the common room behind her.
Adrian wasn’t alone either; he was escorting a woman, probably in her mid-thirties, tall and slender with long wavy hair that fell around her face like a waterfall. Mara knew she was probably pretty, but the other woman didn’t seem aware of anyone else in the room, and Mara was distracted by Adrian himself, whose dark hair was shorter than usual and his eyes were shining in a way she hadn’t seen before. He seemed very pleased to be here.
