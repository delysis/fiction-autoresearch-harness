
His expression changed, not quite enough to smile. But he said, “Adrian.”
“Hello.”
“Hi.” He raised his free hand a fraction of an inch in greeting, keeping it close to his chest.
She walked toward him, feeling slightly awkward but unable to find an alternative. The smell of the building, new and neutral and faintly antiseptic, was suddenly strong in her nostrils; he exhaled slightly as he watched her approach, and she could feel his body heat. The sound of feet and voices from outside became louder; a door opened and someone else came in.

As Mara neared the couch, he said, “I think your bag is open,” in the low but clear voice she’d come to expect in this place.
She looked down at her bag. Sure enough, the zipper wasn’t fully closed. She tugged on it and the bag slid open farther, spilling her belongings out. She crouched down to gather them up, her face still turned toward him.
He shifted his position again. He must know that she could see him watching her, and it didn’t seem as if he cared whether he was watched in return. The sense of a breath, or a hand, behind her head was stronger. Her spine tingled.
She zipped her bag closed, then sat back on her heels and looked at him. He was leaning forward now, watching her face closely.
“Are you the same person?” she asked abruptly.
He frowned and tilted his head to one side. “I think so.”
“Does that ever happen?”
“What?”
“Does your body change? Do you have multiple bodies?”
He smiled. “No,” he said.
“Good,” she said, and stood up. She felt her cheeks reddening, but there was nothing to be done about that. “I’m glad.”
His eyes widened slightly. She turned and headed toward the back of the room, away from his gaze, where she sat on the floor against one of the walls and tried to calm her breathing.
Her phone buzzed.
“Mara,” it said, “welcome to the Fulcrum Institute. You’ve just completed your first exercise in observation and interpretation; we hope that you found it illuminating.”
The room seemed suddenly much bigger. Mara had a brief image of Adrian looking back over his shoulder to watch her as she moved away from him, but he must have noticed that she was still watching him too closely for him to do anything so obvious.
The buzzer vibrated again. She checked the message: “Adrian has been alerted that you are watching him. If you wish to engage further, he may choose to respond.”

She stood up and walked out of the room, feeling lighter than when she entered it. It was getting late; time for dinner.
