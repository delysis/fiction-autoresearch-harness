
The man on the couch didn’t respond.
“Adrian.” He had said it so quietly that she didn’t even notice until a moment later. She blinked and turned toward him again, her eyes focusing. He wasn’t looking at her anymore, but at the blank wall beside the couch. She could see a little of his face in profile, an aquiline nose and a high cheekbone.
“So, Adrian,” she said, “do you want to play a game?”

He turned his head back to look at her, one eyebrow quirked up, and she felt as though a small breeze had moved the air between them. It was as though he were saying, Yes, what did you have in mind?
She had no idea what he would be like, or whether he would respond to anything at all, but she had been trained at Fulcrum to expect the unexpected, and to trust her gut instincts when it came to other people’s behavior.
“I don’t know,” she said after a moment. “Let me think.”
She let his image slip from focus. She felt the movement of his breath shift slightly as he settled into his seat, and she imagined a soft whoosh of air coming from his open mouth. He was still there, waiting.

She blinked and turned toward him again, trying to refocus on his face. But it was hard to find the right angle; her eyes kept slipping away.
“Okay,” she said after a moment. “Let’s go with this.”
And as though from nowhere, there were people around her—an invisible crowd that seemed to materialize in response to her words. She turned to see them, but they were like shadows, ghostly and indistinct.
A few of them shifted toward her, but most seemed to be watching something behind her. She turned to follow their gaze and saw the outline of a figure against the far wall of the room. Adrian.
He was looking back at her. For a moment, she imagined that he was standing there with her, looking up at something in the sky. Then she realized that he was sitting on the couch across the room from where she stood, and she turned to face him.

The crowd dissolved into thin air. She could feel the faint movement of the air where they had been, as though it were disturbed by their passing, but they were gone.
She saw Adrian sitting up very straight, and she sensed his breath catch in his throat. He was tense, poised like a cat about to pounce.
“What happened?” she asked. “Where did they go?”
“I don’t know,” he said. “They were here a second ago.”
“I think you moved too fast,” she told him. “You frightened them away.”
Adrian raised his eyebrows. “How do you know what they saw?”
She laughed. “Because they are me. And I was there.”
He looked at her oddly, like he was trying to decide if she were mad or simply daft.
“Are you sure?”
She laughed again. “Of course I am.”
