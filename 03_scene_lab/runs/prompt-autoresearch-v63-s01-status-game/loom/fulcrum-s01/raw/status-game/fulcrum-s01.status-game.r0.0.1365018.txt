
His head tilted a fraction in her direction. His hair fell across his face, and a breeze moved it. For an instant, he looked like someone she knew: an old friend who was dead now.
Then his face came clear again and he blinked. It wasn’t his voice that answered.
“This is Adrian,” Livia said. She came into the room from the hallway and crossed to stand beside Mara, looking at the young man on the couch.
“Adrian,” she said, “this is Mara. He’s been here a few years already, but I thought he was due for some fresh conversation. Do you mind?”
He shrugged again. The motion looked casual, but it felt to Mara as though it carried some significance.
Mara didn’t say anything, but looked at Livia and smiled. She felt as if they were taking the measure of each other, testing boundaries and possibilities.
It wasn’t clear what kind of test Adrian had intended, but he seemed content enough to let Livia step in. He watched them both with his head tilted and his eyes narrowed.

Mara could hear the faint sound of music coming from outside the house. “What is that?” she asked, pointing to the sound.
“The dining hall,” said Livia. “It’s not far away. We all go there to eat.”

Mara had seen a map when she first arrived and remembered the outline of the compound. She felt a surge of curiosity about the rest of the building and looked around for someone else to talk to. But no one else was in the room, and Adrian still watched her silently from his corner of the couch.

Mara walked over to the window and stood looking out at the yard beyond. There was some sort of metal sculpture on the grass, and several chairs and tables. A few people sat around a fire pit. The sky was clear, but a thin cloud covered most of the moon.
Mara wondered whether she’d be able to find her way back alone. She turned around and saw that Adrian had disappeared from the couch, leaving only a half-full glass sitting on the table beside the spot where he’d been lying.
The glass was almost empty, but there was still about a third of it remaining. The liquid looked dark red, almost purple. It smelled like wine.

“Who’s drinking the wine?” she asked.
Livia laughed. “The wine is for you, dear. Go on ahead. I’ll meet you at the dining hall in a few minutes.”
“But I—” Mara started to object. She stopped herself and turned toward the door. Her mind whirled with thoughts of where Adrian had gone. Had he left? Was he watching her from some hidden spot?
A woman came into the room, wearing the same kind of blue uniform as Livia. She glanced around and saw the wine glass on the table. Without a word, she picked it up and set it on the counter by the door.
