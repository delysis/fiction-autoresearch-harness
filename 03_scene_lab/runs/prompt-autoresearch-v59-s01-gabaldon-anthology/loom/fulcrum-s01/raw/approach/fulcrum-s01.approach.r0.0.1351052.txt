She opened her eyes wider to take in the view of the redwood forest stretching back from the terrace toward the ridge above, and breathed a deep sigh.
“So do you mean I will be your colleague?” she asked him, putting a smile on her face as she spoke. She didn’t know whether it was appropriate or not, and hoped he wouldn’t find it strange; his was the first face she had seen upon arrival in California, and she would rather like it to be a friendly one.
“I … think so,” he said, a little hesitantly. “I suppose it will depend on how things go for you—in terms of whether you complete the program or not.”
She laughed, wondering what would happen to her if she failed to complete the program; what sort of fate awaited those who washed out. But he seemed too much at ease with the idea of being washed out to be alarmed by it.
He turned off the car engine and got out of his seat to help her with her luggage; he smiled warmly when he saw how little she had brought with her—two suitcases, neither more than a few feet long.
“Not many clothes?” he asked as they stood at the door of the entrance, waiting for a staff member to unlock it so that Mara could enter. She shook her head in response.
“I didn’t know what would be expected,” she explained, and watched as a man came out of one of the side doors, opening it wide so that light streamed down upon them. “You’re here for the orientation,” he said when he saw the driver.
“I am,” Mara replied. The man nodded politely as he walked past them toward the entrance, where she stood with her luggage.
