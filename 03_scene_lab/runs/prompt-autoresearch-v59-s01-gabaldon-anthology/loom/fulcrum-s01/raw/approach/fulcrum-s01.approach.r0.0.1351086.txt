 she blundered out, wondering whether she should have come after all. She couldn’t back out now; he had her luggage in the car.
Hal smiled at her again.
“That depends on what you mean.”
“I mean—is it what people say? What they told me, on the phone?”
He turned onto the driveway that led to the reception area, and she looked out the window again. The view of the bay, visible in glimpses through the trees, had taken her breath away.
“Is it strange?” she asked. “What are you, all of you? Are you—is this a cult?”
Hal’s smile didn’t leave his face. He brought the car to a stop just in front of the door, and let his foot rest on the brake, the car idling quietly.
“This is Fulcrum,” he said simply. “Fulcrum is what it is.”
And then she saw something that froze her throat. A young fellow, no more than seventeen or so, walked out from one of the buildings set farther back on the hillside—she could see nothing in detail, but she recognized him, all right—the lean lanky body, the dark hair, and the long white sleeves of his shirt.
“Jesus,” she whispered. She tried to get the door open, but it didn’t move.
Hal was watching her. “Is this a problem, Miss?” he asked. “Can I call someone for you? A friend? You don’t have to come in right now if you’re not ready.”
“No—I mean, no.” She shook her head, hard. “He can’t see me. Please. Please get him out of there, don’t let him see me.”
Hal frowned at her. “He wouldn’t remember you. Not since your accident, you told me that yourself.”
“I’m sure he will.” Her heart pounded, and she couldn’t catch her breath. “He’ll know who I am.” She shook her head again, feeling the blood rush to her head in a wave, her vision going white. “I have to go. Please. Let me out.”
“Is it him you’re afraid of?” Hal said gently. “He’s not allowed off the grounds, Mara. If he were going to hurt you, he’d have done it long since.”
She breathed in and out, once, twice.
“I can’t—can you please let me out, Mr. …?” She couldn’t remember his name again, but he held up one hand to stop her.
He unbuckled his safety belt and reached over, opening the door of the car.
“You’ll be safe here,” he said, as she ducked out, clutching her purse. “Just come in and talk to them. There’ll be a lot of people there—they’ll look after you.”
He let go of her arm, and she stood there, staring down at the black asphalt of the drive in front of her. The sound of Hal’s car, fading as he drove back toward the road, echoed faintly in the warm, empty silence.
She lifted her purse, pulled it tight on her arm, and walked into the building.
