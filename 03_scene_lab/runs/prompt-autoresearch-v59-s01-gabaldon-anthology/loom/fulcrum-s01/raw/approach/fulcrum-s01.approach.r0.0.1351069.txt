
He glanced at her, then away. He’d caught the slight hesitation in her voice—she’d nearly called him “Mr. Hal,” before correcting herself—and she didn’t know if he was offended, amused, or just disinterested.
“Well,” he said after a moment, “I don’t think there’s anything better. There’s other schools, for cue reading and calibrating, of course. But I’ve been to the top ones, and this is the best.”
He paused, as though she was going to say something, but she just nodded.
“And so—” Hal finished his thought with a shrug. “I keep coming back here, when I can. They’ve got some … special programs for people who work in politics. It’s … been interesting, to say the least.” He cleared his throat.
Mara felt that this was an opening of some sort; an invitation to ask more, or even volunteer information. The question hung between them like a spider’s thread, the faintest line against the open sky. Should she follow it? She had a feeling that there was some risk involved.
She wondered why that should be, and wondered why she felt her heart beating faster at the thought. She hadn’t been taking any drugs; that was certain. The two pills of Valium that she had taken the night before were long gone from her system. It had been easy enough to smuggle them; she had brought only her passport and driver’s license with her, but Hal had asked for her credit card, and the pills had been easy to slip in. She had carried a few more in her purse, just in case, but it seemed that there hadn’t been any need.
The Valium had allowed her to sleep last night, but it hadn’t helped at all with the pain. The headache was still with her today, worse than before; she had taken two ibuprofen pills and an aspirin, but they hadn’t made much difference. The pain throbbed in time with her heartbeat, in a rhythm so slow and measured that she felt as though her head must be swollen with water.
The thought of taking another drug now, a narcotic, was unappealing, even dangerous. She wasn’t sure how her body would react; it had been six months since the accident, and the painkillers prescribed after that hadn’t helped much. She was still taking them, but not regularly; only when the pain was very bad, which was most days.
She shook her head to clear it, and looked ahead of her. The car was already slowing, as they approached a large glass building on a hilltop. A line of parked cars was already there, but the space was ample. Hal brought the car to a halt near the end of the parking lot, and took his bag from the back.
“I’ll be back in half an hour,” he told her. “You can come inside, if you like. I’ll see you at the front door.”
Mara got out of the car, a little surprised that he wasn’t taking her. She hadn’t really thought about it before, but she supposed that Hal was one of Fulcrum’s security team; perhaps he would be checking the cars for drugs and weapons, or something.
She waved at him as he drove off, and made her way up a gentle slope toward the building. She stopped in her tracks when she saw the sign:
The Fulcrum Institute for the Study of Human Interaction
Welcome, New Participants
