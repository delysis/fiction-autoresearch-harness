THE FIRST MORNING after moving in, Mara walked downhill along a gravel path through old oaks and pines to the gatehouse, stopping every so often to admire the view of San Francisco Bay below or of the redwoods soaring over a ravine. At the main gate she swiped her badge on a card reader to be buzzed through. She stood there with her purse on her hip, listening to the muted sounds of the water beyond, the occasional human voice from the grounds, and the wind in the pine branches overhead. The sun was warm on the back of her neck.
Then she walked down a paved driveway to her office at the bottom of the hill. It was an ordinary white one-story building, long and narrow; she had come to think of it as a warehouse. When she’d first seen it on the institute’s campus map she assumed it was storage space.
