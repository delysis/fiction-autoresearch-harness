The first thing I noticed about the people on the plane were their bodies. My seatmate—a tall, well-dressed, lean-muscled man who had a hint of the dancer or gymnast about him—glanced over to see if I’d read the little laminated card in the back pocket. I shook my head and glanced away, but he said, “You wouldn’t happen to have a phone charger, would you? Mine is in my checked luggage and my phone’s almost dead.”
“No, sorry,” I said. “I’m in airplane mode, anyway.”
He smiled faintly, as if we both knew something, and picked up the laminated card. As it turned out, we did know something, which was that a few minutes later he was going to turn pale, his skin flushing, and ask me if he could keep his head on my lap, though he hadn’t been ill before
