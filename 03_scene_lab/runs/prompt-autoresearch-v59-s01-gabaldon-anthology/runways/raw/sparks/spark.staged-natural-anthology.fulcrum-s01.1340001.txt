“They can do that?”
The young woman spoke with her mouth full, a sandwich of smoked salmon and avocado held in two hands as she stared up at the wall.
“That,” Mara said, indicating the half-empty glass resting unsupported on the wall, “is the reason you’re here.” She glanced back down at her plate, looking for signs of an unannounced dinner-table companion, but was satisfied to see that her chicken teriyaki had not yet attracted any of the ants.
“It’s like a sort of…telepathy, isn’t it?” The young woman took a cautious bite of the sandwich. “Or do they have to be trained to do it? It can’t be easy.” She nodded at the glass, which was still sitting in midair. “You couldn’t just learn to do it by watching him do it, could you?”
