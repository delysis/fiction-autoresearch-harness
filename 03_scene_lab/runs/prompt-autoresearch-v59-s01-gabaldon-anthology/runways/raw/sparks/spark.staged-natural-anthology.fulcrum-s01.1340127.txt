Mara saw the man’s hand first. It emerged from between the walls of the kitchen alcove like a crustacean from a rock pool, reaching silently toward the glass that she herself had just left on a tall pillar at the edge of the bar.

The hand was not hers, and yet she would have known it if she had been blind. The nail beds and pads of the fingertips were dark; there was a round scar just inside the left wrist, above the long veins that showed blue through her skin.

She took a breath, inhaling the smoky smell of the bar, and watched her own hand with curiosity—for of course it wasn’t hers. Her nails were unpainted, chewed short; her fingertips rough-skinned from grease and dirt in her father’s workshop, never smooth and slightly transparent as these were.
