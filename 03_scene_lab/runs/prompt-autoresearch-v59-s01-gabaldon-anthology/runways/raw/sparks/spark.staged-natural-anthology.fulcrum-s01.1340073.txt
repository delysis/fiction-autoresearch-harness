The building seemed to be waiting for them; the evening’s light lay heavy on the glass walls of the entrance.
“We’re the last ones here,” Mara said aloud, to break her silence.
Her driver was a white man in his late forties, bald, with a lined and thoughtful face. He gave her a friendly smile, which she returned.
“Not much traffic on Route One after rush hour. We got lucky.”
“How long have you been coming out to this place, Mr. …?” She couldn’t remember his name, from her phone call; she was still reeling from the drive in and the view of the buildings, rising in a terrace toward the ridge above them.
“Call me Hal,” he said. “I don’t mind. I’ve been coming here for two years, on and off.”
“And do you … think much of it, sir?”
