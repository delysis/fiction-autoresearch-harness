She didn’t know why they called it the Fulcrum Institute for Experimental Psychotechnologies. The campus had nothing to do with fulcrums and very little to do with experiments in the practical application of cognitive science; that happened at their sister institute down the coast near the capital, the one you needed a security clearance for. The Fulcrum was an outpost, a place to think, a refuge from the world. But not too much of one; you still had to send your email, and phone, and write proposals for new grants, and pay your taxes.

It wasn’t so easy in Northern California, where they tried to make technology invisible. Even when the wireless infrastructure was buried and the power lines were undergrounded, you could see the results: there was just so much glass on the houses in this part of the world. Glass doesn’t hide anything but itself.
