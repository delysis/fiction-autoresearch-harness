She circled back around to the main building, taking in the broad campus. This was not the company headquarters or primary office complex. The only Fulcrum office in Northern California was about an hour south in San Francisco’s Mission Bay, near CalTrain, buses, and light rail. But here in Portola Valley, on land that had once been a retreat center, there were open fields, meadows, and hiking trails that led up into the hills.

Mara imagined herself and her fellow Fulcrum Fellows spending long evenings here, lounging on these chairs, playing frisbee on the grass, or wandering down the paths to look at the setting sun over San Francisco Bay. The air was clean and clear, and the sunset colors so sharp they seemed like something painted on an artist’s palette.

A small glass door at ground level led into the main building, opening onto a large, polished concrete floor. Tables with fruit and charcuterie, and trays with glasses of wine and bottled beer, were scattered here and there around the edges. The space was long and narrow; it had three stories. On the left side was a wall of glass; on the right, a huge wooden staircase angled up at a slant. The floors above it were partially blocked by walls of cedar.

The room was full of chatter and the tinkling of glasses. Many of the people had laptops open. Some talked in groups of two or three, sitting or standing, while others looked out through the glass wall and watched the sunset over the bay.

It was the sort of place that someone would feel overdressed at a normal party, but Mara knew it would be an informal event. She felt comfortable enough in her dark slacks, gray T-shirt, and black Converse sneakers.

The only thing she wasn’t used to was being here alone. She’d tried to get friends of hers to apply to the program along with her, but none had the necessary computer-science background—the same problem Mara had experienced with trying to find friends to come to Def Con with her.

But if she was nervous, it didn’t show in the way she walked up to one of the tables of food and drinks and grabbed an apple and a glass of wine, which she balanced expertly in one hand. She found a wall and leaned against it, watching the people around her.

Several people Mara recognized from the fellowship program passed nearby. They were talking, laughing, but didn’t see her. It was only a matter of time, though.

A young fellow was striding back and forth along the wall beside her, deep in conversation. Mara watched him, noting his movements. He had short dark hair and wore jeans and a black shirt, with a white hoodie zipped over it. One sleeve of the sweatshirt had two stripes running up it—the shirt must have cost a fortune, but the young man didn’t look the least bit pretentious about it.
