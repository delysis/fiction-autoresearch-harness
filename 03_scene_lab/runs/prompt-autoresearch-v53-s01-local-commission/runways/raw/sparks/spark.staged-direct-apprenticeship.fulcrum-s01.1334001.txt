Fulcrum was a redwood-and-glass compound overlooking the Pacific, a half-hour
drive north of San Francisco. Its founder and chief, Adrian, had said, "I wanted
people who spent all their time in this place to have access to nature's grandeur
and feel a sense of scale." And so a single-lane blacktop curved up from
Highway 1 through a scrubby grove of eucalyptus trees toward Fulcrum's glass-fronted
administration building and the rest of its complex. A handful of white-walled,
flat-roofed offices stretched north along the bluff overlooking a lonely rocky shore,
all of them built on stilts to accommodate the rocky rise that separated their
terraces from the ground below.

To Mara this was no idyllic retreat but a place of business: one hundred and fifty
employees, all in technology infrastructure roles
