Mara looked back over her shoulder into the kitchen, where a woman wearing a white chef’s hat stood at the sink with a tray of plastic cups on the counter next to her.
She held a glass in one hand as she twisted the tap in the other and filled it up. “Sorry to take so long,” she said again without looking up.
The blond man looked from Mara back toward her voice and spoke aloud, without changing his expression. “Thank you,” he said. “I’ll be back.”

Then he strode across the room, with a casual confidence that Mara had seen many times before in many different men—never in women, not quite like that, but men had often walked around Mara that way, as if they knew exactly where to go and how to get there. And now it was Jonah Reed doing it, and it made Mara smile, even though her brain felt like it had been dunked in cold water.

“Not at all,” he said when he reached the kitchen.

And then Mara looked away, because she didn’t want him to know she was still watching. But when he put his hand up against the doorway, she glanced back without thinking and caught another glimpse of the woman standing there in front of the sink. Her dark hair framed her face as if it had been sculpted from clay; and when she spoke again, Mara realized she didn’t sound anything like a typical caterer at all—but who was to say that this wasn’t a caterer after all?
