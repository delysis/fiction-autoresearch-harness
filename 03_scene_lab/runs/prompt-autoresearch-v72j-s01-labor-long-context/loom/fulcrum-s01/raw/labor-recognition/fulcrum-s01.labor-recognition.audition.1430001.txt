Gerald’s companion came around the corner now, a young woman with her hair tied back; she was wearing an orange dress, carrying an empty wine glass.
“And this is Adrian,” Gerald finished, “and I don’t have to tell you who this is.”

The woman in orange smiled and came forward to shake hands. “Livia,” she said.

As Livia turned away and raised her hand in greeting toward Mara, the woman in brown—who was still nameless, still unknown—glanced briefly back over her shoulder with a slightly puzzled look, as though she’d only now registered how many people there were in here, all of them not her. And then she turned fully to face the gathering and moved closer to the low table at the end of the couch.

In the empty wine glass.
A drop of liquid.

An unnamed older catering worker—not Livia—retrieved the abandoned glass without acknowledgment while its owner's conversation continued. Through observable action, Mara noticed who assumed whose labor. End on that concrete recognition before anyone turned it into a lesson.
