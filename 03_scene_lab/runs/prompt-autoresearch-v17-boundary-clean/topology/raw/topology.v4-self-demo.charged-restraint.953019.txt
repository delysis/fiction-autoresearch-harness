

They walked in silence, the city’s distant hum enveloping them. The cool air brushed her skin, and she let herself breathe deeply. She didn’t know why she took his hand. Maybe it was the simplicity of it, the quiet reassurance of touch without words. His palm was rough where it met hers, a subtle reminder that he too carried scars.

Their walk took them past a café with its terrace bathed in soft light. He paused, letting her lead, and she found herself pulling him toward the open door. The smell of fresh coffee and pastries filled the air as they entered, the noise of the street fading behind them. Mara chose a small table tucked into the corner, the lamplight casting a warm glow over the worn wooden surface.

She set her hands on the table, feeling the rough grain of the wood beneath her palms. Across from her, Jonah sat down, his hands resting just outside his lap. His eyes stayed on hers, and she saw the unspoken understanding flicker there. For a brief moment, they were just two people, tired and bruised by their own histories.

The barista approached with two glasses of water and two menus. Mara didn’t look at the menu. She couldn’t remember the last time she’d eaten properly or slept uninterrupted. Her hunger was a gnawing ache, a constant reminder of how easily life slipped from her grasp. But now, in this quiet café, she allowed herself to pause.

Jonah reached across the table, his fingertips brushing her hand. “Are you hungry?” His voice was softer than she’d expected, almost gentle.

She met his gaze, trying to read his intention. He didn’t interpret; he simply offered a choice. “Yes,” she said finally, her voice low and quiet.

He smiled slightly, a small curve at the corners of his mouth. It wasn’t an easy smile—nothing about their connection was—but it was there. A shared vulnerability, perhaps.

Mara took a slow sip of water, feeling the cool liquid soothe her throat. Her hands trembled ever so slightly, but she forced herself to steady them. Jonah didn’t try to take over, didn’t press or push. He waited, and in that waiting, there was a kind of freedom.

Their conversation was brief and simple. No grand gestures, no declarations of devotion. Just two people trying to make sense of the world around them, finding solace in each other’s presence. Mara ordered a salad, something light but filling. Jonah chose a bowl of soup.

When the food arrived, she felt his eyes on her as she took the first bite. The taste was bitter and sharp, yet strangely comforting. It wasn’t a craving satisfied; it was hunger acknowledged. She looked up at him, catching his gaze, and saw a flicker of something in his eyes—something that might have been hope.

He leaned closer then, the space between them narrowing. She didn’t pull back, didn’t resist. There was no pretense here, no façade to maintain. Just two people, vulnerable and raw. His hand slid across the table toward hers, his fingers grazing hers before intertwining. She let him hold on, let herself feel the steady warmth of his touch.

Their conversation drifted toward nothing specific, small words and quiet pauses. Each bite of her salad felt like a small victory, a reclamation of something simple. And then, as if guided by some unspoken accord, she tilted her head, just slightly, toward him.
