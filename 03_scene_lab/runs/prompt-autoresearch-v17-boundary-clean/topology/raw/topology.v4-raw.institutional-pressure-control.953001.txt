
She took the cup, a mug that was much too wide and shallow, and sipped at the thick brew, which was some variety of broth but mostly salt and fat. Jonah cleared his throat, and Mara turned toward him, looking up from the mug, where her focus had been blurred for a long moment.
"There's something you need to take care of?" he asked. "Your brother?"
Mara sighed. "Yeah." She'd been debating how much to tell anyone; she knew the answer from Livia but wasn't convinced that she was making a rational decision about her brother's welfare when she was this close to collapse.
"There's a few hours left today before he goes back to school, and I was planning to leave before then," Jonah said, and Mara could tell he was trying not to let it sound like an ultimatum. "I'd rather talk about this now instead of waiting. What's up with your brother?"
Livia had moved just a bit closer to her when she noticed Mara's breath starting to catch; she took the mug back while Mara drew in a few deep, shuddering breaths, the last ones coming in harder as the tears started. "I'll be back," Livia murmured, and hurried away with the mug in her hands.
Jonah came over and put one hand on each of Mara's shoulders; he wasn't trying to hold her up. She let go of her own shoulders a moment later and put her hands up, wrapping her arms around his neck and shoulders. She drew her face up against his neck. Her cheek was hot, and her eyes were tired and painful, and she was shaking again, but she hadn't fallen apart yet; the tears were in her throat, trying to choke her but not yet coming out.
Jonah tilted his head and pressed his own cheek to her temple; he didn't say anything. It was easier when she didn't have to listen to anything other than him, even if he wasn't saying anything.
Mara took one more deep breath, trying to get it in slow enough so she wouldn't hyperventilate. She felt the tears spill out then. Her face was warm and sticky with tears and snot and sweat. She had no idea how much time had passed, but when Livia came back, she still didn't feel like she needed a tissue. Livia stood beside them instead of kneeling down; she waited until Mara wiped her face against Jonah's shoulder, then she held out her hands. Mara didn't want to let go of him yet, and she didn't want to look at either of them.
Livia moved Mara's hands, gently but with a solid grasp, and let her own hands take their places. She rubbed her thumbs slowly back and forth over Mara's shoulder blades while Mara breathed into the back of Jonah's neck and cried quietly into his hair. Livia said nothing. Mara wanted to pull back so she could say something, but she didn't know what words she needed yet. She waited.
After a time, Jonah cleared his throat again. "There's a sofa," he said, and guided her back to it. She let him help her sit down, still not letting go of him until he took one hand and pulled it gently down. Mara stayed curled forward and rested her head on the sofa's arm; Jonah stood over her, rubbing the backs of his fingers slowly over her hair.
