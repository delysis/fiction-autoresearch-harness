
Livia's expression was serious. "I know that we need you at the conference," she said. "And I know that you've pushed yourself before now and recovered. But right now, your body is saying no. I think we ought to listen."
Mara held out her hands; Jonah took them and pulled her to standing. Her head spun a little but she didn't stagger; the dizziness wasn't so bad as it felt.
"I'll be fine," she said again. "Look, I can walk. I'm going to be useless to you for a few hours no matter what, so how about you leave me alone with my tea for now?"
"Do you need anything else?" Livia asked, her hand resting briefly on Mara's arm. "A sandwich?"
Mara shook her head and moved away from Jonah, needing the space to breathe without feeling him close and present. He moved back without comment. She went to the desk and reached for the phone.
"No, thanks." Her voice was steadier than she felt; the quiet anger in Livia's gaze sharpened into a brief flare of fury that vanished at once.
"Mara."
"We really do have to get there," Mara said, trying not to snap. "I need to call an Uber."
Livia turned her back on Mara abruptly and went out of the room without another word. Jonah stood where he'd been before, his hands loose at his sides as he looked at her.
"I need to make the call," she said again, not liking the sound in her voice.
He stepped closer, moving into the narrow gap between Mara and the desk. She felt him leaning on her without any actual touch.
"How bad is it?" he asked.
She shrugged one shoulder. "Bad enough." She paused, and then added, "I'm sorry."
Jonah leaned in another few inches and put his hands on either side of the desk. He bent close and put his mouth against Mara's ear. "It's not your fault," he whispered. "You know that."
Mara closed her eyes and let out a ragged breath. She wanted to stay like this, feeling his body against hers and hearing his voice. She didn't want to move or think or do anything except this. But she couldn't afford not to go to the conference.
"I know." She opened her eyes again. "But that doesn't mean it won't hurt Livia."
Jonah sighed and stepped back, his hands dropping to his sides. He looked at Mara for a long moment before he spoke again.
"She's your friend," he said. "Not your mother."
