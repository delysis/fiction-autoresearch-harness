The words were simple, but they cut through the exhaustion. She wanted to nod, to reassure him as he had her, but the knot in her throat betrayed the truth. He squeezed her hand slightly, a signal.

Mara drew a shaky breath and met his eyes. "Thank you," she said, the two words carrying everything left unspoken. Jonah's brow softened as he inclined his head, the brief acknowledgment more than enough.

Their walk was slow, punctuated by the sounds of the city—distant horns, distant laughter from a nearby park. She let her thoughts drift to simpler times when companionship was easy.

Jonah stopped at an intersection, waiting for a pedestrian signal. Mara stood beside him, her gaze drifting toward the skyline. She was hyper-aware of the space between them and the deliberate way he never intruded into it. He respected every boundary, every unspoken word—a constant reminder that, with him, she was safe.

When the walk signal changed, they crossed the street, the cold breeze tugging at their clothes. Jonah held her hand a little tighter, not possessive, just grounding. The light from a streetlamp caught the glint of his eyes, making them appear lighter in the artificial glow.

A pause hung between them as they approached her apartment building. She expected him to release her hand here, to step back. Instead, he leaned closer, his lips brushing her temple in a fleeting touch.
"You did good today," he whispered, barely touching her ear with his breath.

The words should have soothed her. Instead, they ignited something she hadn't prepared for—an unexpected flare of need. She tilted her head slightly, seeking more contact. He hesitated for a heartbeat, just long enough to let her know it was her choice before meeting her halfway.

Their kiss was tentative, tentative, like a question rather than a demand. Her fingers tightened around his hand, the connection grounding her as he pressed closer, his lips warm and firm against hers. It was soft but charged, every touch amplifying the desire that had simmered beneath their encounters.

She tasted him then—Jonah, always composed, always in control, and suddenly, intensely close. His free hand found the back of her neck, the touch feather-light as though he were gauging her reaction. The proximity was electric; she wanted more, but he pulled back first.

He leaned away slightly, his thumb brushing over her lower lip. "Mara," he said, his voice low. "We don't have to do this right now."

She swallowed hard, her chest tight with both relief and something more. He wasn't wrong. She hadn't been ready for this—not entirely. But the hunger was undeniable.

"I know," she said softly, her breath unsteady. "But... I want to."

His gaze dropped to her mouth before returning to her eyes. He didn't move forward again, didn't press the issue. Instead, he took a small step back, slipping his hand from hers. "Not yet," he whispered.

She felt a flash of disappointment, but it was quickly replaced by something else—trust. He understood her better than anyone, and right now, she wasn't ready for whatever might come next. She nodded once, slowly, accepting the decision they had made together.
