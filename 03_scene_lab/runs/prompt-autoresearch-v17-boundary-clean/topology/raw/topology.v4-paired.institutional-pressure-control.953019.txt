
Livia's lips curved in response. She reached past Mara to the counter and set down the mug she'd been holding. Mara had thought it might be a cup of tea—it was steaming, and smelled good, like citrus—but no, it was a plain clear glass, thick enough to hold heat.
"Actually, no," Livia said. Her voice was calm; her eyes focused on Mara's. She picked up a small white plastic bottle from the counter and shook it lightly; there was a dry rattling sound. "Your blood sugar dropped dangerously low during the alignment." She glanced at Jonah without moving her head. "Not that we couldn't get you out of it, but you're still running low. Drink this now, or I'll have to hook you up to a drip."
Mara took a breath; the steam rose to meet it and made her feel briefly dizzy. She'd learned years ago not to ask what someone like Livia meant by "dangerous." The woman had enough expertise to make any question seem stupid, and asking one was liable to result in an answer that Mara would regret.
"This is sugar water," she said instead. Her head felt too heavy for her neck; the words were almost too much effort. She wanted to reach out and rest her hand against Livia's shoulder. "A lot of sugar."
Livia's mouth twitched. "Yes," she said. She set the plastic bottle on the counter and gestured toward Mara. "Drink."
Mara sipped the glass gingerly. The sweetness was like a jolt. She drank slowly; she could feel the liquid hitting her bloodstream, one sharp stab at a time. She wasn't going to drink all of it in one go; Livia might not let her leave without it, but there had to be a middle ground.
"Are you staying here?" Mara asked Livia, as she drank.
Livia nodded. "I'll take the next few hours," she said. She gestured toward the door. "The rest of us will see you later."
"Okay." Mara finished the glass. She set it down on the counter. "Thanks."
She looked up at Jonah, who still hadn't moved. His expression was impossible to read; he didn't smile, but he didn't frown either. He nodded slightly in acknowledgment.
"I'll be in touch," he said.
He stepped forward and put his hand on Mara's shoulder for a moment, squeezing gently. His thumb traced the hollow of her collarbone before he let go.
"Take care of yourself," he said, his voice low.
Mara nodded. "I will." She hesitated for a second; she wasn't sure how much to say, or what would be safe. "Thanks for coming," she said.
He looked at her for a moment. "I'll always come when you call." He stepped back into the hallway. "Take care."
"I will," she said again, this time to his retreating form. "You too."
She turned back to Livia, who was still watching her, a slight smile on her face.
"He likes you," Mara said quietly. "That's nice."
Livia gave a soft laugh and picked up the plastic bottle of sugar water from the counter. "You think I should date him?" she asked, her tone light but her eyes serious.
Mara smiled weakly in response. "I think you should do what you want," she said, "but maybe don't ask him out right now."
