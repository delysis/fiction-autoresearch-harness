

"You don't have to," he murmured. But her hand closed over his, and they walked in silence, the city's murmur enveloping them.

After a few blocks, their pace slowed. She glanced up at him, and there was no judgment in his eyes—just understanding, shared weight. Without a word, she pulled him against her. His breath hitched, warm air brushing her cheek, and then he kissed her.

It started slow, soft, like testing the waters. But soon, her lips moved with purpose, molding to his, exploring with a quiet hunger that had been building all day. The city's noise seemed to fade into a distant hum.

She tasted his lips, warm and slightly bitter from coffee. His hands came up, cupping her face, drawing her closer. His thumb traced a line along her jaw, slow and deliberate, and a shiver raced through her.

He kissed her neck next, the shell of her ear. The touch was feather-light at first, but it pressed deeper as she leaned in, her back arching slightly. Her nails dug into his shoulders, grounding herself.

When they parted, eyes still closed, he pulled away. His hands stayed on her face, thumbs brushing away tears she hadn't realized had formed.

“I’ll see you tomorrow.” His voice was hoarse, but steady. “Rest.”
She nodded, barely able to speak.

As the distance between them grew, she turned and walked away, still feeling the press of his lips against hers.

Her phone vibrated in her pocket:

Fulcrum: "We will resume at 10 a.m. Prepare to continue your progress."
The message held no hint of the emotions she carried. She tucked her phone back and kept walking, her mind already drifting to the possibilities she hadn’t allowed herself to consider before.
