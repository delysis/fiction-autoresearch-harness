
Simon closed the novel with a snap, his eyes locked on hers. “Don't you dare. I've had enough separation for one lifetime, and I have no intention of reading alone tonight.”
The words hung in the air, carrying a weight beyond their surface meaning. Esther stepped closer, her fingers lightly touching his hand where it rested on the blanket.
“I'm glad,” she whispered. “Because I have some things to talk about too.”
Simon nodded, his gaze steady. “I think we both do.”
They fell silent for a moment, each absorbing the gravity of that admission. Simon's expression softened, and he gently guided Esther to sit beside him on the bed.
“Let's start with something light,” he suggested, his tone careful but still teasing. “How about you tell me what you've missed the most about living alone?”
Esther couldn't suppress a smile at his attempt to break the tension. “Oh, let's see,” she mused, feigning deep thought. “The solitude, maybe? The peace and quiet, free from all your annoying interruptions?”
Simon chuckled. “Annoying interruptions like me barging in on you whenever I pleased?”
“Exactly.” Esther playfully nudged him. “Though to be fair, you did manage to make those interruptions quite entertaining.”
The atmosphere lightened, allowing them both to breathe a little easier. Esther leaned back against the pillows, her eyes on Simon as she spoke.
“I'm glad you're home,” she said sincerely. “I was really worried about you.”
Simon's expression softened, and he reached for her hand, entwining their fingers. “I'm glad to be back, too,” he admitted, his voice tinged with vulnerability. “I missed you more than I ever imagined possible.”
They both knew the conversation wouldn't stay on safer ground for long, but for now, the shared laughter and teasing felt like a lifeline, pulling them together after the storm of their separation.
