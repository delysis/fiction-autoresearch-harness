 "You're going to make sure I get the time."
Livia nodded, satisfied. "We are." She handed Mara a warm drink. Mara took a sip and enjoyed the taste.
"It's tea," she told Mara. "With honey, if you don't like the taste of plain tea. And caffeine, but not enough to hurt. I thought you could use it. Are you hungry?"
Mara gave a small nod. "I'm always hungry after an alignment."
"Yes. I have something for you." Livia pointed at Mara's head, and Mara's hair vanished into a tight black bun, tied off with a band that would never slip.
"Thank you." Mara smiled up at her. She was tempted to lean on Livia; she knew Livia would offer but didn't want to draw on that strength yet. Maybe later, after the caffeine had time to work and Mara could feel a little stronger.
Livia and Jonah turned and started for the exit. Mara followed, looking around as she went, still feeling a little shaky from the alignment. When they were close enough, she leaned over and kissed Livia's cheek, then turned to do the same to Jonah.
"Thanks again," she said.
"You know we love doing this," Livia said, turning to grin at her.
Jonah gave her a nod but didn't say anything. Mara took his hand and squeezed it, then looked up at him. "I'm sure you have other things to do."
Jonah's gaze moved from Mara to Livia, who had already stopped and was reaching into a pocket on her belt. She pulled out a small case. "We've been waiting for you," she told Mara. "Jonah wanted to see what kind of a job I could do on your hair."
Mara raised an eyebrow at Jonah. He shrugged and nodded toward Livia, who had opened the case and was pulling out something.
It was a long black cord. Mara recognized it; she'd used one once to tie her hair into a knot for an alignment session. She didn't remember when it had been, but the memory came back to her now. It wasn't a good memory, but that didn't matter. What mattered was that Livia was holding it out to her, waiting for her to take it.
Mara looked down at her hands, at Jonah's hand in hers, and then back to Livia. She had to think fast—but then she remembered that she'd already thought this through.
"Do you know how to do it?" she asked. "I mean, if you haven't tied a knot in your own hair."
Livia grinned. "Yes. You told me how to do it once."
Mara looked over at Jonah again, then back at Livia. She was tempted to say something else, to argue that it wasn't necessary for her to tie her own hair, but she knew what would happen if she did. Livia was a professional, and a damn good one. Mara had seen her work; she knew what kind of job she could do.
"I don't have anything better to do than learn something new," Jonah said, taking the cord from Livia and handing it over to Mara.
She nodded, then turned back to Livia and looked up into her eyes. "Thank you," she said. "I appreciate it."
"You're welcome," Livia said, and turned back toward the door. "And if you do get hungry, I brought you some fruit and cheese."
