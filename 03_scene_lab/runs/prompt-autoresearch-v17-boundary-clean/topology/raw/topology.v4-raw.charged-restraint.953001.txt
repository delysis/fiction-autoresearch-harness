

“You’re always offering your hand,” she murmured, turning back to look at him. “Do I seem so unsteady?”
“I am unsteady,” he replied honestly, his gaze steady. “I want you to know that. If I want something, Mara, I'll ask for it.”

She nodded and stepped beside him. Their bodies were close but not touching. A breeze carried the scent of jasmine from a distant garden. She glanced sideways at him. The evening cast his profile in a soft glow, revealing the depth of his gaze. Her breath hitched.

“Thank you,” she said, her voice quiet. “For giving me that.”

He tilted his head, curious.
“What, precisely?” he asked.

A flicker of uncertainty crossed her expression, but she pushed through it. She reached out, cupping his jaw with the back of her hand. It was firm and warm to the touch, a reassuring presence. Heat rose to her cheeks, yet she stayed rooted in place, fingers still tracing the contours of his face. She could feel the tension in his posture ease under her touch, a subtle sign that her gesture was welcome.

“Choice,” she said simply. “Even if it feels small.”

He nodded slowly, eyes reflecting both curiosity and an unexpected vulnerability. Then, before her mind could overanalyze the moment, he leaned in closer. Their mouths met, soft and exploratory. She didn’t know how it started—a mutual desire or a sudden act of instinct. She only knew that it was undeniable now.

Their lips moved against each other, brushing and deepening. He tasted like coffee and something sharp, yet strangely comforting. Mara could feel the intensity building within her—the kind that threatened to spill over, to make her demand more. But she resisted the urge to give in. Instead, she pulled back just a fraction, lips parted, breathing shallowly as they looked at each other.

“This is not what you wanted,” she said, her voice barely above a whisper.

“But it is what I want now,” he replied, his eyes reflecting honesty and something more.

“I thought you were a man of principle.”

His lips curled slightly.
“You underestimate how easily my principles can be compromised. And you’ve managed to do that already.”

They smiled at each other in the fading light. The city continued around them, unaware of the intimacy unfolding between them. He lowered his hand and traced the curve of her cheekbone, his fingers lingering just long enough for her to feel his intention before pulling away again.

“You don’t have to wait,” she said quietly.

He hesitated for a moment, then shook his head slightly.
“I think I will,” he replied, his tone firm but tinged with something else—maybe anticipation or respect.

She didn’t press him further; there was no need. Instead, she reached up and kissed his cheek softly before stepping away completely.

“We can continue this another time,” she murmured.

As they walked together down the empty street, their hands brushed lightly against each other—a small but meaningful gesture in contrast to the rest of their journey together. And as they continued onward into the night, they knew that this moment had set something new in motion between them—a quiet understanding born out of vulnerability and trust.

And maybe, just maybe, it wasn’t just another experiment gone wrong but a chance at something real.
