

Mara knew Jonah would offer to get her a taxi. She did not want one. She had learned the city. She had learned it was safe here if she stayed alert. She could walk from Fulcrum to her apartment in an hour and ten minutes. The walk would let her think. She could not think during work. She needed a way to recover, even though recovery might make things worse.

Mara did not need Jonah to stay with her, either. She did not want him to. Her first impulse was to say good night and go. But if he asked where she would go, she did not know how to answer. If he offered to get her a taxi or walk her home, she would say no. He might press his questions until she had no choice but to lie.

She thought about what a lie would cost her. It was impossible to keep one secret from him, even when she had tried. She did not know how she could hide the others. The lies and the secrets made everything worse. He had asked if she ever trusted him. Her answer was no.

Jonah stopped walking and looked back. “Are you all right?” he asked.

Mara saw the crease between his eyes again and wanted to touch it. She wanted to hold his hand, too. He stood looking at her like a man who wanted her to take something, as if there were something inside him that belonged to her, but he did not know how to give it to her.

She said, “I’m sorry I was cold tonight. I shouldn’t have let it get to me.”

“It wasn’t your fault,” he said.

She said, “We have an agreement that I’ll tell you if something goes wrong at Fulcrum. But it’s also important to me to keep my word, and not make false reports. If we’re going to have an agreement, it has to be mutual.”

He took a step closer to her, so that their shoulders touched. She looked at him. Her hair covered her face. The scent of his shirt covered her. She breathed the same air he did. This was not a safe distance from him. But she felt safe, here, with him.

She put out her hand. He took it. “If you don’t trust me enough to ask for help, how can I trust you?” he said.

“You can trust me if you let me go.”

He did not let go of her hand. “You would have gotten hurt at Fulcrum.”

She knew that was a true thing. She said it to herself.

He said, “I don’t know what I would do if anything happened to you.”

“If something happened to me, you would get over it. You would recover. It would be all right.” She wanted to believe her own words. She had believed them before, but not in this moment. Not with him touching her. Not looking at him and seeing the crease between his eyes.

He said, “I can’t let you go. Not like this. You won’t be safe if you go.”

“If you stay, I won’t be safe, either. It’s better if I go, alone.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because you might change your mind.”
