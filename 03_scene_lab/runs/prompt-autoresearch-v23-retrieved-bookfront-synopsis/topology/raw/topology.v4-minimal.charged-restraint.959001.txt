 She wished it was still Christmas; it was more real than this.

“Did you want to walk home alone?”

The voice came out of the dark next to her without warning, and Mara jumped. He had spoken softly but deliberately. The words did not echo in her head with his usual cadence. They sounded like something that had come to him as a surprise, even as he was saying it. She felt her face turning bright red under her cap. She looked for the lights on the street, but they were far back, and there were too many shadows, too many angles on the sidewalk. She tried to think of an answer that would not give him the power to say the things she didn’t want to hear. She knew he could smell her hesitation on the air between them. “I didn’t think about it,” she said finally. “I usually go with you.”

His eyes were very close to her as if they had moved closer at the sound of her voice. Mara turned away from him again, trying to find a little space between them in the dark. She heard him sigh softly. He said something that she couldn’t understand. The sound was like an engine in a dark garage. He was very near again, almost breathing on her face as if he had moved closer while she wasn’t looking. His hand touched her arm gently, but his touch was very warm for someone who had been out in the cold for so long. “You’re a lot more attractive than I am,” she said to fill in the empty space between them. “I know I’m not supposed to say that,” she added quickly. “But you can hear it anyway.”

“I don’t think you’re trying to make me feel bad,” he said after a moment. His voice was low and steady, and she wondered how he had heard her at all. “You just want to say something, and I didn’t hear it.” He moved closer, and she felt a sudden pressure on the inside of her thigh from his fingertips. Mara tried to move away, but the sidewalk was slippery where they had been walking for so long. She felt his warmth against her side, and her heart was beating so fast she thought she would pass out. He turned his face away from her, and she could feel him breathing on her ear. She shivered in the cold air and wished she could feel a breeze. She took a few slow breaths. His hand was still touching her leg, and she couldn’t hear anything else because of the pounding in her chest.

He stopped moving for a second. “I’m not making this up,” he said quietly. “You don’t have to hide from me.” His voice was low and very serious, but she felt him moving closer again. “I’m not going to let you walk home alone.” His hand had moved to her waist, and it was very warm even though he had been out in the cold for a long time. Mara felt as if she might throw up on the sidewalk, but she took another slow breath and tried to think about something else. His touch was like an electric shock, but it seemed to go away again when she moved her hand across her body. She tried to move away again, but he followed her easily, as if they were moving together naturally.

The sidewalk was a lot less slippery than before, and Mara managed to move her feet along the cold stones. He didn’t seem to be watching where he was going at all; he just kept walking as if she would go with him without thinking.
