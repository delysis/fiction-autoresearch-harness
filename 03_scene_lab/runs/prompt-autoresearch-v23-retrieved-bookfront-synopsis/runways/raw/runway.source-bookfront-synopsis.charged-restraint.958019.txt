Fulcrum’s late session had ended an hour ago. The streets of the district were nearly deserted by one in the morning. A single bicycle passed by without a headlight. The light rain had stopped an hour ago, and the sidewalks were wet and slick. Mara walked beside Jonah. They had not spoken in thirty minutes. She looked toward him and saw a little crease between his eyes. She had seen it before, at the end of a long day. She turned toward the dark windows. The light of a streetlight caught a raindrop on her jacket and made her think of a diamond. She touched her chest, where she kept her silver cross.

She slowed down as they walked in silence. When Jonah turned toward her, she found she wanted to look at his face, so she kept looking, and found a moment later that she had crossed the street. Jonah moved with her. He put out his hand. He did not look at her. She paused. He waited.

His palm was open. His fingers were spread but not stiff. Mara had seen Jonah before with a hand held out like this, waiting. She had not known it was for her. She did not know what it meant, but she took his hand. It was warm and not too soft, not too callused, and she felt his thumb brush her fingers. He waited. When he said nothing, Mara noticed the space between them. She looked up into his eyes, then closer.
