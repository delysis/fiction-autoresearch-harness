The day had ended, but their work did not. It would not. Their work would continue on without them. The research fellow program at the Fulcrum Institute did not expect them to live, or even to age.

That day had ended, at least, and Mara Voss knew it by the way that the door swung shut behind her, locking her out of her office for the night. She would not be allowed to return. All the offices at the Fulcrum Institute locked down every night for maintenance. This meant that not even the cleaning staff had access to the offices. Everything from the temperature to the lighting to the airflow was strictly controlled. Everything, that is, except the door. A simple latch would hold a door in the open position, so long as someone stood guard.

This was a tactic that had saved her a dozen times, at least, in the six years she had spent at Fulcrum. They let you do your work until it stopped working for them, and then they locked you out. You had to finish before you were locked out. That meant sometimes using the bathroom or drinking water was a privilege you had to trade for finishing that one critical breakthrough before they locked you out and did not let you back in.

Mara’s life, her work, was worth that sacrifice, and more.
