
Mara felt it. She could feel him feeling it. He seemed to be wondering if she was going to say anything more.
She tried. “I—uh, didn’t know you were going to be here?”
“I usually show up early,” he answered, shifting the hand. His smile faded into uncertainty, as though he’d been caught doing something he was too polite to admit. “What do you have there?”
She looked down at her hands and saw that she still hadn’t put down the bag with her books and laptop inside.
“Nothing,” she told him, placing it on a nearby chair and coming back to the present. “Nothing but my stuff.”
His glasses didn’t hide the curiosity in his eyes. “What kind of stuff? You didn’t say anything about that.”
“Just stuff,” she said. “I have stuff in the bag. That’s what you carry your stuff in. It’s a bag for stuff.”

He turned it over a couple of times, checking out its features.
“Huh. I haven’t seen this model before. Where’d you get it?”
Mara felt the heat creep up her neck as she tried not to look him in the eye again, but he didn’t seem to notice anything different about her. The man was too busy inspecting her bag.
“I dunno,” she said. “I think it was a gift or something.” She sighed deeply. “Did I bring something from another time period? Is there a sticker on it with my name that says ‘MARA’ on it?”
