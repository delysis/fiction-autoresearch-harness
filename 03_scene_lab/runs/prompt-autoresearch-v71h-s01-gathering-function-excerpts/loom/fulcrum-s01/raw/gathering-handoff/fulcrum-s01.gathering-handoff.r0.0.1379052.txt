

She looked over his shoulder. The room had an odd, intimate feel that caught her attention: a fireplace with an unlit woodpile against one wall, the couches and low tables, all of it in subdued tones, warm light, the heavy wood walls and deep pile rugs. A library’s worth of books lined one wall; the bookshelves were interrupted only by one painting of a red-gold sunset over black waves. She turned back to face Jonah.
“Sorry. This place is just…”
He smiled.
“It’s fine. Everyone gets the same thing.” He nodded toward the window beside her and held up his wrist. “It’s six seventeen, so everyone should be trickling in soon.”
His watch was silver, elegant; she would not have thought it to be his, but he wore it as if it had always been his.

He released her hand.
“Make yourself at home,” he suggested. “I’m going to go grab a drink.” And he walked out through the doors into the hallway.
She let the hand fall to her side and turned to survey the room. The fire was inviting; she took one of the unlit logs and rested it on the hearth. It smelled of pine.
She took in the room once more, scanning for a place to sit that seemed both hospitable and strategic. The couches were too close to the windows. The armchair near the fireplace had a view of the hallway outside but didn’t look like a good place to start a conversation—it faced the wall of books, away from the doorway. She settled on the chair facing the window, which was set near the door and offered a full view of the entrance.

The clock read six twenty-two.
