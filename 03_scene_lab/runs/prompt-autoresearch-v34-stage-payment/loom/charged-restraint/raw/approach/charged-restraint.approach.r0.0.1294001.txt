

They stepped forward together onto a path through the grass. Jonah led with his left leg while their hands swung close between them.

“I can’t get that man off my mind.” Mara slowed and looked at him. “You said you could help me talk through sessions. That was true?”

Jonah kept his hand but stepped back to wait until Mara looked ready. “It’s true.”

She heard a quiet calm she hadn’t been ready to ask for.

“When could you be ready to start? Would you meet at your place?”

Mara moved in front of him. Jonah touched her upper arm with his other hand then dropped it. “I can see you starting tomorrow, same time.”

His eyes held her gaze without looking away. Mara wanted to look back and keep holding.

“Is there somewhere we can walk through what I’m feeling right now?” She pulled his hand down from her waist to the small of her back.

Jonah turned so that they faced the pickup with their hands touching. “Do you want to see a beach, or the harbor?” His voice didn’t push. Mara chose by the rhythm in her chest.

They stood on top of a short hill with the ocean just to their right. Jonah was three inches taller and stayed beside her as she looked down at the shore.

He cleared his throat to get her attention but did not move. Mara let him guide the walk.

“Is that it?” Her voice caught in a sharp note. “A little distance from the other session participants?”

Jonah’s eyes said he knew exactly what Mara was feeling. She wanted a moment with someone who didn’t expect her to say a lot.

He took off his glasses, held them at a right angle to his eyes, and handed them toward Mara. She caught them before they could fall and stood waiting.

Jonah nodded and set both hands on top of hers as if they were two palms flat on a desk.

Mara took the glasses and examined the metal frame for signs of sweat or moisture. “Can I keep these?”

“If you want.” His hand moved to her back, right side.

Mara closed her eyes, put on the glasses, and turned away to face the dark.

“Don’t touch me,” Jonah said in a calm, patient voice. “Just stand and breathe while I see if there’s somewhere more comfortable to sit.”

Mara stood still and listened until she felt sure Jonah was farther away. She pushed his hand away, opened her eyes, and walked slowly down the path toward a small stone bench in the grass.

The night breeze smelled like wet moss and mud with a trace of something else—salt water, or maybe the ocean itself. It was warm and humid but not too hot for her thin coat. Mara set her back to the sea and waited for Jonah to find his own way down to meet her.

She stood still and watched as Jonah came into view again from the other direction. He walked slowly around to stand on her right side, just behind her shoulder. His left arm moved around so that the hand cupped over her wrist while the fingers brushed the pulse point.

“We need somewhere closer than the bench.” Jonah leaned toward her ear and whispered in his usual flat monotone. “Do you have anywhere we could go together tonight where we could both talk? Somewhere private?”

Mara looked up from her watch to see that he was looking at her intently and waiting. “I think there’s a coffee shop near here.” She pulled on Jonah’s hand to turn them toward the building. “Maybe they’ll let us use their bathroom?”
