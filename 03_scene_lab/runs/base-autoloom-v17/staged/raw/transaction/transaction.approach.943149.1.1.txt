

Simon withdrew and said, "The kind who still has two hands to trace his wife's bruises."

Esther took his right hand and pressed it to her side. Beneath the silk of her shirt, the bruises spread like a map sketched with chalk. His fingers felt the raised ridges of fresh skin, the tender hollows where the ribs curved inward.

"Show me yours," she said.

He lifted her shirt first. One finger traced the outline of the bruise, naming it as a point on their shared landscape.

"You can't read a map by candlelight," he said.

"Neither can you hide one in it."

The room grew darker around them. The weight of the words thickened the air between their bodies.

Simon undid the top three buttons of his shirt. His own bruises lay on the left side, mirroring her injuries almost perfectly.

"Your name would not have saved all of them," he said.

Esther took his hand and placed it on her skin again, where the new bruise met the older scar.

"We are past naming," she said. "Trace the pattern."

He began to draw the map with both hands, moving over the bruises until the pattern emerged. Two circles connected by a line. The numbers of their lost. Their bodies as evidence.

She moved her hand to his, mirroring the gesture.

"What do you see?" he asked.

"A choice."

Esther turned to him suddenly, putting both hands against his chest.

"Ask," she said.

Simon lifted her shirt slowly, watching her face. "May I see your injuries?"

She nodded. "You may trace the patterns."

His hand found the fresh bruises first. The fingers pressed softly, drawing a map over her skin. "This was before we left the surface?"

"Yes."

"And this?" He touched the long scar across her thigh.

"Longer than our marriage."

Esther mirrored his movements now, one hand on his ribs. She traced the curve of bone, the tender spots where flesh had given way.

"You carry the pattern too," she said. Her voice caught in the moment of truth, as if speaking this aloud would break them.

Simon held her gaze. "We did not protect everyone we could have."

"No," she whispered. "But we survived."

The words hung between them like an admission of guilt. They had chosen to live when others might not. The pattern on their bodies was the price paid for that choice.

She lowered her hand from his chest.

"Now show me the seal."

Simon took her palm and led it to the broken seal on the drive.

"This was my failure," he said. "I opened it in haste, believing I could find the way out alone."

"And in that haste," Esther said, "you left others behind."

He nodded. Her fingers traced the broken edges of the seal.

"We should have worked together," he admitted. "Your name would not have been enough. We needed something more."

Her touch moved over the drive to his hand, holding it now with intent. "Then let us find what comes after names."

Simon turned her hand toward him.

"This is where we begin," he said.

Her breath caught when his fingers touched her skin again.

He traced the map on her side slowly, naming each point with care. "The choice," he said softly.

"The price we paid."

Esther moved his hand to her thighs and met his gaze.

"We have made maps before," she said. "Now we must make the path together."
