When a short-haired woman in a black skirt came out into the room behind them, she smiled at both men at once, introduced herself as Livia, shook both hands, then walked across the room toward Mara, carrying the empty plastic cup, still smiling.

“Hi, I’m Mara,” she said as the woman got closer and finally stepped up to shake her hand. The smile broadened when she spoke the name.

“Great to meet you.” The older woman was taller than Mara by a few inches, in a skirt, a plain white top, and flat shoes, carrying the plastic cup. She handed the cup over to Mara before shaking hands with her; and Mara took the cup, finding it heavier than she would have thought from the lightness of its material, as if there were some liquid left at the bottom and clinging to the sides; which, indeed, there was.

She looked into the cup without understanding the reason for that weight. Then she saw the tiny bit of dark liquid sloshing around against the logo when she moved her arm. She could have tipped it and found out what it tasted like, but there was a woman smiling at her with no expression of explanation in her face; so she set the cup on the table again where it had stood before, still not understanding.

And as she turned to look back up at the woman’s face, she saw that the smile was gone. So was the woman, standing away from the door now, talking with Jonah Reed.
