 She didn't. She looked from the open case to his face and said only: "That's not how you usually do it."

"I—" He stopped. She knew about the backup. Knew he hadn't run the code on the encrypted copy to break it, even when all they could hear in that dark apartment was the other man breathing, and they'd already decided to leave.

"Don't tell me," Esther said. "Not here."

She stepped behind him, drew a breath into the back of his neck. Simon waited until her fingers came up beneath his shirt. Only then did he let his hands move to the small curve of her waist.

"Not now either?" she asked quietly.

"Soon. Not yet."

The drive case lay open in his peripheral vision, its seal unbroken, the false story of itself shining. Esther tightened her grip just enough to pull him off balance. He shifted to keep the table between them. She put his body back into its rightful place against hers. Her weight grounded him. For that moment, he could forget how much the decision had cost her.

He began to move. The weight remained constant. Simon felt her hand trace the shape of his chest beneath his shirt: each breath a separate measurement. Her thumb moved in a line he knew from old training sessions: from clavicle to breastbone and back again, back and forth, slow enough to teach the new lines left by the last surgery.

"The old scars are almost gone," she murmured against his neck.

He nodded. Didn't say anything. Esther moved her thumb slowly over the long clean line his surgeon had left, then lifted one side of his shirt to show him the scar he couldn't see in a mirror. The same incision: breastbone, ribcage, healed but not fully faded.

Simon caught her fingers with his and guided them back over both lines. The touch felt solid beneath his skin where he could see the new line and the old one, side by side.

"When they changed the insurance," she said quietly, "did you—"

"No." Simon pulled her closer. The old scar moved under his fingers now, too, like the dark road map of a river drawn over its straight route.

"The night we left that apartment—"

"I didn't do that either."

"But you should have." Esther's voice was lower than her hands. Simon felt the shape of the words against his back where the old scar ended.

He closed his hand around her wrist and held it steady.

"Simon," Esther said, her weight shifting with each syllable, "I gave it up so you could leave. If you'd had it that night—"

"No." He pressed their joined hands to his chest. "Not then."

"If we'd stayed, the data was mine. You told me that."

"I did. We weren't there."

"But you were, Simon. And now they can't ever find that room again. We did this together."

"We didn't—"

"Simon, don't—"

"No." He drew his hand back to her mouth before she finished the sentence. She tasted of mint and silence. Simon traced the old scar with his other hand while he kissed her. His lips pressed lightly against hers and harder until the pressure broke both their voices and held them steady for one breath at a time.

The drive case lay open on the table behind them, its seal broken. Esther lifted her head when he pulled away, her face hidden in shadow.

"They can't find it," he said slowly. "They can never find it."

Simon felt her nod against his chest. When he reached out to move a strand of hair from her face, she caught his hand and guided it back to her throat. He traced the dark wet spot there with his finger while Esther spoke in a whisper he could feel as much as hear.

"That tree. The one with the broken branch. That's what brought us to the right town."

"I know."

"You can't give that up."

"No," Simon said quietly. "Not that."

The drive case opened in the other room. Esther stepped through the beam of light to reach him and pressed her body against his. He caught her by the shoulders and guided her hand back to where the old scar met his heart.

"Tomorrow," she said, her breath hot on his skin, "we'll tell them together."

He nodded. The light flickered again. Esther turned on her heel, opened the door, and stepped through to the next room, her hand on his chest, moving him into the next moment with the same motion that had started this one. He felt his breath catch as the darkness surrounded them both, and held it until the light returned and with it the certainty of being together again, their bodies still joined, their hands on one another's scars.
