 He could smell the repair shop's faint disinfectant in the metal.

"Your other hand is shaking," she said instead, moving to the mirror. "Have you been awake all night?"

"Most of it."

Esther caught the drive's reflection as she straightened her shirt. Her expression tightened around the familiar question Simon had seen her ask herself a thousand times since the accident. He put his own hand in his pocket. The new grip mechanism responded too quickly, like a young soldier with too much training and not enough judgment.

"You're planning to surprise me with it," she said.

"No."

"You want me to find it myself."

"Yes."

Esther turned to look at him. Her hair fell across her shoulder. Simon remembered when he could hold one long strand between his fingers and twist it tight until—

He breathed past the memory.

"What's wrong?" she asked.

Simon reached for a word that wouldn't reopen last night's conversation about the new prototype. "I thought I had done everything."

The answer lay in the mirror where she could see the truth he couldn't meet. Her eyes narrowed with the precise anger he knew so well. "You always did. You still do."

Simon opened his prosthetic hand to show her the missing drive. It clattered across the metal table. Esther closed her fist on the seal, then put the drive in her shirt pocket like a secret she might not tell him.

He watched her straighten the loose thread running through the cloth. She wore his old flannel shirt to sleep sometimes. The frayed cuffs spoke of hundreds of small nights with one hand buried in the folds.

"How's your hearing?" she asked suddenly.

The question caught him off-guard. He raised a brow. "Last time I checked, it still worked."

Esther looked past him to the far wall. "You keep saying that," she said softly.

Simon took a step toward her. "What do you mean?"

"You always say 'last time I checked'. Like something's already broken."

A flush rose in his cheeks as he realized what she meant. The words he'd used with the drive, with the new prototype, with everything that might fall apart if they tried to push too far again—

He could feel her studying his face in the mirror. "Is there something else you haven't told me?" she asked.

"No," he said quickly. "It's just how I talk now. Being careful doesn't mean I'm afraid of you."

The answer came out harsher than he intended. He saw the flinch in her reflection before she turned away to run water over her hands.

Simon watched the steady stream flow into the sink, the cool sound filling the empty space between them like something real. He wanted to touch her, to reach out and trace the line of her shoulder with his fingers. But the memory of his own trembling hand stopped him.

After a moment, she turned off the water and faced him. "Maybe we should do something else tonight," she said quietly.

The words hung between them like a warning. Something he had never heard in their years together. A line she had never crossed before.

"What do you mean?" he asked slowly.

She shook her head, pulling the wet strands of hair away from her face. "I just... I don't want things to break tonight, Simon."

He felt the weight of her words settle heavily in his chest. He wanted to tell her everything would be fine. That the new prototype was ready. That they could try again and nothing would break this time.

But the drive lay silent in its broken seal, and his own hand still trembled when he tried to use it.

They stood in silence for a long time, the water from her hands drying on the counter. Simon wanted to reach out to her, to show her that he wasn't afraid. That he would protect her from whatever damage might come.

But he couldn't find the words to convince her. Not when she was looking at him like he was already breaking something precious just by standing there.

Finally, Esther broke the silence. "Let's go out tonight. To that new restaurant downtown."

The suggestion was so ordinary it almost broke him. Not because it was wrong, but because it was everything he had tried to protect. Every normal evening spent together. Every quiet moment where they could just be Simon and Esther, husband and wife, without the weight of what they had lost between them.

He nodded slowly. "That sounds good."

She smiled at him then, a small private smile that said more than any words could. "I'll put on some clothes. Meet you downstairs in five?"

He watched her disappear down the hall. The silence stretched out behind him like a long empty room they both knew far too well by now.

Five minutes later Simon stood outside the building watching for her to come down. He could feel the night air cooling his face. The streetlamps cast long shadows across the sidewalk.

She came down the stairs wearing his old jacket over her shoulders. Her hair still damp from the shower, sticking to her neck. When she saw him waiting for him she broke into a smile.

"Let's go home," he said softly as she reached his side.

And they did just that.
