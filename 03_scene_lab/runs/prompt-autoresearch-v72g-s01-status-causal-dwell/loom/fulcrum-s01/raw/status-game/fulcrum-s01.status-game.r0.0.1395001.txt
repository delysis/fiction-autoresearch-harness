
The man was a thin, middle-aged figure with a gentle face framed by dark hair. The woman was younger, but still older than Mara, slender and blond, walking ahead of her partner with an air of anticipation on her features. She wore a long dress that looked almost like pajamas but wasn’t quite so relaxed—more elegant than casual.

Mara wasn’t sure how to react when the couple walked up to her. The man smiled at her as they passed; he didn’t look like anyone she would have expected here, and that made it harder to gauge his intentions. The woman nodded at her in greeting, then moved past without stopping or saying anything else.
“Well hello there, Miss Vale!” a voice called from the doorway behind Mara. She turned around to see a tall figure standing there; he raised his hand in greeting as he came forward to stand beside Jonah, who was still looking toward them with a curious expression on his face. “We were just talking about you!”
This man was at least sixty—older even than Adrian Voss—and he wore a thin mustache under a pair of spectacles that sat on top of an aquiline nose. His eyes were dark and expressive, almost startlingly so; they looked as though they saw everything around them, and might judge it too. His hair was gray but styled carefully to resemble what it once had been: curly black waves parted down the middle. He wore a light suit with a tie, both of which seemed slightly old-fashioned for such an informal setting—but his posture seemed relaxed enough that he didn’t seem uncomfortable in them.

“I’m James,” the man said as he reached out for her hand again. “And this is my colleague, Miss Sloane.” He gestured toward the woman standing beside him; she nodded politely toward Mara but otherwise remained silent while James continued to speak: “We work here together at Fulcrum—you must have heard of us from Adrian?”
“Yes,” Mara answered, unsure whether she should be introducing herself back or just letting James finish speaking first; he seemed eager enough to fill any gaps in conversation with words of his own. “It’s nice to meet you.”
James smiled brightly at her again before turning back toward Jonah, who had stayed in place behind the man rather than joining them at the doorway. “Well I see we’ve been joined by some younger company tonight! How about that? You know what we oughta do? We should get ourselves a bottle of champagne!”

Jonah took a step forward and said something softly; Mara caught the end of it—he sounded like he was asking after her drink preference. She blinked at him for a second before turning back toward James, who had already begun to head out into the hall again. He stopped in his tracks when she opened her mouth and said, “I’ll have whatever you’re having.”
He grinned broadly as he continued down the hallway without looking back; Jonah followed after him at once while the woman remained behind with Mara.

“What should I get you to drink?” Livia said. She spoke with a lightness in her voice that suggested she was teasing Mara about something, but the woman didn’t say anything else; she simply turned on her heel and walked out into the hall again before Mara could answer.
Jonah followed suit almost immediately after that—he looked back at Mara briefly, giving her just enough time to wonder whether he wanted her to come with him or not, but then continued on toward the bar without waiting for an answer from either of them. Mara was left alone in front of the large window, watching him walk away and wondering if this meant she should leave too.

But Adrian had told her that everyone would be gathered together here tonight; it didn’t seem likely to him that anyone else was going out tonight when they all needed some rest. Still—she couldn’t shake off this feeling of being left behind, as though there were something important going on somewhere else and she had missed it.
She turned back toward the room again, trying not to think about anything except for what she should do next.
