The liquid was warm.

Livia pushed the carafe toward Mara.

“Finish it,” she said. “Don’t let the data settle into dust.”

Mara poured. The ceramic thumped against the counter twice as the stream ended.

“That was a very deliberate mistake,” Jonah said.

Livia gave him an approving look.

Mara watched the amber liquid rise over the dark base of her cup.

“You’re new,” Livia said. “There are things you don’t know. I’ll teach you.”

“Like what?” Mara asked.

“The world is deeper than your calibration skills. More complex. More interesting.”

“That sounds like a sales pitch.”

“The world isn’t a commodity. It’s a discovery.”

“Who are you selling it to?”

Livia lifted her right arm as if to wave and stopped at Mara’s startled movement.

“Just the people who are already here,” she said. “No one new.”

Mara took the tea between both hands.

“That,” Livia said, “was the wrong answer.”

She watched Mara’s throat move as she swallowed.

“The world,” Mara said, “is a gift. Not a discovery.”

Livia leaned back, looking pleased. “You didn’t mean that.”

“I just learned it.”

The room held still. The last trace moved on one screen. Mara’s heart beat under her ribs.

Livia picked up the tea carafe and tapped it against the table.

“You have work to do,” she said.

Mara nodded.

Livia set the carafe down and pushed it toward Jonah.

“You want something,” Mara said.

“Don’t be greedy,” Livia said. “Think of it as payment. You did a great job. This is a thank-you.”

Mara lifted the tea and took one deep sip.

Livia leaned closer. “Don’t mistake me for kind. I’m making you an offer because you’re useful. That’s the only currency here.”

The tea had grown bitter. Mara set it down and watched Jonah’s fingers dance across the keyboard.

“You’re not going anywhere,” Livia said. “Not until you understand.”

Mara looked at her.

Livia’s smile was sharp.

“The world,” she said, “is mine.”
