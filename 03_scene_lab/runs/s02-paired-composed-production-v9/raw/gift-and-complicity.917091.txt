Her pulse slowed.

“Fine,” Livia said. “I’ll make the offer. Stay in the lab after dinner. Adrian left the key on the door.”

Mara set the cup down. “Did you put a tag in it?”

“I offered you the key.”

“You offered me the room.”

“I offered you a conversation. Without an audience.”

The tea had been good and the cup was solid under her fingers. “That’s not a condition of employment.”

“No. But you should know what you’re up against.”

She meant it. The sincerity showed in her eyes. Mara felt the gesture’s weight: not as an invitation, but as a promise to see.

“I’ll stay until nine,” Mara said. “Then I have to go to bed.”

Livia looked at Jonah. “He’s not invited.”

“That makes me feel like a test condition,” Jonah said.

“You are one.”

“You think I don’t know this already.”

“You think you know.”

Jonah’s face changed. He let the mask settle back in place before Mara could name it.

“Nine,” Livia repeated. “Don’t let me find out.”

Mara watched her go.

Jonah turned back to the screen. “We’re still waiting for the next set to run.”

Mara could not remember what she had been doing. The session was realigned, the data density lowered, the interface responsive but not insistent. Jonah had closed his own files and opened the remaining shared window, where Mara could see only what the puck and sensors had recorded. The room had been divided, the stakes raised, and neither change had come from her.

“If you need to go, go,” she said.

Jonah looked up from his screen.

“I’m not a test condition,” she said.

He smiled. Not the way that Livia would. It was kinder.

“I’m leaving with you,” he said. “I’m not allowed to stay.”

He pushed away from the table before he could see her face. His chair scraped the floor. Mara watched him pack his laptop with a stillness that bordered on grace.

The next run was ready. The sequence remained open.
