Her thumb warmed on its rim. The tea itself tasted of honey and earth and the fact that someone had given it to her. She could hold it or she could set it down. Setting it down would not make it go away.

“You want to fail spectacularly,” Livia said.

Mara did not deny it. She had felt this way before, as a child trying to decide whether the church had found her real. As an adolescent making fun of the church on the internet, imagining it had found her there. As an adult turning her phone to the wall and going to her knees every night. The church had found her at least three times in the last hour. If the church had known. The church had known. She could not stop imagining.

Livia watched the thought arrive. “You want to be forgiven.”

“I want to stop waiting for forgiveness.” Mara let her hand fall away from the cup.

“That’s harder.”

“I’m capable of hard.”

Livia looked at the cup. “I have a gift for you. A proof.”

“What’s it worth?” Mara asked.

“Your time.”

“I already agreed to four nights.”

“You didn’t know the time.”

Mara watched the clock change by another minute.

“The gate’s unlocked,” Jonah said.

Mara stood before she realized she had been waiting for an excuse to do so. Her bag shifted on the chair. The weight was not lighter or heavier than she had expected. She took it.

Livia said nothing. She kept watching.

Mara lifted the bag in one hand.

Jonah finished a line of code and pushed back his chair.

Mara set the bag down.

“Stay,” Livia said.

Mara waited.

Livia clicked a button on the shared interface and opened the raw telemetry. The monitors lit up with red marks and blue peaks. Livia left the feed open where Mara could see it.

“Don’t let me catch you,” Livia said.

Mara’s heart jumped. The sensors were still packed.

She saw Livia’s face for the first time as a person rather than a status threat. The eyes narrowed again, but with less edge this time. The pulse rate appeared on the shared monitor as a blue trace. Livia’s hand came up to rest near her own throat. The next peak appeared on the blue trace exactly when Mara saw it.

“I’m capable,” Livia said. “This time is yours.”

The room let her have it.

CHAPTER THREE

The raw telemetry was not a proof, but it was evidence. Mara spent three hours and twenty-one minutes examining the night’s raw data. She found that Livia’s trace corresponded to Mara’s own every time Livia had been close enough to watch.

Mara found that Jonah’s trace never matched Livia’s.

Mara found that the night’s summary report had omitted a short section where she and Jonah had shared the same state without knowing it.

She found that Adrian had edited the summary before releasing it.

She found that Jonah had looked away immediately after seeing this.

Mara found that she kept looking.

She found that she kept finding things.
