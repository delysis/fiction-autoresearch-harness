

Mara’s eyes went directly to Livia Sloane, who was standing just outside the door, waiting for the man to finish what he was saying before they came inside. He was a tall and stately man, wearing a dark suit and carrying an iPad under one arm; Livia wore the same sort of loose white linen dress she’d worn before. Her gaze was fixed on Mara, level and friendly.

Then Adrian Voss spoke; Mara’s attention switched to him. He had a low voice that resonated deeply in his chest and seemed to hang in the air for a while, a sound that made it easy for a listener to imagine he meant exactly what he said. It was the same voice from the phone, from the interview in the hotel room.
“—we have a short reading session together,” he was saying as Livia’s companion pulled open the door and let them both through. “But only as long as it needs to be, of course; we’re not trying to force you or rush you.”

Adrian’s eyes met Mara’s, dark in his olive skin and still so focused on her that they seemed to almost glow, as though she were the only person he saw. He lifted both hands away from his sides to gesture toward Jonah, who had stayed very still since the moment Mara first set her eyes on him.
“You’ve met Jonah already, I see. But you don’t know Livia yet.” Adrian smiled warmly at her and turned his attention back to Mara. “She works here—she’s one of our most skilled observers.”

Mara looked from Adrian to Livia, who nodded. She felt very aware of Jonah at her side, though he still didn’t say a word—and she realized she hadn’t heard him introduce himself to Adrian or Livia earlier. His presence was solid in the room as they talked together, but his words were somehow nonexistent.

“She’s not the only one we have,” Adrian said, “but I want you to meet her right now.” He gestured once more toward Livia, who nodded again and smiled. She was standing behind him, just far enough away that it felt like she might come forward and hug him any moment—but then Adrian spoke again.
“Livia is a very experienced reader,” he said. “She’ll be able to tell us all how we’re doing together.”

He gestured toward the two of them, at their standing in this small room with Jonah beside them. They were so close now that Mara could feel the heat radiating off his skin through her shirt, almost as though it was touching him. He seemed to be holding something in his right hand, but she couldn’t tell what it was.
Adrian turned back toward Livia Sloane. “Thank you so much for coming,” he said to her, and held out one arm to embrace her.
Livia reached back with both arms and kissed him on either cheek before releasing her grip. “You’re welcome,” she whispered in his ear as they separated, then stepped away from him a few inches. “I’m happy to be here.”

“And so am I,” Adrian said, grinning at Mara again. “But now let’s see what happens when we put you two together!” He gestured once more between them—at Mara, at Livia Sloane. “It will be very interesting indeed.”

And he was already moving away toward the door again, turning to face them. “I’m sorry,” he said as he stepped out into the hallway again. “I know it’s a long drive from San Francisco; I hope we’ll be able to find someplace nearby where you can stop for a bit before you get back.”
Then he paused, as though he’d forgotten something, and turned his head to look at Mara. “And do you have anything else in mind that you’d like us to bring over?”

“A little later,” Mara said immediately, not knowing where the words came from but feeling very sure of them. She smiled at him, but Livia had already answered first.
“No,” she said in a quiet voice that was as clear as ever. “No, I think we’re okay.”
Mara didn’t feel like responding to that right now, so she just nodded instead.
