Jonah nodded at them both in quick succession, still keeping himself turned toward Mara’s shoulder and smiling. He said something about wanting to wash up, then walked back to the couch to get a glass and some kind of bottled water. He opened it, took a sip, then walked over to a cluster of tall potted plants against the wall—

“Hi,” Livia greeted them with a wave. Mara barely registered her expression.
Then again: “Hi, Mara.”
The white-haired woman looked younger than Mara had expected, her face small and heart-shaped. She was wearing a long gray dress that pooled around her feet like water, with silver beads sewn in patterns across her shoulder and throat. Her eyes were brown, the same shade as her hair, but framed by so many thick lashes that they seemed to gleam with an inner light.

“Hi,” Mara said. She offered no wave in return.
The woman seemed not to notice this slight. She walked closer and took Mara’s hand again, her gaze now fixed on it. Mara didn’t know what to do with the contact; there was no polite way to withdraw, but it felt like the gesture should have some purpose. The woman’s eyes flicked up at Adrian briefly, who was standing a few feet away watching them both—

She heard the soft thud of Jonah’s glass being set back on the table, and felt it vibrate through her bones as Livia released her hand at last. Mara watched her go back to Adrian; they continued speaking quietly as though she wasn’t there.
She stood very still for a moment more, taking in their poses and movements—the way Livia leaned forward while she talked to Adrian, the slight tilt of her head toward his shoulder—

The group had come together around a low coffee table at the far end of the room; Mara sat down on one side while Adrian took up position facing her across from Livia, who settled herself on another couch. Jonah came in at that moment, dressed in jeans and a pale blue shirt with sleeves rolled to his elbows—

“You look good,” he said as he handed Mara a glass full of red wine.
She smiled at him and took it, placing the glass carefully on the table before turning her attention back to Adrian and Livia, who were watching her expectantly. Livia had unbuttoned the top half of her dress, leaving bare the smooth curves of her shoulders beneath a cloud of fine white hair, which she’d taken care to brush to one side as though it were an elegant shawl. She seemed very much in control—

“So,” said Adrian, “I understand that you don’t want to talk about yourself today.”
His voice was deeper than she’d expected, but rich with warmth and humor; his face still held no expression beyond a faint smile. He was looking straight at her now—or rather, she realized, he seemed to be looking right through her eyes into the depths of her being. The intensity of it made her feel naked somehow—

“Yeah,” Mara said after a moment. “That’s about it.” She couldn’t think of any other way to put it, no other way to make herself clear without sounding ridiculous. She was supposed to be smart enough for this job, right?
She looked over at Jonah, who was watching her with an odd expression that didn’t quite reach his eyes, and Livia had a slight frown on her face as well; Mara felt herself flushing and wondered if that was the correct reaction. It occurred to her belatedly that maybe they’d expected her to be more forthright, more outspoken—

“Well,” Adrian said thoughtfully, “I think there might still be some value in meeting you face-to-face at this point.” He smiled warmly. “Mara, welcome to Fulcrum.”
She blinked, confused by his sudden change of tone. It felt like an invitation rather than a statement of fact, and she wondered if she should stand up and shake hands again; instead she simply nodded and said thanks while Adrian rose from the couch and began speaking quietly with Livia—
