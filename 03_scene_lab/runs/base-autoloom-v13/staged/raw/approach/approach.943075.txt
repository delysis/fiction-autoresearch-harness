Esther took a step back but kept her gaze steady on his face.

"Simon," she said, "the entire point of the drive's security is that it can't be opened without breaking the seal. It was never meant to be opened at all except in case of complete network failure. And even then—" she drew a breath "—you still would have had to decide between saving one set of data or preserving the chain of custody that proved its authenticity."

His eyes stayed locked with hers as she spoke. Simon didn't answer until she was finished.

"I made that decision for both of us," he said quietly.

Esther's hand dropped from his face to rest against his chest. Not hard enough to stop him, but firmly enough to hold him in place while she spoke again.

"I have carried that decision alone," she said, her voice even softer than before. "Every time I set data into a sealed drive, I knew it might be the last proof of our work if something happened to the network or to me."
