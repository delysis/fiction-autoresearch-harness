

Simon had counted on his own answer: three hours of careful scanning through data streams while she slept and the house remained quiet. He had not anticipated being asked to say it.

"A friend from intelligence," he told her. "An analyst with good instincts about movement."

Esther let her hand drop. The empty silence between them thickened. Simon's lips went numb where her finger had rested. He noticed the chill on the back of his neck only after he had moved.

Her voice came from beside him in the shadows.

"Does she know which file to look for?"

"Yes."

"You have told her about the rest."

"About—"

Simon realized the question held more than its words. Esther had put her palm against his chest where his heart beat too fast.

"I never knew you would find out," he said. "So I didn't need to tell anyone else."

The pressure against his ribs held firm. Esther traced a small circle on the new scar tissue from his last surgery.

"I carried this decision alone," she told him. "While you thought you had taken it for us. Say that."

"I carried this decision alone," Simon said into the silence of their room.

Esther lowered herself to the bed. Simon could not follow her. He had forgotten how often he avoided this exact moment: the space between admission and apology, where neither one could continue without changing.
