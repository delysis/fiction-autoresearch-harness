

"Nobody," Simon said, his voice rough.

"There is no such thing as nobody," Esther told him. "There is always somebody. Which one?"

"The younger of the twins."

Simon took the finger from his lip and closed his hand over it, hard enough to make her wince. His knuckles showed white.

Esther did not pull away. The heat between them needed no demonstration.

"You are asking me to tell you all the things that could have happened if I did not open the drive," Simon said. "If I did not tell the person inside to keep their head down."

"I am asking why you took my decision," Esther told him. "Not what could have happened if you did not do the thing I forbade you to do."

"I am telling you that things could have happened," Simon replied. "And I think you want me to do nothing with that information. And I think you would regret it."

Esther took a breath. "You are asking me a question," she said. "So I will answer it. If someone died because I did not ask them to be careful, I would regret it."

"I am asking you to stop asking me things," Simon told her. "I am asking you to close the door. I am asking you to sit down and explain what you meant by saying that there is no such thing as nobody. I am asking you why you have been holding me like this for ten years."

Esther laughed. It was not a happy laugh.
