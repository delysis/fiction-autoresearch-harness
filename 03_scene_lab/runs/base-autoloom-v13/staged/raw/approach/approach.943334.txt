

"Nobody." He let her hand stay where it was, pressed against his mouth, while he remembered the false warning and the call that led to compromise.

"When did this happen?" she asked.

Simon counted backward to the moment before the first mistake, before the wrong move began to unfold in slow motion. "Two months, thirteen days ago."

"How long before we got your body back?"

"Fifteen days."

Her fingers slid into his mouth with a wet sound. He made one too, quiet and surprised. She pulled her hand out slowly, watching his reaction.

"They left it open," she said.

Simon felt the pressure in his chest like ink drying unevenly on paper. "I took the risk for you. And for—"

"Tell me what else they might have." Her other hand found him, testing with one finger at each beat of his heart.

He pressed her hand down, trying to stop the rhythm. It changed, not stopped. Faster. Smaller. Against the scars she made for him.

"We can't make that list here," he said.

Esther put both hands on his chest and counted each heartbeat as if it held a decision. One, two, three—

"One," he said aloud. "You were my primary asset."

Her hands tightened, then released. He met her eyes, not her hands. "Two," she said.

"I was willing to trade any other."
