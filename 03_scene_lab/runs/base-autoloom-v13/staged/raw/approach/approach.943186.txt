

"Three questions left."

Esther reached for the inkwell, hesitated, and touched his chest instead. "I'm keeping those questions where they belong." She closed her hand over his sternum and began counting on his ribs. "One: We made a rule about that decision. Two: You didn't make it alone this time. Three: Our house is still secure."

Simon placed his own palm over hers. "Not one, not two, and maybe not three." He lifted her hand to his lips. The seal was still there: a slight bitter taste he had grown accustomed to.

"Maybe we should wait before trying again," Esther said against his mouth.

Simon tasted the salt in her skin. "That's what I do alone," he said. "I wait for something to happen."

Esther paused, then brought one hand up to cup his head. Her fingers touched the scars on the back of his neck where the old wounds had opened again during transport. He closed his eyes and let her memory work through him: how each scar was raised like the edges of a map, how each had felt when it healed.

She kissed him on the side of the throat, careful and deliberate. Simon traced the scar on her lower lip where his teeth had caught. Esther made the small sound he could count among his own decisions.

"You need to come home first," she said, opening her eyes.

Simon took her wrist in his hand. "Where am I now?" he asked.
