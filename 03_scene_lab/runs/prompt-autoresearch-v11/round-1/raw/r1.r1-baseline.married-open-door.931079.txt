

He was willing to explain why he had kept it from her. He could show her the one file he’d risked downloading in the safehouse. But the broken seal was a failure of trust and protection that neither reason nor apology would ever correct.

“If you had said a word,” Esther said, “I could have told you to leave it.”

Simon took one breath to clear his throat. “It wasn’t there when I left.”

“So you came back.”

“Yes.”

“You knew he was watching the safehouse.”

“I didn’t have time to think about that.”

Esther picked up the drive. She turned it over. She looked at him.

“I knew where it was. I knew you would come back. I knew it was too late.”

“Yes.”

“I still should have told you to leave it.”

Simon put two fingers on his forehead and leaned against the bedroom door.

“I thought I could still stop him. I didn’t think about you.”

“You had to get out. You couldn’t just let him get to me.”

“I had to know it wasn’t there first.”

Esther’s lips pressed together. Then she leaned back against the wall and smiled. “It wasn’t.”

“No.”

“You’ve been quiet for a week.”

He pressed both hands against the door. “It would have been a mistake to tell you how quiet.”

She slid a finger inside her shirt collar. Her pulse moved slowly and exactly under her skin. Simon tracked it to her throat and thought for one second it had stopped. Then he realized how long the room had been quiet.

Esther looked away. “Don’t do that.”

“Why?”

“You know why.”

“I’m not trying to make you do anything.”

“You don’t know what I think.”

“Then tell me what you’re thinking.”

He heard his voice in the room and felt a small private regret.

Esther stepped closer. Her chest brushed his shoulder.

“No,” she said. “Because if I tell you, it’ll stop being real.”

Simon looked at her. “That’s not a real thought.”

“Oh, it is.”

“What else did you think about while you were waiting for me to die?”

“I didn’t. I was in shock. I thought I would die.”

“Were you scared?”

“Yes.”

“You said I wouldn’t make it. You said I should stop.”

“And you ignored me.”

“Because you’d done something else you didn’t tell me.”

Esther took a step back. “I needed to know whether we could still win this.”

“By yourself?”

“I knew I wouldn’t have to.”

She gave him one clear, certain look. Her finger moved back down to the necklace clasp at her throat. Her pulse quickened there.

“I kept you alive,” Esther said.

Simon looked at his hands. His left ring finger felt like someone else’s.

“I’ll kill him,” he said. “For that.”

Esther’s breath caught.

“Oh no,” she said. “I want you to kill him too.”

“I’m not asking permission.”

“It’s not permission. It’s a promise.”

He lifted his eyes. “What kind?”

“The kind that makes it worth coming back alive.”

Simon set his hand on the doorjamb. His skin was still cold where he’d touched the broken seal.

“I can’t kill him if I die first,” he said.

“You won’t.”

“It’s a risk.”

“Which would you take if it were me?”

He leaned his forehead against the wood.

“I’d die,” he said.

“So would I.”

Esther crossed the space between them. Her right hand settled on the small of his back. Her left wrist was almost against the door.

“I can’t,” Simon said. “Not now. Not like this.”

She put one hand around his neck. Her thumb went to his collarbone.

“I’ll tell you one thing I didn’t know,” Esther said. “You’d come back anyway.”
