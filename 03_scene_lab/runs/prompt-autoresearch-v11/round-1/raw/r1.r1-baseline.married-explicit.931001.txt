 He could feel her watching. The room remained silent. The seal remained broken.

“Did you know?” he said.

The question came out with no preparation.

Esther looked at the drive, not at him. “There are reasons.”

“Reasons.”

“It’s better if you don’t.”

“How did you know?”

She crossed the room toward the small desk with the spare power cord. He had not moved. She had not looked at his face. The light on the drive turned solid green.

He stopped her before she reached the desk. She made no sound. The room stayed dark around them.

“Why would it be better?” he asked.

She turned her head. Her hair was short now, darker. The light made a single knife-sharp line under her cheekbone.

“We do dangerous things,” Esther said.

“Yes.”

“And we fail.”

“And we fail.”

“You’re not meant to see me fail.”

“Is that what I did?”

Her hand twitched as though she wanted to pull away. He did not move his.

“It’s safer if you don’t know,” she said.

“You think knowing would change my loyalty.”

“No,” she said quickly. “No. Never that. I meant—”

She stopped.

He waited. The room gave nothing. The light on the drive kept glowing green.

“It’s easier if you believe,” she said.

“That I would choose it.”

“That it’s always a choice.”

“And if I believe you lied.”

She flinched. The movement was small and precise. He felt her wanting to say something that would change the moment. Nothing came.

“I knew,” he said. “The drive. The seal. You didn’t know that I knew.”

She looked at his face at last.

“What changed?” she said.

“You did.”
