

The arc of stone separated them from Fulcrum by two full blocks, but a chill lingered as if he had taken some of the laboratory’s machinery into his hand. Mara pulled the scarf up over her mouth, leaving just enough room for breathing. Her hair was already damp against the back of her neck, and the fog had thickened since they had left the pavilion.

“It’s not cold in Fulcrum,” she said.

“The labs stay calibrated for the machines.”

He put his hand on the wall beside the arch. She did not touch him. She was aware of his presence as if each had a boundary no wider than one step.

“So you keep the air dry,” Mara said. “That’s a choice.”

“It’s a compromise.”

She heard it in his voice and in the space behind his silence. “Someone has to breathe there.”

“Most people,” Jonah admitted. “One is less efficient.”

She pulled the scarf down to keep talking. The air smelled of salt and fog without being either.

“What did you want from me?” Mara asked.

Jonah did not answer immediately. His hand remained on the wall, the weight of it a promise he could not keep.

“I wanted to see how you reacted,” he said. “There are things you do when you think nobody is watching that other people can’t un-see.”

“And?” Mara asked. “Did I do it well?”

“Not badly. But not as well as I might have.”

“You’re telling me a failure is acceptable.”

“I’m telling you that you could still be better.”

It was a cruel thing to say. Jonah knew it was cruel before he finished.

“Because that’s how Fulcrum works,” Mara said.

She stepped back, the bare minimum of movement to separate herself from him. His hand slid down the wall.

“Do I get to see your calibration too?” she asked.

“You don’t need to.”

“Then why do you need mine?”

“Because you’re different.”

“Am I?”

She looked at him in a way that made him uncertain of his own certainty.

Jonah let his hand drop completely. He had nothing to hold against the weight of that silence.

“Because you’re an outsider,” he said. “Because you’re not one of us.”

Mara lifted the scarf again. She spoke through it, the words muffled but precise.

“Am I?” she asked. “Or did you just decide I am?”

“I didn’t decide anything.”

“Then who did? Or is it simply a property of Fulcrum that some people are on the inside and some are on the outside?”

His mouth stayed open because he couldn’t bring himself to lie about how true that was.

“That’s not fair,” Jonah said.

“Fair is not the problem, Jonah. The problem is that you want what you can’t have because you refuse to ask for it.”

She moved close enough to touch him. She didn’t reach out.

“You want me to come back,” Mara said. “You want me to be useful. You want me to be predictable. You don’t want me to be what I actually am.”

“And what are you?” Jonah asked.

The question cost him something he wasn’t sure he wanted to give.

“I’m a person,” Mara said. “I have a name. I have a face. I have a life outside your damn institution.”

“And?”

“And you have a choice about whether to let me into yours.”

He wanted to tell her it wasn’t that simple. He didn’t want to admit that she was right.

“Do you want that choice?” he asked. “Really?”

Her scarf dropped away from her lips.

“Yes,” she said.

It was a small word. But the way she said it changed everything between them.

“Then make it,” Mara told him. “Choose.”

His hand found hers without meaning to. She didn’t pull away.

“What if I’m not ready?” Jonah asked.

“Then tell me what you need,” she said. “But stop pretending like I’m not real. Stop pretending like I’m not here.”

He couldn’t look away from her. The fog had taken the last light with it, but there was enough from the streetlamps to see the small motion of her breath, the way her eyes stayed fixed on his face as if he were the last truth left in the world.

“You’re not,” Mara said softly. “And I don’t think you ever were.”

“Then what was I?”

“You were afraid.”

“Of what?”

“Of losing control.”

“And?”

“And you still are.”

It was a blow, but one delivered without malice.

“I’m sorry,” Jonah said.

Mara touched his wrist. He almost didn’t feel it because the rest of him felt like the weight of every choice he had made and every one he hadn’t.

“You don’t have to be,” she said. “But you do have to stop pretending like I’m not here.”

“I’m not.”

“Then look at me.”

He did.

She was still there. Solid. Real. Unavoidable.

“You’re not going to make this easy for me, are you?”

“I’m not going to make it hard. But I’m not going to pretend like it’s something it’s not.”

“And what is it?”

“It’s real.”

His hand tightened on hers.

“Then I’m here,” Mara said. “And you’re here. And we both have choices.”

He nodded.

She leaned in just a little. His breath caught.

“Tell me what you want,” Mara said.

Her voice was low but clear. He wasn’t sure he had ever heard such a simple demand.

“I want—” he began.

She brought her free hand to his face.

The touch was light but firm. She looked at him like he was the only thing in the world.

“Don’t lie,” Mara said. “Not here. Not now. Not to me.”

“I won’t,” Jonah said.

His hand moved of its own will, his fingers curling against her neck. He felt her pulse beneath his skin.

Mara tilted her head. Her eyes never left his.

“Then tell me.”

“I want—”

He stopped.

“You’re stalling,” she said.

“I’m not.”

“Then tell me.”

Jonah let the breath out.

“I want—” he said.

She leaned in just a little farther.

“Finish it,” Mara whispered.

His lips brushed hers.

She didn’t pull away.

He stopped.

She didn’t push him.

“You still have a choice,” Mara said.

Her mouth was so close he could feel the heat of it.

“You can say no.”

Her breath was warm on his skin.

“You can ask me to wait.”

His pulse raced in his wrist.

“You can ask me to leave.”

Her eyes remained fixed on his face.

“But if you want to kiss me—”

She leaned in just a little farther.

His hand tightened on hers.

“Then kiss me.”

His lips touched hers.

Mara didn’t move.

Jonah closed his eyes.

Her mouth tasted like cardamom and honey.

He opened his eyes again.

Mara was still looking at him.

“You can still stop,” she said softly.

Her voice was like a thread pulled taut between them.

Jonah didn’t move.

Mara leaned in just a little farther.

Her mouth met his again.

She didn’t kiss back.

He didn’t pull away.

Mara opened her eyes.

Jonah looked at her.

She let the breath out.

“You’re not going to make this easy for me, are you?”

His mouth was still pressed to hers.

Mara tilted her head just a little.

She pulled away.

Jonah didn’t follow.

“You still have a choice,” Mara said softly.

She looked at him in a way that made him want to tell her the truth.

Jonah let go of her hand.

“I made my choice,” he said.

Mara took a step back.

He watched her.

“I’m glad,” she said.

Jonah didn’t move.

Mara put her hand on the wall beside the arch.

She turned to leave.

Jonah watched her walk away.

The fog closed behind her.

He stood there for a long time.
