 She reached him in three long steps. The hand he had offered was still out, palm upward. Her fingertips brushed the calluses, the scars, the long fingers.

“I’m sorry,” she said.

“That you believed me?”

She felt his thumb press the center of her palm. “No.”

“Then what?”

Mara lifted her head, making room for a kiss. He kissed her hard, the way people did when they were not ready to say they had been wrong. Her back hit the brickwork of the archway, the cold stone breaking into her spine. He pressed her hair into her shoulders, his body braced in a way that should have made her want to push him away. Instead she slid one hand up his shoulder and let herself feel how much harder he was breathing than she was.

When she moved the hand back down to his chest, he caught it. His grip was tight, but he did not pull her into the touch. She could still take it away.

Mara looked up at the arc of the night sky, the brickwork lit by the light pollution of the city, the faint sound of traffic.

“What are you doing?” Jonah asked.

“Nothing,” she said, and meant it.

He pressed his hand flat against the brick. “Do you think I can’t tell what you’re thinking?”

“I’m thinking about what happens if I do this again. Or if I don’t.”

He stepped away from the wall. The hard pressure of his body against hers vanished, and her hand dropped from his chest.

“Is that why you didn’t call the police?”

Mara felt her pulse jump. She could feel the blood thrumming in her fingertips.

“You could have called them,” Jonah said.

She felt the heat rise in her face and took a deep breath, trying to ground herself in the present.

“I didn’t,” Mara said.

“And why is that?”

Mara looked up at the sky, still trying to steady her breathing.

“It’s not just about me,” she said.

“It never is,” Jonah said. “But it should be.”

Mara felt her pulse jump again, and this time she knew why.

“I didn’t want to make things worse,” she said.

Jonah’s hand was still on her wrist, and she felt the muscles in his arm tighten.

“By reporting a crime?”

Mara let out a long, slow breath.

“No,” she said. “By escalating the situation.”

Jonah’s gaze was still sharp, but his expression had softened slightly.

“What situation?” he asked.

“This situation,” Mara said. “The one where we’re both here, right now, and you’re asking me why I didn’t do something I could have done.”

Jonah’s hand dropped from her wrist, and for a moment there was only silence.

Mara took a step back, and then another.

“I need to go,” she said.

Jonah didn’t move.

Mara walked toward the gate, her steps slow and deliberate.

Jonah watched her go.
