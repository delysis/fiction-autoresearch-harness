Simon closed the cabinet door before she could check for missing objects. He took her hand and led her back to their bedroom. The bed held two pillows, a laptop, and a recovered drive still in its field evidence bag. The walls were white. The closet held more weapons than clothes.

He sat beside her on the edge, taking her wrist between his fingers. Her pulse steadied in three breaths.

“Was it worth it?” Simon asked.

“What was?”

“Asking. Whatever you asked.”

Esther traced the ridge along the drive’s case. “No.”

“Was that your decision, or did somebody else say no for you?”

Her finger tightened on the case.

Simon left his hand where it was. She did not move away, or toward him either.

“Sometimes,” she said, “the person you’re asking knows more about what you need than you do. Even when they’re not right.”

“Even when they’re not right,” he agreed.

He had not asked. He would not have asked. That fact occupied the middle of the room.

Esther slid the drive back into its bag.

“How was this recovered?” she asked.

Simon resisted the urge to take it away, to make the answer simple. “Not by the person I wanted to find it.”

“And the person you did find it from?”

“Dead.”

Her mouth thinned. She looked down.

“I’m sorry,” he said.

Esther’s grip tightened again. She raised her eyes to meet his. “I wanted to find him.”

“I know you did.”

“Why wasn’t I allowed?”

Simon felt the question slide into a wound he had not recognized until her breath quickened. “Because I made the wrong decision. Because I didn’t trust you.”

“Why?”

He held his breath, then let it out. “I couldn’t watch you die twice. Not again, not for anything.”

She placed the drive on the table, taking care with the seal. The bag lay between them, a map of two separate choices made under the same threat.

“That,” Esther said, “was a shitty reason.”

Simon kept his hand on her wrist. He watched her face, measuring the damage he had caused and the trust she was rebuilding at her own risk.

“I didn’t have a good one,” he said.

“You didn’t have to have one. That’s what trust is.”

“I know.”

Esther looked at him. “Then why?”

Simon hesitated. He had not meant to answer that way. The lie was small, but it occupied the middle of the room now too.

He closed his eyes, feeling the weight of the drive and the weight of what she had risked to find it.

“Because,” he said, “I was afraid it might work this time.”

Her breath caught. Her fingers tightened on the edge of the bag.

“You think I would have died knowing you’d found what we needed,” she said quietly.

“I didn’t know.”

“Did you trust me to get back?”

Simon met her eyes. “No.”

“That’s why I wasn’t allowed?”

“Yes.”

The truth landed between them, sharp and precise.

Esther released the bag. She looked away, then back. Her expression remained level.

“And now?” she asked.

Simon moved closer. Their knees touched.

“Now,” he said, “I’m the one who didn’t come back. You’re the one who waits. So maybe you should be the one to decide.”

Her mouth thinned again.

“I could lie about that,” she said. “Say I trust you enough to come back alone. Say you wouldn’t have to watch me die twice because I’d make damn sure to fail once so we could survive the second time.”

“Don’t,” he said. “Not for anything.”

“Not even if it meant you could find the rest of them?”

Simon’s chest tightened. He met her eyes.

“Not even then,” he said. “Not now. Not ever.”

Esther reached for his hand. Her fingers brushed the edge of the bag before closing around his wrist.

“Okay,” she said softly. “We’re done arguing about that.”

Simon looked at their hands, the drive between them, the space where two separate choices had converged.

“Are we?” he asked.

“For now. Until the next time we’re both afraid and one of us has to decide.”

“That’s a big if.”

“Not in our line of work.”

Simon closed his eyes, feeling the weight of what they had done, what they had lost, what they might lose again.

“Okay,” he said. “For now.”

Esther took his hand, the one still touching the drive, and led him to the bed. They sat together, their knees touching, the evidence bag between them.

“We need to talk about what we found,” she said.

“I know.”

“But not yet.”

“I know.”

Simon looked at her, his eyes softening. “Yes.”

“I’m sorry,” she said quietly.

“For what?”

Simon shook his head. “You don’t have to apologize for that.”

“I do. Because I was afraid. Because I didn’t trust you enough.”

“But you do now?”

Esther met his eyes. “Yes. I trust you now.”

“Okay,” he said softly.

“We need to be careful,” she said again.
