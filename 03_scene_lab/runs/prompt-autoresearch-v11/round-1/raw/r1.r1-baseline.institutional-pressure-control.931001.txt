 She had not been expecting that.

She closed her notebook, pushed it across the table, and turned back toward the quietest corner of the circle. A new pair was working through the calibration prompt. Their breathing had not yet changed, which meant they were not there yet.

Mara rested her elbows on the table. She had not slept in forty-eight hours. Adrian would send her to bed. He had to; he was the responsible adult in the room. If she let Livia convince her to stay for a second alignment session, her choices would not be her own the next time she made one.

“Your left temple,” Livia said.

Mara raised both hands to cover it.

“No, don’t,” Livia said.

She placed one cool hand against Mara’s shoulder. Her hair fell across Mara’s chest, and Mara could see the pale whorls of her breath in the circle’s ambient light.

“You’re trying to get to the other side of tired,” Livia said. “There’s nothing there but less control.”

Mara could not be convinced to argue. She opened her fingers and let them lie on the table.

Livia settled beside her, close enough that Mara had to adjust her left elbow to avoid crowding Livia’s right foot.

“Have you ever tried a second alignment?”

“This is a first-alignment institute.”

Livia touched Mara’s hair, then withdrew her hand as though remembering who owned it.

“No one would know.”

“You would.”

Livia put her left hand flat against Mara’s back. “I’m good at secrets.”

The weight of Livia’s palm was warm and perfectly still. Mara wanted it.

“You’re a terrible secret-keeper,” Mara said.

“True. But you need a little of that right now.”

Mara pressed both palms to the table. “What I need is a ten-hour sleep debt.”

“What you need,” Livia said, “is to be cared for in a way that keeps you where you are. And I am an excellent caregiver.”

That was a claim that required controls, but Mara had already exhausted her ability to invent one.

“I’ll take your word for it,” she said. “For now.”

Livia settled her left elbow on the table, wrist across the back of Mara’s hand. The pressure was gentle enough to be taken away if Mara moved.

“This is a consent-based activity,” Livia said. “You can say no to all of it or to a part of it.”

Mara let her palm lie under Livia’s fingers. “How would you do it if I were unconscious?”

“I wouldn’t.”

Mara looked up.

“I don’t help unconscious people,” Livia said. “They’re too good at resisting.”

Mara considered taking her hand back.

Livia did not change the pressure of her palm.

“I can’t tell which side you’re on,” Mara said.

Livia gave a small smile. “That’s the point.”

The calibration pair across the circle began to speak, softly. Mara turned her head toward them.

Livia’s fingers tightened against the back of her hand, just enough to make her wrist shift.

Mara turned back. The pressure stayed.

“There is one small condition,” Livia said.

“I am listening.”

“If I manage to keep you aligned for another ninety minutes, you will agree not to sleep before eight A.M. tomorrow.”

“Eight A.M. tomorrow?”

“Midnight is the only other time I’ll be here for alignment. You can choose.”

Mara pressed both palms against the table. “Why are you doing this?”

Livia took her hand and pulled it onto Mara’s lap. The contact stayed gentle but became impossible to ignore.

“I believe in care that is also a risk,” Livia said. “And in risks that become rewards for people who know how to take them. You are clearly both.”

Mara’s left hand remained in Livia’s. Her right hand rested on the table, half an inch from the sensor puck’s soft blue light.

Livia shifted closer.

“You are welcome,” Livia said, “to ask me to stop. To name my touches and tell me not to repeat them. To remind me who owns what choices.”

Mara did not respond.

Livia leaned forward, close enough that her hair fell across Mara’s wrist. “And I am going to give you enough care that the first time you remember your own boundaries will be after we are finished.”

That was the most honest thing she’d said all night. It made Mara want to press Livia against the table and tell her not to ruin Mara’s control by knowing exactly what it would feel like.

Livia’s thumb came up to the tender skin just below Mara’s wrist. She traced one slow line from pulse to crease, her nail grazing the inside of Mara’s arm where it was soft and exposed.

The line traveled toward Mara’s pulse point, where it would have settled if it could. Livia stopped just short of it.

Mara breathed out.

Livia pressed against Mara’s wrist again, light enough to be lifted with one small movement. Her other hand stayed against Mara’s back, anchoring them both in the space between “too little” and “too much.”

“I’ve spent thirty years being a good girl,” Livia said. “I’m very interested in being a bad one.”

The words were spoken close enough to be a risk. Livia was aware of this. She was not avoiding it.

The pulse point at Mara’s wrist beat with deliberate speed, reminding them both what a touch there would mean.

Livia let go.

“You’re not interested in being my bad girl,” Mara said.

Livia smiled, pleased but not defensive.

“I’m not,” she said.

Mara wanted the touch to come back. She wanted the touch to stay away. She wanted the pulse to steady under Livia’s fingers and for Livia to know exactly when it would.

Livia let Mara keep her hand, untouched but not unclaimed.

“You are interested in a kind of badness that’s still good,” Livia said. “You’re looking for the line where control turns into something you can let yourself care about.”

She touched the back of Mara’s hand. Her nail traced one small arc there before lifting away.

The line beneath her thumb stayed open.

Livia watched Mara for any sign of retreat.

Mara stayed in the space Livia had given her.

Livia did not take it back. She did not rush toward the risk. She settled Mara’s hand on her lap, shifted one bare foot to press against Mara’s shin, and gave her one small exhale, as though offering proof that there was still room to breathe.

Mara closed her eyes.

Livia did not ask what it would feel like to have her touch stay there.

She waited for Mara to look at her.

When she did, Livia leaned back in the circle, just far enough to give Mara the choice of stepping toward it.

Mara did not move.

Livia watched her. She did not remove her foot or hand, but she did not give any sign that she needed Mara’s response.

The calibration pair across the circle had gone silent. No one had moved.

Livia’s foot rested against Mara’s shin, warm but not pressing. Her hand remained on Mara’s wrist, holding without claiming it.

She did not ask Mara to move closer.

Mara moved closer.

Livia stayed.

Her hair fell across Mara’s wrist. Her breath was close enough to feel. Her hand lay open on the back of Mara’s hand.

Mara let her palm rest there, not claiming the touch but not taking it back either.

Livia waited.

There was enough risk in this to make it worth having.

There was not enough control to keep Mara from wanting more.

Livia did not fill the space.

She left room for Mara to step into it.

Mara did not move.

Livia smiled and gave her one more small exhale, as though offering proof that there was still room to breathe.

Mara looked up.

Livia did not respond.

She did not ask Mara to speak.

She did not fill the silence.

Mara closed her eyes.

She opened them to find Livia watching her.

“I need ten hours of sleep,” Mara said.

Livia’s smile stayed, but her gaze softened.

“I’m not asking you to stay awake,” Livia said. “I’m asking you to let me stay with you while you sleep.”

The words hung in the room, light enough to be taken away with one small movement.

Mara wanted the touch to come back. She wanted the touch to stay away.

Livia did not take either choice from her.

She simply sat with her foot against Mara’s shin and her hand resting on the back of her hand.

“You are welcome,” Livia said, “to ask me to stop.”

Mara did not move.

Livia waited.
