

Her suitcase bumped against his calf.

“If you’re leaving Fulcrum,” he said.

“Yes?”

“I’ll miss having you around.”

“You’re welcome to follow me to the bus station.”

He laughed.

They were the only ones left in the lab. The fellowship building’s lights went out behind them, one window at a time, until only the observatory glowed blue. Jonah’s hand rested on the concrete wall as though the gesture were a question.

“You don’t need to stay with me,” Mara said.

“I know that.”

She felt his attention without turning toward it. She would have done anything to make the silence a trap.

He did not offer to carry her suitcase.

The path beneath the redwoods was narrow. Mara kept her distance without pretending it was a choice. Jonah walked easily beside her, tall enough that he could have taken her hand without bending.

“Is there something I can carry?” he asked.

“Yes.”

He took the handle, which was small enough to fit in one hand, and fell in step without breaking her rhythm. It felt like an accusation, a gesture that made no sense.

Mara had never left a place she needed to leave. Her suitcase stayed upright. The wheels touched grass between stones.

“What’s the longest kiss you’ve ever had?” he asked.

The question was deliberate and useless. Her skin tightened.

He stopped, looked back, and waited. Mara was far enough away from the lab that returning without explanation would change too much. She set the suitcase between them instead.

“You can keep the question,” she said.

“I might prefer an answer.”

Mara put her hand on his wrist.

He closed his fingers around it.

The pulse she felt there had nothing to do with the trace on a screen. A full beat slipped away. Mara’s palm warmed beneath his thumb.

“This,” she said.

Her pulse jumped. She felt the moment of his recognition against the back of her fingers.

“One beat,” she said. “I can’t get past one.”

“You don’t have to get past it,” he said.

The pulse beneath her palm stayed quiet. Mara thought about kissing him. She had not felt so much desire for such a small gesture in a long time.

He leaned down. Her lips met his without breaking the contact between them. She smelled cinnamon from a candle he had burned and something else.

A choice.

Mara breathed through it and opened her eyes.

He stopped. His gaze was warm and clear and not the thing he had promised her. It was not even the thing he had done. It was something more.

Her body wanted more of his body. Her hands opened. His mouth came nearer without touching again.

“If we keep going,” he said. “You have to stop.”

The desire she had not expected remained intact. Mara slid her palm up his wrist until she could feel the pulse again. The touch was not as intimate as a kiss. It was not as dangerous.

The hand on his wrist did not tremble. Her breath slowed. Mara was still breathing.

“You can stop first.”

His thumb pressed against her pulse. The touch was small and precise. Mara could feel the moment when his pulse quickened.

They both knew they could kiss. They both knew they could stop.

“I think you’re better than I am,” she said.

He laughed. The sound moved through her arm.

“I’m not going to keep you here,” Mara added.

Jonah put a hand on her waist, but did not pull her closer. She could feel the warmth of his palm through the fabric.

Mara breathed the air at her throat. “I’m not going to make you promise not to keep me here. I’m not going to make you promise not to follow me to the bus station.”

The pulse beneath her hand quickened again. Jonah put his mouth against her hair without touching her skin. Mara could feel the warmth of his breath on her scalp, the soft sound of his breathing.

“You’re a creature of habits,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Habits don’t make sense when they become cages.”

She let the habit stay. Mara’s fingers tightened around his wrist. She did not ask him to kiss her. She did not need him to kiss her. She needed something more.

Jonah moved closer, his hand still resting on her waist, his thumb moving slowly against her pulse. Mara closed her eyes and pressed against the palm at her side. Her pulse quickened. She could feel the warmth of his skin, the steadiness of his breath. The air at her throat stayed calm.

“You’re better than I am,” she said.

He pressed his mouth against her temple, the kiss light enough that Mara could still feel the pulse beneath her hand. The contact was warm and steady. Mara opened her eyes.

The light was clear. The air was soft.

“I could kiss you again,” Jonah said.

“You could.”

He moved back, the hand at her waist still warm. Mara let the silence stay.

“We could kiss until we ran out of air,” he said.

Mara felt the heat of his attention against her skin. It was not the same heat as the kiss. It was something more.

“We could stop before then,” she said.

“We could stop now.”

She pressed her palm against the pulse at his wrist. Her breath stayed slow. Mara let the air at her throat remain soft.

“We could stop now,” Jonah said.

His thumb pressed gently against her pulse. Mara let the contact stay.

She did not need to kiss him. She did not need to touch him.

“You’re better than I am,” he said.

The hand at her waist moved down until it held her fingers.

Mara turned to face him. Her breath stayed calm. The air at her throat remained soft. She could feel the warmth of his attention against her skin. It was not the same warmth as the kiss. It was something more.

“If we kiss again,” Mara said, “we should stop when we run out of air.”

The hand at her waist tightened. Mara’s pulse quickened again.

Jonah did not kiss her. He put one hand against her cheek instead, his thumb moving gently along her jawline. The touch was warm and steady. Mara let the air at her throat remain soft.

“We could stop now,” he said.

Mara closed her eyes. The warmth of his attention remained intact. It was not the same warmth as the kiss. It was something more.

Jonah moved back, his hand still against her cheek. Mara let the contact stay. She felt the pulse beneath her palm. The hand on her wrist tightened.

“I’m not going to keep you here,” she said.

“I know that.”

His hand moved down until it held her fingers. The touch was warm and steady. Mara let the air at her throat remain soft.

The kiss did not come. Her breath stayed calm. The air at her throat remained soft. Mara opened her eyes.

The light was clear. The air was soft. Jonah’s gaze was warm and clear and not the thing he had promised her.

“We could kiss,” Mara said.

The hand on her wrist tightened again. Mara let the silence stay.

Jonah moved back. The contact was warm and steady. Mara let the air at her throat remain soft.

“We could kiss until we run out of air,” Mara said.

He looked at her.

“Or we could stop now,” Mara said.

His hand tightened around hers.

“Which do you choose?” Mara asked.

Jonah looked at her.

“You’re better than I am,” he said.

She pressed her palm against the pulse at his wrist.

“You’re better than I am,” Mara said.

Her pulse quickened. She let the contact stay.

The air at her throat remained soft.
