
“You’re very early, aren’t you?” he observed at last, eyes darting a quick glance over her shoulder as if hoping someone would arrive to break the awkwardness.

But no one else came in, and Mara found herself still holding Jonah’s hand as she watched him scan the room. He shook his head with a casual, conspiratorial expression, as if they were sharing an inside joke about being here together.
“Adrian and Livia will be along shortly,” he said. “They’re finishing up some things. Why don’t you come and have a seat?”
The young man turned toward the couch again, gesturing for her to join him in it. She followed his lead, settling into the cushions beside him while he let go of her hand.

For a few minutes they sat together without saying anything more. Jonah gazed around at the decor as if seeing it all for the first time: a small wooden table between their feet with books stacked neatly on top; a floor lamp that stood taller than either one of them; framed photographs hung from the walls depicting various scenes both rural and urban; rows upon rows of paperbacks arranged by color on a bookshelf near where he sat.
