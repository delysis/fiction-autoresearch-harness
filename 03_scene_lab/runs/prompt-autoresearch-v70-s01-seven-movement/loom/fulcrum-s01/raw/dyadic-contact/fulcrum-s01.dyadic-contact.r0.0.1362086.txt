“No, you’re not,” he said, after a long while.
She blinked at him. “Excuse me?”
“You’re not Adrian.”
That seemed like an accurate enough assessment of reality, though she’d expected more of a greeting. “No, I’m Mara. Nice to meet you.” She offered her hand. He didn’t take it, or move an inch, just kept looking at her as if she were some strange species, and she didn’t retract hers right away. She felt like an idiot. She turned to face the door and realized there was an open door at the other end of the common room too. He might be waiting for Adrian to come in through the other one.
She cleared her throat and said, “Sorry, I thought this was where we were meant to be.”
“I didn’t say you weren’t. I said you were not Adrian. So it looks like we’re both waiting.”
