
“They tell me to expect you around this time, so I’ve come to say hello.”
“Good evening,” Mara returned. Her voice sounded much louder than he would need.
His right arm lifted; the hand spread, palm up. “Adrian said I was your type.”
“So it seems.”
He grinned, leaning forward with hands flat on the table, elbows apart, and the bare legs uncrossing at a deliberate pace. “You’re much prettier than I thought.”
“So am I,” she said; her voice had the edge of a laugh, but the statement was true. She crossed the room toward him, aware of the body moving in response.
When she reached the table she saw his eyes take her in, her hair and dress and earrings and the bare feet, one after the other without any hint of disapproval. His face was a study of admiration; it made her uneasy, but not uneasy enough to stop walking.
“Do you want me to sit?” he asked. “I’d rather stand.”
“Fine with me,” he said. She sat on the arm of his chair and watched as he leaned forward again and studied her bare legs.
She put down her cup. “Do you always do this, Jonah?”
He was frowning slightly, but he didn’t stop watching her. “You know I like to. Why are you asking now?”
“Because I’ve had enough.” She reached out to touch his hand and he flinched.
“Are you all right?” she asked.
“Fine, I said. Just surprised is all.” He made a sound somewhere between a sigh and a chuckle. “Well, don’t worry about it.”
