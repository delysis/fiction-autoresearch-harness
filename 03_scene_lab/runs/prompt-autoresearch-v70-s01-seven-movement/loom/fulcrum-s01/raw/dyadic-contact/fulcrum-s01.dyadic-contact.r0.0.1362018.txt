
Her heart stuttered. She knew that face. Hadn’t seen it in years but recognized it like a brother.
For a while they looked at each other; the longer she stood there, the surer she was that he didn’t recognize her. But he had to know that she knew him; it showed, somewhere on her face. She kept staring.
“You’re early.” His voice, low and faintly amused: familiar. She swallowed.
She looked away, realizing suddenly that he was watching her mouth. “Yeah.” The breath came easy now.
He smiled but still didn’t get up.
“How’s it going?”
“Good.”
They stood in silence for another minute or two. Mara’s back was starting to ache. It didn’t help that she was wearing a pack, too.
Finally she asked, “Do we all just sit around and wait? Is that the procedure? Or do we eat?”
He nodded slightly but stayed still, evidently not ready to answer.
A few moments later someone else entered the room—a man in his fifties, carrying a small black notebook. When Mara turned away from the couch she was startled to see him there, watching her. But she wasn’t surprised; she knew who he was.
He gave them both a tight smile, waved, and went around the table to his chair. Then he took off his glasses and opened his book.
