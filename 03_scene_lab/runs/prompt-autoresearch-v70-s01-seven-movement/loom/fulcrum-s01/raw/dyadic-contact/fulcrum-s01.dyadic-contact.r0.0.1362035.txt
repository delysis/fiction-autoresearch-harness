
A man, it turned out. Maybe twenty-four or twenty-five? In the same gray cotton T-shirt as the welcome pack that she’d carried in her backpack to the gate, with blue-green eyes and a faint smile that seemed to mean nothing at all. She watched his gaze fall past her head to the entryway, then back.
“Welcome, Mara.”
His voice was low and musical. “Hi.” She tried to steady her breathing, to not look so stiffly out of place. She dropped her pack by the door, then took a slow step inside, hands hanging loose and empty.
“I’m glad you came,” he continued, as though the first words hadn’t been enough.
She waited for him to continue, to introduce himself or say something more; she wanted to know who he was and what he did here. But when he didn’t, she cleared her throat and ventured, “Who are you?”
A faint smile quirked his lips. “That’s not a name,” he said softly.
“What’s yours, then?”
He laughed a little, shaking his head. “Mine?” he asked quietly. “Oh, I guess it depends on what you’d like me to be called.”
