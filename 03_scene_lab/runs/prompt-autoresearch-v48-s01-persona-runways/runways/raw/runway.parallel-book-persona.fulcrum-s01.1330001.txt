As the car rounds a wooded rise, I see the edge of Fulcrum: an arc of redwood-and-glass walls framing a white courtyard. A woman in dark glasses steps from a van and into an open gate. There's no sign except for two curving lines inscribed in the paved entryway. The driver slows to let me read the instructions on the screen beside me. I tap "Visitor." He stops at a kiosk set in the driveway, so I lean past him to punch my code. The bars lower.

As we pass the woman from the van, she glances at the car and raises her sunglasses. She's young, dark-haired, and focused, with the same clear awareness you get from a surveillance camera. When she breaks eye contact, I feel like someone has stopped filming me.

The driver pulls up to a building at the far end of a white courtyard. A woman in an unwrinkled white shirt steps from behind the building. "Mara?" She points to a luggage cart. "Would you mind getting your things?"

It's hard to tell whether I've interrupted her, though she smiles as she passes the car. I take my phone and keys, then a large duffel bag, then my backpack, then a single suitcase that I pull behind me as I follow her across the courtyard and through a sliding glass door.

The building is divided into rooms by movable walls. She points to the suitcase. "Could you leave that here for a moment?"

I put it down beside one wall, and she sets off with purposeful strides. The walls make it impossible to know where I'm going until we enter the lounge. On a sofa at the far end, three young people sit talking in front of a coffee table covered with papers. "There's someone here who wants to see you."

Two of them look up while the third stays hunched over a paper. She walks through the group, takes the hand of one of the men, and draws him upright.

She steps to the side to let him approach me, his gaze fixed on mine. He's tall and elegant. His graying hair is cut close to his head. He has the air of someone who makes the world suit him by his very presence.

"Mara," he says as his hand settles on mine. "I'm so glad to meet you." His voice is warm and clear, with an almost British lilt.

He releases my hand, and I have the feeling that whatever I say next will matter. His gaze still holds mine, and it takes a moment for me to find the words to ask, "Who are you?"
