

A man in a dark blue shirt and gray slacks. And another woman—an older woman this time, with short white hair and very strong eyebrows, carrying a tray of glasses and a pitcher of iced water.
Mara gave the woman a little nod, not sure what to say.
“Have you met Jonah Reed?” the man was saying to her. “He’s one of our new hardware-security researchers. I’m sorry he didn’t get to introduce himself before.”

His voice had a quiet firmness, and his face was open, pleasant; dark eyes crinkled slightly at the corners when he looked directly at her. She nodded again. “No. We—” She glanced back at Jonah. “We met right now,” she said, then turned back to the man and stuck out her hand.
“Hi. I’m Adrian.”
She took it. He was tall, slender, with an easy smile.

“So this is the common room,” he said to Mara, gesturing around at the couches and the low table with books on it. “The one we told you about. Where we meet together once a week to talk and play games and just—”
“Get used to each other,” the older woman put in, lowering her tray to the table and placing four glasses around its edge. She turned to Jonah. “You were here last time, weren’t you? Good to see you again.”
“Good to see you too,” Jonah replied. He looked at Adrian. “You’re sure you don’t mind? I mean—she said it was okay—”

Adrian nodded quickly. “Yes. I’m glad you’re here. Thank you for coming.” He smiled again, then turned back to Mara. “Did I ever explain who this lady is?” he asked. She shook her head. “Well—her name is Livia Sloane, and she’s a caterer. She’s also one of our best readers.”

A reader of what, exactly? It felt like a loaded word.

“And—” She glanced over at the older woman, who was standing near the table with her arms crossed. Her expression had shifted to something slightly stern. “She reads—?”
“People,” said Adrian, very slowly. “She’s one of our top readers. And she helps us keep this place running smoothly.”
Mara frowned slightly. “I see.” She didn’t. Not at all.

Livia was still watching her, and Mara tried to meet her gaze with a small smile. The older woman returned it briefly before turning back to Jonah again.
“I’m sorry I interrupted,” he said, raising his eyebrows.
She shook her head. “It’s okay,” she replied in the same quiet tone, as though he had been speaking to her rather than answering Adrian’s question about where the tray should go next. He nodded quickly.

“Let me get these set up over there.” She lifted one end of it slightly, then carried it around toward Mara and placed it down on a nearby counter. Mara watched her move; her movements were very deliberate—precise but not stiff. “There,” Livia said at last, setting the tray down.

Mara took another look around the room. There weren’t any windows to speak of—just two skylights high above them, which let in some light from outside. The walls were a soft blue color, with no paintings or anything else hanging on them.
There was more furniture than she had seen when she first entered the place: two couches facing each other across the middle of the room, another smaller one in front of the wall where she’d noticed Jonah sitting, and several large armchairs arranged in various directions. There was also a low table in the center of the room with a few books on it, plus some sort of puzzle game that looked like an upside-down pyramid made out of wooden triangles.

“Please sit anywhere you’d like,” said Adrian, indicating the furniture with a sweeping motion. Mara glanced at Livia again, who gave her another quick nod. She took one last look at Jonah—he was leaning against one of the couches with his arms crossed, watching them both—then walked over to one of the armchairs near the table in front of him and sat down.
