His suit was the same gray as the man’s, but not quite as crisp, with a rumpled collar and a few buttons left undone; he stopped next to her and put his hand on her back without taking his eyes off Jonah’s face. “I’m sorry,” he murmured in her ear; his breath smelled like coffee, sweet and bitter. “These two can be shy sometimes.” He turned to face the newcomer properly now, still keeping hold of Mara with one arm around her waist. She felt herself straighten up under his gaze, ready for whatever he might want from her next.

Jonah took another step forward, closing the gap between them.
“Adrian,” he said quietly, nodding his head in greeting before offering a brief smile to the woman who had come in behind him—Livia, from Mara’s email correspondence with Adrian. She had long brown hair pulled back from her face into a neat braid; she wore dark-rimmed glasses like Jonah’s and a short black dress that showed off her toned legs but didn’t draw attention away from anything else about her.

“How are you?” Adrian asked in response. His voice was still low, almost conversational, but Mara could hear the note of anticipation underlying it as well; she could feel how intently he was watching for some reaction from Jonah.
“Fine.”

Adrian dropped his hand from Mara’s back and took a step toward the young man himself.
“Do you know each other?” he asked, keeping one eyebrow raised as though expecting either of them to say yes.
There was silence in the common room, punctuated only by the sound of distant laughter coming from somewhere else in the building. Outside on the street below, someone yelled at a passing car; Livia leaned forward and peered out the window to see what it was about.

Adrian turned back to Mara. “What do you think?” he murmured again, this time addressing her directly instead of simply trying to include her in their conversation.

“I—” Mara opened her mouth, but nothing came out except for a few hesitant breaths.
The sound of raised voices was growing louder now; she could make out angry words being hurled across the street at whoever had made such an unnecessary commotion there earlier on.

“Livia,” Adrian said suddenly, cutting off whatever Mara might have been going to say with his own voice rising over hers for the first time since they’d entered the room together; he still didn’t look away from Jonah though.
“Mara is a technical assistant,” he went on quietly. “And this is Jonah Reed.” He gestured at each person in turn as he spoke; then both of his arms dropped by his sides again. “I believe you have corresponded before?”
There was silence.
Livia nodded once, slowly.

Adrian smiled then, a short and simple gesture. “Well,” he said in a low voice, still addressing Mara directly but clearly expecting her to follow along anyway. “I believe it’s time for introductions.”
He stepped forward again, putting himself between them now as though blocking any further approach from either side of his body. “Livia is a behavioral scientist,” he continued with a slightly raised voice, just enough so that everyone in the common room would have to pay attention even if they weren’t looking at him right then—and not quite enough so that someone might think he was trying to keep them separated.

Jonah raised one eyebrow, as though considering an argument but not yet knowing what form it would take.
Mara found herself holding her breath; she had no idea where this conversation might go from here and was afraid to let herself get too invested in anything until after they’d all met one another properly first.

She breathed out slowly, feeling slightly embarrassed by how much easier Adrian’s introduction seemed to have been than hers.
Livia cleared her throat before speaking; when she did, Mara could hear the effort behind each word as though it were difficult for her to make herself say them aloud.
“Good evening,” Livia said finally with a small smile directed at no one in particular—but then again, perhaps everyone at once. “I’m pleased to meet you.”
Jonah nodded his head once more.
