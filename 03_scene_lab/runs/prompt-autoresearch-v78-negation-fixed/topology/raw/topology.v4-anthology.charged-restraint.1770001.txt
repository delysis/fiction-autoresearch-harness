They passed the darkened reception hall without stopping to wait for the night desk clerk. The front door swung shut behind them, but he didn’t stop and turn back. She felt the lock click in the wood.
Outside, the breeze was cool, almost cold. But the wind smelled like the sea, a strong sweet-briny tang that reminded her of nothing so much as the way Jonah smelled: salt and ozone and something deeper, the animal warmth he didn’t always mask with cologne. She’d noticed it almost the first time she’d seen him, in the first session she’d attended at Fulcrum three weeks before.
It hadn’t mattered then; the scent had been nothing more than an observation. But it mattered now. She smelled him from his back.
There were no street lamps between Fulcrum and the downtown area. She could have seen where she was going, but Jonah didn’t look to check whether or not she was there; he went straight on as if she were a ghost following behind him.
He didn’t speak for almost a block before turning into the first lane of a side street. Mara stopped at the corner, one hand touching a lamp post. It was cold metal under her palm. Jonah hesitated. She thought she saw him turn to look back at her, though he didn’t speak, but when he realized she wasn’t coming, he moved on again.
He walked quickly, even more rapidly than before. She felt the pull of him going faster ahead of her, as if he were moving forward in a stream and leaving her behind on the bank.
Mara tried to breathe at the same rate he had, but it didn’t help. Her lungs ached, and her head began to clear; she could feel the first tremors of exhaustion under the adrenaline.
Her knees felt weak. She stood still. She wanted him to come back.
The walk took longer than it should have. She had been there a few times since the first day, and she knew that it didn’t take a quarter of an hour to reach the hotel from Fulcrum.
It wasn’t so far. She was just tired.
But he would probably turn the corner at the end of this street; if she didn’t move soon, she’d lose him in the next block.
Mara started walking again. It felt like a dream when her feet moved and she saw the sidewalk move beneath them, as though she were in one of the lab chairs, watching another subject move in real time. She had no sensation of moving her legs or taking steps; it was only when she saw her feet on the sidewalk that she knew she was actually walking.
Jonah looked back at her before the next corner. She could see him against the glow of the streetlamps just ahead; she thought she heard something from him, but she wasn’t sure. She began to run and caught up to him.
“You’re moving fast.” His voice was soft, but she knew what it meant. He was telling her not to talk: let’s walk in silence. She didn’t know what to say, and didn’t want to try. She walked beside him.
Her breath came in ragged gasps; she couldn’t seem to breathe slowly no matter how hard she tried. Her heart pounded in her ears, and she felt lightheaded. She held her hand against his back as she walked behind him.
