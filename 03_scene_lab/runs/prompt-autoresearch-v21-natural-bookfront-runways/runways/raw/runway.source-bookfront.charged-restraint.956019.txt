She was only a shadow, but I’d know those curls, the curve of that cheek, the set of her shoulders. The way she walked, the swing of her arms, the angle of her head to her neck, as she left the building in the direction of his house.

“Mara. Wait.”

She slowed down, but kept moving. I stopped. It took effort and a bit of time before I could bring myself to continue.

He had never bothered me before, or flirted with me. In all our years of knowing each other, he had treated me courteously but politely. Distantly.

The most he would ever do is smile and say hello, or goodbye, or ask if I needed anything. And I’ve never even wanted him to do more.

Not until the last few months.

And tonight, I found it difficult to keep up the politeness with him, the professionalism.

He hadn’t helped. He was too relaxed, too easy-going, with his casual clothes, and his tousled hair. And that way he had of just standing there with his eyes closed, while I ran the instruments and read out the results.
