The late session with their research colleague had stretched long.
“It is getting too late for us to eat at a restaurant. I do not feel hungry anyway.” Mara turned toward the door, and the other researcher did not try to stop her.
“Please do not do that,” she heard him say softly. “I will be here until tomorrow, waiting.”
A part of her thought that this was probably a ploy to get her to return before morning, but it did not stop her from opening the door and slipping into the corridor. She might feel hungry by the time she returned, so she would do better to be walking there.
“I was not talking to you,” she said to herself, “but to him.”
Even the memory of it left a knot of discomfort in her belly.
“I do not trust him. He is the enemy. Why are you talking to him instead of to me? Do not think I cannot tell the difference.”
The elevator doors slid open, and she stepped inside. She was walking out of it before she realized she had missed her floor. She had not even looked at the buttons.
“That’s odd,” she thought, walking down the stairs that led back up. She took care to press the correct button and walk off the elevator at the correct floor.
