Esther had been afraid her husband had died during the battle.

He lay with one arm outstretched across the mattress, as though to hold off a possible intruder. A shadow in the darkness.
“Simon,” she whispered. “Wake up.” He stirred a little and murmured, but did not speak. “Simon, wake up.” She reached out, laid her fingers lightly on the curve of his wrist.
A faint warmth emanated from him in the cool room, like a slow glow from the banked coals in the hearth.
Her fingertips touched his pulse. Steady. Calm, despite the small movement of the blankets about him. He was no longer sweating, his skin was cool, but the air that stirred his hair was heated, like a little summer wind in his sleep.
A summer wind, warm with heat and scented with pollen and flowers, which ruffled the grass at her knees. A tall figure rising among the reeds and wildflowers, a tall man standing in the bright sunlight, his hair shining like polished bronze…
She pressed one hand against her chest, fighting the sudden urge to sob, and turned her head away, swallowing hard. The light from the hearth-fire played in stripes across her face. The scent of hay and manure, the scent of gunpowder, the smell of summer…
She laid her hand on his chest, and felt his heart beat in sympathy with her own.
