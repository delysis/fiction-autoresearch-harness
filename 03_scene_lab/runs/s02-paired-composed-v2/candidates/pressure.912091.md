Mara stood by the cedar deck with Jonah’s black sensor between her thumb and middle finger. The sun had already gone, but the sky held some heat. She felt it at her cheeks and in the grass between her bare feet.

Jonah came toward her from the pavilion. At first she thought he was reading her face. Then he put both hands on the deck railing and looked across the lawn at nothing she could see.

“We’re out of time,” she said. “We have five minutes before dinner, and it’s too far to take this to the lab.”

“Miriam knows it’s broken.”

“Miriam doesn’t know why it’s broken.”

He looked up and found her. His hair fell over his forehead as he did. “I haven’t broken it.”

“I never said that. I said you know why it’s broken.”

His mouth tilted, a refusal and an acknowledgment together.

“Will it take five minutes?” Mara asked.

“Maybe.”

“Then do it.”

He stayed where he was.

“We’re going to eat dinner,” she said.

“Maybe.”

His black shirt had loosened from his waist. His eyes were darker than she had noticed. Mara wanted to leave, which made him harder to leave.

“Or you could tell me you’re still angry about something I said.”

“I’m not.”

“So you could tell me something else.”

His fingers tightened on the railing. He let them go.

“I’d prefer not to be angry about something you said.”

She smiled.

His eyes closed as though he had expected an insult. Mara did not say anything else.

Jonah turned away again. She watched the dark edge of his jaw.

A door opened behind her. Mara did not look.

“I’d like to show you,” Livia said.

Mara turned. Livia stood on the deck in bare feet, a cup of amber tea between her palms. She drank from it before Mara could decide whether to accept the offered cup.

“Tea,” Livia said.

Mara took it. The liquid warmed her fingers through the ceramic.

“We’re out of time,” she said.

“We’ve found a way around that.” Livia drank again and put the cup on the deck rail. “The first run has a small anomaly. We don’t know which way the deviation came from, but Adrian is letting us try a one-way stream on the next calibration session. If that shows the anomaly again, he’ll authorize a replay.”

“What happened on the first run?”

“You did something different from what you intended to signal, and Jonah saw it.”

“And we want to know which direction the mistake came from?”

“I’m not sure it was a mistake.”

Mara let the air out of her lungs in one breath. “Jesus. I’m tired.”

Livia watched her with sudden interest. “That kind of tired?”

“The kind that comes from four people in my head for two hours. Do you want one of them?”

“They all want the tea.”

“At least they agree on something.”

Livia poured more tea and slid it toward Mara. “You’re afraid we’ll find you competent but not extraordinary.”
