“You found that already.”

“What we find isn’t static.”

The tea was hot enough to taste without becoming the thing itself. Mara drank and waited for the sharpness to fade.

Jonah remained still against the deck rail.

“There was a one-way stream from the second session,” Livia said. “You’ve had permission to watch it overnight.”

Mara glanced at her. Livia drank again and let the cup rest in the center of both palms. “Did you forget?”

“I didn’t forget.”

“Because it was a kindness and you don’t trust kindness that doesn’t hurt you.”

Mara held Livia’s gaze and found no easy answer. She considered the tea, the sun-warmed grass, the people in the room, the man at her back who had already held her wrist, the one remaining deviation Miriam had marked on the final session’s log. The permission to watch remained her decision.

She could have declined it, but it would have cost her something to know whether it was necessary.
