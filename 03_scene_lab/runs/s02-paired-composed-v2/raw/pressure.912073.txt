Mara had never seen Jonah stay quiet for so long.

The table held three kinds of tea, a carafe of sparkling water, and six empty coffee mugs. Mara’s back felt warm enough for tea to be a bad decision. She watched Adrian Voss put a blue glass in front of her and waited for it to contain sparkling water.

Adrian went to the coffee pot. He came back with a blue cup.

“Coffee?”

“I’m fine.” Mara accepted the glass.

Her fingers were colder than his had been. The glass held ice, no bubbles. She took a drink, returned the glass to the tray, and held out both hands for tea.

“We need a better model for depletion,” she said.

“Not all of us are.”

Mara looked up. Jonah had not moved from the side of the table. He wore a black sweatshirt with no decoration. His hair hung across one eye. The black-on-black silhouette was too casual for Adrian’s living room.

The room’s attention had not left him. Mara could feel it as clearly as the weight of Jonah’s gaze.

“Not all of you are what?” Livia asked.

“Depleted.” He looked at Mara. “This is the fourth time tonight that you’ve tried to explain why you’re tired.”

She blinked. “I haven’t said anything.”

“You offered to explain the session data. When I stopped you, you asked someone else to do it. When they finished, you stood up and put away your notebook.”

Mara set down the cup and stood up. The room remained.

“The session was dense,” she said.

Livia’s expression changed. She came beside Mara. “The session wasn’t dense,” she said, reaching for her own notebook. “That’s why you’re tired.”

The air changed. Jonah’s gaze moved to Livia without leaving Mara.

Mara took the notebook from Livia. The pages showed three passes through the calibration game with the cohort acting as observers: Jonah’s pulse trace and breath cycles, Mara’s corresponding responses, and notes on which cues Livia had used in her readings. One row had a wide slash through it, crossed by an arrow.

Livia held Mara’s elbow. “I’m sorry. I’m sorry I touched it.”

The warmth of her hand felt welcome, and that was worse. “It’s fine.”

“It’s not. You haven’t touched the telemetry in two weeks. Not a single trace.”

Mara looked at the others. No one appeared shocked; none made themselves into a barrier. Only Jonah looked away.

Livia took the notebook. “You’re afraid seeing your responses will show you’re only capable of this, and this is not enough. So you’re not looking at anything until it’s too late to look.”

The read landed. Mara’s hands were shaking. She put her coffee in Adrian’s tray.

“That’s very specific,” Adrian said. “Thank you, Livia.”

His tone turned the event into something to be analyzed together. Mara did not like the sound of being analyzed together.

“If you’ll give me a few minutes,” she said.

“Of course.” He took the cup.

Mara left the room.
